[3.2.0] - 2026-10-03

Everything 3.1 was going to be, and the two things that stopped an update from arriving. Check for Updates now reads the version this computer actually installed instead of the version the shared runtime happens to carry, so it stops offering an update you already have; and Install and restart survives the launcher closing, so a one-click update installs instead of quietly doing nothing. It was prepared as 3.1.1 and never published under that number, so it ships as 3.2.0 (see below). Five new things, and fourteen fixes. Quill Radio checks your subscribed podcasts on its own and tells you what arrived, in the way each podcast asks for; there is a list of everything you have been told; you can leave yourself a note on any station or podcast; and Ask QUILL Radio answers questions about what is playing, on the ChatGPT plan you already pay for; and a Website button beside Play and Stop opens the station’s own site. Then: the sound card switches, or says why it cannot and puts the setting back; Windows Media gets a route to a device of its own; Preferences names only the engines this copy has; a minimized window comes back when its menu item or key asks for it; a stale “start with Windows” entry is repaired by the installer rather than waiting for a launch; Preferences opens in a blink instead of two seconds; and four smaller things that came in the same week. Also in 3.2.0: Apple’s podcast categories fill up and the Podcast Index says why when it cannot; browsing long lists stays smooth; Preferences has Find a setting; Undo History lists the last ten things you can take back; and a few choices can be shared with your other Quill apps.

  • Internet Archive searches put the best matches first. A listener looked for the Yours Truly, Johnny Dollar half-hour series that Old Time Radio Researchers uploaded on 1 October 2026 and found only older releases: Find on an Internet Archive folder, and the libraries part of Search, took the first 40 results in alphabetical order of the Archive’s item names, and the new uploads start with “YTJD”. They now ask the Archive for its own relevance order; browsing a folder keeps its stable order so paging still works (quill/core/radio/internet_archive.py).

  • Local Media: your own music, audiobooks and video, in playlists (Station > Local Media…, Ctrl+O). A peer window (Window menu, Ctrl+Tab) with your playlists and their items, and a second Browse Stations branch right under Favorites. Add Media Files (Ctrl+O), Add a Folder (Ctrl+Alt+O, recursive, in natural order, and the playlist follows the folder for new files), New Playlist, and Import or Export M3U/M3U8/PLS. With nothing added yet the window opens on a sentence and the Add Media Files button. Enter plays from an item and carries on; the playing row says playing or paused, and a file that moved says missing, with Locate and Remove Missing Items. Shuffle (Ctrl+H) is one stable order until Shuffle Again (Ctrl+Shift+H), repeat (Ctrl+R) is off, all or one, and Play Next, Add to Up Next, Stop After This Item and Continue Where I Left Off come with it. Next and previous are Ctrl+Right and Ctrl+Left in the window, and the chapter keys (Ctrl+Shift+. and Ctrl+Shift+,) in every window; Where Am I adds the place in the playlist. Alt+Shift+Up, Down, Home and End move items and say where they landed; Ctrl+X then Ctrl+V (or Ctrl+Alt+V) moves them further; Insert and Shift+Insert add files before or after an item; Delete removes from the playlist, never from the disk; Ctrl+Z undoes; Sort Playlist sorts once. Every verb is on the Applications key. Playlists are kept as paths and tags in a versioned radio-local-media.json, and nothing is sent anywhere (quill/core/radio/local_media*.py, quill/core/radio/browse_local_media.py, quill/ui/radio/local_media_*.py).

  • Play files from File Explorer, and make Quill Radio your media player. Julie asked whether she could pick a song on her external drive and choose Quill Radio to play it, as you would choose VLC. The installer now registers Quill Radio in Open with for music, audiobooks, video and playlists (MP3, M4A, M4B, AAC, Ogg, Opus, FLAC, WAV, WMA, AIFF, MKA, MP4, MKV, WebM, MOV, M3U, M3U8, PLS), and in Settings > Apps > Default apps, without ever taking a type over; uninstalling removes every key. Preferences > Windows and your files > Make Quill Radio My Media Player… (also in the Command Palette) registers a portable copy for your account and opens Windows’ Default apps page for Quill Radio, where you choose – the same shared command, words and registration as the editors’ Make QUILL My Text Editor. quill-radio <file> ... plays the files; when Quill Radio is already running they go to that copy through its single-instance queue and play without pulling the window in front. Several files at once (Explorer starts the app once per file) become one Opened files list in Local Media that plays from the first, so Next and Previous work; the next files opened replace it unless Save as Playlist… (Ctrl+S in the Local Media window, or on its Browse menu) keeps it. What is playing is said once; a missing or unplayable file is one plain sentence. A folder plays like Add a Folder, and an M3U or PLS is imported into Local Media once and played. Two right-click verbs on every supported type: Play with Quill Radio and Add to Quill Radio Playlist. (quill/core/windows_media.py, quill/core/windows_installer_lines.py, quill/core/radio/opened_files.py, quill/ui/radio/opened_files_ui.py, quill/ui/radio/media_player_registration.py, standalone/radio/installer/quill-radio.iss.)

  • Activity (Help, Shift+F9) and Repeat Last Result (F9). Everything the radio told you this session, newest first, each with its next actions – Retry, Open Folder, Copy Details – and one key that says the newest result that mattered again. A settings file that could not be saved is said once, with the reason, and offers Retry and Open Folder in Activity; a later save that works is said too (quill/core/activity.py, quill/ui/activity_window.py, quill/core/persistence_outcome.py).

  • Check for Updates knows which version you installed. A listener reported that Check for Updates kept offering an update after that update was installed. Every QuillVille installer ships the shared QuillVille Runtime, and the runtime carries one frozen copy of every app’s code and every app’s version number – so the version Quill Radio reported was whichever runtime was newest on the computer rather than the Quill Radio the installer put there, and the comparison against the published release was against the wrong number. It can be wrong in both directions: an update offered again and again when you already have it, or “you are up to date” when you are not. Now the installer writes the version it installed into the app’s own folder (quill-app-version.ini, beside QuillRadio.exe), Quill Radio looks for it there rather than in the shared runtime’s folder, and Check for Updates and Help > About read it; About names the runtime’s code version beside it only when the two differ, which is exactly what support needs to see. A portable copy carries its own code, so there its own number is the truth (quill/core/app_version.py, standalone/radio/installer/quill-radio.iss).

  • Install and restart installs. Choosing Install and restart closed Quill Radio and then nothing happened at all – no installer, no error, and no log to look in. The QuillVille launcher runs the app inside a Windows Job Object that kills everything left in it when the launcher exits, and the small update helper the app starts was inside that job: the helper waits for the app and the launcher to close before it runs the installer, and Windows killed it mid-wait. The helper is now started outside the job (CREATE_BREAKAWAY_FROM_JOB, and through Windows’ own process service when a launcher already installed forbids breakaway), the new launcher permits breakaway, and the folder the helper logs to is created before it writes there. A refusal is reported instead of swallowed, so the app stays open and Open folder still works (quill/core/self_update.py, quill/native/launcher/launcher.c).

  • This one update is by hand, once. A copy of 3.0.4 or earlier still runs the old helper, and that helper is what this release replaces – so Install and restart cannot install 3.2.0 from 3.0.4. Download Quill-Radio-Setup-Shared-3.2.0.exe and run it, or unpack the portable zip over your copy, this once. Every update after this one installs itself.

  • Backups say what they hold, and name anything they leave out. A listener about to reset two computers, with recordings and backups in a OneDrive folder, heard that some items were skipped and nothing about which. That was Export My Setup, which is shared with QUILL Cast and counted the Cast stores a Radio-only computer has never made as “skipped”; nothing was lost, but nothing said so. Now every backup, restore, Export My Setup and Import My Setup that leaves anything out says how many and offers Show what was left out, and why? – one row per item with its reason (stored only in OneDrive, in use by another program, a path too long for Windows, could not be read) and what to do – and a backup also writes the list to Recent Problems. Back Up Stations and Settings (Ctrl+Shift+U) now carries everything a Radio listener builds: the podcasts you follow and where you are in each episode, notes, tags, bookmarks, Go To, reminders, recording settings and more, where it used to carry only favorites, settings, the wake-up timer, the recording schedule and tags. If your favorites, settings or podcasts cannot be read the backup fails and says why, rather than saving one without them, and the finished file is read back before success is announced. Recordings come from the folder Recording Settings names – the backup used to look in the default Music folder whatever you had chosen – and a recording stored only in OneDrive is downloaded as it is copied, with progress, unless there is not room for it. Backups in the recordings folder are not copied into the next one, and a restore leaves a recording that is already there alone (quill/core/radio/backup.py, quill/core/radio/backup_recordings.py, quill/core/skipped_files.py, quill/ui/radio/skipped_items_dialog.py).

  • Portable copies start from a folder whose name has a space in it. A portable copy unpacked to a folder such as C:\portable\Quill Radio said it did not start, with “can’t open file …pythonw.exe”. The QuillVille launcher passed its own path to Python unquoted, so Python read it as two words. The same mistake split a file opened from File Explorer out of a folder with a space in its name, which Play with Quill Radio depends on. Every 3.0 release had it (quill/native/launcher/cmdline.c, quill/native/launcher/launcher.c).

  • Why 3.2.0, straight from 3.0.4. This release was prepared as 3.1.1 (and before that 3.1.0) and never published under either number, so there was never a public 3.1 and the next published version is 3.2.0. It also settles the old muddle: Quill Radio’s number sat at 3.1.0 in the shared source for four days while QUILL Lite 1.1 was released, so the runtime Lite installed carries a Quill Radio that calls itself 3.1.0; 3.2.0 is above anything a runtime can already claim, so every copy is offered it (scripts/check_sibling_versions.py fails a release build that would repeat this).

  • A Website button beside Play and Stop. A listener asked for a way to reach Double Tap Live’s schedule. The main window now has Website (We&bsite, Alt+B) right after the Play/Stop button in Tab order. It opens the website of the station that is playing, or of the selected favorite when nothing is playing, in your browser, and says which site it opened; a station that gave no website is said so (“Mystery FM did not give a website.”) rather than opening anything (quill/ui/radio/station_website_button.py, tests/unit/ui/radio/test_station_website_button.py).

  • Ask QUILL Radio (Community menu, Ctrl+Shift+8): a conversation that knows what is playing, on your own ChatGPT plan. The radio’s own assistant, and the first AI in Quill Radio. It runs on the ChatGPT subscription you already pay for – Use My ChatGPT Subscription (Alt+F5) opens your browser on OpenAI’s own sign-in page, you allow “QUILL Radio” to use your plan, and you come back signed in; no key, no per-question bill, and nothing through QUILL’s servers – and it knows one thing ChatGPT on the web does not: the station and the title the stream is announcing go with every message (it works from that text alone: it never listens to the stream, records or transcribes). “What is this song, and who made it?”, “tell me about this station”, “what was the news story they just mentioned” need no names typed, and Ask About What’s Playing asks for you. A Quick questions list offers nine prepared questions: six about what is playing, and three that attach something you keep – your favorites, the songs logged on this station, or the stations you have played recently – each saying exactly what and how many before you press Enter, so nothing about you leaves the computer unless you choose it. It is a peer window on the Window menu with the transport keys live, replies are read aloud as they arrive, and the model is your plan’s own list. Allow web search is off until you check it. Only a refresh token is kept, in the credential store under Quill Radio’s own name; Sign Out (two presses) revokes it at OpenAI and forgets it here, Forget on This Computer forgets it here only, and neither button exists until you are signed in. Deliberately absent: QUILL’s free AI service and an API-key route – it is your plan, or nothing, and the window says so. Off in Safe Mode (quill/core/ai/chatgpt_account.py, quill/ui/radio/ask_radio_window.py, quill/ui/radio/ask_radio_wiring.py).

  • The portable copy has the Windows Media engine too. A stick copy of an early 3.1 build offered only “Windows Media (classic)” in Preferences and could not change the sound card, though an installed copy could: the modern engine’s Windows components (the pywinrt wheels) were frozen into the shared runtime the installer ships and were never listed for the portable build. The portable now carries them, so Audio > Output Device, the now-playing card and the modern engine behave the same way in both downloads (standalone/studio/scripts/build_portable.py).

  • Double Tap Live, in Browse Stations. Double Tap – the daily show where blind people talk tech, from Steven Scott and Shaun Preece at Accessible Media Inc. – launched a 24-hour radio channel on 30 September 2026: talk, tech, music and the best of the podcast, around the clock. It is its own branch directly under ACB Media, one station, listed with no network call and playing from the station’s own Radio.co stream; What’s Playing reads what the stream announces, and it can be favorited, recorded and scheduled like any station (quill/core/radio/double_tap.py).

  • The sound card switches, live, or says why it cannot and gives the setting back. A listener reported that choosing a device in Audio > Output Device did not move the sound. libmpv switches devices at runtime and on reload (probed on a real machine with two cards, watching mpv’s own audio-output log), and Quill Radio handed it the device before every load – so the sound stayed put in exactly one case: the mpv engine could not open the chosen device (a Bluetooth or USB headset asleep, a device another program holds, an id Windows changed), the load failed, and the station was quietly rescued on Windows Media, which plays on the device Windows gives the app. Nothing was said, nothing was logged, and the setting kept naming a device that was not in use. Now: a playing mpv station switches device live, with no reconnect and no gap; a load that fails with a device chosen is retried once, still on mpv, on the device in use before the choice, the listener hears which device could not be opened and what the setting is now, and the setting itself goes back (“… could not be opened, so the output device is back to System default”); a saved device that will not open at start-up is given back the same way; and a rescue that lands on Windows Media with a device chosen is said out loud instead of hidden. Every decision is logged (quill/ui/radio/output_device_guard.py; the mpv client now reports a refused property instead of swallowing it).

  • Windows Media plays to the sound card you choose. Quill Radio’s Windows Media engine is Windows’ modern media player (Windows.Media.Playback.MediaPlayer, quill/ui/audio/winrt_engine.py), and it takes a device, so Audio > Output Device moves the sound whichever engine you are on – live, with no reconnect. A device that will not open is named and the setting goes back rather than pointing at something you cannot hear. The engine choice itself is left alone: an earlier draft of this release switched the engine to Automatic to get around the old limitation, and it does not – the engine is yours. On a machine without the modern player Quill Radio falls back to the classic wx.media control, which has no device API at all; there, and only there, choosing a device says so in one sentence and opens Windows’ own Sound settings, where under Volume mixer every app has its own output device and the choice sticks across restarts – no question first. This is the answer QUILL Cast already gives (quill/ui/media/output_device.py), now shared with the radio.

  • Preferences offers only the playback engines this copy has. With mpv present the rows are Automatic (recommended, uses mpv), Windows Media and mpv; without it there is no mpv row and Automatic reads “Automatic (uses Windows Media; mpv is not installed)”, and a saved mpv preference lands on Automatic, which is what it resolves to. The Windows Media row reads “Windows Media (classic)” only on a machine without the modern player, so the name says which one this copy has. A row for an engine that is not installed was a choice that did nothing (quill/core/radio/playback_engine_choices.py).

  • Ctrl+T says the title every time. What’s Playing said station, title and artist the first time and only the station after that, though Copy still copied all three (reported by John). Each press was closing the Now Playing window and opening a fresh one, and a screen reader reads a brand-new window once; a replacement with the same title and the same focused field got the title bar and nothing else. A second press now refreshes the window that is open, brings it forward and speaks the text (quill/ui/radio/now_playing_dialog.py, now_playing_commands.py).

  • Enter in Preferences presses OK. After changing a dropdown, such as the playback engine, Enter did nothing: the dropdown kept the key instead of handing it to the default button. Enter now presses OK from anywhere in the dialog except a button, which is its own answer, and a multi-line box, where it is a new line (quill/ui/app_preferences_dialog.py, shared by every app’s Preferences).

  • Delete on a source branch hides the source. On a top-level branch such as Podcasts, Delete used to explain that there was nothing to delete and point at Hide This Source on the context menu, although in earlier versions the key itself hid the source. It does again, through the same function the menu uses, so the two cannot disagree; Reset Sources to Default is the way back and the announcement names it (quill/ui/radio/browse_delete.py).

  • Quill Radio checks your subscribed podcasts and tells you what arrived. Set how often in Preferences, or on one podcast in its own settings – Manually only through Once a day, and both Quill Radio and QUILL Cast read the one setting now, so turning it on in either turns it on. (They used to read two, which meant setting it in one app did nothing in the other; a cadence you had already chosen in Radio is carried over once.) Per podcast, Check this feed for new episodes can say Manually only while the rest keep their cadence, and Refresh on a row always asks whatever it says. Nothing subscribed, or everything set to never, and no check happens at all – no thread, no request (quill/core/podcasts/episode_alerts.py).

  • What happens when something arrives is yours to set, globally or per podcast. Notify me shows a desktop notification and plays a short new sound. Quietly adds it to Notifications and says nothing at all – which is what makes a daily show bearable, because nine new episodes is something you want to be able to find rather than something you want announced nine times. Not at all records nothing. Quiet is the default: an app that starts putting notifications over what you are reading has made a decision that was yours. Quiet hours hold back the notification and the sound and deliberately not the list entry – being quiet about something is not the same as never having been told.

  • Notifications… (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F3, Help menu): everything the apps have told you. A desktop notification is a good way to be told something and a terrible way to remember it – it appears over what you were reading, leaves on its own schedule, and if your screen reader was mid-sentence it may never have been read at all. So the notification is not the record; this list is. One line per item, newest first, unread marked with the word “New” rather than a colour or a bullet so it survives being read aloud. Enter opens what it was about. It is a shared list, so a notification Quill Radio raised is one QUILL Cast can show you (quill/core/notifications.py, quill/ui/notification_center.py).

  • A note to yourself on any station or podcast. Note to Self… on any row, beside Set a Reminder. Whatever you write is read back in the details pane the next time you arrow onto that row – which is the point: a note you have to open a window to see is a note you have to remember you wrote. “The morning show is the good one.” “Only worth it on Sundays.” Emptying the box removes the note, and it is kept against the station’s address rather than its name, so it survives a rename or a catalogue refresh (quill/core/radio/item_notes.py).

  • Find a station by what is known about it: the team it carries, and your own tags. Listeners asked to store what they know about a station – which team it carries – and find it that way. Edit Station Tags… on every live station’s row menu (Browse and Search rows, Search Stations results, the main window’s favorites and the Favorites Manager) and, for the station on air, Edit Tags for the Playing Station in the Command Palette: one labelled box for your tags, comma separated, with the directory’s tags shown read-only below it. A favorite keeps its tags in the favorites file, so they ride along in backups and Export My Setup; any other station’s tags go in radio-station-tags.json, which both carry too. Your tags appear in Station Details and the details box beside the directory’s. Quill Radio also ships a hand-checked list of English-language radio flagships for MLB, NFL, NBA, WNBA, NHL and MLS teams (quill/data/sports_flagships.json, a public source recorded for every team; teams that only stream or could not be confirmed are left out). Search Stations, Search All Sources and the Favorites filter now match station names, directory tags, your tags, and the flagship list’s team names, aliases, leagues and sports, so Tigers, Detroit baseball and MLB all find WXYT, and a row found that way says why (“carries the Detroit Tigers”, “has your tag Red Wings”). The list holds call signs, never stream addresses: a match is turned into a playable row from the local station catalog first, and from Radio Browser’s ordinary name search only when the catalog has nothing, a few times per search at most (quill/core/radio/station_tags.py, sports_flagships.py, station_lookup.py, quill/ui/radio/station_tags_dialog.py, station_lookup_lane.py).

  • Windows can say what Quill Radio is playing. The now-playing card on the volume flyout and the lock screen – one per app, and Quill Radio’s was blank – now carries the station with the song underneath. The screen reader reads that flyout when it opens, so the machine can answer “what is playing?” rather than only the app knowing. It claims none of the media keys: those are already the app’s through RegisterHotKey, which works from the tray and without focus, so every SMTC button flag is switched off deliberately and a test asserts they stay off. It also says nothing out loud – the reader reads the card, and saying it again is the over-announcing GATE-13 exists to stop. Live radio additionally asks Media Foundation for latency over smoothness (real_time_playback), and a transport control the engine says cannot work is now disabled rather than enabled-and-silent (quill/ui/audio/now_playing.py).

  • Preferences opens at once instead of after two seconds. Reported: “why is it taking two seconds or longer to bring up preferences?” The output-device list behind that window asked Windows for every device on the computer and then picked the sound cards out in Python – on the reporter’s machine, 4,048 devices in 2.4 seconds, to find 2. pywinrt will not take DeviceInformation.find_all_async’s overloads positionally (both spellings raise “Invalid parameter count”), which is why the first version swept everything; it names its overloads instead, and find_all_async_device_class returns the real sound cards in 8 milliseconds. The family list ran that sweep twice and hit its own five-second timeout, so the device list could also come back empty. The machine-wide sweep is kept as the fallback for a runtime without the named overload – slow, never broken (quill/ui/audio/winrt_engine.py).

  • Running from a source checkout finds the mpv it already has. Reported: “why is mpv not working when running the run-quill-radio script from the d:\quill folder? I only see automatic and windows media.” The engine list was right – this release offers no row for an engine this copy does not have – but the search for libmpv looked at every shipped location (the QUILL_LIBMPV override, an Offline Edition’s tools\mpv, the runtime folder, the downloaded engine pack) and never at the repository’s own build\deps\mpv, which is exactly where the installer scripts stage it. A source run now looks there too, and a frozen build never does – a shipped app must load only what was shipped with it (quill/ui/audio/mpv_engine.py).

  • A “start with Windows” entry an older build wrote is repaired by the installer. A listener who had set Quill Radio to start with Windows met PyInstaller’s “Unhandled exception in script” box at every login instead of the radio – “Failed to execute script ‘runtime_launcher’… ‘NoneType’ object has no attribute ‘write’” (reported 2026-09-29). Builds between the shared-runtime move and 3.0.1 wrote the entry as the bare QuillVilleRuntime.exe, which is the engine rather than an app; run with no module it printed an explanation, and a windowed process has no console to print to, so writing it was the crash. 3.0.2 fixed both halves – the runtime’s streams, and a bare launch starting the installed app – and Quill Radio has repaired the entry at every launch since. That repair needed a launch, which is the one thing the broken entry prevented: nothing told the listener that opening the radio by hand would cure their login. The installer now repairs the entry as it stages the new build, before the next login, and names this copy’s own QuillRadio.exe so the entry survives the next runtime upgrade. An app you never asked to start with Windows stays absent, a portable copy is left alone, and a locked-down registry costs the repair and not the install (quill/core/runtime_cli.py, installer/shared-runtime.iss).

  • A minimized window comes back when its menu item or key asks for it. A listener reported that the recordings list was out of reach: Record > Recordings… and Ctrl+Shift+R did nothing, though the folder itself was fine. Since 3.0 every big surface – Recordings, Browse Stations, Search Stations, Favorites, the player – is a window of its own, and every opener asks the WindowManager to raise one that is already open rather than build a second copy. activate() called Show() then Raise(), and on Windows neither restores an iconized frame: a window minimized from the taskbar, Windows+M or Windows+D counted as open and stayed minimized, the opener returned None, and the command went silent with nothing said. activate() now restores an iconized frame first, for every peer window, then brings it forward with focus on the control it was left on (WindowManager.activate in quill/ui/window_menu.py).

  • A portable copy’s Yes to “copy my favorites” now copies them. A 3.0.0 portable that had been opened once had a favorites file with nothing in it, and the import skipped every file the bundle already had – so the question was asked, answered Yes, remembered as done, and nothing arrived. A favorites file with no favorites is replaced; everything else the bundle has is still left alone (quill/core/radio/portable_migration.py).

  • Apple’s podcast categories fill up, and the Podcast Index says why when it cannot (2026-10-03). A category or subcategory under Podcasts (Apple) now lists Apple’s own top shows for that category, up to 200. It used to filter the storefront’s overall chart by category, which left History with four shows and most subcategories with none; History and Comedy Fiction are full lists now (quill/core/podcasts/apple_genre_charts.py). A Podcast Index folder that cannot load now says why instead of “Nothing in here”. With no Podcast Index key, the branch shows one row, Add a Podcast Index Key…, which opens the Podcast Index Credentials window; a free key comes from api.podcastindex.org/signup, and a key entered in Quill Radio or QUILL Cast works in both. A key the service rejects is reported as not accepted, not as “could not be reached” (quill/core/radio/browse_failure.py, browse_podcast_index.py, quill/core/podcasts/podcast_index.py).

  • Smoother browsing in long lists. Arrowing through a long list such as Top Podcasts no longer loads every show you pass. Quill Radio waits until you stop on one, so the list stays responsive with a screen reader, and a big folder fills in one go (quill/ui/radio/browse_prefetch.py).

  • Find a setting in Preferences. A box at the top of Preferences: type a word, press Down to reach the matching settings, and Enter goes to one. Ctrl+F returns to the box, and searching never changes or saves a setting. It is the same box QUILL Cast and QUILL Lite have (quill/ui/preferences_search.py).

  • Undo History (Edit menu, Ctrl+Alt+Shift+A). The last ten things you can take back, newest first, each with Undo This One, so a mistake from three steps ago can be undone without losing what came after. Ctrl+Z still takes back the newest, and the one before when pressed again. QUILL Cast has the same list (quill/core/undo_last.py, quill/ui/undo_history_dialog.py).

  • Share these choices with my other Quill apps. A Preferences checkbox, off until you turn it on. With it on here and in another Quill app, Announce dialog transitions stays the same in both, and Quill Radio says when it picks up a choice from another app. Nothing about keys, focus or the screen reader is shared (quill/core/family_preferences.py, quill/ui/family_sharing.py).

  • Each app keeps its own ChatGPT sign-in. Signing in to QUILL Cast, QUILL Lite or QUILL no longer overwrites Quill Radio’s sign-in, and signing out of one leaves the others signed in. Use My ChatGPT Subscription (Alt+F5) says when another app on this computer is already signed in, so signing in here is one Allow in the browser (quill/core/ai/chatgpt_state.py).

Three answers, meant to work together (<code>core/radio/schedule_wake.py</code>, <code>platform/windows/recording_wake_task.py</code>, <code>ui/radio/schedule_wake_ui.py</code>): the scheduling window <strong>states the requirement</strong> (Quill Radio must be running; the tray counts); standby is <strong>held off for the few minutes before</strong> a recording is due, re-evaluated on the existing one-minute tick since “a recording is now five minutes away” is not a playback state change; and Windows is asked to <strong>wake the machine</strong> a couple of minutes beforehand via a per-user Task Scheduler entry with <code>WakeToRun</code>, replaced whenever the schedule changes and removed when nothing is scheduled. The two automatic halves are separate Preferences checkboxes, both on by default – inhibiting standby is local and permission-free, while waking the machine changes how the computer behaves, and one switch could not express wanting only the first. All of it is best effort: a machine whose policy blocks <code>schtasks</code> keeps the other two defences and never sees an error.</li> <li><strong>Choose Browse Sources (Station menu).</strong> Every branch of Browse Stations can be hidden (<code>core/radio/browse_visibility.py</code>, <code>ui/radio/browse_sources_dialog.py</code>), under the same rule as Search Sources: a branch that is off is not in the tree at all and is never contacted. Rows say their own state (“On. LibriVox Audiobooks. …”), one button flips the focused branch, and hiding everything leaves one honest row saying the way back. Stored as an explicit choice with “never set” kept distinct, so a source added in a later release appears on its own for anyone who never touched the setting.</li> <li><strong>Download Preferences (Station menu, and a button inside View > Downloads).</strong> The queue’s standing rules got their surface (<code>ui/radio/download_prefs_dialog.py</code>): the downloads folder, a folder per show and per book, author grouping once an author has more than one, keep-going-on-close, and ask-me-where – asked once per batch, never per chapter, with a cancelled ask cancelling the enqueue out loud. A live sentence in the dialog answers “what will happen to the next thing I save?”, and a saved change takes effect on the very next download.</li> <li><strong>A download queue, and downloaded books that play like books.</strong> Downloads go through one queue (<code>core/radio/download_queue.py</code>, <code>ui/radio/download_runner.py</code>): one transfer at a time in the order asked, because every source behind this is a free library run on donations and four parallel fetches is a worse citizen for no audible benefit. <strong>View > Downloads… (Ctrl+Shift+J)</strong> shows it – waiting, going, saved, failed – with finished rows kept until cleared, since “did that actually download?” is the question asked most. Cancel one, remove one, clear the finished, clear the lot; every path keeps what is already on disk. Closing the window either keeps the queue running or stops it, per preference, and <strong>says which either way</strong>. <strong>Where things land is arranged</strong> (<code>core/radio/download_prefs.py</code>): a podcast under its show, a book in a folder of its own, and an author folder only once there is a second book by them – an author folder holding one book is a folder you open and immediately leave. Reserved and trailing-dot names are made usable, because a folder Windows silently renames merges two books into one. <strong>And a downloaded book plays as one</strong> (<code>core/radio/downloaded_books.py</code>, <code>ui/radio/book_playback.py</code>): chapters in natural order (2 before 10), auto-advance when one ends, each announcing “4 of 40” position-first, and the end of a book said out loud rather than left as silence. The folder <em>is</em> the record – no index, no scan, so moving or renaming a chapter by hand simply changes the book. <code>ui/radio/track_end.py</code> is the GATE-11 extraction that made room: an end-of-playback is three different events (a dropped live stream, a chapter finishing, a recording genuinely ending) and the order they are asked in is the logic.</li> <li><strong>Continue Listening includes files on this computer.</strong> The position store keys on a file’s <em>contents</em> so your place survives moving and renaming, which is why it deliberately stores no path – and why such files could never be listed. A local-only sidecar (<code>core/media/local_paths.py</code>) records where each was last seen; it is never synced, because a path is a fact about one machine and two machines disagreeing about it is two correct answers rather than a conflict. A file that has moved is skipped rather than offered and then failing; its position is untouched and is found again next time it plays.</li> <li><strong>Download what is yours to keep – a file, or a whole book.</strong> Quill Radio played a great deal that is genuinely yours (public-domain audiobooks, old-time radio, Creative Commons music, podcast episodes) and could save none of it. <strong>Download…</strong> on a row saves it where you choose; <strong>Download All Files…</strong> on a book’s folder saves every chapter, in order, off the UI thread so you keep listening throughout. In order matters: a part-finished book is the first twelve chapters, something you can start, rather than a scattering. A part-finished file resumes rather than restarting; one bad address costs one chapter and is named in the summary; Stop takes effect inside a chapter, not at the end of a 90 MB one, and keeps what arrived. Progress is counted in chapters (“12 of 40”), never a percentage of a total nobody has. <strong>The rights rule is affirmative</strong> (<code>core/radio/downloadable.py</code>): a source must be on the allowed list or it is refused, because never assuming a downloadable-looking file may be redistributed is the whole discipline. Four refusals, each with its own sentence rather than a missing menu item: a <strong>live station</strong> has no file and is pointed at Record Station; <strong>Spotify</strong> is copy-protected; <strong>YouTube</strong> is a deliberate decision, not a limitation; <strong>Audius</strong> depends on the artist’s choice, which the listing does not state, so Quill Radio does not guess. A <strong>Creative Commons licence is written beside the audio</strong>, because saving the work and dropping the terms strips exactly what the licence exists to carry.</li> <li><strong>Audius, Mixcloud and ccMixter can be searched after all.</strong> All three were carried as “browse only” – the tree offers trending, categories and tags, and that had hardened into a recorded belief that the services published no keyword search. All three do, and always did; it was one URL away in each case, and the existing parsers needed no change, because a search result and a trending result are the same object from the same service. Type a title in Find Stations and their rows arrive with everything else. Audius tracks and ccMixter uploads play in Quill Radio (a ccMixter row carries its Creative Commons licence in the row); a Mixcloud row is still <strong>metadata only</strong> and opens the show’s page in your browser, which the row says before you press Enter. The machinery for reporting a genuinely unsearchable source stays, because the next source added may be one – and having nowhere honest to record that is what let three wrong claims sit unchallenged.</li> <li><strong>Everything you started, in one list.</strong> <strong>Playback > Continue Listening…</strong> shows every recording begun and unfinished, newest first, with how far in you are – “The Moonstone, chapter 4, recording, 10 minutes in, 8% through”. Resume starts one where you left off through exactly the path Browse uses; <strong>Forget This One</strong> drops the saved place and removes the row, because a resume list you cannot clear fills with things you abandoned on purpose. A live station never appears, and neither does anything finished. Saved places from before this release still resume when reopened but cannot be <em>listed</em>: the store kept a position and no name to show, and it now keeps both.</li> <li><strong>Anything with an end now remembers where you stopped</strong> – a LibriVox chapter, an Old Time Radio episode, a podcast episode. A live station does not and should not: you tune in and you are where everybody else is, so offering to “resume” one would be offering nonsense. Two deliberate refusals inside it: four seconds in is not a position, so saving one clears the entry rather than leaving a prompt to dismiss for no gain; and finishing clears it too, so replaying starts at the start rather than at the closing credits. Every failure – an unwritable profile, a corrupt file, a full disk – degrades to “no saved position” and never reaches the player.</li> </ul> <h3 id="transcripts-learned-to-keep-time">Transcripts learned to keep time</h3> <ul> <li><strong>A transcript now knows when each line is spoken.</strong> Quill Cast could already fetch a podcast’s transcript, read it, cache it, search it, and open it as a QUILL document. What it could not do was connect it to the audio, because the parser discarded the timings – exactly right for “open this as a document”, and useless for anything that follows along. Transcripts are now read into timed lines, which is what makes <em>follow the audio</em>, <em>jump playback to this line</em>, and “found at 12 minutes 8 seconds” possible at all.</li> <li><strong>YouTube captions stopped being thrown away.</strong> Every time Quill Radio resolved a YouTube link it also received that video’s caption track, and dropped it on the floor. That format is now understood alongside WebVTT, SubRip and Podcasting 2.0’s own, so a YouTube video’s transcript costs nothing extra – it had already been downloaded.</li> <li><strong>Nothing you already relied on changed.</strong> The plain-text form of a transcript is now defined as the timed form with the timings removed, so there is one reader rather than two that drift apart, and every transcript Cast could read before reads identically.</li> </ul> <h3 id="the-playlist-formats-the-internet-actually-uses">The playlist formats the internet actually uses</h3> <ul> <li><strong>PLS, XSPF and ASX files now open.</strong> Quill Radio understood M3U, which covers a lot of the web and not the part you usually have in hand. A “Listen Live” link is at least as likely to be a <code>.pls</code> – the standard SHOUTcast and Icecast listen link – or an <code>.xspf</code>, which is what the Xiph directory itself serves, or an <code>.asx</code>, which is still what several radio reading services publish. ASX in the wild is frequently not valid XML at all, so it is read twice: once properly, and once forgivingly when the file will not parse.</li> <li><strong>A live stream can no longer be imported as a list of stations.</strong> An <code>.m3u8</code> file is either a playlist of stations or a live stream’s manifest, and the two share an extension <em>and</em> a first line. Handing the second to the playlist importer produced a list of two-second fragments presented to you as stations, which is a genuinely baffling thing to be shown. They are told apart before anything else happens, and what is inside the file wins over what the file is called – a server naming a live stream <code>.m3u</code> is common.</li> <li><strong>Favorites export in four formats.</strong> M3U, PLS, XSPF and ASX, every one of which reads back in. A station name containing an ampersand survives the round trip, which is not true of most playlist writers.</li> <li><strong>A playlist file can no longer attack you.</strong> XSPF and ASX are XML from strangers, so they are now read with entity expansion switched off: a small crafted file that would expand to gigabytes of memory is refused out loud instead of opened.</li> </ul> <h3 id="the-real-optilab-engine-not-an-imitation-of-it">The real OptiLab engine, not an imitation of it</h3> <ul> <li><strong>Exact OptiLab processing.</strong> Quill Radio’s three broadcast-polish modes have always been a faithful <em>adaptation</em> of <strong>OptiLab Core by Lanes Audio / dgl1984</strong> – ffmpeg filter chains that reproduce the shape of its modes. The engine itself is now included (vendored unmodified at v1.4.0, Apache-2.0 with the Commons Clause, its LICENSE and NOTICE shipped beside it) and one new choice in Sound Enhancements says where it runs: <strong>off</strong> (the built-in chain everywhere, the default), <strong>when saving</strong> (recordings and converted files), or <strong>when saving and while listening</strong>.</li> <li><strong>Saved files are the recommended setting, and cost nothing.</strong> A recording is processed after it finishes – and after any dropped-and-resumed parts are joined – writing a new file that replaces the original only once it exists, so nothing that goes wrong in the engine can cost you a recording. A raw stream capture is never post-processed, because re-encoding it is exactly what raw capture exists to avoid.</li> <li><strong>Listening through the engine is possible, and it is a trade.</strong> The engine is a separate program, and live playback normally hands the audio to nothing: Quill Radio tells the player what to apply and the player does it, which is why every slider is audible the instant you move it. Running the real engine live means routing the stream through that program, so the station starts a little slower, uses more processor, and needs a brief reconnect each time you change a setting. That is stated in the option itself rather than discovered afterwards, and it is why the default is off.</li> <li><strong>Never both at once.</strong> Whichever setting you choose, the built-in polish leaves the chain when the real engine is running, so audio is never processed twice. The equalizer, Even Out Volume, channel mode and night mode are unaffected either way.</li> <li><strong>Honest about the difference.</strong> The one thing the built-in chain provably cannot do is OptiLab’s limiter feedback loop – easing the lift while the final limiter works hard – because a filter chain cannot see how hard a later stage is working. There are smaller differences (no gated AGC, no six-band density, no adaptive bass, different output ceilings), and on gentle material the two may be indistinguishable.</li> <li><strong>Entirely optional.</strong> Where the OptiLab component is not part of your build, the choice is disabled and says so, and everything else works exactly as before.</li> </ul> <h3 id="fixes-to-things-that-were-quietly-wrong">Fixes to things that were quietly wrong</h3> <ul> <li><strong>The Xiph genre list was losing 412 genres every time it loaded, and the number changed on every refresh.</strong> That directory’s index had grown past a size limit Quill Radio applies when reading any page, so it was being cut off mid-file. The reader is deliberately forgiving of a mangled page – right for a website tweak, wrong for a size limit – so it failed in perfect silence. The limit now fits the page, and a page that ever outgrows it again says so rather than dropping entries.</li> <li><strong>That same genre list was also sorted into uselessness.</strong> Xiph publishes its genres in order of how many stations use them: various, Pop, Rock, Dance, 80s, House, Jazz. Quill Radio sorted that alphabetically, so the list opened on <code>00</code>, <code>00s</code>, <code>100.1</code> and <code>104.5</code>, and Jazz was three thousand rows further down. The directory’s own order is now kept, obvious non-genres are dropped, and the branch offers the 120 most-used rather than every free-text string three thousand broadcasters have ever typed into a field.</li> <li><strong>A station that hiccuped once was dead, and said “Stopped”.</strong> Reported against KFI Los Angeles: plays for about twenty seconds, then stops; another station replayed its last five seconds. iHeart serves those stations as HLS – three ten-second segments, a thirty-second window, behind a redirect to a per-listener address whose token expires in five seconds and is topped up every ten. Miss <strong>one</strong> refill and the buffered audio keeps playing for twenty to thirty seconds and then runs out; Quill Radio read that as the stream ending and stopped. Fixed in three places. <strong>The connection heals itself</strong>: the player was given no instruction to reconnect a dropped read at all, so one transient failure was final; it now reconnects at that level, silently, because nothing was lost. <strong>A genuinely dropped station is reconnected out loud</strong>: three attempts at two, five and fifteen seconds, each announced with its number (“Reconnecting to KFI AM 640. Attempt 1 of 3.”), “Reconnected” when it works, and a plain statement that the station may be off the air when it does not – a player that retries in silence is indistinguishable from one that has hung. A <em>recording</em> is excluded, because reaching the end of a LibriVox chapter is not a fault, and pressing Stop or playing something else cancels a waiting retry rather than letting it seize playback later. <strong>And iHeart stations now use their steadier stream</strong>: the progressive form rather than HLS, which is one long connection with no segment window, no five-second token and no per-listener session to lose – removing the failure mode rather than recovering from it, with HLS kept as the fallback. The network timeout also moved from fifteen seconds to thirty, which was tight for a playlist that only advances every ten.</li> <li><strong>A recording that captured nothing said nothing at all.</strong> Reported alongside the dropout above: Record on a station that would not stay connected gave no confirmation that a recording had started, none that it had stopped, and left an empty recordings folder. A recording file is created the moment recording begins – before any audio arrives – so its existence never meant anything had been recorded, and a capture that ended having recorded nothing was treated exactly like a finished one. Such a capture is now reported as a failure, in words, with the reason (“the connection failed”, “the station refused the connection”, “that stream address is no longer there”, “the disk is full”, “that folder could not be written to”), on the error sound rather than the saved sound so the two can never be confused, and the empty file is removed rather than left to be found. Where the reason genuinely is not knowable it says so rather than inventing one.</li> <li><strong>TuneIn stations could be handed the fragile stream format when a steadier one was on offer.</strong> The same fault as the iHeart dropout above, arriving through a different directory: 96.5 The Fan in Kansas City is listed with both an HLS manifest and a plain MP3, and Quill Radio took the manifest – the form that has to be topped up every few seconds and dies twenty to thirty seconds after one failed top-up. It now prefers the steadier form. Deliberately <strong>only when both addresses come from the same host</strong>, and that restriction is the interesting part: for that very station the two addresses are served by different companies, and the second one’s own parameters name a different station id and a music genre where the station is sports. Preferring it blindly could play somebody an entirely different broadcaster, which is far worse than a dropout – and where the hosts differ, the automatic reconnection above covers it anyway.</li> <li><strong>TuneIn could hand you an unencrypted stream when an encrypted one was on offer.</strong> Where a station returned several addresses, Quill Radio took the first that was not TuneIn’s own un-followable redirect – which is not the same as taking the best. It now prefers HTTPS, while still preferring a working plain address over an encrypted one that nothing can play.</li> <li><strong>Browse levels are remembered between sessions.</strong> Opening a source used to re-download its whole index every single time, and the Xiph genre index alone is a 5 MB page. Levels are now cached, a failed refresh keeps what you had rather than blanking the branch, and anything served from a cache can say how old it is instead of quietly implying it is current.</li> </ul> <h3 id="the-columns-are-yours">The columns are yours</h3> <ul> <li><strong>View > Choose Columns… (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+C).</strong> A report list is read out one column at a time, so whoever chose the columns chose the sentence every row speaks – and until now that choice was made once, in code, for everybody. Somebody who never leaves one country heard a country on all sixty thousand rows; somebody who browses by network heard the source last or not at all. Find Stations results and the Recordings list can now be reordered and pruned, and both offer more than they show: Language, Genres, Popularity and Bitrate on a station row, Length on a recording.</li> <li><strong>Continue Listening moved to Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L</strong>, because Choose Columns took the C. It had <code>Ctrl+Shift+Alt+C</code>, which reads as a different key and is not one: wx ignores the order the modifiers are written in, so the two menu items claimed one chord and whichever wx bound second silently never fired. Choose Columns keeps the C – QUILL Cast binds it there too, and a family key that differs per app is worse than an unfamiliar one. The gate that exists to catch exactly this missed it because it compared the accelerator <em>text</em>; it now compares the way the Keyboard Manager already did, so a re-ordered spelling can never hide a collision again.</li> <li><strong>Two lists, not checkboxes</strong>, for the reason Quick Actions reorders by position: a checkbox inside a list is a state a screen reader has to be asked for, while a list position is a place you land on and the announcement after a move says where you are now. Hide moves a column out of the spoken row and into Hidden; Show puts it back <em>where its order says it belongs</em>, so a column hidden for a week does not come back at the end.</li> <li><strong>Hidden means absent, not last.</strong> A screen reader speaks every column a report list is given, so the only way to stop hearing something is for the column not to exist. Order and visibility are two answers, not one list with the unwanted part pushed down.</li> <li><strong>The preview is the announcement.</strong> Under the two lists, one line reads out exactly what a row will say, comma-separated, because that is how a screen reader runs a report row’s cells together. Somebody deciding whether to hide Country can hear the answer before pressing OK.</li> <li><strong>One column per list cannot be hidden</strong> – the station’s name, the recording’s name – because a row with nothing to identify it is a row nobody can act on, and a preference that can produce one is a preference that can break the app. The refusal says why rather than being a disabled button that says only no.</li> <li><strong>Length is blank where the number is a cap.</strong> Every capture carries a <code>minutes</code> value, and for a Record Now capture that number is a disk-safety limit rather than a plan; showing it as a length would announce an intention nobody expressed, so <code>JobSnapshot</code>’s existing requested/cap answer decides whether the cell says anything at all.</li> </ul> <h3 id="questions-the-app-could-not-answer-about-itself">Questions the app could not answer about itself</h3> <p>A dozen gaps, each one a question the app could not answer about itself. They came out of a product review of Quill Radio that predated 3.0; going back through it against the shipped app found most of its recommendations already built, a few deliberately decided against, and these genuinely open.</p> <ul> <li><strong>A favorites folder is somewhere you listen from now.</strong> Radio has had folders on favorites for a long time and never had a single action <em>on</em> one, so filing forty stations into “News” organised the list and did nothing at all for the listening. A folder’s menu now offers <strong>Play All in Folder</strong>, <strong>Shuffle Folder</strong> and <strong>Export This Folder…</strong>. Playing a folder starts the first station and remembers the rest, so <strong>Next Station in Folder</strong> and <strong>Previous Station in Folder</strong> (the command palette) walk them – a live station never ends, so there is nothing for a sequential playlist to advance <em>on</em>, and what somebody asking to “play the News folder” actually wants is one keystroke to the next station in the set they chose. Shuffle is a fixed permutation rather than a coin flip per step, so it can be walked backwards; and the ends of a folder say so rather than wrapping, because silently looping is how a listener hears the same station twice and cannot say why. A folder always means its whole subtree, and “News” does not swallow “Newsroom” – the separator has to be there.</li> <li><strong>Quick Actions came to Radio.</strong> QUILL Cast has let you put the actions you use at the top of every row menu, and choose what Enter does, since 1.1.0; Radio built a fixed menu and there was nothing to reorder, so the same idea worked in one of the two apps somebody uses for the same job. <strong>Station > Quick Actions…</strong> (Ctrl+Alt+Q) reorders the right-click menu of two kinds of row – a station and a browse folder. (It first arrived promising a recording list and Ctrl+1 through Ctrl+9 as in Cast; neither did anything in Radio, and the final release removed both promises.) The preference only <em>orders</em> what a row already offers: a station already in your favorites still offers Remove and not Add, and a live stream still offers no Download, whatever order somebody puts them in. The ordering machinery itself is now shared between the two apps rather than written twice, and a test fails the build if Radio’s catalogue ever offers an action no row menu builds – an entry a listener could put first and then never reach is a preference that silently does nothing.</li> <li><strong>Listening statistics.</strong> Radio kept a recently-played list and a song log, and neither of them was <em>time</em>, so the app could say what you had had on and never how much of it. <strong>View > Listening Statistics…</strong> (Ctrl+Shift+Q) reports how long you listened, by station and by network, over this week, this month, this year or all time – with Copy, Save as CSV, and Delete My History. Time counts <strong>only while audio is actually coming out</strong>: not while a stream is connecting, not while it is buffering through dead air, not while it is paused, and not while the app sits stopped overnight, because a statistics window that counts any of those is worse than none. Anything under ten seconds is not counted either – skipping past a station in a list is not listening, and a log full of three-second samples makes every per-station total a lie. Speed and silence-trimming are omitted entirely rather than reported as zero: you cannot listen to a broadcast at 1.4x, and there is no silence to remove from something that was never recorded.</li> <li><strong>An episode row can hand things to QUILL Cast.</strong> From the browse tree you could play a subscribed show’s episode and mark it played, and that was all – so the same episode offered different things depending on which of the two apps you happened to be looking at it in. <strong>Play Next in QUILL Cast</strong>, <strong>Add to QUILL Cast Queue</strong> and <strong>Send to the QUILL Cast Inbox</strong> are on those rows now. They are a handoff rather than a write into Cast’s library, because both apps load and save that library wholesale and a Radio write while Cast is open would be a last-writer-wins clobber waiting to happen; Cast carries them out at its next launch, which is why every confirmation is in the future tense. They travel in a file of their own, deliberately: an instruction written as a field on the <em>existing</em> handoff record would have been matched by an already-installed Cast, done nothing, and then been deleted – losing somebody’s queue intent silently, in what is the common case, because Radio ships first.</li> <li><strong>Chapters, and a way to check a mark without losing your place.</strong> The chapter list is no longer only for a video’s published chapters: for a recording or a downloaded episode it reads the file’s own chapter frames, and for an episode QUILL Cast has already analysed it reads the result Cast left in the shared cache – so the chapters somebody worked out in Cast this morning are there in Radio this evening, without doing it twice. Radio works none out itself and is not getting an engine for it: it is the lite app, and 91 MB to answer a question its sibling has already answered is not a trade worth making. Where there is a local file, the list also gains <strong>Preview This Mark</strong>, which plays ten seconds either side of a boundary through its own player – your place does not move, because the question is <em>does the programme turn here</em> and playing forward from the mark answers a different one.</li> <li><strong>The status strip says how long a recording has left.</strong> The Recording cell read <code>Idle</code> / <code>Recording</code> / <code>2 recording</code> while the sleep-timer cell immediately beside it said “12 min left”, so the one question people arrow over to that cell to ask went unanswered. It now counts <em>down</em> to a length you chose and <em>up</em> when you did not – because every capture carries a <code>minutes</code> value, and for a Record Now capture that number is a disk-safety cap rather than a plan. Counting down to a cap would announce an intention nobody expressed, so <code>JobSnapshot</code> now records which of the two it was, and a reconnect inherits that answer rather than re-deriving it from the remaining minutes it is handed. The cell’s hint says which measurement is on screen.</li> <li><strong>Find Stations remembers the searches you ran.</strong> The dialog remembered which sources it searched and which Source facet you filtered by, on the stated principle that a preference you re-set every time is not a preference – and remembered nothing about the searches themselves. The name field is now a combo: press Down for the searches already run, newest first, and picking one restores <strong>all three fields</strong> and re-runs it. Whole queries rather than bare words, because <em>jazz in France</em> and <em>jazz in Brazil</em> are different searches. De-duplicated case- and space-insensitively, capped at 15, never storing an empty search, and riding <code>radio_history.json</code> so clearing that history clears this too.</li> <li><strong>What’s Playing says where the track came from.</strong> Quill Radio reaches for a title in three places – the ICY block carried with the audio, the player’s own metadata, and the station’s status page – and presented all three as one confident sentence. They are not equally direct: a status page can lag the audio by a song. The full details window now names the route, and where the shown text is a <em>reading</em> of what arrived (<code>text="..." song_spot="M"</code> and the rest), it shows the original and says so.</li> <li><strong>A row that will not play says so.</strong> Radio Browser runs its own checker against every stream it lists and publishes the verdict as <code>lastcheckok</code>; Quill Radio downloaded it on every search and threw it away, so every row made the same silent promise. Rows the directory could not play now carry “may not be playable”, and rows that resolve at play time (TuneIn, YouTube) say that instead. Everything else stays silent – badging every row “unknown” would convey nothing – and the details panel, which has room, says the good news and the no-check case too. Nothing here scores or estimates: the only negative verdict is the listing directory’s own.</li> <li><strong>Audio Health (View > Audio Health…, Ctrl+Alt+Shift+M).</strong> <code>media_preflight</code> speaks once at launch when something is missing and, correctly, says nothing when nothing is wrong – which left no way to <em>ask</em>. One window now reports the engine actually in use (including “auto” having quietly fallen through to Windows Media because libmpv is absent), mpv and FFmpeg, the output device and whether the system still offers it, what Sound Enhancements are doing and to what scope, whether the OptiLab adapter shipped, and whether a recording started now could actually be written. It probes nothing – no test tone, no device opened, no file written – so it is safe to open mid-recording, and it counts problems rather than scoring health.</li> <li><strong>A keyboard shortcut sheet (Help > Keyboard Shortcuts Sheet…, Ctrl+Alt+Shift+K).</strong> 3.0 made every menu item name its key, which fixed discovery <em>inside</em> a menu and left “open six menus and arrow to the end of each” as the only way to see them together. The sheet is built by walking the live menu bar, so it lists the keys you actually have – rebindings included – and cannot drift from the menus the way a list in a manual always eventually does. Filterable by key, by action or by menu; the keys that have no menu item (F6, the Winamp letters) are listed with the surface they work in.</li> <li><strong>The Recordings window says what happens next.</strong> Its status line counted rows – “14 recorded, 1 recording now, 3 scheduled” – which answers “how many?” and never answers “when?”. It is a pure text rewrite – no network lookup, no scraping, nothing sent anywhere, nothing to gate in Safe Mode – and a URL that isn’t Live365 is passed through exactly as you typed it.</li> <li><strong>Stations on SecureNet’s player now resolve — Find Streams stopped throwing away the one address that worked.</strong> Point <strong>Find Streams from a Website…</strong> at a <code>securenetsystems.net/v5/…</code> player page and you used to get either a couple of useless results or nothing at all. Three rules govern it: braille never costs speech (an unplugged display, a reader that rejects the call, a bridge without the capability – all degrade to “spoke but did not braille”, never to silence); nothing is truncated, because clipping would drop the end of a track title; and an identical message inside two seconds does not steal the display twice, since a flash message replaces whatever is under your fingers. Quill Radio now stitches them when the recording finishes, under the name you expected. The join runs through FFmpeg’s concat demuxer as a straight stream copy: no decoding, no re-encoding, so not a single bit of audio quality is lost and even a three-hour overnight capture is done in seconds. The order of operations is the whole point of the design – the joined recording is written to a private working file, checked that it exists and is plausibly sized against the parts it came from, put in place in one atomic step, and only <em>then</em> are the continuation files removed. So a file that has not gained a byte across four consecutive checks – roughly a minute – is not recording, whatever the process table says, and it is handed to the existing drop path to reconnect and continue or stop and save what it captured.
