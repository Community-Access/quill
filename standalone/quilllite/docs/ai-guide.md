# QUILL Lite's free AI: the complete guide

*QUILL Lite 1.1. Updated September 29, 2026.*

QUILL's free AI helps with the writing in front of you. It can summarize it,
rewrite it, proofread it, explain it, or answer a question about the document
you have open. It is built into QUILL Lite and it is free. You do not need an
account, a password or a card.

This guide walks you through all of it: how to start, what each command does,
exactly which text is sent, what happens to the answer, the limits, and your
privacy. If you would rather learn by doing, open **Help > Tutorials...**
(**Ctrl+Alt+F1**) and choose *Asking a question about a document*. It takes
about six minutes.

## Starting, in about a minute

You only do this once on each computer. Here is how.

1. **Open the agreement.** Press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+K**, or choose **Tools > AI >
   Privacy Agreement**. You can open it even while AI help is switched off.
2. **Read it, then choose I Agree.** It tells you what is sent, what is kept,
   what is not kept, and how to change your mind later. Agreeing also switches
   AI help on, so there is nothing else to turn on. If you choose **No
   Thanks**, nothing is sent and nothing changes.
3. **Connect this computer.** The Connect window opens on its own. It shows an
   **eight-character code**, and it reads the code out to you too. Press
   **Open the Connect Page**. Your browser opens with the code already filled
   in, so all you have to do is press **Confirm**. The window tells you when
   you are connected.
   - Not sitting at the computer you are connecting? You can type the code at
     the address the window gives you, on any device. A phone is fine.
   - **Say the Code Again** reads the code one character at a time. **Copy the
     Code** puts it on the clipboard.
4. **Try it.** Put your cursor in any paragraph and press **Ctrl+Alt+G**.
   Choose **Summarize**, then press **Send**.

That is all. There is no account, no password and no email address. The code
is the whole thing.

If you reach for an AI command before you have agreed, QUILL Lite shows you the
agreement first. If you reach for one before this computer is connected, it
takes you to the Connect window.

### What you learned, and where to go next

You agreed once, connected this computer with a short code, and sent your first

shows you around the window you just used. The tutorial *Asking a question about

All six of these are in **Tools > AI**.

|---|---|

## The AI pad

Press **Ctrl+Alt+G** and the pad opens on whatever your cursor is in. Here is
what you find, from top to bottom:

1. **About to send.** One sentence telling you how much will be sent, where it
   comes from, and whether it fits the free limit. For example, "About to send
   84 words from this paragraph."
2. **What will be sent.** A read-only box holding *exactly* the text that will
   go, and nothing else. Focus starts here, so the first thing you hear is the
   text itself. Arrow through it at your own pace.
3. **Send this much.** Which part of your document to send. You only see this
   when there is a real choice to make. See *Exactly what is sent*.
4. **What do you want done?** A list of the seventeen things the AI can do.
   Press a letter to jump. Press **F1** on any of them to hear what it does.
5. **Your question.** This only appears for the choices where you type:
   **Ask a question about the document**, **Ask a general question**, and
   **Have a conversation** (where it is your first message, and you can leave
   it empty).
6. **Send**, and **Close**.
7. **Status.** What happened to your last request: still working, how many
   requests it used, or what went wrong.

You can reach every line in the pad with **Tab**, including the summary at the
top and the status at the bottom. So if you miss something, you can always go
back and read it.

**You never have to wait for the AI.** Requests run in the background. Keep
typing, save, switch documents, or close the pad while the answer is on its
way.

### What you learned, and where to go next

The pad shows you exactly what will be sent before anything goes, and you can
Tab to every part of it. Next, *The seventeen things it can do* explains each
choice in the list.

## The seventeen things it can do

### The first four

- **Summarize.** A few plain sentences saying what the passage says. Handy for
  a long email, a dense report, or terms and conditions.
- **Rewrite.** The same meaning, clearer and shorter. Handy for the paragraph
  you have rewritten four times and still do not like.
- **Proofread.** Fixes spelling, grammar and punctuation, and leaves your
  wording alone. The corrected text comes back for you to compare with your
  own. Your document does not change until you choose to put it there.
- **Explain.** What the passage means, in plain language. Handy for jargon or a
  legal clause.

### The writing tools

These are new in 1.1. Each one works on the same passage the first four do, and
each one uses one request.

- **Shorten.** About half the length, with every important fact kept. Handy
  for a message that has to fit a character limit.
- **Simplify.** Plain, easy-to-read language: short sentences, everyday words,
  and technical terms explained. Handy for a letter from a bank, a hospital or
  a lawyer, or when you want to write something anyone can read.
- **Make more formal.** The same meaning in a professional tone. Handy for an
  email to a manager or an official complaint.
- **Make friendlier.** The same meaning in a warmer tone. Handy for the note
  that came out colder than you meant.
- **Turn into a list.** Bullet points, or numbered steps when the passage
  describes a process. A list is much quicker to review by ear than a
  paragraph.
- **Find action items.** Every task, who does it and by when, and every date or
  deadline, one per line. Handy for meeting notes and long email threads. If
  there are none, it tells you so.
- **Suggest headings.** Headings that would split a long passage into
  sections, and where each one goes. Your screen reader can jump between
  headings, so this is a good way to make a wall of text easy to move around.
  Nothing is inserted for you. You place the ones you want.
- **Continue writing.** A next paragraph in the same voice, for when you are
  stuck. Insert Below puts it after the paragraph you are in.
- **Write an email reply.** Select an email you received and you get a polite,
  clear reply to edit. It answers each question the email asks, and anything
  only you can decide is left in [square brackets] for you to fill in.
- **Translate.** Choose a language from the **Translate into** list, which
  appears when you pick Translate: English, Spanish, French, German, Italian,
  Portuguese, Dutch, Swedish, Polish, Russian, Ukrainian, Turkish, Arabic,
  Hebrew, Hindi, Chinese (Simplified), Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese and
  Tagalog. The meaning, tone, line breaks and lists are kept.

### The three where you type

- **Ask a question about the document.** Type a question, and QUILL Lite finds
  the parts of the document that answer it. See *Asking about a whole
  document*.
- **Ask a general question.** Type any question. Only your question is sent,
  nothing from your document. You get one answer, of up to about 750 words.
- **Have a conversation.** Talk back and forth in a window of its own. Each
  reply remembers what was said before. See *Having a conversation*.

**Ctrl+Alt+Z** opens the pad with the question already chosen, so you can start
typing straight away.

### What you learned, and where to go next

You have four everyday choices, ten writing tools, and three choices where you
type your own question. Next, *Exactly what is sent* tells you which part of
your document goes with each request.

## Exactly what is sent

For Summarize, Rewrite, Proofread and Explain, the pad sends **one piece** of
your document. It picks that piece like this:

1. **If you have selected text**, it sends your selection.
2. **If you have not**, it sends the **paragraph your cursor is in**. A
   paragraph is the text between one blank line and the next. You do not need
   to select anything. Put your cursor anywhere in the paragraph and press the
   key.
3. **If your document has headings**, you can choose **This section** instead.
   That is everything from the heading above your cursor down to the next
   heading.

**Send this much** lets you switch between whichever of these apply: **What I
have selected**, **This paragraph**, or **This section**. The **What will be
sent** box changes the moment you pick a different one, so you always hear
what is going. When only one of them applies, you do not see the chooser at
all, which saves you a Tab.

### A few details worth knowing

- **Sections follow Markdown-style headings.** That means lines that start with
  `#`, or a line underlined with `===` or `---`. If your document has none,
  **This section** is not offered.
- **A document with no blank lines is one paragraph.** In that case "this
  paragraph" is the whole file. The size check in *When something is too long*
  stops one that is very long.
- **Nothing else goes with it.** Not the rest of the document, not the file's
  name, not where it is saved. The box shows you the whole of what is sent.

### What you learned, and where to go next

The pad sends your selection, or the paragraph you are in, or the section you
are in, and you hear exactly what that is before it goes. Next, *Asking about a
whole document* explains how a question about a long document works.

## Asking about a whole document

**Ask a question about the document** works a little differently, and that is
what makes it fine to ask about a long document.

QUILL Lite does not send the whole file. Instead, it reads the document **on
your own computer** and splits it into passages of about **180 words**. Each
passage is labelled with the heading it sits under. Then it picks the **three
passages most likely to answer your question** and sends only those, with your
question.

- **You see the passages it chose** before you press Send, in the **What will
  be sent** box. Each one is named, for example "Excerpt 1, from" followed by
  the heading. If they are not the right ones, try asking again using the words
  the document itself would use.
- **A passage whose heading matches your question counts for more.** A heading
  is the author's own label for what a passage is about.
- **If your question has no useful words in it**, like "what is it?", you get
  the opening passages of the document. That is usually a decent guess.
- **A very long document** is searched up to about the first 375,000 words. The
  pad tells you when that happens. Put your cursor nearer what you mean, or
  select a part and use one of the other choices instead.
- **The answer comes only from those passages.** If the document says something
  somewhere else, the AI did not see it.

### What you learned, and where to go next

For a question about the document, QUILL Lite picks the three best passages on
your computer and shows you them before sending. The tutorial *Asking a
question about a document* lets you try this with the app guiding you. Next,
*When something is too long* covers the size limit.

## When something is too long

One request can hold about **3,000 tokens**. That is roughly **2,250 words** or
**12,000 characters**, counting your text, your question and the instructions
together. The answer itself is up to about 500 tokens, which is a few hundred
words.

If what you are about to send is bigger than that, the pad tells you **before**
you press Send, and it says it in words, not tokens. For example: "That is
about 4,000 words, and the free limit is about 2,250." Select less, or choose a
smaller part in **Send this much**. Nothing is sent and nothing is used.

### What you learned, and where to go next

The free AI takes about 2,250 words at a time, and the pad warns you before
you go over. Next, *The answer window* explains what you can do with an answer
once it arrives.

## The answer window

The answer arrives in its own window. Its title says what you asked for:
**Summary**, **Rewrite**, **Proofread**, **Explanation** or **Answer**. Focus
lands on the answer, so your screen reader starts reading it straight away.
Below it you find these buttons:

- **Replace My Selection.** Puts the answer where your selection was. You only
  get this button if you had text selected, and only while that text is still
  exactly as it was. If you changed it while the answer was on its way, the
  button is left out, so the answer never lands on words it was not about.
- **Insert Below.** Puts the answer underneath the paragraph your cursor is in,
  after a blank line, so it never lands in the middle of a sentence.
- **Copy.** Puts the answer on the clipboard.
- **Try Again.** Sends the same text again for a different answer. This uses
  one more request.
- **Follow Up.** Carries on from this answer as a conversation, so you can say
  things like "make it shorter" or "and the second point?". See *Having a
  conversation*.
- **Requests.** How much of your allowance this answer used.

**You can undo every edit.** Replace and Insert Below work like your own
typing, so **Ctrl+Z** takes an AI edit back.

### What you learned, and where to go next

You can replace, insert, copy, try again or follow up, and Ctrl+Z always takes
an AI edit back. Next, *Having a conversation* shows you how to keep talking
about an answer.

## Having a conversation

Choose **Have a conversation** in the pad, or press **Follow Up** in an answer
window. The **AI Conversation** window opens. Here is what is in it:

1. **About this conversation.** What goes with each message and what it uses.
2. **Conversation.** Everything said so far, newest at the end, as "You:" and
   "AI:" paragraphs. You can read it but not change it.
3. **Your message.** This is where focus stays. Type and press **Enter**.
4. **Send**, **New Conversation**, **Copy Last Reply**, **Insert Last Reply
   Below**, and **Close**.
5. **Status.** Still working, what was used, or what went wrong.

Each reply is **read aloud as it arrives** and added to the conversation, while
you stay in the message box. Press **Shift+Tab** to reach the conversation and
read it again at your own pace.

### What is sent

Your message, and the conversation so far. If you are following up on a
question about your document, the same passages that question used go with
every message too, so you can keep asking about them. A conversation you start
from the pad sends nothing from your document.

### What it uses on the free AI

Each message is **one request**. Conversations also have their own monthly
share: **40** of your 100. To keep every message the same price as any other
request, the conversation so far is sent only **as far as the ordinary
3,000-token limit allows**, newest first. That means a long conversation slowly
forgets how it began. The window tells you **the first time that happens**, and
**New Conversation** starts you fresh. If a single message is too long to fit
on its own, it is refused before anything is sent.

### With your own AI key

None of those limits apply. The whole conversation goes with every message, and
it is billed to your OpenAI or Google account, so a long conversation costs more for each
reply. It is only shortened if it grows bigger than the model can read at once,
and the window tells you when that happens.

### What you learned, and where to go next

A conversation remembers what was said, reads each reply aloud, and on the free
AI uses one request per message. Next, *Usage, About and your support ID* shows
you how to check what you have left.

## Usage, About and your support ID

**Usage** shows what you have used and what is left this month and today, when
each count starts again, and your **support ID**. It asks the service fresh
every time you open it, so the numbers are always current. You also find **Sign
Out This Computer** and **Copy Support ID** here.

**Help > About** shows the same allowance and support ID, under **QUILL's free
AI**, once this computer is connected. Give it a moment after About opens,
because the numbers are fetched while you read.

If you have saved **your own AI key**, both of these show something
different: the model you are using and where to find your provider's usage page,
instead of an allowance. See *Using your own OpenAI or Google Gemini key: no
limits*.

Your **support ID** is a short code like `A1B2-C3D4`. Support needs it to find
your account, because there is no name on it. When this computer is connected,
**Get Help from Support** puts it into your message for you.

### What you learned, and where to go next

Usage and Help > About tell you where you stand, and your support ID is how
support finds you. Next, *Limits, and when they start again* has every number.
The tutorial *Asking a question about a document* ends with a step on checking
what you have left.

## Limits, and when they start again

The service is free because everybody shares it, so there is an allowance:

| Limit | How many | Starts again |
|---|---|---|
| Requests a month | 100 | The 1st of the month |
| Requests a day | 20 | Every night |
| Requests an hour | 8 | At the top of each hour |
| Any one kind of request a month | 60 | The 1st of the month |
| A new connection's first 48 hours | 15 | Rises to 100 on its own |

These are the service's current numbers, and they can change without you
updating anything. **Usage** always shows the numbers that apply to you right
now.

### When the counts start again

The service keeps time in UTC. The daily count starts again at midnight UTC,
which is **8 PM Eastern** or **5 PM Pacific** in summer, and an hour earlier in
winter. The monthly count starts again at midnight UTC on the 1st.

### What counts

**Only answers count.** If a request does not come back with an answer, it
uses nothing. That includes a request that was too long, one sent with no
connection, one that hit a problem on the service, and one refused because you
were already at a limit. Opening Usage or About uses nothing either.

### A new connection starts smaller

For its first 48 hours, a newly connected computer has 15 requests. After that,
the full allowance applies on its own. Signing out and connecting again starts
a new connection, so it will not get you more.

### Computers that share an internet connection

A household, a class or a library may have several computers on one internet
connection. Those computers also share a few limits on that connection: how
many can be connected at once, how many new connections can be made in an hour,
and a monthly total. Signing a computer out gives its place back. If a class
runs into these limits, write to support.

### Need more?

Choose **Get Help from Support** and say what it is for: a course, a deadline,
a book. Support can raise your allowance, and can end the smaller new-computer
allowance early. The change applies from your next request. There is nothing
to reinstall and nothing to reconnect.

### What you learned, and where to go next

You get 100 requests a month, 20 a day and 8 an hour, and only answers count.
Next, *When something goes wrong* explains the messages you might hear.

## When something goes wrong

When a request fails, the pad's **Status** line says what happened and what to
do. **Focus moves there**, so your screen reader reads it, and you can read it
again with the arrow keys. If the pad is not in front at the time, the message
is spoken instead, and focus is waiting on it when you come back.

The message ends with an error code, such as `QUILL-AI-GATEWAY-QUOTA` for a
limit. Include it if you write to support. These are the ones you are most
likely to hear:

- **A limit was reached.** The message names the limit and when it starts
  again. Nothing was used.
- **No connection.** Nothing was sent and nothing was used. Try again when you
  are back online.
- **The free AI is paused.** The service has one spending ceiling for everyone
  together. When it is reached, the free AI pauses for everybody until the 1st.
  This is rare, and nothing is wrong with your computer.
- **This computer was signed out.** Choose **Connect or Sign Out** and connect
  again.

### What you learned, and where to go next

When something fails, you hear why and what to do, and focus waits on the
message. Next, *Your privacy* explains what is sent and what is kept.

## Your privacy

- **What is sent:** only the text shown in **What will be sent**, plus your
  question if you asked one. It goes to QUILL's service and on to OpenAI, which
  writes the answer.
- **What is kept:** how many requests you made, how big they were, which kind
  of request each one was, and what it cost.
- **What is not kept:** what you wrote. What came back. Your name, your email
  address, or anything else that identifies you. Connecting a computer creates
  an account with no name on it at all.
- **When it is sent:** only when you press **Send**. Nothing is sent as you
  type, when you save, in the background, or when you open the pad. If you
  close the pad without pressing Send, nothing is sent.

The service's own page, **https://ai.community-access.org**, explains the same
things and publishes what the service costs to run.

### What you learned, and where to go next

Only the text you see in the box is sent, only when you press Send, and your
words are not kept. Next, *Signing out and changing your mind* shows you how to
step back out.

## Signing out and changing your mind

- **Sign Out This Computer** is in **Usage**. Press it twice. The second press
  confirms. You can connect again whenever you like.
- **It is safe to read the agreement again.** Once you have agreed, **Privacy
  Agreement** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+K**) opens it with two buttons: **Keep Using
  AI**, which is what Enter and Escape do, and **Withdraw and Sign Out**.
  Nothing changes unless you choose the second one.
- **Withdrawing** switches AI help off **and** signs this computer out, both in
  one step.
- **You can always open Privacy Agreement**, whether AI help is on or off, and
  whether or not you have ever agreed.

### What you learned, and where to go next

You can sign out from Usage, and withdrawing your agreement also signs you out.
If you would like more than the free allowance, the next two chapters, *Using
your own OpenAI or Google Gemini key: no limits* and *Using your ChatGPT
subscription*, explain the other ways to run AI help.

## Using your own OpenAI or Google Gemini key: no limits

If you have an OpenAI account or a Google Gemini key, you can use **your own
key** instead of QUILL's free service. Then **every limit goes away**: no
monthly, daily or hourly allowance, no 2,250-word ceiling, and no smaller first
48 hours.

### Setting it up

Choose **Tools > AI > Use My Own AI Key** (**Alt+F2**). One window opens. Here
is what is in it:

1. **Provider.** Choose **OpenAI** or **Google Gemini**. Everything else in the
   window follows this choice: where your text goes, which key, which models
   and whose prices. AI help always uses the key saved for the provider you
   choose here, and never the other one. Your choice is saved with your
   settings, and QUILL never guesses it from a key or a model name.
2. **About this.** A read-only box saying exactly where your text goes. Arrow
   through it once.
3. **API key.** Paste the key for the provider you chose. An OpenAI key comes
   from platform.openai.com, under API keys, and starts with `sk-`. A Gemini
   key comes from Google AI Studio at aistudio.google.com/apikey.
4. **Model.** A list of every model your key can use for text. It fills in
   from your account once the key is checked. For OpenAI, **Luna 6 comes
   first, then the other GPT-6 models**. For Gemini, **2.5 Flash comes first,
   then 2.5 Pro**. The rest follow by name. Each row says roughly what it
   costs, for example "gpt-6-luna, about $0.57 per 100 requests (estimate)",
   so you can compare them just by arrowing down the list. Models that cannot
   answer in text (speech, transcription, images, embeddings) are left out.
5. **Cost estimate.** The chosen model's estimated cost per request and per 100
   requests, at that provider's prices. **These are estimates to help you
   compare, not the provider's prices.** A typical request here is about a page
   in and a paragraph back. The real prices are at openai.com/api/pricing and
   ai.google.dev/pricing.
6. **Status.** Whether a key is saved for the provider you chose, what the last
   test said, and whether the other provider's key is saved too.
7. **Test the Key.** Checks the key with your chosen provider and fills the
   model list. Then it sends one tiny request to the chosen model and tells you
   whether it answered. That request costs a fraction of a cent.
8. **Remove the Saved Key.** Forgets the chosen provider's key **straight
   away** and puts AI help back on QUILL's free service.

Press **OK** and your key is saved. QUILL Lite says "AI help uses your own
OpenAI key, with no limits", or "your own Google Gemini key".

### Changing the model, or the key

**You can change the model at any time.** With a key saved, the window lists
your models as soon as it opens. Press **Alt+F2**, pick another, and press OK.

**There is no separate on and off switch.** While a key is saved for the
provider you chose, AI help uses it. Remove the key and AI help goes back to
the free service, with its free allowance, straight away.

**You can save both keys.** The Provider list decides which one is used. If
you choose OpenAI, a saved Gemini key just sits there unused. The status line
tells you it is there, and choosing Google Gemini is how you use it. Your
request never goes to a company you did not choose.

**With a Gemini key, Ask About an Image works too.** Gemini's models can read
pictures, so **Ctrl+F5** works on a Gemini key just as it does on a ChatGPT
subscription. The free service and an OpenAI key cannot do this here.

### What changes with your own key

- Your text goes **straight from this computer to the provider you chose**,
  OpenAI or Google, on your account. Nothing passes through QUILL's servers, so
  QUILL records nothing at all, not even the count it keeps for the free
  service.
- **OpenAI or Google bills you** for each request, under that company's own
  terms and privacy policy.
- **Usage** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F9**) opens a different window. It tells you
  which model is answering and that no allowance applies, and it has an **Open
  My Usage** button that takes you to the usage page on your OpenAI or Google
  AI Studio account, where your requests and charges are. There is no Sign Out
  and no support ID here, because you do not need either. **Help > About** also
  shows the model and that page's address instead of the free allowance.
- You do not need to connect this computer, and you are not asked for the free
  service's agreement. That agreement is about QUILL's servers, and your own
  key never uses them.
- The pad, the things it can do, and what comes back are **exactly the same**,
  right down to the instructions sent with your text. The pad still warns you
  before sending something very large, because with your own key a large
  request costs you money.
- While an answer is on its way, **Send** becomes **Stop** (`Alt+S`). Stopping
  closes a Gemini stream immediately; for another route it discards that
  request's eventual answer. It never stops a request from another AI window.

### Where your key is kept

Your key is kept in Windows' own credential store, not in a settings file. Once
saved, it is never shown again. The box stays empty and tells you a key is
saved. A portable copy keeps it in an encrypted file instead. QUILL and QUILL
Lite share the key: save it in either and it works in both, and remove it in
either and it is gone from both.

### If a request fails

You hear why, followed by the error code `QUILL-AI-OWN-KEY-FAILED`, and the
sentence names the provider. The usual causes are a mistyped key, a model your
account cannot use, an account with no credit, or the wrong provider chosen for
the key you saved. **Test the Key** tells you which one it is.

### What you learned, and where to go next

With your own key saved and its provider chosen, AI help has no limits and
your text goes straight to OpenAI or Google on your account. Remove the key and
you are back on the free service. If you already pay for ChatGPT, *Using your
ChatGPT subscription* is next, and it needs no key at all.

## Using your ChatGPT subscription

If you already pay for ChatGPT, QUILL Lite can run every AI command on your
plan, and you never have to handle a key. You sign in once in your browser,
the same way you sign in to ChatGPT itself. From then on, the pad, the
seventeen things it can do, Ask About This Document and conversations all go to
OpenAI on your plan. There is **no monthly allowance, no size limit and no
per-request bill**, and nothing goes through QUILL's servers. You also get two
things the free service and an API key cannot do: **pictures, described**, and
**web search** for an answer about today.

### Signing in, step by step

1. Press **Alt+F5** (**Tools > AI > Use My ChatGPT Subscription**). The window
   opens on **About this**, which tells you where your text goes. Arrow through
   it once.
2. Tab to **Continue with ChatGPT** and press it. Your browser opens on
   OpenAI's own sign-in page.
3. Sign in to ChatGPT there. A page asks whether **QUILL Lite** may use your
   plan. Allow it. That is the name you will see under Apps in ChatGPT's
   settings from now on. Each QuillVille app signs in on its own: QUILL, QUILL
   Cast and Quill Radio each ask under their own name, and signing out of one
   leaves the others signed in. If another app is already signed in on this
   computer, the Status line tells you. Your browser is then already signed
   in, so this step is just one Allow, with no password.
4. Come back to QUILL Lite. The window says "Signed in with ChatGPT as"
   followed by your email address. Focus lands on the **Model** list, and the
   first model your plan offers is already chosen. You are done.

If the browser did not open, the window tells you and offers **Copy the Sign-In
Address**. Paste that into any browser on this computer and finish there.
**Stop Waiting** gives up without changing anything.

### Choosing the model

The **Model** list is read from your account the moment you sign in, so it
holds exactly what your plan offers. Luna 6 and the GPT-6 models come first,
then the rest by name. Models that cannot answer in text are left out. Arrow to
one and it is saved as soon as you land on it, so there is no OK to press.
**Refresh Models** reads the list again, in case your plan has gained one.

### What changes

- **Every limit goes away.** Nothing is refused for size, the answer is not
  cut short, and Ask a question about the document sends the whole document
  instead of three passages. Before you send, the *About to send* line tells
  you how many words are going and that they count toward your plan.
- **What you use counts toward your plan's usage.** OpenAI keeps track of that
  and shows it on ChatGPT's own usage page. **Open ChatGPT Usage** in the
  window takes you there. When you reach a limit, QUILL Lite tells you in
  words, with the code `QUILL-AI-CHATGPT-LIMIT`, and the usage page shows when
  it resets.
- **Usage** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F9**) opens this same window, and **Help >
  About** names your account and model instead of the free allowance.
- You are not asked for the free service's agreement, and this computer does
  not need to be connected to it. That agreement is about QUILL's servers, and
  your subscription never uses them.
- **If you also have an OpenAI key saved, your ChatGPT sign-in is used first.**
  Your plan is already paid for, while a key is billed for each request. Sign
  out, and the key takes over again.

### Signing out, and forgetting

**Sign Out** (press it twice) asks OpenAI to cancel QUILL Lite's sign-in and
forgets it on this computer. **Forget on This Computer** only forgets it here.
Use it on a computer that is offline, or when you have already removed the
sign-in in ChatGPT's settings. Until you remove it there, QUILL Lite stays
listed in ChatGPT's settings. You only see these two buttons while you are
signed in.

### Where the sign-in is kept

Only a refresh token is stored, in Windows' credential store (or an encrypted
file in a portable copy), under QUILL Lite's own name. QUILL Lite never sees
your ChatGPT password. The access token that does the work stays in memory and
is renewed when needed. **Each QUILL app signs in as itself.** QUILL Lite,
QUILL and Quill Radio each appear under Apps in ChatGPT's settings, so signing
one out leaves the others as they were.

### What you learned, and where to go next

Press Alt+F5, sign in once in your browser, and every AI command runs on your
ChatGPT plan with no allowance and no key. Next, *Ask About an Image* and *Web
search* cover the two things only a subscription (or, for images, a Gemini key)
can do.

## Ask About an Image

**Tools > AI > Ask About an Image** (**Ctrl+F5**) tells you what is in a
picture. That could be a screenshot a colleague sent, a photo of a letter, a
chart pasted into a report, or a menu from a restaurant's website. Choose the
file, ask a question or ask nothing, and hear what the model sees.

1. Press **Ctrl+F5**. Focus is in **Image file**.
2. Type or paste the file's path, or press **Browse** (Alt+B). JPEG, PNG, WebP
   and GIF files work.
3. **Your question** (Alt+Q) is optional. Leave it empty and you get a
   description written for a blind reader: what the picture is, what matters
   most, and every word of any text in it, copied exactly. Or ask something,
   like "what does the sign say" or "what is the total on this receipt", and
   the answer comes first.
4. Press **Enter**. You hear "Working." The picture is checked on your computer
   first. A file that is not really an image, or one over 20 MB, is refused
   with a sentence telling you why, and nothing is sent. Otherwise it goes to
   OpenAI on your plan.
5. The **Image Description** window opens with focus on the text. **Insert
   Below** puts it into your document under the paragraph you are in, which is
   a handy way to caption a picture. **Copy** puts it on the clipboard, and
   **Ctrl+Z** takes an insert back.

You need your ChatGPT subscription for this. If you are not signed in,
**Ctrl+F5** tells you so and opens the account window. Only the picture and
your question are sent. Nothing from your document goes with them.

### What you learned, and where to go next

Ctrl+F5 describes a picture, or answers your question about it, and Insert
Below turns that into a caption. Next, *Web search* explains how to let the
model look things up.

## Web search

Open **Use My ChatGPT Subscription** and check **Allow web search**. Then the
model may search the web through OpenAI when your question needs it, such as an
event, a price, or what a page says today, and tell you what it found. This
works for Ask a general question, Ask a question about the document, and
conversations.

It is **off until you turn it on**. A search sends your question somewhere
else as well, so that is your choice to make. Your choice is saved as soon as
you make it.

To turn it on:

1. Press **Alt+F5**.
2. Press **Alt+W**, or Tab to **Allow web search**.
3. Press **Space**. You hear "Web search is allowed." It is saved straight
   away.

Press Space on the same checkbox to turn it off again.

### What you learned, and where to go next

Web search is off until you check Allow web search, and then the model can look
up current answers. Next, *Tidy Dictated Text* shows how AI can clean up what
you dictated.

## Tidy Dictated Text

Dictation writes down what it heard, and sometimes it hears wrong. **Tools > AI > Tidy Dictated Text** (**Ctrl+F3**)
fixes what was misheard: sound-alike words, names, words run together or split apart, punctuation, capital letters,
fillers and false starts. It changes nothing else.

1. Leave your cursor in the dictated paragraph, or select the part you want
   tidied.
2. Press **Ctrl+F3**. You hear "Working."
3. The **Tidied Dictation** window opens on the corrected text.
4. Press **Replace My Selection** to put it back where the dictated text was.
   **Ctrl+Z** undoes it in one step. Press **Escape** to keep what you had.

This runs on a ChatGPT subscription or your own AI key, from OpenAI or
Google Gemini. If you have neither, it tells you so and opens the account window.

### What you learned, and where to go next

Ctrl+F3 fixes what dictation misheard and leaves the rest alone. Next, *The
Dictionary: ask about the word you are on* covers questions about a single
word.

## The Dictionary: ask about the word you are on

**Tools > Dictionary** is a dictionary that reads the sentence your word is in.
You can also reach it from the *Dictionary for "word"* submenu when you press
the Applications key on a word. The thesaurus that comes with QUILL Lite knows
every meaning of *bank*, but it cannot tell which one your sentence means. These
commands send **the word and the sentence it sits in**, never the rest of the
document. The answers come back in plain sentences that are easy to listen to,
and where it helps, with a list of choices that each say why.

### The questions you can ask

- **Define in Context** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+'**)
- **Synonyms That Fit** (**Ctrl+Shift+;**)
- **Simpler Word** (**Ctrl+Shift+-**)
- **More Formal Word** (**Ctrl+Shift+=**)
- **More Vivid Word** (**Ctrl+Shift+'**)
- **Opposites** (**Alt+Shift+-**)
- **Is This the Right Word?** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+/**)
- **Use It in a Sentence** (**Alt+Shift+=**)
- **Where It Comes From** (**Alt+Shift+,**)
- **How to Say It** (**Alt+Shift+'**)
- **Rhymes** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+;**)
- **Word Explorer** (**Ctrl+F10**), which answers all of them at once.

**Find the Word For...** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+]**) works the other way round.
Describe the meaning you are after, hear the words that might fit and how each
one differs, and insert the one you choose at your cursor.

### The answer window

The answer window opens with focus on the answer, so your screen reader starts
reading it. Tab to reach **Choices**. Press Enter, or **Use This Word**, to put
the selected word in place of yours. **Ctrl+Z** undoes that in one step. This
only works while your word is still where it was. If you type over it while the
answer is on its way, the button is turned off and tells you why. You also find
**Copy Choice**, **Copy Answer**, and **Ask Something Else...**, which opens
the Dictionary submenu again on the same word.

### What it needs

**It runs only on your ChatGPT subscription or your own AI key (OpenAI or
Google Gemini), never on the free service.** People look words up many times a day, and that would use
up a free allowance very quickly. If you have neither, each command tells you
so and opens the account window. The user guide's *Dictionary* section has the
full table, and *Look Up* in that section is the dictionary without AI.

### What you learned, and where to go next

Tools > Dictionary answers questions about one word in its own sentence, and
Find the Word For... helps when the word is on the tip of your tongue. Last,
*Questions people ask* answers the things people ask most.

## Questions people ask

**Do I need an account?** No. No name, no email, no password. The
eight-character code connects this computer, and that is all.

**Does it cost anything?** No. It is paid for by Community Access. If you use
your own AI key instead, OpenAI or Google bills your account and there are no
limits.
If you sign in with a ChatGPT subscription, what you use counts toward that
plan and nothing else is billed.

**I pay for ChatGPT. Do I still need a key?** No. Press **Alt+F5**, choose
Continue with ChatGPT, and sign in the way you do on the web. That is the whole
setup, and it is the way to have pictures described and the web searched.

**Can it describe a picture?** Yes, on a ChatGPT subscription. Press
**Ctrl+F5**, choose the file, and listen. See *Ask About an Image*.

**Can it change my document without asking?** No. Nothing goes into your
document until you press **Replace My Selection** or **Insert Below**, and
**Ctrl+Z** takes either one back.

**Does it read my whole document?** Only when you ask a question about the
document, and even then only the three passages it picks are sent. The pad
shows you which ones before you press Send.

**Why is my allowance 15 when I was told 100?** This computer connected less
than 48 hours ago. The full allowance applies on its own when that time is up,
and Usage tells you exactly when.

**Why did my daily requests come back in the evening?** The service keeps time
in UTC, so in North America the new day starts in the evening.

**Who do I ask for help?** Choose **Get Help from Support** in the Help menu,
or write to **support@community-access.org**. A person reads every message.

### What you learned, and where to go next

That covers the questions people ask most. To practise with the app guiding
you, open **Help > Tutorials...** (**Ctrl+Alt+F1**) and choose *Asking a
question about a document*. The QUILL Lite user guide's *AI help* chapter
covers the same ground in the context of the rest of the editor.
