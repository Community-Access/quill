Chapter 5: Comments, categories, tags and menus

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers the parts of a site that grow around your writing: the comments visitors leave, the categories and tags that sort your posts, and the menus that let visitors find your pages. Each has one window in **Tools

WordPress**, built the same way as every other list in this manual: a list, the details of the row you are on, and buttons for what you can do.

How every list window works

Most windows in this manual share one shape, so learning one teaches you the rest:

  • The list comes first. Arrow through it; each row is one sentence.
  • Find, where there is one, narrows the list. Type and press Enter.
  • The details below the list say more about the row you are on.
  • The buttons act on the row you are on. A button that needs a row is dimmed until you are on one.
  • Enter runs the window’s main action, such as Open or Read.
  • Close, or Escape, leaves.
  • F1 on any control says what it is for.

Only the buttons your role can use are there. If your role cannot use the window at all, it does not open, and you hear why.

Comments

Tools > WordPress > Comments… opens on the comments waiting for you to approve. Replies sit under the comment they answer, one level deeper, so you read a conversation in order.

Show… switches between the four lists WordPress keeps: Waiting, Approved, Spam and Trash. Each list has the buttons that make sense for it:

  • Approve publishes a waiting comment. You hear, for example, “Comment from Alex approved.”
  • Unapprove takes an approved comment off the site; it waits again.
  • Spam and Not Spam move it to the spam list and back.
  • Trash and Restore move it to the Trash and back.
  • Reply… answers the comment, published under your name. You hear “Reply to Alex published on Community Radio.”
  • Edit… changes the comment’s text, for fixing a typo or removing a phone number someone posted by mistake.
  • Open on Site opens the page the comment is on, in your browser.
  • Approve All… approves every waiting comment, after one question.

Many at once. Mark marks the comment you are on, Mark Everything marks the whole list, and Do with Marked… approves, sends to spam or to the Trash every marked comment at once, after telling you how many.

The waiting count. The first time you use a site in a session, QUILL says how many comments are waiting, such as “Community Radio: 3 comments waiting.” If you would rather not hear it, press Waiting Count on First Use in the Comments window to turn it off for that site, and again to turn it back on.

A walk-through: answering a question in the comments

  1. Choose Tools > WordPress > Comments…. You hear “Comments: Waiting”, the site’s name, and the first comment.
  2. Arrow to the comment, and read it in the details.
  3. Press Approve, so it shows on the site.
  4. Press Reply…, type your answer and press OK.
  5. Press Show…, choose Spam, and check nothing real has been caught there.

The guided lesson on comments, in Help > Tutorials, does the same on a test site.

Categories and tags

Categories are the broad shelves of a blog, such as News and Events. Tags are the small labels, such as “volunteers” or “spring fair”. Only posts have them.

Tools > WordPress > Categories and Tags… asks which, then lists every category or tag with how many posts use it. You can:

  • Add… a new one;
  • Rename… one, which changes it on every post that has it;
  • Delete… one, after a question that says how many posts use it;
  • Merge Into… another, which moves every post across first and then removes the empty one, in one step;
  • Convert to Tag…, or Convert to Category, which turns one into the other and keeps its posts;
  • Find Unused…, which lists the ones no post uses so you can tidy them away;
  • Make Default…, as an administrator, which chooses the category a post gets when nobody chooses one.

Choosing categories and tags for one post is in the post’s own Properties (Chapter 2), not here.

Menus

A menu is the list of links in your site’s header or footer that visitors use to find your pages. WordPress does not add a new page to a menu by itself, which is why QUILL asks “Add it to a menu?” the first time you publish a page.

Tools > WordPress > Menus… shows a menu as a tree. Each row says the item, what kind of thing it links to, its level and its place, such as “Sessions, page, level 1, 3 of 7”.

  • Add Page…, Add Post…, Add Category… and Add Link… add an item at the end. Add Link takes any web address and its words.
  • Rename… changes what the item says.
  • Move Up and Move Down change its place.
  • Move In puts it under the item above it, as a dropdown. Move Out takes it back up a level.
  • Remove… takes it out of the menu. The page itself is not touched.
  • Check Menu finds items with no name, and links to pages that are drafts or gone.
  • Locations… chooses where the theme shows the menu, such as the header or the footer.
  • Other Menu… opens another menu on the same site.

Sites with a block theme keep their menus in Navigation blocks instead. Those open in the same window and work the same way.

Menu History

Tools > WordPress > Menu History… lists earlier versions of a menu.

  • Bring Back Removed Items… puts back anything taken out since, at the end of the menu.
  • Restore This Version… makes a Navigation block’s menu exactly as it was then.

For a classic menu, QUILL keeps a version on this computer each time you open the Menus window, so there is always something to go back to.

A walk-through: putting a new page in the menu

  1. Publish the new page. QUILL asks “Add it to a menu?”.
  2. Choose Yes, open Menus. The Menus window opens with the page added at the end.
  3. Press Move Up until it sits where you want it. Each press says its new place.
  4. If it belongs under another item, such as About, press Move In.
  5. Press Check Menu to make sure nothing points to a draft.
  6. Close the window. The change is already on the site.
