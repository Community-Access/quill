Chapter 4: Pictures and the Media Library

What this chapter covers

This chapter covers everything to do with pictures, sound and video: putting a picture in a page with good alternative text, the Media Library window, listening to and watching your site’s files without downloading them, uploading, making a picture smaller, changing how big it shows, editing it on the site, making a new picture with AI, the site logo, and hearing what a sighted visitor sees. It ends with captions, transcripts, listening versions of your pages, and posting from QUILL’s other apps.

Putting a picture in a page

There are two ways in, both in the Insert menu:

  • Insert > Picture from the Media Library… puts in something already on your site.
  • Insert > Picture from This Computer… uses a file of yours. It goes into the page straight away, marked as not uploaded yet, and is uploaded the next time you choose Save Draft to Site or Publish. Its address is swapped in then.

Either way the picture window opens, and that is where you say everything about it:

  • Alternative text: what the picture tells someone who cannot see it.
  • Decorative: check this for a divider, a border or a texture, so screen readers skip it.
  • Caption: visible text under the picture, for names or a photo credit.
  • Display width: how wide it shows. The proportions are always kept.
  • File size: which of the sizes WordPress made of the picture to use, such as “Medium, 300 wide”, when the picture is already in the library.
  • Use as featured image: the picture that stands for the whole page in lists and when the page is shared.
  • Link to: where the picture links to, if anywhere.

Press Insert. You hear “Picture inserted:” and its alternative text.

Sound and video from the library go in as players, and documents, such as a PDF, go in as a link.

Writing good alternative text

Alternative text is read instead of the picture. A few habits make it good:

  • Say what the picture is for, in a sentence or two. “Volunteers sorting donations at the spring fair” says more than “people at tables”.
  • If the picture shows words, such as a poster or a sign, read them out.
  • Leave out “image of” or “picture of”. A screen reader already says it is a picture, and Check Before Publishing flags it.
  • Never use the file name. IMG_2041.jpg tells nobody anything, and QUILL flags that too.
  • If the picture adds nothing a visitor needs, such as a decorative flourish, check Decorative rather than writing something.
  • For a chart or a diagram, keep the alternative text short and put the detail in a paragraph after the picture.

Asking AI for help with pictures

Two buttons in the picture window, and the same two in the Media Library, use the AI you chose in Choose Your AI, the same way Ask About an Image does. QUILL tells you what it sends before it sends anything.

  • Suggest Alternative Text sends the picture and the text around it, and puts the suggestion in the Alternative text field for you to read and change. Nothing is saved until you choose OK or Insert. If the AI thinks the picture is decorative, QUILL says so instead of inventing words. For a chart it may also write a fuller description, and QUILL offers to add it as a paragraph after the picture.
  • Describe Image gives you a longer description, for you rather than for visitors, in a window you can read and copy.

If AI is switched off, or you are in Safe Mode, QUILL says so and sends nothing.

The Media Library window

Tools > WordPress > Media Library… lists every file on your site, newest first. Each row gives the title, the type, the size, the alternative text and when it was uploaded. Type in Find and press Enter to narrow the list by title, file name or alternative text.

The details under the list say more about the file you are on, including where it is used. On a site with 500 files or fewer, QUILL works this out while the window is open and tells you when it is ready. On a bigger site, press Used In to search for the one you are on.

The buttons:

  • Insert… puts the file at the caret. A picture opens the picture window first.
  • Play… plays a sound or video file (see the next section).
  • Used In says which pages and posts link to the file.
  • Attach to This Page or Post makes the open page the file’s parent on the site, which some galleries and themes use.
  • Set as Featured Image makes the picture the open page’s featured image.
  • Suggest Alternative Text… and Describe Image…, as above.
  • Edit Alternative Text… changes the library’s own alternative text on the site. You hear “Alternative text saved on Community Radio.”
  • Copy Address puts the file’s web address on the clipboard.
  • Remove from Library… deletes the file from the site.

Enter on a row plays a sound or video file and inserts anything else.

Removing a file is the one thing that cannot be undone on the site. WordPress has no Trash for media. So before removing a file, QUILL tells you how many pages use it, and saves a copy in your Documents folder, under “Removed from WordPress”. You hear where the copy went.

Listening and watching

Play in the Media Library opens a small player and starts the file at once. It streams the file from your site and saves nothing on your computer.

  • The keys are the same as QUILL’s own player and Quill Media Player: Play and Pause, Stop, Rewind, Forward, Where Am I, Speed, Volume and Mute.
  • Go to Position… jumps to a time you type, such as 0:01:30.
  • Captions speaks the caption on screen at the moment, when the video has a captions file on your site.
  • Open in Quill Media Player hands the file over, for bookmarks and resume. Playback here pauses.
  • Open in Browser opens the file’s address in your browser.
  • Close, or Escape, stops playback and closes the window.

Video shows its picture where the playback engine can. If it cannot, the window says so once and points you to Open in Browser.

Play on a picture says “That file is not audio or video.”

View > Play Media Here does the same for the sound or video block the caret is in, inside a page.

Uploading

These are in Tools > WordPress > More Media:

  • Upload Files… sends one or many files at once. For each one QUILL asks for a title and caption, and, for a picture, its alternative text first. Cancel skips that file and the rest carry on. If the same file is already on the site, QUILL asks whether to use the one already there. You hear how far along it is, such as “Uploading 2 of 5”, and at the end what was uploaded and what was not, and why.
  • Upload from Web Address… fetches a picture, sound, video or document from an address starting https, then uploads it to your site. It will not fetch an ordinary web page, an address that is not secure, or a file over 64 MB.
  • Paste Image takes a picture you have copied, asks for its alternative text, uploads it and puts it at the caret.
  • Upload a Large File… sends a long recording or video up in pieces, so a slow connection or a strict host does not lose the whole upload. You hear how far it has got.

Making a picture smaller, and changing how big it shows

A photograph straight from a phone can be several megabytes, which is slow for visitors on a phone connection. Two commands help, and they do different things:

  • Tools > WordPress > More Media > Make a Smaller Copy… makes a new, smaller file. Choose the largest width and a format: JPEG for photographs, WebP for smaller still, PNG for sharp edges such as a logo. The copy is made on your computer, QUILL tells you the size before and after, and only then asks whether to upload it as a new file. Your original stays in the library.
  • Format > WordPress > Image Size… changes how wide a picture in your page shows, without touching the file. Put the caret on the picture and choose from the sizes WordPress made of it. Nothing is cropped. Next time, Back to Earlier Size is at the top of the list, even after you have saved and closed the page.

Editing a picture on the site

Tools > WordPress > More Media > Edit a Picture on the Site… turns, flips or crops a picture (square, 4 by 3, or 16 by 9), where your site offers it. The result is a new item in the library, so the original is kept. With AI on, QUILL describes the new picture for you.

Tools > WordPress > More Media > Regenerate Sizes… remakes a picture’s smaller copies after an edit, so the theme shows the change everywhere.

Alternative text for a whole page, or the whole site

  • Tools > WordPress > Alt Text Review… lists every picture in the page you have open, with its alternative text. It points out the ones with none, the ones that are just a file name, the very long ones and the ones that start “image of”. You can edit each one, mark it decorative, ask your AI for a suggestion, or go straight to it.
  • Tools > WordPress > Alt Text for the Whole Library… steps through every picture on the site that has no alternative text, with Suggest, and saves your answers in batches.

Making a picture with AI

Insert > Make a Picture… opens the Picture Maker, the same one QUILL Studio uses.

  1. Describe the picture you want: the subject, the style, the colours, and anything to avoid. Not sure? Suggest from Page Content reads the page, or just your selection, and fills in a description for you to change.
  2. Choose a shape: wide, square or tall.
  3. Choose a background: Normal, Solid colour, or Transparent when your provider can make one.
  4. Press Make the picture. QUILL names the provider and model, and asks first.

The picture is saved on your computer only, and a description of what was actually drawn opens straight away, so you know what you got. Every version is kept. To change one, type only the change, such as “make the sky darker”, and press Make the change.

Insert into Document opens the usual picture window, and the picture is uploaded to your Media Library when you press Insert.

A made picture is an illustration, never a screenshot. If you ask for an app’s screen or a dialog, QUILL says so once.

The site logo and icon

Tools > WordPress > More Media > Site Logo… shows your site’s logo and its small site icon: the file, its real size and its alternative text. Replace either one from the library, from a file, or from the Picture Maker. QUILL names the site and the new picture and asks before it changes anything. The old image stays in your Media Library, and Restore Earlier puts back anything QUILL replaced. Changing the logo needs the Administrator role.

Hearing what a sighted visitor sees

Tools > WordPress > Visual Review… tells you what a sighted visitor sees on a published page.

  1. QUILL opens the public page in Microsoft Edge, or Chrome if Edge is missing, hidden in the background, at a desktop width and at a phone width.
  2. It measures every picture: its size, whether it spills out of the page, whether it loaded, and its alternative text.
  3. It tells you what it would send to your AI, and sends it only if you say yes.

The report starts with anything you should know, such as unpublished changes not being in it, then the review, then the measurements. Each finding is labelled as likely a problem, a design choice, or worth checking. QUILL never downloads a browser.

Tools > WordPress > More Media > Preview and Review… does the same with a change you have not made yet. Choose a picture on the page, or the site logo, and type a width. QUILL shows that width in its captured copy only, so nothing on your site changes.

Both work on published pages. A draft can only be opened by someone signed in to WordPress in a browser, so QUILL explains that instead.

Captions, transcripts and listening versions

These are in Tools > WordPress > More Media. The heavy work happens on this computer, and nothing reaches your site until you have reviewed it.

Captions for Video…

  1. Choose a video from the site’s Media Library.
  2. QUILL makes captions on this computer, with the same transcription it uses everywhere else, so the video is never sent anywhere to be transcribed.
  3. Review Captions opens. Each caption is one line: its time, then its words. Correct anything misheard, change “Speaker 1” to a name, and add sounds such as [applause].
  4. Choose OK. QUILL uploads the captions and connects them to the video in your page, so the player’s CC button works. If the video is not in the page yet, QUILL puts it at the caret with its captions.

Transcript for Audio… does the same for a sound file. After you review the transcript, choose whether it goes under the player folded away as “Read the transcript”, or in full.

Make a Listening Version… reads your page aloud with your Read Aloud voice, saves it as an MP3, uploads it, and puts a player at the top labelled, for example, “Listen to this post (6 minutes)”. Run it again after you change the page: QUILL tells you the recording is out of date and offers to make it again, and the new recording replaces the old one.

Name Embeds asks your site what every embedded YouTube, Vimeo or podcast link in the page is, and gives each one without a caption its title, so nobody meets an unnamed frame. A report says what each one turned out to be.

Publish a Document as a Page… Choose a PDF or Word file. QUILL converts it on this computer, uploads the original to the Media Library, and opens the text as a new page draft with a link to the original at the top. Many people find a page easier to read than a PDF. A scanned PDF is only read with OCR after you say yes.

Your page changes go to the site only with your next Save Draft to Site or Publish.

Posting from QUILL’s other apps

Three other apps in the family can send things to your site, through the same WordPress code and the same saved sites:

  • QUILL Cast can post an episode you have downloaded, with its player, show notes, chapters and transcript: Episode > Post This Episode to WordPress….
  • QUILL Studio can post a finished recording the same way, from its WordPress menu: Post an Episode to WordPress….
  • Quill Converter can upload the files it made straight to your Media Library: File > Upload to WordPress….

Cast and Studio write a local WordPress draft and open QUILL with it, so you can read it over before sending. If QUILL cannot start, the draft waits for you, in QUILL and in QUILL Lite, under File > WordPress > Open Local Draft….
