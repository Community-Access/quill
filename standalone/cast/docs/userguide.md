# QUILL Cast User Guide

## Introduction

Welcome, and thank you for giving QUILL Cast a try.

Cast is a podcast player for people who listen with a screen reader. I am
blind, I use one every day, and I built Cast because I wanted a podcast app
that felt as if it had been made for me. Everything in it is a list you can
arrow through. Every row reads as a whole sentence, so one press of an arrow
key tells you what an episode is, which podcast it is from and whether you
have heard it. Every command has a key,
and Cast tells you how each thing you do turned out.

If you love podcasts, or would like to, and you find your way around Windows
with JAWS, NVDA or Narrator, Cast is for you. There is no account to make and
nothing to sign up for. You install it, open it, and start listening.

### What you will be able to do

By the time you have read this guide you will be able to find and follow
podcasts by name, bring your list across from another podcast app, and play
any episode with a single key. You will know how to pause, skip, change the
speed, jump between chapters and set a sleep timer. You will have new episodes
gathering in your Inbox each morning, a Play Queue lined up for the drive to
work, and Cast set up to sound and behave the way you like. And when something
puzzles you, you will know where to find help.

None of this needs to happen on day one. Chapter 1 gets you listening in about
ten minutes. Everything after that is there for when you are curious.

### How this guide is laid out

The chapters follow the order you are likely to want things.

1. **Chapter 1: Your first ten minutes.** A hand-held first session. You open
   Cast, follow a podcast, play an episode, pause and skip, and learn where to
   go next.
2. **Chapter 2: Finding your way around.** A walk around the one window: the
   Places list, the episode lists, Find, Go To, the status bar and the menus.
3. **Chapter 3: Sit back and listen.** Everything for the episode that is
   playing: skipping, chapters, speed, volume, show notes, bookmarks and the
   sleep timer.
4. **Chapter 4: Finding podcasts you will love.** Following a podcast by name
   or by its address, private feeds, bringing your list from another app, your
   own recordings, and keeping it all tidy in folders.
5. **Chapter 5: Keeping up without keeping watch.** How new episodes reach you
   through the Inbox and New Episodes, the Play Queue, downloads, playlists and
   notifications.
6. **Chapter 6: Making Cast yours.** Preferences, settings for one podcast,
   Quick Actions, changing keys, switching off what you never use, and AI help.
7. **Chapter 7: Your podcasts, wherever you are.** Listening statistics,
   backups, moving to a new computer, and what Cast shares with QUILL and
   Quill Radio.
8. **Chapter 8: When you need a hand.** F1 help, finding a forgotten command,
   undo, tutorials, updates, support and answers to common snags.
9. **Chapter 9: QUILL Lineups.** Lists of episodes, and anything else Quill
   Radio plays, that fill themselves by rules you write and keep the order you
   choose. Shared with Quill Radio, and played through the Play Queue.

After the chapters comes the **Keyboard reference**: every key Cast comes
with, in small tables grouped by where you use them. Keep it handy for the
first few weeks.

Cast also has guided tutorials, short lessons you do with Cast open in front
of you, on Help > Tutorials (Ctrl+Alt+F1). Whenever a lesson covers the same
ground as part of this guide, I give you its name, so you can choose between
reading about something and being walked through it.

Now and then a short note from QUILLBee, QUILLVille's guide, is set apart as a
quote: a faster key, a word explained, or something worth knowing before it
catches you out. QUILLBert, the curious porcupine, wanders in once or twice
when he has found something. The guide reads perfectly well if you skip every
one of them, and you can meet the pair properly on the [Meet QUILLBert and
QUILLBee](https://www.quillforall.org/meet-quillbert-and-quillbee.html) page.

### Reading this guide with a screen reader

The guide is built from headings so you can move through it quickly. Each
chapter is a level 2 heading, and the main topics in a chapter are level 3
headings. When a topic has parts of its own, those parts sit one level deeper,
at level 4, and once or twice at level 5. In JAWS or NVDA, press 2 to move
from chapter to chapter, 3 to move between the main topics, and 4 to step
through the parts of a topic. Shift with any of those numbers goes back. To
see every heading at once, press Insert+F6 in JAWS or Insert+F7 in NVDA and
choose headings.

The notes from QUILLBee and QUILLBert are block quotes. In a web browser, Q
moves to the next one in JAWS and NVDA, and Shift+Q goes back, so they are as
easy to skip as they are to find.

Every chapter ends with a short section called What you learned, and where to
go next. If you are skimming, those are a quick way to hear what a chapter
covers before you read it.

Keys are written the way Cast writes them in its menus. Ctrl+Shift+I means
hold down Ctrl and Shift together and press I. A menu path such as Podcasts >
Add Podcast means open the Podcasts menu and choose Add Podcast.

When a section walks you through something, the steps are numbered. Follow
them with Cast open beside the guide and you will learn faster than by
reading alone. Alt+Tab moves you between the two.

You do not always need this guide to answer a question. Every window in Cast
answers **F1** with a short note on what that window is for and what the
control you are on does. When you wonder what a checkbox will change, F1 is
often the quickest answer.

## Chapter 1: Your first ten minutes

This chapter is your first session with Cast, and I will stay with you the
whole way. You will open Cast, follow a podcast, play one of its episodes,
pause it, skip around in it and turn the volume up. By the end you will have
something playing that you chose, you will know the handful of keys that do
most of the work, and you will know which chapter to read next. Put the kettle
on. Ten minutes is plenty.

### A few words before you start

A **podcast** is a show that publishes episodes, a bit like a radio programme
you can hear whenever you like. When you **follow** a podcast, Cast keeps an
eye on it for you and collects each new episode as it comes out. Following is
free, and you can unfollow any time.

Behind every podcast is a **feed**, a web address where the podcast lists its
episodes. You rarely need to know it, because Cast can find a podcast by its
name. It is handy to know the word if someone sends you one.

An episode you have not listened to yet is **unheard**. Cast uses that word
everywhere, so you always know what is new to you.

### Opening Cast for the first time

Open QUILL Cast from the Start menu.

The installer is for the Windows account you are using. It does not offer an
all-users installation, and a normal install does not require administrator
approval. The shared runtime is installed for that account too.

On a new install, Cast greets you with one short window called **Welcome to
QUILL Cast**. It asks one question and then gets out of your way.

1. The welcome words are in a text box at the top. Arrow through them at your
   own pace, and read any line again whenever you like.
2. Press Tab to reach **Where would you like to land each time?** (Alt+W).
   This is the place Cast puts you each time it opens. Leave it on **What is
   new** if you are not sure: Cast then opens on your Inbox when something is
   waiting, otherwise on an episode you are part way through, and otherwise
   on your podcasts. You can change it later in Preferences.
3. Press **Add Your First Podcast** (Alt+A) to go straight to finding one,
   which the next section walks through. Or press **Done** (Enter) to start
   with an empty library.

**Skip** (Escape) leaves the welcome without changing anything. Skipping
counts as done, so it will not come back next time. If you already have
podcasts, from a backup or another QUILL app, Cast does not show it at all.

The welcome also has a checkbox, **Show me a tip now and then** (Alt+T).
Leave it checked. The first time you reach somewhere new, such as the Play
Queue, Cast says one helpful sentence about it. Each tip is said once, ever.
It goes to your braille display as well as to speech, and it never takes your
focus or makes you press anything.

Everything the welcome does not cover is in the **Tutorials** (Help >
Tutorials, Ctrl+Alt+F1), short guided lessons you can take whenever you like.
The very first one, **The first hour**, walks the same path as this chapter,
one step at a time. Chapter 8, When you need a hand, has more on the
tutorials.

### Following your first podcast

Think of a podcast you have heard about. Perhaps a friend mentioned it, or you
heard it on the radio this morning. Let's add it.

1. Press **Ctrl+N** (Podcasts > Add Podcast). The Add Podcast window opens
   with your cursor in the **Podcast name** box.
2. Type a few words of the podcast's name and press Enter.
3. Cast tells you how many podcasts it found.
4. Press **Alt+R** to move to the **Results** list, and arrow through it.
5. When you hear the one you want, press **Alt+O** for **Follow**.

Cast says "Now following", the podcast's name and how many episodes it has.
Its episodes are fetched in a few seconds. Press Escape to close the Add
Podcast window when you have added all you want.

Not sure you have the right one? Plenty of podcasts share a name. Press Enter
on a result instead and Cast shows you a preview of the podcast and its recent
episodes before you decide. Chapter 4, Finding podcasts you will love, covers
the preview and every other way to add a podcast, including by its feed
address.

#### Two quick ways to fill your library

If you would like more than one podcast to start with, here are two shortcuts.

**Follow ACB Media's podcasts.** Choose Podcasts > Follow ACB Media Podcasts
(**Ctrl+Alt+B**). Cast follows ACB Media's whole live directory in one step,
which gives you plenty to explore straight away.

**Bring your list from another app.** Most podcast apps can save the podcasts
you follow as an OPML file. Save one from your old app, then in Cast press
Ctrl+N and choose **Import OPML...** (Alt+I) in the Add Podcast window. Pick
the file and press Import. Your folders come across too, and you can keep
listening while it works. Chapter 4 has the details.

Until you follow something, the Podcasts place (Ctrl+Shift+S) holds three
rows to get you started: **Add a Podcast by URL...**, **Import Podcasts from
OPML...** and **Find a Podcast...**. Press Enter on any of them. They go away
by themselves as soon as you follow your first podcast.

### Hearing an episode

Now for the good part.

1. Press **Ctrl+Shift+S** to go to **Podcasts**. This is your library, laid
   out as a tree with one row for each podcast you follow.
2. Arrow to the podcast you followed a moment ago.
3. Press **Enter**. Cast plays its next unheard episode and tells you what it
   is playing.

If you have heard every episode already, Enter plays the most recent one and
says so.

Want a particular episode instead? Press **Right Arrow** on the podcast to
open it, arrow down through its episodes, newest first, and press Enter on the
one you want. Left Arrow takes you back up to the podcast. On an expanded
folder, Left Arrow collapses it and keeps focus on that row in the Podcasts
tree; it does not jump to Places.

Lose track of what is on? Press **Ctrl+T** from anywhere in Cast and it tells
you.

#### Pausing, skipping and volume

Let the episode play for a moment, then try each of these. The transport
shortcuts work in the main window unless the focused control needs a key for
text entry or arrow navigation. Text fields keep their editing keys; trees and
lists keep their arrow navigation.

1. Press **Ctrl+P** to pause. Press it again and the episode carries on from
   the same spot.
2. Press **Shift+Right** to skip forward 30 seconds. Press **Shift+Left** to
   skip back. Bare Left and Right stay with the focused control; in the Now
   Playing window, they seek while its slider has focus.
3. Press **Ctrl+Up** to make it louder and **Ctrl+Down** to make it quieter.
4. Press **Ctrl+.** (Ctrl+Period) to stop.

When you stop, Cast remembers where you were. Play that episode again and it
picks up where you left it, today or next month.

The buttons in the main window do the same jobs, if you prefer them. **Play**
(Alt+Y) says in its label which episode it will play. While something is
playing it becomes **Pause** or **Resume** (Alt+S), and **Stop** is Alt+T.

Chapter 3, Sit back and listen, has everything else for the episode that is
playing: chapters, speed, a sleep timer and more.

### Queueing up what comes next

Here is one more habit worth learning on day one. The **Play Queue** is your
listening list for later. When one episode finishes, the next one in the queue
plays.

1. In Podcasts, press Right Arrow on a podcast to open its episodes.
2. Arrow to an episode you would like to hear later.
3. Press **Space**. Cast says it has been added to the Play Queue.

Press **Ctrl+Shift+Q** to go to the Play Queue and hear what is lined up.
Chapter 5, Keeping up without keeping watch, shows how the Play Queue and the
Inbox can keep you supplied without you lifting a finger.

### Where everything lives

Press **Alt+L**. This is the **Places list**, the map of Cast. Each row is a
place, with a count beside it: Inbox, New Episodes, Continue Listening,
Favorites, Playlists, Personal Audio, Play Queue, Downloads, Notifications and
Podcasts.

Arrow down the list and the area beside it changes to match as you go; focus
stays in Places. Enter or Right Arrow updates the place without moving focus
out of this list. Press Tab to move into the content and Shift+Tab to return
to Places. Backspace
returns from an opened show or playlist to its place. Two places worth knowing
about today: the **Inbox** is where new episodes wait until you decide about
them, and **Continue Listening** holds anything you have started and not
finished.

The menus are across the top: Podcasts (Alt+P), Edit (Alt+I), View (Alt+V),
Episode (Alt+E), Downloads (Alt+D), Window (Alt+W) and Help (Alt+H). Every
menu item shows its key, so opening a menu is a gentle way to learn keys as
you go.

Chapter 2, Finding your way around, walks you through each place and the rest
of the window.

If you like learning by doing, the tutorials on **Ctrl+Alt+F1** walk you

Keyboard reference at the end of this guide has every other key, grouped by
where you use them.

> **QUILLBee's shortcut:** One key isn't in the table, and it's the one to
> reach for when the others slip your mind. Press Ctrl+Shift+P for the Command
> Palette and type a word, like "sleep" or "queue". Each command it finds
> comes with its key, when it has one, so you learn the key while you use it.

### How Cast talks to you

Cast tells you how everything you do turned out. It speaks through your screen
reader and writes to your braille display at the same time. It only tells you
things your screen reader would not already say, such as a download finishing
or the result of a command, and it never moves your focus to do it.

Missed something? Press **F9** and Cast says it again.

> **A note from QUILLBee:** F9 only goes back one. If two things were said
> while you were busy elsewhere, press Shift+F9 for Activity instead. It lists
> everything Cast has told you this session, newest first, one sentence to a
> row.

When you add an episode to the queue, remove one from a place, or mark one as
played, Cast plays a short sound as well as saying what happened. Once you
know the sounds, you may prefer just the sound, or just the words. Choose in
Preferences (Ctrl+,), in the **Telling you** section, under **When a one-key
action works**. If something does not work, Cast always says so in words.

Braille settings are shared by the whole QUILL family. If you also use QUILL,
set them once there under Preferences > Accessibility and Cast follows along.

### When you press the wrong thing

We all do it. Any question that would delete something starts on **No**, so
pressing Enter too soon loses nothing. To say Yes, arrow to it first.

If something goes anyway, press **Ctrl+Z** (Edit > Undo Last Action). It
brings back the last podcast you unfollowed, the last episode you removed or
the last thing you marked, and tells you what it restored. When an action can
be undone, Cast ends its message with "Ctrl+Z undoes this."

### Listening with the window out of the way

Cast keeps playing when you put it away, so you can get on with your email
while the episode carries on.

1. Press **Ctrl+W** (Podcasts > Send to Tray).
2. Cast says "QUILL Cast is still running in the system tray."
3. To bring it back, double-click the Cast icon in the notification area,
   or choose Show from its menu.

The tray icon also has a menu. Open it with the Applications key or Shift+F10
and you will find playback controls, Show and Exit.

To choose what closing the window does, open Preferences (**Ctrl+,**) and pick
**The window** in the Section list. Closing can exit Cast, send it to the
tray, or ask you each time.

If you choose to be asked, closing the window brings up a small question
called **Closing QUILL Cast**. Press **Exit** to close Cast, or **Minimize to
Tray** (Alt+M) to keep listening with the window out of the way. Check **Don't
ask me again** (Alt+D) and Cast remembers your answer. Cancel, or Escape,
leaves everything as it was.

One more choice sits beside it: **Alt+F4 minimizes to the system tray**. With
it checked, Alt+F4 tucks Cast into the tray, still playing, instead of closing
it.

If your keyboard has media keys, Play/Pause, Stop, Next Track and Previous
Track control Cast from any program, even while it is in the tray. Next and
Previous move by chapter.

There is one more key that works from anywhere: **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F12** shows
or hides Cast, whatever window you are in. Press it while Cast is showing and
Cast tells you it is hidden in the tray. Press it again and Cast comes back
with focus and tells you it is shown. The episode keeps playing either way.
If another program already uses that key, Cast leaves it alone, and the tray
icon still works.

### Opening Cast next time

Cast opens where something is waiting for you. If new episodes have arrived,
it opens on the Inbox. Otherwise, if you are partway through an episode, it
opens on Continue Listening. Otherwise it opens on Podcasts.

Would you like Cast to carry on playing the moment it opens? Choose Podcasts >
Resume Last Episode on Launch (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L**) once. From then on,
opening Cast picks up exactly where you left off.

### Cast, QUILL and Quill Radio share your podcasts

If you use QUILL or Quill Radio too, they share your podcasts with Cast.
Follow a podcast in Cast and QUILL has it as well. Your Play Queue, your place
in each episode, your notes and your downloads are one set across all three.
Start an episode in Cast and any station playing in Quill Radio stops, so you
never hear two things at once.

Uninstalling Cast never deletes your podcasts. They will be waiting if you
come back. Chapter 7, Your podcasts, wherever you are, has more on what the
apps share.

### What you learned, and where to go next

You have followed a podcast, heard it, paused it, skipped through it and lined
up something for later. That is a real first session, and those few keys will
carry you through most days.

If you would like to go over it again with Cast keeping you company, Help >
Tutorials has a lesson called **The first hour**. It follows the same steps
and moves you on as you do each one.

When you are ready, Chapter 2, Finding your way around, takes you for a walk
around the window. If you would rather get a few more podcasts in first, skip
ahead to Chapter 4, Finding podcasts you will love.

## Chapter 2: Finding your way around

You have a podcast playing, and now you want to know where everything else
lives. This chapter is a walk around QUILL Cast with me beside you. Everything
in Cast happens in one window, so once you know the shape of that window,
there is nothing hidden behind another door.

We will go through the window from top to bottom, step in and out of the
places where your episodes live, find an episode by typing a word or two, and
try the shortcuts that jump straight to where you want to be. Have Cast open
while you read, and try each thing as we meet it.

### A walk from top to bottom

Think of the main window as a short column of things, one under the other.
Tab moves you down the column and Shift+Tab moves you back up. Try it now:
press Tab a few times and listen to what you meet.

1. **Now playing** (Alt+G). A line naming the episode that is playing, its
   podcast, and how far in you are. Cast never reads it out on its own, so it
   stays out of your way. Come here when you want to hear a title again or
   spell a name letter by letter. **Ctrl+T** says the same thing from
   anywhere in Cast.
2. **Find** (Alt+N, or Ctrl+F from anywhere). Type here to find anything in
   your library. There is a section on it below.
3. **Places** (Alt+L). The map of Cast: the Inbox, the Play Queue, your
   podcasts and the rest.
4. **The place you are in.** Whatever the selected place holds, usually a
   list of episodes.
5. **Show notes** (Alt+O). The notes for the episode under your cursor. They
   sit one Tab after the list, so you can check what an episode is about
   before you play it. Chapter 3, Sit back and listen, shows you how to read
   them.
6. **The buttons.** Each one says in its label what it will act on, so you
   always know what you are about to press.
   - **Play** and the name of the selected episode (Alt+Y). While something
     plays it becomes **Pause**, and while paused it becomes **Resume**
     (Alt+S).
   - **Stop** (Alt+T).
   - **Add to Favorites** (Alt+F), for the podcast that is playing. If it is
     already a favorite, the button says **Remove from Favorites**.
   - **Unfollow** and the selected podcast's name (Alt+U). Cast asks first,
     and Ctrl+Z brings the podcast back if you change your mind.
   - **Add Podcast** (Alt+A).

The **status bar** runs along the very bottom. Tab skips it on purpose, so it
never slows down your trip through the window. F6 takes you in and out, and
there is a section on it below.

When you open Cast and new episodes arrived while it was closed, it greets you
with one sentence, something like "Since yesterday: 4 new episodes from 3
podcasts. They are in the Inbox." It is a nice way to start the day.

### The Places list

Your episodes are sorted into **places**. A place is a list with a purpose:
the Inbox holds new episodes waiting for you, the Play Queue holds what you
want to hear next, Downloads holds what is saved on this computer, and so on.
The Places list is how you move between them. Each place has a count beside
it, so you can hear at a glance how much is waiting.

Here is every place, in the order Cast starts with, and the key that takes you
straight there from anywhere in the window.

| Place | What it holds | Key |
|---|---|---|
| Inbox | New episodes waiting for you to decide about them | Ctrl+Shift+I |
| New Episodes | Every unheard episode | Ctrl+Shift+W |
| Continue Listening | Episodes you stopped partway through | Ctrl+Shift+L |
| Favorites | Your favorite podcasts | Alt+Shift+V |
| Playlists | Your playlists | Alt+Shift+Y |
| Personal Audio | Your own recordings and audiobooks | Ctrl+Shift+U |
| Play Queue | What plays next, in order | Ctrl+Shift+Q |
| Recently Expired | Episodes that waited in the queue too long (hidden until you show it) | Ctrl+Shift+X |
| Downloads | Episodes kept on this computer | Ctrl+D |
| Notifications | What Cast has told you, newest first | Ctrl+Shift+N |
| Podcasts | Your folders and podcasts, as a tree | Ctrl+Shift+S |

Every one of these is also on the View menu, so you never have to remember the
keys. You will pick up the ones you use most without trying.

To wander between places:

1. Press **Alt+L** to go to the Places list.
2. Arrow up and down. The list beside it changes as you go, so you can peek
   into a place without going in.
3. Press **Enter** or **Right Arrow** to update that place without leaving
   the Places list. Press **Tab** to move into its content.
4. Press **Shift+Tab** to return to Places. Up or Left at the first content
   row does not jump into another control. **Backspace** returns from an
   opened podcast or playlist to its containing place.

Walk into an empty place and Cast tells you, once, what fills it. In an empty
Play Queue you hear "The queue is empty. Space on any episode adds it."

In the beta builds, a large place shows **loading...** while Cast prepares
its counts and rows from a detached catalogue. Choosing another place discards
the earlier result rather than moving you back when it finishes. Downloads
remains a place in this main window, not a separate storage-report dialog.
Opening, row publication and transport/status updates happen in small UI
steps. If you move focus while an opening is pending, its completion does
not take focus back. Closing Cast during preparation also retires unfinished
detached input in the background rather than waiting to free it.
Responsiveness with a very large library is still being checked in the
beta builds.

**Ctrl+F5** checks every podcast without repeatedly rebuilding the Podcasts
tree as each feed answers. Expanded podcasts keep your selected episode while
new rows and updated titles arrive. Moving a podcast to another folder leaves
you on the next podcast where it came from, or on the previous podcast or
containing folder if there is no next one. Rapid arrow presses keep the buttons
pointed at your latest selection instead of updating every intermediate row.
Closing Cast finishes pending file
work and saves in the background while Windows continues processing events.

#### Making the Places list your own

The list starts in the order most people find useful. You are not most people.
If you live in the Play Queue and rarely open the Inbox, put the Play Queue at
the top. Go to the Places list, arrow to a place, and then:

- **Alt+Shift+Up** and **Alt+Shift+Down** move it, and Cast says where it
  went: "Continue Listening moved to first."
- **Delete** hides a place you never use. Its key still works, and Ctrl+Z
  brings it back straight away.
- **F2** gives it your own name. Call the Inbox "Morning pile" if you like.
  Cast remembers.
- **The Applications key** (or Shift+F10) opens a menu of what the whole place
  can do, such as Clear Entire Queue in the Play Queue or Mark All as Played
  in the Inbox. The same menu has **Rename This Place...**, **Hide This
  Place**, **Move Up** and **Move Down**, in case you forget the keys, and
  **Places...** at the bottom for the whole list at once. On a list of
  episodes, such as the Inbox or the Play Queue, it also has **Choose
  Columns...**, which Chapter 6, Making Cast yours, explains.

To rearrange everything in one go, choose **View > Places** (Ctrl+Shift+G).
You can move, hide, show and rename places there, and **Reset to Default**
puts it all back the way it came. This is also where you show Recently Expired
if you want it.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Places**. It also takes you into the status bar and Go To.

### Lists of episodes

Most places hold episodes: the Inbox, New Episodes, Continue Listening,
Personal Audio, the Play Queue, Recently Expired and Downloads. They all work
the same way, so learn one and you know them all. Each row reads as the title,
the podcast, the length and the status.

Arrow to an episode, then:

- **Enter** plays it.
- **Space** adds it to the end of the Play Queue.
- **Delete** takes it out of this place only. It stays in your library.
- **F2** gives the episode a name of your own, handy when a podcast calls
  every episode "Episode". Your name sticks, even after Cast checks the
  podcast again. Clear it and the podcast's own title comes back. You can
  rename a podcast the same way.
- **Shift+F10** or the Applications key opens everything else you can do with
  it: play next, download, mark as played, read the show notes, look at its
  chapters, share it and more.
- **Ctrl+1** to **Ctrl+9** run your Quick Actions. Enter is really Quick
  Action 1, which is Play until you change it. Chapter 6, Making Cast yours,
  shows you how.

Delete always does the sensible thing for the place you are in, and Cast says
what it did. In the Play Queue it takes the episode out of the queue. In
Downloads it deletes the saved file, and you can still stream the episode. In
Continue Listening it forgets your place, so the episode starts from the top
next time. In the Inbox it clears the episode out of the Inbox, and it is
still unheard in its podcast.

Two choices on the View menu change what every list shows:

- **View > Show** narrows every list at once: All Episodes, Unheard Only,
  Started Only, Downloaded Only or Played Only. Choose Unheard Only and every
  list hides what you have already heard.
- **View > Sort Episodes** puts them in the order you like: newest first,
  oldest first, by title, or by length with **Longest First** and **Shortest
  First**. Shortest First is a good way to find something that fits a short
  walk.

#### Working with several episodes at once

Sorting a busy Inbox one episode at a time gets old fast. In any episode list,
the Play Queue included, you can select several and act on them together.

1. Go to the list, for example the Inbox (Ctrl+Shift+I).
2. Select the episodes you want:
   - **Shift+Up** or **Shift+Down** extends the selection.
   - **Ctrl+Space** adds or removes the episode under the cursor.
   - **Ctrl+A** selects the whole list.
   Cast counts as you go.
3. Press **Shift+F10**. The menu opens with actions for the whole selection:
   add them to the queue, download them, mark them played, add them to a
   playlist, remove their downloaded copies, or file them to an Inbox folder.
4. Choose one. Cast tells you how many it did.

Removing downloaded copies deletes only the files. The episodes stay in your
library with your place in each, ready to download again whenever you like.

### Favorites, Playlists, Notifications and Podcasts

Four places hold something other than a plain list of episodes.

**Favorites** lists the podcasts you love most. Press Enter on one and its
episodes open right there in the same list, on the episode you last had
selected in that podcast (see "Where you were in each podcast" below).
Backspace takes you back to your favorites. Delete takes a podcast out of Favorites, and you still follow it.
To add a favorite, play one of its episodes and press Add to Favorites
(Alt+F).

#### Where you were in each podcast

Cast remembers which episode you last had selected in each podcast, and when
you open that podcast again -- later today, or after closing Cast -- the cursor
is back on it. It knows the episode by who it is, not by its row, so new
episodes arriving at the top, a different sort, **View > Show** and a refresh
do not lose your place. If the episode has gone from the feed, you land on the
one published closest to it.

For example: you are working through a history podcast from the beginning and
stop on episode 41. Next week four new episodes have arrived. Open the
podcast from Favorites and the cursor is on episode 41, not on the newest.

Only a podcast's own list moves its place. New Episodes, the Inbox and the
Play Queue mix podcasts, and arrowing through them leaves each podcast's place
alone.

**Playlists** works the same way. Enter opens a playlist in place, Backspace
comes back. Chapter 5, Keeping up without keeping watch, shows how to make
your own.

**Notifications** keeps what Cast has told you, newest first, so a message you
missed is still there. Enter takes you to whatever the notice was about.
When some are unread, **Help > Notifications...** says how many, such as
"Notifications (3 new)...". Chapter 5 has more on it.

**Podcasts** is your whole library as a tree, with folders at the top and
podcasts inside them. Try it now: press **Ctrl+Shift+S** and arrow down
through the podcasts you follow. In the tree:

- **Enter** on a podcast plays its next unheard episode. This is the one I
  use every morning.
- **Right Arrow** opens a podcast to show its episodes, newest first. Left
  Arrow closes it again.
- **Delete** on a podcast unfollows it. Cast asks first, and Ctrl+Z brings it
  back. On a folder, Delete removes the folder and moves its podcasts out of
  it, so you never lose a podcast by deleting a folder.
- **F2** renames a folder.
- **Alt+Up** and **Alt+Down** move a podcast or folder into your own order.
- **Shift+F10** opens the menu for the row you are on. On a podcast it has
  **Play Next Episode**, Add to Favorites, Move to Folder, **Move Up in Custom
  Order** and **Move Down in Custom Order**, Download All Episodes, Remove
  All Downloads, Remove All Episodes, Feed Credentials for a private feed,
  Unfollow and New Folder. On a folder it has **Rename Folder...**, Delete
  Folder, New Folder and Add Podcast. On an episode inside an open podcast it
  has **Play Episode**, and either Download Episode or, once it is saved,
  Stop and **Remove Download**.

When your library grows, **View > Hide Caught-Up Podcasts** (Ctrl+Shift+H)
takes the podcasts with nothing unheard out of the tree, so what is left is
what has something new for you. Chapter 4, Finding podcasts you will love,
shows how to make folders and file podcasts into them.

### Finding a podcast or episode

You half remember an episode about a ferry in Bristol, but not which podcast
it was on. Find will turn it up.

1. Press **Ctrl+F** from anywhere, or Alt+N.
2. Type part of a podcast's name, an episode title, or something you wrote in
   one of your own notes. Try "bristol".
3. The list beside Places fills with matches. When you pause typing, Cast
   says how many it found: "14 matches for bristol."
4. Press **Down Arrow** to move into them. Each row says what it is, such as
   "The Daily, a podcast". Podcasts come first, then episodes, newest first,
   then your notes.
5. Press Enter to play, or Shift+F10 for everything else you can do.
6. Press **Escape** when you are done. Your library comes back with the
   cursor right where you left it.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Find**.

### The status bar

The status bar is a row of small readouts along the bottom of the window:
what is playing, mute, volume, speed, how many episodes are in the Queue, the
Inbox and Downloads, unread notifications, the sleep timer and the clock. It
is a good place to check everything at once.

1. Press **F6** to step into it.
2. **Left Arrow** and **Right Arrow** move from cell to cell. Home and End go
   to the first and last.
3. Press **Enter** to use a cell. On the Queue cell, for example, it takes you
   to the Play Queue, and on the Speed cell it tells you the speed and whether
   it belongs to this podcast or to every podcast. **Shift+F10** opens a
   cell's own menu.
4. Press F6, Escape or Tab to step back out.

Prefer it gone? **View > Show Status Bar** (Ctrl+Shift+B) hides and shows it.

### Go To

The place keys are quick, but there are a lot of them. Go To is the one key
that reaches everything: a short numbered list of places, and the numbers
never move, so your fingers can learn them.

1. Press **Ctrl+G**.
2. A numbered list opens: Podcasts, Continue Listening, the Play Queue,
   Downloads, Bookmarks, Listening Statistics, Lineups, Add a Podcast and so
   on.
3. Press a number to go there, or Escape to stay where you were.

Press **Settings** inside Go To to choose which ten places are on the list
and in what order. Put the one you use most at number 1. Besides every place
in the Places list, you can add Lineups, Sound Enhancements, **Skip Settings** (which
opens Preferences at the Playing section), Quick Actions, **Episode Extras**
(About This Episode), Keyboard Shortcuts, the Keyboard Shortcuts Sheet,
Media Tools and the Places window. Each row also shows
the place's own key, so after a few weeks of Go To you will find you have
learned those too.

### The menus

The menu bar reads **Podcasts** (Alt+P), **Edit** (Alt+I), **View** (Alt+V),
**Episode** (Alt+E), **Downloads** (Alt+D), **Window** (Alt+W) and **Help**
(Alt+H). Every row shows its key, so a stroll through the menus on a quiet
afternoon is a fine way to learn Cast.

- **Podcasts** is your library. Refresh All Now (Ctrl+F5) and Add Podcast (Ctrl+N)
  are at the top, with folders, Settings for This Podcast (Ctrl+Alt+,),
  Preferences (Ctrl+,), Send to Tray (Ctrl+W) and Exit (Ctrl+Q) further down.
- **Edit** has Undo Last Action (Ctrl+Z).
- **View** gets you places: Find, every place with its key, and the Show and
  Sort choices.
- **Episode** is everything about listening: play, skip, speed, chapters, the
  sleep timer and sound. Chapter 3, Sit back and listen, goes through it.
  Lineups... (Ctrl+U) is here too; Chapter 9 is about it.
- **Downloads** pauses and resumes all your downloads.
- **Window** lists your open Cast windows. Ctrl+1 always takes you back to the
  main window, and Ctrl+2 is always Now Playing.
- **Help** has the user guide, tutorials, the keyboard sheet, AI Features and
  ways to reach support. Chapter 8, When you need a hand, covers it.

Now and then a menu row is dimmed. Cast tells you why, so you know what would
make it work. Chapter 8 has more on that.

#### Simple and Advanced

Cast starts in **Simple** mode, with menus that hold what you do week to week:
follow, find, play, queue, download, settings and help. **Advanced** mode adds
the rows you go looking for once you know they exist, such as backups, OPML
import and export, Choose Columns, Quick Actions and the Quillins menu.

1. Choose **View > Advanced Features**, or press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G**.
2. Cast tells you which mode you are now in, and the menus change straight
   away.
3. Press the same key again to switch back.

Even in Simple mode, every Advanced command still works from the Command
Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P) and from Go To. Nothing is out of reach. An
Advanced row's own key, though, works only while Advanced Features is on,
because the key belongs to the menu row.

If you would like an even smaller Cast, **View > Customize Features**
(Ctrl+Alt+C) can switch whole parts of it off, such as Downloads or the sleep
timer. Whatever you turn off, you still have a podcast player. Chapter 6,
Making Cast yours, tells you more.

### Windows that stay open

Most of what Cast opens is a window of its own rather than a box you have to
finish with first: Add Podcast, Feed Check, Settings for This Podcast,
Episode Filters, Smart Playlist rules, Show Notes, the Transcript, Listening
Statistics, Year in Review, About This Episode, Sound Enhancements,
Notifications, Watched Folders and Lineups. They all work the same way:

- Each one has a menu bar, and is listed in the Window menu, so you can go
  back to your library and come back to it.
- Escape or Ctrl+W closes it, and you are back on the row or button you
  opened it from.
- Opening it again brings the same window to the front instead of a second
  copy. If you ask for it on a different podcast or episode, the same window
  shows that one instead.
- Where a window changes settings, its button says **Save** rather than OK,
  and the window stays open afterwards, so you can try something and adjust
  it. Ctrl+S saves too. **Close** leaves without saving anything you have not
  saved.

The few that still ask a single question, such as Delete Folder or the Sleep
Timer, wait for your answer before you can go on, the way any dialog does.

> **QUILLBee's tip:** With three or four of these open, it's easy to lose
> track. The Window menu (Alt+W) lists every Cast window you have open, and
> from any of them, Ctrl+1 takes you back to the main window.

### Getting home

Wherever you are in the window, **Ctrl+Home** takes you back to the place
Cast opens on, with the cursor on its first row. It is the quickest way back
when you have wandered. In a text box, such as the show notes or Find,
Ctrl+Home keeps its usual job of moving to the top of the text.

You can now get around the whole window: the Places list and the place beside

place and back out, how to act on several episodes at once, and how to get
home with Ctrl+Home when you have wandered off.

If you would like some practice, Help > Tutorials has two short lessons for

## Chapter 3: Sit back and listen

This is the good part. You have an episode playing, and this chapter is about
everything you can do with it while it plays, without hunting for anything.

We will start with the everyday keys: pause, skip, change the volume and the
speed. Then we will move around an episode by its chapters, make a podcast
sound the way you like, and set a sleep timer. After that come the things
that make listening richer: the Now Playing window, the show notes and their
links, transcripts, bookmarks and your own notes. There is a lot here, so dip
in for whatever you need today and leave the rest for another evening.

Nearly everything here is on the **Episode** menu (Alt+E), and nearly every
row on that menu has its own key. Give it a few days and you will stop opening
the menu at all. Have something playing as you read, and try each key as you
meet it.

### Playing and pausing

Arrow to an episode in any list and press **Enter**. It plays, from where you
left off if you have heard some of it before.

Once it is playing, these keys work from anywhere in Cast:

1. **Ctrl+P** pauses. Press it again to carry on.
2. **Ctrl+Up** and **Ctrl+Down** turn the volume up and down. Cast says the
   new volume.
3. **Ctrl+Alt+M** (Episode > Mute/Unmute) mutes, and pressing it again brings
   the sound back.
4. **Ctrl+.** (Ctrl+Period) stops. Cast remembers your place, so next time the
   episode carries on where you left it.

If the episode is still loading, Play/Pause changes whether it will start
playing when it is ready. Pressing it again changes that choice back. Cast
reports a playback problem if the audio backend refuses a request; it does
not say that playback paused or resumed when it did not.

Lost track of what is on? Press **Ctrl+T** (Episode > Say Current Episode)
from anywhere, and Cast tells you the episode, the podcast and how far in you
are.

The buttons in the main window do the same jobs, if you prefer them: Play,
Pause or Resume (Alt+Y or Alt+S) and Stop (Alt+T). If you also use Quill Radio,
your hands already know these keys.

### Moving around in an episode

Someone is reading a long sponsor message, or you missed what the guest said a
moment ago. Skipping is the answer.

- **Ctrl+Right** skips forward 30 seconds.
- **Ctrl+Left** skips back 15 seconds.

Cast says where you landed. If you would like bigger or smaller jumps, open
Preferences (Ctrl+,), choose the **Playing** section, and change **Skip
forward by** and **Skip back by**. To change them for one podcast only, use
Settings for This Podcast (Ctrl+Alt+,) instead.

**Going to a time.** A friend says the good bit starts an hour and two minutes
in.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+J** (Episode > Go to Position).
2. Type 1:02:00. Typing 62:00 works too, and so does a number of seconds.
3. Press Enter. You are there.

**How much is left?** You are about to set off on a twenty-minute drive and
want to know whether this episode will fit. Press **Ctrl+Shift+T** (Episode >
Time Remaining). Cast says something like "12:04 of 31:50, 20 minutes left".
If you are listening faster than normal, it also tells you how long that
really takes, such as "13 minutes at 1.5 times", and if the sleep timer is
running it adds when it will stop. Nothing opens and nothing changes.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Keys**. It covers these skips, going to a time, scanning forward and the
Winamp letters.

#### Skipping the intro and the outro

Some podcasts open with the same two minutes of music every week, or close
with a long list of credits. Cast can skip them for you, every time.

1. Select the podcast and press **Ctrl+Alt+,** for Settings for This Podcast.
2. Set **Skip the first** to how many seconds to skip at the start.
3. Set **Stop this far before the end** to how many seconds to leave off the
   end.
4. Press **Save** (Ctrl+S), then **Close**.

The intro skip only happens when an episode starts from the beginning, so
picking up halfway through never throws you past where you were. When Cast
stops early for the outro, it treats the episode as finished and moves on to
whatever comes next. The same two settings are in Preferences > Playing if you
want them for every podcast.

#### Scanning forward

Skipping in jumps is fine when you know how far to go. When you want to hear
where the ad break ends, scanning is better.

1. While an episode plays, hold down **Shift+Right**.
2. Cast says "Scanning forward, 4 times speed", and the episode races along,
   still recognisable as speech.
3. Let go when you hear the part you want. Cast drops back to exactly the
   speed you were listening at and tells you what it is.

### Chapters

Many podcasts split their episodes into **chapters**: the news, the interview,
the listener questions. When an episode has them, you can hop straight from
one part to the next.

- **Ctrl+Alt+Right** goes to the next chapter.
- **Ctrl+Alt+Left** goes to the previous one.

Cast says each chapter's name as you arrive.

To see the whole list:

1. Arrow to the episode and press **Shift+F10** or the Applications key.
2. Choose **Chapters...**. The list shows each chapter's name and where it
   starts.
3. Arrow to one and press Enter, or **Jump To Chapter** (Alt+J), to go
   there.

Cast finds chapters in several places: a list the podcast published, marks
inside a downloaded file, and timestamps the podcast typed into its show
notes. Some podcasts mark only a few highlights instead of a full list. Then
the list calls them **Moments this podcast marked**, so you know you are
looking at the best bits of the episode.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Chapters, and skipping what you did not come for**.

#### Skipping a chapter

Every episode of a podcast has a sponsor chapter you never want to hear. You
can tell Cast to skip it.

1. While the episode plays, open its **Chapters...** list as above.
2. Arrow to the chapter you want to miss and press **Skip This Chapter**
   (Alt+S). The button changes to **Stop Skipping This Chapter**, in case you
   change your mind.
3. Mark as many as you like, then close the list.

When playback reaches a marked chapter, Cast says "Skipping chapter" and its
name, and jumps past it. **Skip Nothing** (Alt+N) clears every mark at once.

The marks last for this listening session only, so tomorrow's episode starts
with a clean slate.

For a chapter that turns up in every episode, such as "Sponsor" or "Ad
break", tell the podcast once. In Settings for This Podcast (Ctrl+Alt+,),
under Playback, type the chapter names into **Skip chapters named**. From
then on Cast jumps over any chapter with that name in every episode of that
podcast. Nothing is removed from the episode.

#### When an episode has no chapters

Most episodes come without any chapters. Cast can work some out for you, in
the background, while you listen. It does this for downloaded episodes from
the start, and you will hear one short sentence when it has finished. You do
not need to wait for it or press anything.

Worked-out chapters say so in the list, and tell you how sure Cast is about
them, so you know they are Cast's best guess.

To choose how hard Cast tries:

1. Open Preferences (**Ctrl+,**).
2. In the **Section** list, choose **Chapters**.
3. Change **How long to spend looking**:
   - **Not long** uses only what Cast already has. Instant.
   - **A while**, the one Cast starts with, reads the episode's published
     transcript if there is one. A few seconds.
   - **As long as it takes** listens to the whole episode on your own
     computer, writes down what was said, and finds the sections from that.
     It takes minutes, works without the internet, and you can stop it any
     time.
4. Press **Save**.

To listen to an episode, Cast uses a small speech engine that comes with it,
called Vosk. It is already installed, so there is nothing to download, and the
audio never leaves your computer. It writes down English. Cast uses it for one
job only, finding chapters: it is not a dictation engine, and there is no other
engine to choose here. To say something into a search box instead, see
"Dictating into a search box" in Chapter 4, which uses QUILL Transcribe.

**Work out chapters**, in the same section, chooses whether Cast does this
never, only for downloaded episodes, or for every episode. The rest of the
Chapters section lets you choose where Cast looks:

- **Look for chapters in the show notes** reads the times the podcast typed
  into its notes. It costs nothing and is usually right.
- **Look for chapters in the transcript** uses a transcript when there is
  one.
- **Listen to the audio to find chapters** lets Cast write down what was said
  when nothing else worked. It is the slow one, so turn it off if you never
  want it.
- **Give worked-out chapters names** asks an AI to name the sections. It
  sends words only, never audio, and it stays off until you turn it on.
- **Say when a chapter scan finishes** gives you one sentence when Cast is
  done, and says nothing when it found nothing.
- **Chapter preview length** is how much Preview This Mark plays either side
  of a chapter.

Each of these can be set for one podcast in Settings for This Podcast, and
each has its own F1 help.

You can also ask for chapters on one episode, right now:

1. Arrow to a downloaded episode and press Shift+F10.
2. Choose **Analyse Chapters...**. Cast tells you how it is getting on while
   it works.
3. When it has finished, the **Review Chapters** window opens with the
   chapters it found.
4. Arrow to a chapter and press **Preview This Mark** (Alt+P) to hear a few
   seconds either side of it. **Stop Preview** (Alt+S) stops.
5. If a mark is in the wrong place, **Nudge Earlier** (Alt+E) and **Nudge
   Later** (Alt+L) move it a little, and **Set Time...** (Alt+T) moves it to a
   time you type. **Rename...** (Alt+N) gives it a better name, **Add Chapter
   Here...** (Alt+A) adds one, and **Delete Chapter** (Alt+D) removes one.
6. Press **Save Chapters** to keep them.

Both are in the Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P) for the episode that is
playing. **Analyse Chapters in This Episode** works them out and opens Review
Chapters, as above. **Find Chapters in This Episode** works them out and keeps
them straight away, for when you trust Cast's guess and just want to get on
with listening.

### Speeding up or slowing down

Some people talk slowly. Some podcasts are best at their natural pace. Cast
lets each podcast have its own speed.

1. Start an episode of the podcast you want to change.
2. Press **Ctrl+Shift+Up** to speed up or **Ctrl+Shift+Down** to slow down. Each
   press moves a tenth, anywhere from half speed to five times.
3. Cast says the new speed and that it belongs to this podcast.
4. **Ctrl+Shift+0** (Episode > Reset Speed to Normal) puts it back to normal.

Cast remembers the speed for that podcast. Your fast-talking news show can
stay at normal speed while the slow, rambling one runs at 1.8, and you never
have to change it again.

Press the same keys with nothing playing and you set the speed for every
podcast that does not have its own. Cast tells you which one you changed.

> **QUILLBee wants you to know:** If an episode plays at a speed you didn't
> expect, its podcast probably has a speed of its own. Press F6 to reach the
> status bar, arrow to the Speed cell and press Enter. Cast tells you the
> speed and what it belongs to. To have that podcast follow the shared speed
> again, open Settings for This Podcast (Ctrl+Alt+,) and press Use shared
> default for Playback speed.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Sleep and speed**. It does the sleep timer with you too.

#### Shortening the silences

Long pauses add up over an hour. Cast can shorten them, so an episode takes
less time without anyone sounding rushed.

The quickest way is **Ctrl+Shift+9** (Episode > Skip Silence) while an
episode plays. Cast says "Skip Silence on" and the pauses get shorter from
that moment. Press it again to turn it off.

You can also do it from Sound Enhancements:

1. While an episode plays, press **Ctrl+E** for Sound Enhancements.
2. Check **Smart Speed**.
3. Press OK. The episode carries on from the same spot.

Like speed, this belongs to the podcast that is playing, or to every podcast
when nothing is playing. You can also turn on **Shorten silences** in
Preferences > Playing for every podcast at once. Just below it, **How much
silence to shorten** chooses A little, A moderate amount, or As much as
possible. Even at its strongest, it never speeds anyone up. It only trims the
gaps.

### Making it sound right

Some podcasts need a little help before they sound good in your ears. Here is
how to shape the sound, lift a podcast that is always too quiet, and choose
where the sound goes.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Shape the sound**.

#### Sound Enhancements

Some podcasts are muddy, some are thin, and some swing from a whisper to a
roar. **Sound Enhancements** lets you shape the sound.

1. While an episode plays, press **Ctrl+E** (Episode > Sound Enhancements).
2. Choose a **Quick preset**: Flat, Bass Boost, Voice Clarity or Podcast.
   Voice Clarity is a good first try for a muddy recording.
3. If you want finer control, move the **Bass**, **Mid** and **Treble**
   sliders. Up is more, unless you chose the Windows keys in Slider Keys
   (see "Which way the arrow keys move a slider", below).
4. Check **Even Out Volume** to bring quiet and loud parts closer together.
   It is lovely for a laughing guest and a softly spoken host.
5. Press **Apply** (Alt+Y). Playback picks up at the same spot with the new
   sound, and the window stays open so you can try something else.
6. When it sounds right, press **Close**, Escape or Ctrl+W. You are back where
   you were.

Sound Enhancements is a window of its own, so you can leave it open and go
back to your library while you listen. Press Ctrl+E again and it comes to
the front, showing the settings for whatever is playing now.

Like speed, these settings belong to the podcast that is playing, or to every
podcast when nothing is. The same three sliders are in Preferences > Playing
as **Bass, in decibels**, **Middle, in decibels** and **Treble, in
decibels**, with **Even out volume** beside them, if you want a starting
sound for every podcast. Sound Enhancements needs a free tool called FFmpeg.
If it is missing, the episode plays as normal and Cast tells you why. Chapter
8, When you need a hand, explains how to get it.

#### Making a quiet podcast louder

One podcast always seems to be recorded in a cupboard. Turning up the
computer's volume for it means turning it down again for everything else.
Give that podcast a boost instead.

1. Select the podcast and press **Ctrl+Alt+,** for Settings for This Podcast.
2. Set **Volume Boost** to Low, Medium or High.
3. Press **Save** (Ctrl+S), then **Close**.

Cast applies the boost every time that podcast plays, and leaves everything
else alone. Chapter 6, Making Cast yours, has more on what Settings for This
Podcast can do.

#### Which ear and which speakers

**Ctrl+Shift+M** (Episode > Audio Output Mode) changes where the sound goes.
Each press moves on to the next: stereo, mono, left ear only, right ear only.
Left ear only is handy when one ear is for the podcast and the other is for
your screen reader. To make a choice stick, set **Send the audio to** in
Preferences > Playing, or in Settings for This Podcast for one podcast.

**Ctrl+Shift+K** (Episode > Audio Output Device) chooses which speakers or
headphones Cast plays through. If Cast cannot switch devices itself, it says
so and offers to open the Windows sound settings, where you can send Cast to
any device. Windows remembers your choice.

#### Which way the arrow keys move a slider

The volume slider in Now Playing and the sliders in Sound Enhancements answer
the arrow keys the same way, and you choose which way that is. Press
**Ctrl+,** for Preferences, go to the **When Cast opens** section, and press
**Slider Keys...** (beside App Sounds Play On):

- **Up is more** is where it starts. Up, Page Up and Home raise a slider, Home
  being the top, which is 100 percent on a volume. Down, Page Down and End
  lower it, End being 0.
- **As Windows sliders** gives you the keys a standard Windows slider uses:
  Down, Page Down and End raise it, and Up, Page Up and Home lower it.

Right is more and Left is less either way. Ctrl+Up and Ctrl+Down, the volume
keys that work from anywhere, are not changed. Each key press tells your
screen reader the new value straight away, so you never hear the value from
before the key.

This is one choice for every Quill app. Change it here and Quill Radio,
Quill Media Player and QUILL Studio follow at your next key press.

### When an episode ends

When an episode finishes, Cast marks it played and plays the next episode in
your Play Queue. Chapter 5, Keeping up without keeping watch, tells you all
about the queue. While you listen, three keys work with it:

- **Ctrl+Alt+Down** plays the next episode in the queue now.
- **Ctrl+Alt+Up** goes back to the previous one.
- **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Down** marks this episode played and moves on to the next.

Two switches in Preferences > Playing decide what happens at the end: **Play
the next episode in the Play Queue**, which is on, and **When the queue is
empty, keep going with the same podcast**, which is off until you turn it on.
With both off, Cast stops at the end of each episode.

If you notice a pause between one episode and the next, turn on **Start
loading the next episode before this one ends**, in the same section. Cast
fetches the start of the next queued episode a little early, so the next one
begins straight away. It starts off, because it uses some data in advance,
which matters on a capped connection.

About ten seconds before an episode ends, Cast tells you what comes next:
"Next: Episode 412 from Accidental Tech Podcast." It names the podcast only
when it changes, it says nothing when nothing will follow, and it stays quiet
during Quiet Hours. If you would rather not hear it, turn off **Say what is up
next before an episode ends** in Preferences, in the **Telling you** section.

**Stop After This Episode** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+A**) is for tonight only. Cast
stops at the end of this episode instead of moving on, and then the setting
turns itself off. Press the key again before then to cancel it.

### Falling asleep to a podcast

The sleep timer stops playback for you after a while, so you can drift off
with a podcast and wake up with your place still saved.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+T** (Episode > Sleep Timer).
2. Choose how long: a number of minutes from the list, **End of this
   episode**, or **Custom...** to type your own number of minutes.
3. Press **Start** (Enter).

A minute before it stops, Cast warns you, and then the sound fades gently
away.

- **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+T** sets the timer straight to the end of this episode. It
  follows the episode, so skipping ahead moves it too.
- **Ctrl+Alt+X** (Episode > Extend Sleep Timer 5 Minutes) gives you five more
  minutes, even once the sound has started to fade.
- To turn the timer off, open the Sleep Timer again and press **Cancel Sleep
  Timer**.

If you always fall asleep to the same podcast after about the same time, let
Cast remember it. **Sleep timer starts at** in Settings for This Podcast
(Ctrl+Alt+,) sets the number of minutes the Sleep Timer offers first while
that podcast plays. It never starts a timer by itself. The same row in
Preferences > Playing sets it for every podcast.

The status bar's Sleep timer cell tells you how long is left at any time.

> **QUILLBert and QUILLBee:**
>
> **QUILLBert:** The sound started fading, and I wasn't sleepy yet.
>
> **QUILLBee:** Ctrl+Alt+X. Five more minutes, and the volume comes
> straight back up.

### Now Playing

**Now Playing** is a window with every control for the episode that is
playing, in one Tab order. It is a lovely place to sit for an evening without
your library in the way.

1. Press **Ctrl+2** to open it. Episode > Now Playing (Ctrl+Alt+2) opens it
   too.
2. Tab through it. You meet, in order:
   - Lines naming the podcast, the episode and where it is playing from, such
     as the Inbox or the Play Queue.
   - A **Position** slider. Left Arrow and Right Arrow move 5 seconds, Page Up
     and Page Down move 30, Home and End go to the start and the end, and Cast
     says where you are.
   - **Pause**, **Stop**, **Back 30 Seconds**, **Forward 30 Seconds**,
     **Previous Chapter** and **Next Chapter**.
   - **Speed**, a list of speeds plus **Custom...** for a speed you type.
   - **Volume**, a slider, and **Mute**. Up and Home make it louder, Home
     being 100 percent, unless you chose the Windows keys in Slider Keys.
   - The sleep timer, with **Set...**, **At End of Episode**, and **Extend by
     5 Minutes**.
   - **Chapters**. The playing one says "playing". Press Enter on any row to go
     there.
   - The show notes, which work as described in the next section.
   - **Your note**, a box for a thought about this episode. Type it and move
     on. Cast saves it when you leave the box and says "Note saved". Empty the
     box to remove the note.
   - **Add to Favorites**, **Mark as Played**, **Share...**, and **About This
     Episode...**.
3. Press **Ctrl+1** to go back to the main window.

**Ctrl+W** or Alt+F4 hides Now Playing, and Ctrl+2 brings it back with your
place intact. If you would like it to come forward every time an episode
starts, turn on **Switch to Now Playing when playback starts** in Preferences >
Playing.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Now Playing**.

### Reading the show notes

**Show notes** are what the podcast wrote about an episode: who is on it, what
they talk about, and links to things they mention. In Cast they read like a
small document, headings and links included.

1. In any list, arrow to an episode.
2. Press **Tab**. You are in the show notes. **Alt+O** goes there too.
3. Read with your usual reading keys.

The same notes reader is in Now Playing and in the **Show Notes** window
(**View Show Notes...** on an episode's menu), and the same keys work in all
three:

- **H** and **Shift+H** move to the next and previous heading.
- **Tab** and **Shift+Tab** move from link to link. Cast says the link's name,
  such as "Link, Jane and Co". Press **Enter** to open it in your browser.
- A **timestamp** such as 12:34 is a link too. Cast says "Timestamp, 12
  minutes 34 seconds". Press Enter and the episode plays from there.
- **Ctrl+F** finds text in the notes, and **F3** finds the next match.

Under the notes are three buttons. Their keys differ a little between the
main window and Now Playing:

| Button | Main window | Now Playing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copy Notes | Alt+C | Alt+N |
| Links | Alt+K | Alt+L |
| View in Browser | Alt+R | Alt+R |

**Copy Notes** copies the notes as plain text, ready to paste into an email,
and Cast tells you how many words it copied. Press the Applications key on
the button for other choices: **Plain text with links**, which keeps each
link's address beside it, **Markdown**, or **Formatted**, which keeps the
headings and bold when you paste into Word or Outlook. Cast remembers what you
chose. You can also pick it in Preferences > Playing, under **Copy show notes
as**.

**Links** opens a list of every link in the notes, each with its name and
where it goes. The button says how many there are.

1. Press the Links button.
2. Arrow to the link you want.
3. Press **Enter** to open it in your browser, or use **Copy Address**, **Copy
   Title and Address** or **Copy All Addresses**.
4. Press Escape to go back to the notes, on the link you were on.

**View in Browser** opens the notes as the podcast wrote them, pictures and
all, in your web browser.

The **Show Notes** window has two more buttons of its own:

- **Send to Editor** (Alt+S) puts the notes on the clipboard as plain text, so
  you can paste them into QUILL, Word or an email. Cast says "Show notes
  copied to clipboard".
- **Save As...** (Alt+A) saves the notes to a file. Choose plain text, HTML or
  Markdown in the file type list. HTML and Markdown keep every link as a
  link.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Show notes, and what else the podcast sent**. It goes on to About This
Episode and transcripts as well.

### About This Episode

Many podcasts send more than a title and an audio file. They might say who is
on the episode, mark the best moments, recommend other podcasts, or offer a
way to support the show. **About This Episode** shows you whatever this one
has.

1. Arrow to an episode, or play one.
2. Press **Ctrl+Shift+A** (Episode > About This Episode), or choose it from
   the episode's menu.
3. Before the window opens, Cast gives you a one-line summary, such as "Extra
   details for this episode: 2 people, 1 marked moment, 1 recommended
   podcast." If that is all you wanted to know, press Escape.
4. Move between the tabs with Ctrl+Tab. There is a tab for each kind of
   detail the episode has, and empty ones are left out:
   - **People**: the hosts and guests. Enter opens a person's website if the
     podcast gave one.
   - **Highlights**: moments the podcast marked as worth hearing. Enter plays
     from one.
   - **Live**: a live stream the podcast is running. Enter tunes in.
   - **Other Audio**: another version of the episode, often a smaller file for
     a slow connection.
   - **Recommended**: podcasts this one recommends. You can follow one from
     here.
   - **Support**: where to support the podcast. It opens in your browser.
   - **Place**: where the episode is about.
5. The button under each list says what it will do for the row you are on,
   such as Open in Browser, Play or Follow This Podcast. Enter or Space on
   the row does the same thing.

The window stays open after you use a button, so you can play a highlight and
then follow a recommended podcast without opening it twice. It is a window of
its own: you can go back to your library while it is open, and asking for
About This Episode on another episode shows that episode in the same window.
Press Escape or Ctrl+W to close it, and you are back on the episode you
started from.

If the podcast sent none of these, the window says so.

### Transcripts

Some podcasts publish a **transcript**, a written copy of everything said in
the episode. When an episode has one, its menu (Shift+F10) offers three
things:

- **Read Transcript...** opens the transcript in a reader that follows along
  as the episode plays. Arrow to any line and press Enter to hear the moment
  it was spoken.
- **Save Transcript As...** saves it to a file.
- **Open Transcript in Editor** opens it in QUILL, if you have it, ready to
  read, quote or copy from.

Transcripts are also how Cast works out chapters: when **How long to spend
looking** is **A while** or longer in Preferences > Chapters, Cast reads the
published transcript to find where the sections start.

### Keeping a moment

When something in an episode is worth coming back to, you have three ways to
keep it: a bookmark, a note in your own words, or a sentence you can send to a
friend.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Keep a moment**.

#### Bookmarks

A guest recommends a book, and you want to remember where. A bookmark marks
that spot in one keystroke.

1. While the episode plays, press **Ctrl+Alt+A** (Episode > Bookmark This Moment).
2. Cast says the bookmark is saved. There is no window and nothing to type,
   and the episode keeps playing.

To find your bookmarks again:

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+J** (Help > Bookmarks).
2. Arrow through the list. Each row says what it is in and where.
3. Press **Go There** (Enter) to play that episode from that moment.

The Bookmarks window also has **Edit Note...** to add a few words to a
bookmark, **Share** to copy it for someone else, **Delete**, and **Export...**
to save the whole list as a document. If you use Quill Radio, its bookmarks
are in the same list, and Radio sees yours.

**A bookmark with a note.** Sometimes you want to say why, right then. Press
**Ctrl+Shift+D** (Episode > Bookmark with a Note) instead. Type a few words,
such as "the book the guest recommends", and press Enter. The note is
optional; pressing Enter on an empty box still makes the bookmark.

**Just this episode's bookmarks.** Press **Ctrl+Shift+J** (Episode > Bookmarks
in This Episode) to see only the bookmarks in the episode that is playing, or
the one you are on. The same list is one button away in Now Playing
(**Bookmarks**, Alt+O), and About This Episode has a **Bookmarks** tab whose
**Go There** plays the episode from that moment.

#### Your notes on an episode

When you want to say something about a moment, such as "the bit about the
recipe", use a note. A note keeps your words and the time together.

1. While the episode plays, press **Ctrl+Alt+N** (Episode > Add Episode Note).
2. Type your note and press OK. Cast saves it at the point you were listening.

To read your notes:

- **Episode Notes...** on any episode's menu shows that episode's notes,
  whether it is playing or not.
- **My Notes in This Episode**, in the Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P), lists
  the notes for what is playing.

Select a note and press **Jump To Note** (Alt+J), or Enter, and Cast takes
you to that moment, starting the episode if it has to. **Copy Note** (Alt+C)
copies the episode, the podcast, the time and your words together, ready to
send to someone. **Delete Note** (Alt+D) removes the note you are on, and
Cast says "Note deleted". The Your note box in Now Playing is a note too, so
it shows up here as well.

#### Sharing a moment

You hear something wonderful and want a friend to hear it too.

1. While it plays, press Shift+F10 on the episode and choose **Share This
   Moment**. In Now Playing, press **Share...**.
2. Cast copies a sentence such as "Blind Abilities, Episode 214, at 41
   minutes 12 seconds", along with a link.
3. Paste it into a message or an email.

The sentence works for anyone, even read out over the phone. A friend who has
Cast and follows the same podcast can open the link and hear that exact
second.

### Picking up where you left off

You never have to remember where you stopped. Every episode keeps your place,
and Cast saves it as you listen, so even a power cut costs you a sentence at
most. When you finish an episode, Cast marks it played, and it starts from the
beginning if you ever play it again.

**Continue Listening** gathers everything you started and did not finish.

1. Press **Ctrl+Shift+L**.
2. Arrow through the list, newest first. Each row tells you how far in you
   are, such as "Rome, The Rest Is History, podcast, 20 minutes in, 33%
   through."
3. Press **Enter** to carry on from that point.
4. Press **Delete** on an episode you are not going back to. Cast forgets your
   place and takes it off the list, and the episode stays unheard.

If your place in an episode came from another device, the row says so at the
end: "last played on Kitchen laptop", or "last played on Earshot" when it came
from the Earshot app on your phone. Once you listen to it here, that goes away,
because the place is yours again.

If you also use Quill Radio, an unfinished audiobook chapter or recording
from Radio shows up in the same list.

Two more ways back:

- The **Recently Played** submenu on the Episode menu lists your last fifteen
  episodes. Choose one to play it.
- **Resume Last Episode on Launch** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L** on the Podcasts
  menu) starts your last episode as soon as Cast opens. I have it on. I open
  Cast with my coffee and the episode is already talking.

### Clearing out a podcast

You have fallen behind on a podcast and want a fresh start, without forty
unheard episodes staring at you.

1. Select the podcast, or play one of its episodes.
2. Press **Alt+Shift+E** (Episode > Mark All as Played). In the Command
   Palette it is called **Mark All Episodes as Played**.
3. Cast asks first, naming the podcast and how many episodes. Press **Mark Played**.

Every episode is marked played and leaves the Inbox. They stay in your
library, and your downloads stay too. If you do this often, check **Don't ask
me again** and Cast stops asking.

For one episode at a time, press Shift+F10 on it and choose **Mark as
Played**. On an episode you have already heard, the same row reads **Mark as
Unheard**, which puts it back among the new ones, handy when you want to hear
it again or marked it by mistake.

### Where QUILL Cast's own sounds play

QUILL Cast makes short sounds of its own when downloads start and finish, when new episodes arrive, and when an episode ends. You choose where they play, and you can turn them off.

- **Alt+Shift+M** turns QUILL Cast's sounds off. Press it again and they come back, on the same sound card as before. You hear a short sound as they go off, and another where they now play when they come back on, and both are spoken too. **Episode > App Sounds** has a check mark that shows whether they are on.
- **Preferences (Ctrl+,) > App Sounds Play On...** lets you pick where they play:
  - **Same as its audio**. This is where it starts. The sounds go wherever the episode plays, and follow it when you move it to another sound card. The choice names the device it means right now.
  - **System default**. The sounds go where Windows sends sound.
  - **None (no sounds)**. QUILL Cast makes no sounds at all. Everything it tells you is still spoken.
  - **Any sound card or headset in the list.** The sounds get a device of their own.

After you choose, one sound plays on the new device, so you know straight away where it went.

If the device you chose is unplugged or asleep, you still hear every sound, on the system default, and QUILL Cast tells you once. When the device comes back, the sounds go back to it by themselves.

This changes QUILL Cast only. Your other QUILL apps keep their own choice, unless you have checked "Share these choices with my other Quill apps", which copies a change here to the others that share. Your screen reader is never moved.

## Chapter 4: Finding podcasts you will love

This chapter is about filling your library. Someone mentions a podcast on the
radio, a friend will not stop talking about one, or you have years of
favourites sitting in another app. Here you will learn every way to bring a
podcast into Cast: by name, by its address, from a members-only feed, from a
list exported by another app, and from audio files already on your computer.
Then we will sort it all into folders that make sense to you.

A word you will meet often: a podcast's **feed** is the address where the
publisher lists its episodes. Cast reads the feed to learn what is new. Most
of the time you never see it, because finding a podcast by name finds the
feed for you.

Most of this starts on the **Podcasts** menu (Alt+P). Its first two rows are
the ones you will use most. **Refresh All Now** (Ctrl+F5) asks every podcast you
follow whether it has anything new, and **Add Podcast...** (Ctrl+N) opens the
window for finding new ones. The **Add Podcast** button in the main window
(Alt+A) opens it too.

During Refresh All Now, Cast waits until the requested feeds have finished
before rebuilding the library tree and refreshing Podcast Manager, and it
runs maintenance once at the end. This avoids repeating whole-library work
for every feed in the run.

A few rows mentioned here show only in Advanced mode, and this chapter says
which. The Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P) reaches them in either mode.

### Adding a podcast by name

You know what it is called. That is enough. Try it now with a podcast you
already like.

1. Press **Ctrl+N**. Focus is in **Podcast name**.
2. Type part of the name and press Enter, or press **Find Podcasts**
   (Alt+S).
3. Cast tells you what it found, for example "12 results: 9 from iTunes, 3
   from Podcast Index."
4. Press **Alt+R** to go to the **Results** list and arrow through it.
5. Found it? Press **Follow** (Alt+O). Not sure? Press Enter on it for a
   closer look first, which the next section explains.

Cast says "Now following", the podcast's name and how many episodes it has.
The podcast is in your library straight away, and its newest episodes are
ready to play.

Cast looks in two directories at once, iTunes and Podcast Index, which
between them know nearly every podcast there is. Podcast Index is also where
many independent podcasts live, so asking both finds things a single store
misses. If you ever want only one, choose it in the **Directory** list
(Alt+D). Most people leave it on Both. To change where Add Podcast always
starts looking, set **Find podcasts in these directories** in Preferences >
Data.

A podcast you already follow says "Following" in the results, and on that row
the Follow button reads **Unfollow** instead. To look for something else,
clear the name box and type again.

When you follow a podcast, Cast brings in its newest episodes. If you would
like some of its older ones too, open Preferences > Fetching and set **When I
follow a podcast, also fetch** to the newest few, the last few months, or everything
the feed still offers. **How many, or how many months** says how far back.
This happens once, at the moment you follow. If you add a feed you already
follow, **If I add a podcast I already follow**, in the same section, decides
whether Cast asks you, skips it quietly, or adds a second copy.

#### Looking before you follow

Plenty of podcasts share a name. Three shows called "The Daily", a dozen
called "Book Club". A quick look saves you following the wrong one.

1. In the Results list, press Enter on a result, or press **Preview...**
   (Alt+P).
2. Read **About this podcast** (Alt+A): its own description, as text you can
   arrow through.
3. Press **Alt+R** for **Recent episodes**, the ten newest with their dates.

Between them, these tell you whether it is the one you meant, whether it is
still running, and whether it is in your language. Press **Follow** if it is,
or Escape to go back to your results and keep looking.

Every result has a menu of its own, too. Press **Shift+F10** or the
Applications key on it for **Follow** (or **Stop Following**), **Preview...**,
**Copy Feed Address**, **Copy Website Address** and **Open Website in Your
Browser**. The two website items are dimmed when the podcast did not give a
website. Cast tells you when an address has been copied.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**The first hour**. Its first few steps find and follow a podcast with you.

### Dictating into a search box

Typing a podcast's name is not the only way. **Edit > Dictate Here**
(**Ctrl+F11**) lets you say it instead. Cast does not listen to the microphone
itself, and has no speech engine of its own. When you press Ctrl+F11, Cast
asks QUILL Transcribe, the QuillVille app that types what you say into any
program, to start dictating into the box you are in. QUILL Transcribe does
the listening and types the words; Cast only asks.

#### Step by step: say a podcast's name

1. Press **Ctrl+N**. The Add Podcast window opens, and focus is in **Podcast
   name**.
2. Press **Ctrl+F11**. If QUILL Transcribe is running, you hear it start,
   with its own "Dictation on" and start sound. Cast says nothing, so you
   hear it once.
3. Say the name, for example "the daily", and pause. The words are typed into
   the box, and they read back as text like anything you typed.
4. Stop dictating: press QUILL Transcribe's start and stop key
   (Alt+Shift+Backslash unless you changed it), or say "stop dictation".
5. Press Enter to search, and arrow through the results as usual.

Ctrl+F11 works in any text box in Cast: in the main window, and in the boxes
inside dialogs such as Add Podcast and Search Everywhere. Put the cursor in
the box first. Pressing the key does not move the focus, and QUILL Transcribe
types wherever the focus is.

#### What happens when you press Ctrl+F11

- **QUILL Transcribe is running.** Cast passes the request on, and QUILL
  Transcribe starts dictating within a second. If it is already dictating,
  nothing changes: it keeps going, and types into your box because that is
  where you are.
- **QUILL Transcribe is installed but not running.** Cast says "Starting
  QUILL Transcribe. Dictation starts here when it is ready." QUILL Transcribe
  starts in the system tray, so the box keeps the focus, and starts dictating
  once it is ready. The very first time, QUILL Transcribe opens its own window
  instead, to set itself up. Finish that, come back to Cast, and press
  Ctrl+F11 again.
- **Quill Inkwell is dictating.** Nothing starts, and Cast says "Quill
  Inkwell is dictating. Stop it there, then try again." Only one program uses
  the microphone at a time.
- **QUILL Transcribe is not installed.** Cast says "Dictate Here needs QUILL
  Transcribe, which is not installed with this copy." Nothing else happens.
- **QUILL Transcribe is running but does not answer.** Cast says "QUILL
  Transcribe is running, and could not be reached." Quit it with Exit QUILL
  Transcribe on its tray menu, then press Ctrl+F11 again to start it fresh.

#### Which speech engine listens

Because it is QUILL Transcribe dictating, the engine is the one you chose in
QUILL Transcribe, and everything you set up there applies here too: My Words
and Phrases, the command word, the dictation commands, the words it must
never type, and the field lock. There is nothing to set up in Cast.

To see or change the engine, open QUILL Transcribe and choose Model > Choose
Model... (Ctrl+M in QUILL Transcribe). In brief:

- **Moonshine**, the one it starts with, is built in. It needs nothing
  downloaded, is the fastest, and puts in punctuation for you. English only.
- **Whisper** is built in too. It is a little slower, and is worth trying if
  Moonshine often mishears you. It also writes Spanish.
- **Larger models**, such as NVIDIA Parakeet or Nemotron and bigger Whisper
  models, make fewer mistakes for more work. They are free downloads, from
  Model > Model Library... (Ctrl+L in QUILL Transcribe), and Choose Model
  shows which one is **Recommended for this computer**.

QUILL Transcribe's own guide explains each engine, the tiers and the
downloads in full.

#### Your privacy

Cast never records, and never sends your voice anywhere. With the built-in
engines and the downloaded models, QUILL Transcribe keeps your speech on your
computer as well. Your voice leaves the computer only if you chose OpenAI or
a speech provider with your own account as the engine in QUILL Transcribe.
QUILL Transcribe asks before the first time it sends anything, and No is the
answer it starts on.

#### Limits

- Ctrl+F11 in Cast starts dictation but does not stop it. Stop with QUILL
  Transcribe's start and stop key, or say "stop dictation".
- Dictate Here is for typing into a box. Cast's own commands are still keys
  and menus: you cannot say "play" to play an episode.
- QUILL Transcribe is released on 1 November 2026. From then on every copy of
  Quill Cast and Quill Radio shows Dictate Here on its Edit menu, whether or
  not QUILL Transcribe is installed yet.

QUILL Transcribe is a separate, free app in the QuillVille family, beside
QUILL, Quill Radio, Quill Weather, Quill Converter and Quill Inkwell. It types
what you say into any program, not only into Cast. Install it once from its
download page (from 1 November 2026), open it, and let it set itself up; from
then on Ctrl+F11 in Cast starts it for you when it is not running.

### Following ACB Media's podcasts

If you would like a full shelf to explore on your first day, Podcasts >
**Follow ACB Media Podcasts** (Ctrl+Alt+B) follows every podcast in ACB

### Adding a podcast by its address

Sometimes a website, a newsletter or a friend gives you the feed address
itself. It usually ends in ".rss" or ".xml", or the page calls it the "RSS
feed".

1. In Add Podcast, press **Alt+F** to go to **Feed address**.
2. Paste the address with Ctrl+V.
3. Press Enter or **Add** (Alt+A).

There is a shortcut. Copy the address first, wherever you found it, then
press **Ctrl+N** in Cast. Add Podcast opens with the address already filled in
and the cursor on it, and Cast tells you so. Press Enter to follow.

Cast says "Fetching" while it reads the feed, then tells you it is
following.

#### Private feeds with a username and password

Patreon supporter feeds, members-only podcasts and company feeds usually need
a sign-in. Cast handles them like any other podcast once it knows your
details.

1. Paste the feed address into Add Podcast and press **Add**.
2. A **Feed Credentials** window opens with focus in **Username** (Alt+U).
   Type the username and password (Alt+P) your provider gave you. Patreon
   shows them on the same page as the feed address.
3. Press OK, and the podcast is added as usual.

If the password is wrong, the window comes back with your username still
filled in, and says so. Once a podcast has a sign-in, Cast uses it for
everything that podcast needs: checking for episodes, downloading, playing,
chapters and transcripts.

To change or remove a sign-in later, press Shift+F10 on the podcast and
choose **Feed Credentials...**. Type a new password to replace the old one,
or leave the password blank to keep the one already saved. **Clear
Credentials** (Alt+L) removes the sign-in altogether.

Your password is kept safely by Windows, never in a plain file, and never in
a list you export, so an exported list is always safe to share. On a portable
copy, the password stays with the computer it was typed on. On a new
computer, Cast tells you the sign-in failed, and you type it once more in
Feed Credentials.

### Using your own Podcast Index key

Cast already uses Podcast Index for you, with nothing to set up. If you have
your own free key from podcastindex.org and would rather use it, choose
Podcasts >
**Podcast Index Credentials...** (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+I, Advanced).

1. Type your key in **Key** (Alt+K) and your secret in **Secret** (Alt+E).
2. Press **Read It Back** (Alt+R) if you want to hear what you typed.
3. Press **Save**.

Your key is used from then on, and it is kept by Windows like a feed
password. **Forget These** (Alt+F) removes it, and Cast goes back to its own.

### Bringing your podcasts from another app

Every podcast app can export the podcasts you follow as an **OPML** file.
Think of it as a moving box: one file holding your whole list, folders and
all. Cast can read one, however long, even a list of a thousand podcasts
built up over years.

1. In your old app, export your podcasts as OPML and save the file somewhere
   you can find it.
2. In Cast, choose Podcasts > **Import OPML...** (Ctrl+Alt+I, Advanced), or
   press **Import OPML...** (Alt+I) in the Add Podcast window.
3. Pick the file.
4. Decide on the two checkboxes.
   - **Check that each feed is still reachable after importing** (Alt+C)
     finds the podcasts that have stopped publishing, so you are not left
     wondering why one never has anything new.
   - **Add every show as streaming** (Alt+S) is checked to begin with, so a
     long list does not start a download for every podcast in it. Leave it
     checked unless you know you want downloads.
5. Press **Import**.

Your folders come across as folders, exactly as the file has them: folders
inside folders as deep as they go, in the same order, and empty folders too.
Podcasts that sit outside any folder stay at the top level. Folder names with
an ampersand, accents or quotation marks come across as written, and so do
names that another app saved with its punctuation spelled out twice.

A podcast listed twice is imported once, even if one copy has a slightly
different address. If the file puts one podcast in two folders, Cast follows
it once, in the first folder it appears in, and the report says where the
other copy was. Two different podcasts that happen to share a name are both
imported, whether the other one is already in your list or elsewhere in the
same file, and the report points them out so you can check. A line in the file
whose address is not a complete web address, like "http://feed/", is not
imported; the report lists it with the reason.

The first time Cast reads each new podcast, it takes that as a starting
point. You will not be told about hundreds of "new" episodes from shows you
have just brought across, and nothing is downloaded or added to a playlist
because of it. The only exception is what you asked for under "When I follow
a podcast, also fetch" in the podcast's settings.

Reading the OPML file and preparing the import plan also run in the
background, so a long list does not freeze Cast. Subscriptions are added
before the optional reachability check starts. If you asked for that check,
Cast tells you how far along it is as it goes. **Stop Checking** (Alt+T) ends
the check early and keeps the subscriptions already imported. A private feed
that asks you to sign in counts as working.
An address that now opens a web page, a feed with nothing in it, and a host
that turns podcast apps away do not.

When it is done, the **OPML Import Report** window lists anything worth your
attention. It names feeds that did not answer, each with the reason in
plain words, and duplicates it skipped. It lists anything it could not
import, and feeds whose host says they have moved for good. Cast moves a
podcast to its new address only when **Follow permanent feed redirects** is
on (Preferences > Fetching). Otherwise the report says the address was left
alone. Two buttons sit under it:

- **Export Report...** (Alt+E) saves the whole report as a text file.
- **Save Pruned OPML...** (Alt+P) writes your original file back out without
  the feeds that no longer answer, which is a nice way to tidy the list you
  keep elsewhere.

You can ask about dead feeds again any time with Podcasts > **Feed Check...**
(Ctrl+Shift+C). Chapter 5, Keeping up without keeping watch, shows you how to
read it.

To go the other way, choose Podcasts > **Export OPML...** (Ctrl+Alt+E,
Advanced) and save the file. Any other podcast app can read it. Your folders
go into the file as folders, empty ones included, so importing it again, in
Cast or anywhere else, gives you the same arrangement. Inside each folder the
folders are written first and then the podcasts. A file that mixed the two in
another order comes back with the same folders and podcasts in the same
places, just grouped that way.

If you checked the box for it when you installed Cast, pressing Enter on an
OPML file in File Explorer opens Cast straight into the import, and Cast
tells you which file it is opening. A list saved with an ".xml" ending
imports fine through Import OPML too.

### Podcasts that other podcasts recommend

Some of the best finds come from podcasts you already enjoy. Many publishers
list podcasts they recommend, and Cast shows them in **About This Episode**
on the Episode menu, under its Recommended tab. Following one there is a real
follow, with the podcast's proper name and its episodes. Chapter 3, Sit back
and listen, walks through About This Episode.

### Your own recordings and audiobooks

Cast can play audio you already have, such as audiobooks, lectures, sermons
or your own recordings. It treats each collection like a podcast, so you get
the same keys, the same bookmarks and the same "pick up where I left off".
They live in the **Personal Audio** place (Ctrl+Shift+U).

1. Choose Podcasts > **Add Personal Audio...** (Ctrl+Alt+L).
2. Pick one or more audio files. MP3, M4B, M4A, WAV, FLAC and OGG all work.
3. Type a name for the collection and press Enter.

Each file becomes an episode. Cast keeps its own copy, so moving or deleting
the originals does no harm.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Your own audio**. It covers watched folders too.

#### Folders Cast watches for you

This is one of my favourite parts of Cast. Maybe your voice recorder copies
its recordings to a folder on your computer. Maybe a friend drops lectures
into a shared Dropbox folder, or your audiobooks download into the same place
every time. Tell Cast about that folder once, and from then on anything that
lands there turns up in Personal Audio on its own. Cast tells you when it
arrives, it never brings the same file in twice, and your original stays
exactly where it was. You never have to go and fetch it.

To watch a folder:

1. Choose Podcasts > **Watched Folders...** (Ctrl+Alt+W). The Watched Folders
   window opens.
2. Press **Add Folder** (Alt+A) and choose the folder, the same way you would
   choose one anywhere else in Windows.
3. The **Watched Folder Settings** window opens for that folder. The choices
   are explained just below, and the ones it starts with are good ones, so
   you can simply press **Save**.
4. Cast says it is watching the folder and has a first look straight away.
   Anything already there arrives now.

Try it with a folder you already have recordings in. Within a few seconds you
will hear something like "New in Voice Memos: Tuesday meeting, 42 minutes".
Press Ctrl+Shift+U to go to Personal Audio, and there it is, ready to play
with every key you already know.

##### What the folder settings mean

The settings page has one row for each choice:

- **Name** (Alt+N) is what Personal Audio calls the files from this folder.
  It starts as the folder's own name.
- **What to do with the original** (Alt+O). *Leave it where it is* is the
  safe choice: Cast keeps its own copy and never touches yours. *Move it into
  Cast's folder* tidies your folder as files arrive. *Play it from where it
  is* makes no copy at all, which saves space. If that folder is on a drive
  you unplug, the episode says it is unavailable and keeps your place until
  the drive is back.
- **New arrivals** (Alt+A). A new file can simply join Personal Audio, or
  also go to the end of your Play Queue, or also start playing when nothing
  else is.
- **Tell me** (Alt+T). Cast can say each new file by name and length, say
  only how many arrived, or stay quiet. Every arrival goes into
  Notifications whichever you pick.
- **Ignore files shorter than** (Alt+I). Recorders sometimes make a file by
  accident when a button is bumped. Anything shorter than this, in seconds,
  is left out. It starts at 30.
- **Speed** (Alt+P). Lectures and audiobooks often want different speeds, so
  each folder can have its own.
- **Include subfolders** (Alt+S) also watches every folder inside this one.
- **Each subfolder is its own group** (Alt+G). With this on, a folder of
  audiobooks becomes one group per book in Personal Audio.
- **File types to bring in** (Alt+Y). Every kind of audio Cast plays starts
  checked. Uncheck one to leave those files alone, such as raw .wav
  recordings you are still editing.

If you add folders often, you can choose what a new one starts with. In
Preferences (Ctrl+,), the **Data** section has four rows for it: **A new
watched folder's original files**, **When files arrive in a new watched
folder, tell me**, **A new watched folder ignores files shorter than**, and
**A new watched folder includes its subfolders**. Folders you already watch
keep their own settings.

And if you like to decide about everything in one place, turn on **New files
in watched folders also wait in the Inbox**, in the **The Inbox** section of
Preferences. Your new recordings then wait in the Inbox beside new episodes,
as well as being in Personal Audio.

##### Living with your watched folders

The Watched Folders window lists each folder as a sentence: its name, whether
Cast is watching it, how many files it has brought in, when something last
arrived, and where it is. Press Enter on one to go to its files in Personal
Audio. The buttons do the rest, and the Applications key offers them again,
along with **Go to Its Files**, Open Folder in File Explorer and Copy Path.
On that menu, Pause or Resume says exactly which it will do: **Pause
Watching** on a folder Cast is watching, and **Resume Watching** on one you
paused.

- **Folder Settings** (Alt+S) opens that folder's settings page again.
- **Scan Now** (Alt+N) looks at the folder straight away.
- **Pause or Resume** (Alt+U) stops watching for a while, and starts again.
- **Remove** (Alt+M), or the Delete key, stops watching the folder. Cast asks
  first, and the answer starts on No. The files it already brought in stay
  in Personal Audio, and nothing in the folder is touched.

A few things happen without you having to think about them:

- A file that is still being written, such as a long recording copying
  across, is left alone until it has finished. Then it arrives once.
- The same recording renamed, or dropped into two watched folders, is still
  one episode.
- Deleting a file from the folder does not delete the episode. The folder is
  a way in, not a mirror.
- Cast also looks at every folder when it opens and when your computer wakes
  up, so nothing that arrived while it was closed is missed.
- A folder on a network drive is looked at every ten minutes, because
  Windows cannot always tell Cast about changes there. Scan Now is there for
  when you cannot wait. To look at every watched folder at once, open the
  Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P) and choose **Scan Watched Folders for New
  Episodes**.
- If a folder goes missing, for example because a drive is unplugged, Cast
  tells you once and keeps watching. Help > Recent Problems keeps a note of
  it.

### Organising podcasts into folders

Once you follow more than a handful, folders help: News, Comedy, Tech, Long
Drives, whatever suits you. They live in the **Podcasts** place
(Ctrl+Shift+S), a tree of your folders with your podcasts inside them.

To make a folder and fill it:

1. Choose Podcasts > **New Folder...** (Ctrl+Shift+E), type a name and press
   Enter.
2. Go to a podcast, press Shift+F10, and choose **Move to Folder...**.
3. Pick the folder and press Enter.

After moving, focus stays in the podcast tree on the next podcast in the
source folder, or the previous one if you moved the last podcast. If no
podcasts remain there, the containing folder is the fallback. Unchanged
rows stay in place rather than the whole library being cleared and rebuilt.

In the tree:

- **Enter** on a podcast plays its next unheard episode.
- **Right Arrow** opens a podcast to show its episodes.
- **F2** on a folder renames it.
- **Delete** on a folder removes the folder and moves its podcasts out of
  it. Your podcasts stay.
- **Alt+Up** and **Alt+Down** move a podcast or folder in your own order.

A folder is a good place to listen from, too. A folder always includes any
folders inside it. Press Shift+F10 on one for:

- **Play All Unheard**, which queues the newest unheard episode from each
  podcast in it and starts playing. One from each keeps the queue a sensible
  length.
- **Add All to Queue**, for every unheard episode in it.
- **Move Up** and **Move Down**, which move the folder among its neighbours.
  Cast tells you where it is now, such as "News, 2 of 5".
- **Export This Folder as OPML...**, to share one folder and what is inside
  it with another computer or another person.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Arrange a library that has grown**.

#### Choosing the order of your podcasts

Podcasts > **Sort Podcasts** offers **Ascending (A to Z)**, **Descending (Z to
A)**, or **Custom Order**, and the menu always shows which one is in use.
Custom Order is the one you build yourself. The first time you press Alt+Up
or Alt+Down in the Podcasts place, Cast switches to Custom Order for you,
starting from the order you were looking at, so nothing jumps around.

#### Choosing how the library looks

A long library is easier to move around when it is shaped the way you think
about it. Press **Ctrl+Shift+R** (View > Rearrange Library and Inbox). Each
choice in that window takes effect as soon as you make it, so you can try one,
press Escape, listen to the library, and come back. Close keeps everything.

- **Show the library as** decides the shape of the Podcasts place:
  - **Folders first, then podcasts**, the way it starts. Your folders come
    first, then any podcasts that are not in a folder.
  - **Folders only**. Just your folders, closed, so even a library of a
    hundred podcasts is a short list. Podcasts not in a folder get a folder of
    their own at the end, called "Podcasts in no folder". Right Arrow opens a
    folder.
  - **Podcasts only, without folders**. One list of every podcast, with no
    folders in the way.
  - **Folders and podcasts together, by name**. Folders and podcasts mixed in
    one alphabetical list.
- **Sort podcasts by**: title A to Z or Z to A, most unheard first, recently
  updated first, or your own order.
- **Sort folders by**: your own order (the one Move Up and Move Down make),
  name A to Z or Z to A, most unheard first, or most podcasts first.
- **Folders start** open, showing their podcasts, or closed until you open
  one.
- **Counts say** how many episodes are unheard, how many podcasts a folder
  holds, both, or nothing at all, if you would rather hear just the names.
- **Leave out folders with nothing in them** keeps empty folders out of the
  way. They come back the moment you file a podcast in one.
- **Show the Inbox as** is explained in "Folders first in the Inbox" below.

None of these hides a podcast. Whatever you choose, every podcast is still
there, one Right Arrow away at most. The same choices are in Preferences
(Ctrl+,), in the section called **The library**, with the Inbox one in **The
Inbox**. There, Folders start is called **Folders in the library start**, and
Counts say is **Counts in the library say**.

The library section has one more choice, **Show lists that mix podcasts
as**. It
shapes the lists that mix podcasts, such as New Episodes: one flat list,
grouped by podcast, or a folder for each podcast. It only groups the rows; it
never leaves any out.

### Keeping your favourites close

Some podcasts you reach for every day. Select one and press **Add to
Favorites** (Alt+F), or choose it on the podcast's menu, and it appears in
the **Favorites** place (Alt+Shift+V). Enter on a favourite opens its
episodes right there, and Backspace brings you back. Delete takes a podcast
out of Favorites, and you still follow it.

### Settings for one podcast

Every podcast can have its own way of doing things: a slower speed for a
fast talker, a louder volume for a quiet one, its own skips. Select a podcast
and press **Ctrl+Alt+,** (Settings for This Podcast). Anything you change
there has a "Use shared default for..." button beside it to put it back. The
shared defaults are in Preferences (Ctrl+,). Chapter 6, Making Cast yours,
walks through both.

### Unfollowing a podcast

Had enough of one? Select it and press **Unfollow** (Alt+U), or press Delete
on it in the Podcasts place. Cast asks first, and if you downloaded episodes,
asks separately whether to delete those too. Changed your mind? Press
**Ctrl+Z** straight away and it comes back with its episodes and your place
in them.

### Spotify podcasts (experimental)

Cast can play podcasts hosted on Spotify. This is early, experimental work.
Its two rows are on the Help menu from the start, in Simple and Advanced
mode alike, and no unlock code is needed. Before they can play anything,
you need three things:

- a Spotify Premium account, since Spotify only streams to Premium
  members;
- your own Spotify Client ID, from an app you register in the Spotify
  Developer Dashboard with its redirect address set to exactly
  `http://127.0.0.1:43217/callback`;
- the Microsoft Edge WebView2 runtime, which current Windows already has.

With those three in place, use the two Help menu rows in this order.

1. **Connect to Spotify...** (Ctrl+Alt+S). Type your Client ID and start the
   sign-in. Your web browser opens on Spotify's own page. Approve it, and
   Cast takes over from there. Your sign-in is kept by Windows.
2. **Browse Spotify Podcasts...** (Ctrl+Alt+V). Type a podcast or episode
   name, arrow to a result, and press Enter to play.

All the usual playing keys work on a Spotify episode. Spotify episodes play
as streams and have no Download, and many Spotify podcasts exist only on
Spotify.
Spotify is switched off in Safe Mode, and the first sign-in asks your
permission before Cast reaches Spotify.

## Chapter 5: Keeping up without keeping watch

Following a podcast is the easy part. The fun starts the next morning, when
there is something new waiting. This chapter is about that everyday rhythm:
how Cast finds new episodes, where it puts them, and how you decide what to
hear and in what order. You will meet the Inbox, the Play Queue, playlists,
downloads, schedules and Episode Filters, each explained before you use it.

The chapter starts with the ideas, then the morning routine that uses them,
then each part in turn. Read the first two sections and you can stop there
if you like. The rest is for when you want Cast to fit you a little better.

### How new episodes reach you

Here is the whole picture in a few sentences, the way I would explain it over
coffee.

**Checking.** Cast learns about a new episode by reading the podcast's feed,
the list its publisher keeps. You can ask Cast to check at any time with F5.
You can also give a podcast a **schedule**, so it checks itself, every hour,
at six in the morning, or around the time the podcast usually comes out.

**New Episodes.** Everything that arrives shows up in the **New Episodes**
place, newest first, from every podcast you follow. It needs no setting up.
It is a good place to browse.

**The Inbox.** The Inbox is where new episodes wait until you decide about
them. You choose which podcasts send episodes there. Then you go through it,
like a pile of post: play this one, queue that one, file one away for the
weekend, and mark the rest played. An Inbox that you empty each morning means
nothing slips past you.

**The Play Queue.** The Play Queue is your "up next" list. When one episode
ends, the next one in the queue starts. You fill it from anywhere with a
single key, Space.

**Playlists.** A playlist is a list you keep and come back to. You can fill
one by hand, or write a few rules and let it fill itself, such as "everything
under twenty minutes that I have not heard".

**Episode Filters.** Some podcasts publish things you do not want, such as
trailers or a daily segment beside the main show. A filter is a rule for one
podcast that keeps those episodes out of your way. It never deletes them.

**Downloads.** Every episode can play straight from the internet. Downloading
keeps a copy on your computer, for listening offline or on a slow
connection, and Cast can do it for you.

### The everyday rhythm

Here is how most mornings go. Try it now with the podcasts you already
follow.

1. Press **F5** (Podcasts > Refresh All Now) to check every podcast you
   follow. Cast tells you how many it is checking, and the answers come in
   podcast by podcast over the next few seconds. If your podcasts have
   schedules, you can skip this step.
2. Press **Ctrl+Shift+W** for New Episodes, or **Ctrl+Shift+I** for the
   Inbox.
3. Arrow through what arrived. Press Enter to play an episode now, or Space
   to add it to the Play Queue. Cast says "Added to the Play Queue".
4. Press **Ctrl+Shift+Q** whenever you want to see what is lined up in the
   Play Queue. When one episode ends, the next one starts.

That is the whole loop. Everything else in this chapter makes it fit you a
little better.

> **A note from QUILLBee:** An empty Inbox on your first morning doesn't mean
> nothing arrived. Nothing goes to the Inbox until you send a podcast there,
> which "Choosing what goes to the Inbox" explains a little further on. Until
> you do, New Episodes (Ctrl+Shift+W) has everything.

### New Episodes

**New Episodes** (View > New Episodes, Ctrl+Shift+W) shows recent episodes
from all your podcasts, newest first. All the usual episode keys work here:
Enter plays, Space queues, Shift+F10 opens everything else. Delete takes an
episode off this list without touching it anywhere else.

On a morning when you want a clean slate, press the Applications key on the
New Episodes place and choose **Mark All as Played...**. Cast asks first.

### The Inbox

The **Inbox** (View > Inbox, Ctrl+Shift+I) holds the unheard episodes of the
podcasts you send there. It is a place for sorting, so everything you do in it
is about deciding:

- Enter plays an episode now.
- Space adds it to the Play Queue.
- Shift+F10 and **File to Inbox Folder...** puts it aside for later, which the
  Inbox folders section explains.
- Delete takes it out of the Inbox. It stays unheard in its podcast's own
  list.
- **Mark All as Played...**, on the Inbox place's Applications key menu,
  clears the lot when you are starting fresh.

Nothing you do in the Inbox removes an episode from your library. When Cast
opens and something is waiting in the Inbox, that is where it starts you.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**The Inbox**. It finishes by catching a new episode from its notice.

#### Choosing what goes to the Inbox

Nothing goes to the Inbox until you say so, one podcast at a time.

1. Go to a podcast, in the Podcasts place or Favorites.
2. Press Shift+F10 or the Applications key.
3. Choose **Route New Episodes to Inbox**.

From then on its new episodes wait in the Inbox. The same menu row now reads
**Stop Routing to Inbox**, to turn it off.

If you would rather have nearly everything in the Inbox, open Preferences
(Ctrl+,), choose **The Inbox** section, and change **Which podcasts go to
the Inbox** to "Every podcast except the ones I mark". The podcast menu row then
becomes **Keep This Show Out of the Inbox**, and on a podcast you have kept
out, **Put Back in the Inbox**.

#### Inbox folders

Sometimes you want to keep an episode without hearing it today: a long
interview for the weekend, a cooking episode for when you are in the
kitchen. Inbox folders hold those. They belong to the Inbox and are separate
from your library's folders.

1. In the Inbox, go to an episode, or select several.
2. Press Shift+F10 and choose **File to Inbox Folder...**.
3. Pick a folder, or type a new name, and press Enter.

With several episodes selected, Cast asks once for all of them. Once you file
an episode from a podcast, Cast remembers the folder and files that podcast's
later episodes there too, and tells you it has done so.

To look in one folder, use **View > Inbox Folder...** (Ctrl+Shift+O), or
**Inbox Folder...** on the Inbox place's Applications key menu. Deleting an
Inbox folder moves its episodes up a level. It never removes them.

#### Folders first in the Inbox

When a lot arrives at once, a long Inbox is easier to take in by folder. Press
**Ctrl+Shift+R** and choose **Show the Inbox as**:

- **Every episode in one list**, the way it starts.
- **Folders first, then episodes**. The Inbox begins with a row for each of
  your library's folders that has something new, such as "News, folder, 5 in
  the Inbox", and then lists the episodes from podcasts that are not in a
  folder. Press **Enter** on a folder row to see just its episodes, and
  **Backspace** to come back to the folder row.
- **Folders only**. Just the folder rows, with one more called "Podcasts in no
  folder" for everything else.

These are the folders your podcasts live in, the same ones as in the Podcasts
place. Inbox folders, the ones you file single episodes into, are separate and
still work as described above.

#### Keeping the Inbox tidy

An Inbox that keeps everything forever turns into a second library. To stop
that, open Preferences > The Inbox and set two limits:

- **Inbox: keep at most** a number of episodes. Leave it at 0 for no limit.
- **Inbox: drop episodes older than** a few hours up to two weeks.

Any podcast can have its own limits in Settings for This Podcast
(Ctrl+Alt+,), so a daily news show can keep three while a weekly one keeps
everything.

Trimming never deletes anything. A trimmed episode stays unheard in its
podcast's own list, downloaded file and all. Episodes you have started,
queued, or filed into an Inbox folder are never trimmed and do not count
toward the limit.

Now and then a publisher re-issues an episode, with a corrected file or a
new cut. If that episode had been trimmed from your Inbox, Cast brings it
back and tells you: "Episode 42 was re-published by The Daily, so it is back
in your Inbox." Episodes you have played, started, queued or filed stay where
they are. A few podcasts re-issue their whole back catalogue every time they
tidy their feed. For one of those, turn off **Treat re-published episodes as
new** in its Settings for This Podcast, and old episodes stop coming back.

### The Play Queue

The **Play Queue** (View > Play Queue, Ctrl+Shift+Q) is what plays next. Fill
it in the morning and Cast plays one episode after another until it runs out.

Ways to add to it:

- Press **Space** on any episode, in any list.
- Press **Shift+Space** on any episode to play it next, straight after the
  one that is playing. **Play Next** on an episode's menu (Shift+F10) does the
  same.
- On a folder in the Podcasts place, choose **Add All to Queue** for every
  unheard episode in it.
- In the Notifications window, choose **Add to Queue** on a new-episode
  notice.

Several at once? Select them with Shift and the arrows, or Ctrl+Space, then
press Space or use the menu. Chapter 2, Finding your way around, has more on
working with several episodes at once.

While the queue is playing, you can move through it from anywhere. These
are on Episode > Play Queue Run:

- **Next in Queue** (Ctrl+Alt+Down) skips to the next episode.
- **Previous in Queue** (Ctrl+Alt+Up) goes back one.
- **Mark as Played and Next** (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Down) finishes this one for good
  and moves on. I use this one for news I have heard enough of.

In the queue itself, Delete takes an episode out of the queue. It stays in
your library. An episode Cast put there for you, rather than one you added
yourself, says why at the end of its row: "queued by Auto-Queue", "queued by
your listening run" or "queued by a watched folder". So you always know where
an episode came from.

The Applications key on the Play Queue place offers:

- **Clear Entire Queue...**, which asks first.
- **Play Queue Shuffled**, for a change of pace.
- **Save Lineup...** and **Apply Lineup...**, which save the queue's order
  as a QUILL Lineup and put one back at the front. Lineups, below, explains
  them.

When you just want something to listen to, open the Command Palette
(Ctrl+Shift+P) and type "unheard". **Play an Unheard Episode (newest)** plays
the next unstarted episode in your queue if there is one, then looks in the
Inbox, then everywhere else, and picks the newest you have not started.
**Play an Unheard Episode (oldest)** looks in the same order but picks the
oldest, which suits a podcast you are working through from the start.

If your queue is long, **Group the Play Queue by** in Preferences > The
window clusters it by podcast or by folder. The play order does not change,
and each group header says what it is: "News, group, 4 episodes".

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**The Queue**. It covers lineups and expiry as well.

#### Sending a podcast straight to the queue

For a podcast you never skip, there is no need to queue each episode by
hand. Choose **Auto-Queue New Episodes** on the podcast's menu (Shift+F10).
Its new episodes go straight into the Play Queue when they arrive and skip
the Inbox. **Stop Auto-Queueing New Episodes** on the same menu turns it off.
You can also check it in the podcast's Settings for This Podcast.

Starting a series from the beginning? In the same window, set **Auto-Queue
takes the** to **Oldest unheard episode**. Cast then queues the podcast one
episode at a time from the start, instead of the newest first.

#### Lineups

A lineup is the order you like to listen in on a particular day: news first,
then the comedy, then the long interview for the drive home. Arrange it once
and have it back whenever you want.

1. Arrange the Play Queue the way you like it.
2. Press the Applications key on the Play Queue place and choose **Save
   Lineup...**.
3. Type a name, such as "Tuesday", and press Enter. Cast says "Saved lineup
   Tuesday: 5 episodes, in this order."

Next Tuesday, choose **Apply Lineup...** and pick the name. The lineup's
episodes that are still unheard move to the front in your order,
and the rest of your queue waits behind them. Nothing is thrown away. Cast
tells you how many it applied and how many it skipped because you had
already heard them. Saving under a name you already used replaces that lineup.

A lineup you save here is a **QUILL Lineup**, the same kind Chapter 9 is
about, so you can open it with Ctrl+U, rename it, rearrange it, give it rules,
and see it in Quill Radio too. Apply Lineup offers every Lineup that has
episodes, including ones built by rules. Lineups saved before this version
were playlists; Cast copies each one into Lineups once, and leaves the
playlist where it was in Playlists.

#### Letting old episodes expire

A daily news podcast goes stale fast. Yesterday's headlines sitting in your
queue take a turn they do not deserve. To have Cast take old episodes of a
podcast out of the queue:

1. Press Shift+F10 on the podcast and choose **Queue Expiry...**.
2. Pick how long an episode may wait, from a day up to a month, or Never.
3. Press OK. Cast tells you what you chose.

Never is where every podcast starts. The shared choice for all podcasts is
**Expire from the queue** in Preferences > The Inbox.

An expired episode is not deleted. It moves to **Recently Expired** (View >
Recently Expired, Ctrl+Shift+X), where it waits a week with its position and
its downloaded file. This place is hidden from the Places list until you
show it with View > Places... (Ctrl+Shift+G). On an episode there, choose **Restore to the Play
Queue** to bring it back with a fresh start, or **Forget This One** to let
it go. The place's own menu offers **Restore All to the Queue**.

> **QUILLBee's shortcut:** You don't have to show Recently Expired in the
> Places list to look in it. Ctrl+Shift+X (View > Recently Expired) goes
> straight there, even while it's hidden.

### When Cast looks for new episodes

Until you tell it otherwise, Cast checks when you ask. There are three ways
to ask:

- **F5** (Podcasts > Refresh All Now) checks everything you follow.
- **Refresh Feed** on a podcast's menu checks only that podcast. It is a
  Quick Action, so you can put it on a key.
- **Refresh All Now** on the Podcasts place's Applications key menu does the
  same as F5.

When you press F5, a separate, modeless progress window shows how many feeds
have been checked. Feed requests run in the background, so you can switch
back to the editor or keep using Cast. Closing the progress window hides it
but does not stop the run. **Stop remaining checks** cancels feeds that have
not started; a check already in progress finishes.

A check you asked for always answers, even when nothing is new.

To have Cast check without being asked, open Preferences > **When Cast
opens**. **Check when Cast opens** looks once at start-up, and **Check when
the computer wakes** looks once after sleep. **Catch up on a missed check at
the next launch** is on to begin with, so a check that was due while Cast was
closed still happens.

For checking through the day, give your podcasts a schedule, which comes
next. A scheduled check only speaks when something new arrived. Checking
reads the list of episodes. It downloads nothing unless you have asked for
automatic downloads.

If you also run Quill Radio, the two apps share the record of when each feed
was checked, so neither asks the publisher twice.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**When Cast looks for new episodes**. It covers schedules, pausing a podcast,
Feed Check and quiet hours.

#### Giving a podcast a schedule

A schedule decides when Cast looks at a podcast on its own. There is one
shared schedule for all your podcasts, and any podcast can have its own.

To set the shared one:

1. Open Preferences (Ctrl+,) and choose **Fetching**. Make sure **Look for
   new episodes on a schedule** is checked. While it is off, Cast only
   checks when you press F5 or open Cast.
2. Press **Change Schedule...**. The button says what the schedule is now.
3. In **Kind of schedule** (Alt+K), choose one of five:
   - **Manually only**: Cast waits for you.
   - **Every so often**: such as every 45 minutes or once a day. Set the
     number in **Every** (Alt+E) and the **Unit** (Alt+N), or pick from
     **Quick pick** (Alt+Q).
   - **At set times**: such as 6:00 and 18:00. Type up to six times in
     **Times of day** (Alt+T), separated by commas, and check days under **On
     these days** (Alt+D). No days checked means every day.
   - **Around when it usually publishes**: Cast learns when the podcast
     tends to appear and looks closely then. This is the one I use for my
     daily shows. The window tells you what it has learned. **Pin This
     Pattern** (Alt+P) keeps that pattern fixed, and **Reset Learning**
     (Alt+R) starts again.
   - **Follow the publisher's hint**: uses what the feed says about how
     often it changes.
4. Press **Save**.

To give one podcast its own schedule, press Shift+F10 on it and choose
**Change Schedule...**, or use **Check for new episodes** in its Settings for
This Podcast. When you save, Cast reads the schedule back in one sentence:
"The Daily now checks: at 06:00 and 18:00, weekdays."

Scheduled checks wait for the end of Quiet Hours unless you check **Check
during Quiet Hours** in Preferences > Fetching.

#### Pausing a podcast

A podcast between seasons, or one that has finished, does not need checking.
Press Shift+F10 on it and choose **Pause Updates for This Podcast**. Cast
stops checking and downloading it on its own, and everything you already
have stays. F5 and Refresh Feed still check it when you ask. **Resume Updates
for This Podcast** on the same menu wakes it up again.

#### Feed Check

Sometimes a podcast moves house or stops publishing, and its feed stops
answering. When you wonder which of your sixty podcasts has gone quiet, open
**Podcasts > Feed Check...** (Ctrl+Shift+C).

A podcast that has moved house properly usually leaves a forwarding address.
Turn on **Follow permanent feed redirects** in Preferences > Fetching, and
Cast updates the podcast's address for you when it finds one. It starts off.
A saved username and password are never sent on to the new address.

1. Focus is in **Your feeds, worst first** (Alt+W). Failing feeds come first,
   then feeds that are empty, then quiet ones, then healthy ones.
2. Read from the top and stop when the rows turn healthy. A failing row says
   why, in plain words, and each row says when Cast will check it next. The
   "last new episode" column is the date of the podcast's newest episode, so
   a show that stopped years ago says so.
3. On a failing row, press **Retry** (Alt+R). To try every failing feed
   again, press **Retry All Failed** (Alt+F).

The reasons you may hear:

- **The host no longer has a feed at this address**, or **has removed it for
  good**. The podcast has probably moved or ended.
- **This podcast's web address no longer exists.** The website's name has
  lapsed.
- **The podcast's site is refusing every connection**, or **the site's
  secure connection is broken**. The site itself is broken.
- **The site's security certificate could not be verified.** Usually the
  site's fault, but not always. See "Feeds over http and https" below.
- **This address returns a web page, not a podcast feed.** The address now
  leads somewhere else entirely.
- **The feed is there, but it has no episodes.** The publisher has emptied
  it. These rows read "Empty".
- **The publisher has locked this feed.** It may now need a paid
  subscription.
- **The feed's host is refusing podcast apps.** This is usually a bot check
  that only the publisher can switch off.
- **This feed needs a sign-in.** Use Feed Credentials on the podcast's menu.
- **The host took too long to answer**, or **is having trouble right now**.
  This usually passes by itself.

For a podcast that has moved, ended up somewhere unexpected or emptied its
feed, press **Find This Show's New Feed...** (Alt+N). Cast looks the show's
title up in the podcast directories you use in Add Podcast. It reads each feed
it finds to make sure it answers, and lists the ones that do, with how many
episodes each has and the date of the newest. That date is the quickest way
to tell the show you know from a different one with the same name. Choose one
and press **Replace Feed** (Alt+R). The podcast keeps everything: what you
have heard, where you stopped, your notes and its settings. Only its address
changes, and episodes the new feed shares with the old one are not listed
twice. Nothing is looked up until you press the button.

Opening Feed Check checks nothing by itself. **Copy Feed Address** (Alt+C)
puts a row's address on the clipboard. On a row, the Applications key also
offers **Check Now**, **Find This Show's New Feed...** and **Change
Schedule...**. A failing feed is not given up on: Cast keeps trying.

Checking is kind to your connection. After the first time, Cast asks each
host whether the feed has changed, and a feed that has not changed costs
almost nothing to check. A plain "http" address is tried over a secure
connection first. Feed checks also run on their own, so a big check never
holds up a download or anything else you are doing. If a feed is so large
that Cast cannot read all of it, Player Information says so; the newest
episodes are always there.

#### Feeds over http and https

Most podcast addresses start with "https". That means the feed travels to
you encrypted, and Cast can check it really came from the podcast's site.
Some long-running shows, often small and independent ones, still use a plain
"http" address. Cast plays those too.

- **https comes first.** When an address starts with "http", Cast asks for it
  over https anyway, and only uses plain http when the secure way does not
  work. Many sites quietly support both, so you get the secure one without
  doing anything.
- **You can tell which one a podcast uses.** Player Information says **Feed:
  not encrypted (plain http)** for a podcast that really is on plain http. It
  says nothing about the rest, so the line only turns up when it matters.
- **Your password is never sent over plain http.** If a podcast needs a sign-in
  and the secure connection says no, Cast stops there and tells you. It does
  not try again in a way that would send your username and password where
  anyone on the network could read them.
- **Certificates are checked the way Windows checks them.** Cast trusts the
  same certificates Windows does, so a site that opens in your web browser
  should open here too. The same check covers everything else Cast reaches:
  podcast searches, episode downloads and Check for Updates. Wherever a
  certificate cannot be verified, Cast says it in the same words.

When a feed will not load:

1. Open **Feed Check** and read the reason on the podcast's row.
2. If it says the security certificate could not be verified, check that
   this computer's date and time are right, then run Windows Update. An out
   of date clock is the most common cause.
3. At work, or with security software that inspects secure connections, ask
   whoever looks after the network. That software has to be trusted by
   Windows for Cast to trust it.
4. If the site really is broken, nothing on your side will fix it. **Find
   This Show's New Feed...** may find the show somewhere else.

### Being told about new episodes

Cast keeps a list of what it has told you, so the episode that arrived while
you were in your email is still there when you come back. There are three
ways to look at it.

- **The Notifications place** (View > Notifications, Ctrl+Shift+N). Newest
  first. Enter goes to what a notice is about, and Delete removes the
  notice. The place's Applications key menu offers **Mark All as Read**,
  **Clear List** and **Open in a Window...**.
- **The Notifications window** (Help > Notifications,
  Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F3). On a new-episode notice, the row's menu offers **Play
  Now**, **Add to Queue**, **Go to the Podcast** and **Mark Read**.
- **The status bar's Notifications cell**, which counts what is unread.
  Press F6 to reach the status bar, and Enter on the cell opens the window.

In either list, a new-episode notice answers two keys of its own, so you
never need to chase a pop-up before it disappears. **Ctrl+Enter** plays the
new episode straight away, and **Space** adds it to the Play Queue, just as
Space does on any episode.

How loudly Cast tells you is up to you. In Preferences > **Telling you**,
**When new episodes arrive** offers three choices. **Quietly**, where Cast
starts, adds the episodes to Notifications and says nothing. **Notify me**
also shows a Windows desktop notification with a short sound. **Not at all**
keeps no record. Either way the episodes still arrive. The same section has a
switch for each kind of notice, if you want to hear about some things and
not others. Each one starts with **Tell me about:**, followed by a new
episode, a feed that keeps failing, a podcast that has gone quiet, a download
you started finished, the sleep timer's last minute, or a personal audio
import finished. Turning one off only stops the notice. The episodes still
arrive, download and queue as usual.

If a busy morning brings a flood of announcements, **At most this many
spoken announcements an hour** sets a ceiling. Anything over it is folded
into one summary instead of being said twenty times. Two more rows, which
any podcast can set for itself, tell you when something may be wrong:
**Tell me if this podcast goes quiet for** a number of weeks, and **Tell me
after this many failed checks**. Leave either at 0 and it never says
anything.

A podcast can even have a sound of its own. In Settings for This Podcast,
under Announcements, choose a sound in **Play this sound when it publishes**,
and that podcast's new episodes play it instead of the usual sound. The
sounds come from your sound pack.

For a podcast you really care about, choose **Announce New Episodes** on its
menu. When a scheduled check finds something new from it, Cast says the
episode names out loud and in braille. **Stop Announcing New Episodes** turns
it off.

#### Quiet hours

You do not want Cast talking to you at two in the morning about a new
episode. **Help > Quiet Hours...** (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Z) sets a time when Cast
stops speaking on its own. It starts at 22:00 to 07:00, and the time can
cross midnight.

During quiet hours, downloads still run and nothing is lost. Announcements
wait, and the notices are there in Notifications in the morning. Anything
you press a key for still answers, and failures always speak. Quiet hours
are shared with the other Quill listening apps, so you set them once.

### Downloading episodes

You can play any episode without downloading it. Cast streams it. Downloading
keeps a copy on your computer, for listening offline, on a slow connection,
or without a pause at the start.

On an episode's menu (Shift+F10):

- **Download Episode** downloads that one.
- **Pause Download** and **Resume Download** appear while it is downloading.
- **Remove Downloaded Copy** deletes the file and keeps the episode. You can
  download it again later.

On a podcast's menu, **Download All Episodes** downloads everything it has
not got yet, and **Remove All Downloads...** deletes its files and keeps its
episodes and your place in them.

Started streaming something you now want to keep? Press **Ctrl+Alt+K**
(Episode > Keep This Episode) and Cast keeps it as a download, often without
fetching it again.

Now and then a podcast moves its audio, or a download comes out cut short or
silent. You do not need to unfollow and follow again. Press Shift+F10 on the
episode and choose **Refresh Episode Audio...**. Cast reads the podcast's feed
again, tells you whether the address changed, asks once (because it will
download the episode again), and then fetches a fresh copy. Your place, your
played mark and your notes stay exactly as they were. It is also in the
Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P).

To watch what is downloading, go to **Downloads** (View > Downloads,
Ctrl+D). Delete there removes the file, and you can still stream the episode.
The **Downloads** menu (Alt+D) has **Pause All Downloads**
(Ctrl+Alt+Shift+U) and **Resume All Downloads** (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+V) for
anywhere in the app.

If your connection drops halfway, you hear "Download connection dropped;
reconnecting", and Cast picks up where it stopped. **Reconnect and keep
downloading automatically** in Preferences > Fetching turns this on or off,
and **Reconnect attempts** sets how many times it tries.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Downloads, and the disk they live on**.

#### Downloading on its own

If you like having the newest episodes ready on your disk, let Cast fetch
them. In Preferences > **Fetching**:

- **Automatically download** keeps the newest episodes of each podcast
  ready: none, the newest 1, 3, 5 or 10, or every episode. Any podcast can
  have its own choice in Settings for This Podcast.
- **Also download anything you add to the Play Queue** fetches whatever you
  queue.
- **Also download everything routed to the Inbox** does the same for Inbox
  episodes. It starts off, since the Inbox is for deciding.
- **Download automatically on a metered connection** decides whether this
  happens on a phone hotspot or a capped connection.
- **Only download automatically after (hour)** and **...and before (hour)**
  keep automatic downloads to the hours you choose, such as overnight. Leave
  both the same and there is no limit. A download you ask for always starts
  straight away.
- **Always sync the full catalog** downloads every episode a podcast has ever
  published, not just the new ones. It suits an archive you want complete.
  Turn it on for that one podcast in Settings for This Podcast, not for
  everything.

Cast tells you how many downloads it started. To hear when they have all
finished, check **Notify me when downloads finish** in Preferences > Telling
you. You get one notification for the whole batch.

#### Keeping disk use in check

Downloads add up. Preferences > **Data** has two clean-up rules, both off
until you set them:

- **Delete downloads after** a number of days.
- **Total download storage cap**, in megabytes. When you go over, played
  downloads are removed, oldest first.

One more choice, in the same section: **Delete its downloaded file** when you
finish an episode. With that on, an episode you take out of the Inbox with
Delete is finished with too, so its download goes as well, and Cast says so.
An episode you marked Keep This Episode is never deleted this way.

Neither ever removes an episode that is in your queue or that you are partway
through. To protect one podcast completely, check **Never delete this
podcast's downloads** in its Settings for This Podcast.

A few more rows in Preferences > Data, for when you want them:

- **Default retention** chooses between keeping every download and **Keep
  the most recent**, which keeps only the number of newest downloads you set
  for each podcast. Older episodes stay in the list, ready to stream.
- **This podcast may use** gives one podcast a share of the disk, in
  megabytes. When it is full, Cast stops downloading that podcast on its own.
  It never deletes what you already have.
- **Download location** is the folder downloads are saved in. Leave it blank
  for the usual one. Files already saved stay where they are.
- **When I stop following a podcast, delete its downloaded files** sets the
  answer Cast starts on when you unfollow a podcast: Ask me, Always or Never.
- **Prefer the audio that is** picks which version to download when a
  podcast offers more than one: whatever it lists first, the smallest file,
  or the best quality.
- **Auto-trim silence from downloaded episodes** and **Normalize loudness of
  downloaded episodes** tidy each new download as it arrives: the first
  trims silence at the start and end, the second evens out how loud episodes
  are. Both change the saved file, so they apply to new downloads only.

Streaming has a small store of its own. **Keep streamed episodes ready while
they play** saves the audio of what you are streaming, so a dropped connection
does not stop the episode and Cast can find chapters in it. It is on to start
with, and the audio is cleared away by itself. **Space for streamed episodes**
sets how much room that may take.

Two Advanced rows on the Downloads menu act on those rules straight away;
turn on View > Advanced Features (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G) to see them and use
their keys. **Free Up Space** (Ctrl+Alt+F) applies those rules now and
tells you how much space came back. **Run Housekeeping Now** (Ctrl+Alt+H)
does the whole tidy-up at once: expired queue episodes, Recently Expired,
Inbox limits and storage, and tells you what it did in one sentence. It
also runs after every check. In Simple mode, Free Up Space is still on the
Downloads place's Applications key menu, and both are always in the
Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P).

### Playlists

A playlist is a saved list of episodes you can come back to. There are two
kinds. One you build by hand holds exactly what you put in it, like a
mixtape. A **smart playlist** holds whatever matches its rules right now and
keeps itself up to date, like a standing order: "anything from my comedy
podcasts under half an hour that I have not heard".

Go to **Playlists** (View > Playlists, Alt+Shift+Y). Enter opens a playlist
in place, and Backspace comes back. The place's Applications key menu
offers:

- **New Playlist...**, for one you fill by hand.
- **New Smart Playlist...**, to write rules.
- **Add Starter Playlists**, five ready-made smart playlists to try:
  Continue Listening, New This Week, Quick Listens, Downloaded and Unplayed,
  and Long Reads. Change or delete them as you like.

To put an episode in a hand-made playlist, choose **Add to Playlist...** on
its menu, and pick the playlist. Select several episodes first to add them
all at once.

On a playlist's own row, the menu offers **Open**, **Edit Rules...** for a
smart one, **Rename Playlist...** and **Delete Playlist...** (Delete).

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Playlists and smart playlists**.

#### Writing a smart playlist

If you are new to this, press Add Starter Playlists first and open one with
Edit Rules to see how it is made. Then try your own.

1. In Playlists, press the Applications key and choose **New Smart
   Playlist...**. Give it a name.
2. Under **Shows**, check the podcasts to include. None checked means every
   podcast.
3. Choose an **Episode status** (Alt+S), such as unheard.
4. Fill in any of the rest, leaving 0 for "any":
   - **Published within days** (Alt+D), for recent episodes only.
   - **Minimum minutes** (Alt+M) and **Maximum minutes** (Alt+X), for
     length.
   - **Title or notes contain** (Alt+T), for a word or a name.
   - **Most episodes** (Alt+0), to keep the list short.
5. Choose a **Sort** (Alt+R).
6. Press **Save** (or Ctrl+S). The playlist is made, and the window stays
   open so you can keep adjusting it; each later Save updates the same
   playlist. Press Escape when you are done.

The window tells you how many episodes match as you change things. Every
choice has its own F1 help.

### Episode Filters: what they are for

Some podcasts publish more than one thing in the same feed: a daily
two-minute segment beside the weekly show, trailers for other podcasts, a
members-only strand mixed into the public feed, reruns. If you only want the
main show, the rest clutters your Inbox and your queue.

An **Episode Filter** is a set of rules for one podcast that decides what
happens to those episodes. It never deletes anything. A filtered episode keeps
its played mark, its position, its download, its notes and its bookmarks. A
filter only changes where an episode shows up.

A filter works in one of two ways, which you choose in **When a rule
matches**:

- **Keep everything except episodes a rule matches.** This is the usual one:
  "everything but the trailers".
- **Keep only episodes a rule matches.** The sharp one: "only the Daily
  Briefing, nothing else".

You open Episode Filters from a podcast's menu (**Episode Filters...**), or
with **Episode Filters...** (Alt+L) in its Settings for This Podcast. But the
easiest way in is from an episode you do not want, which comes next.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Stop the parts of a podcast you did not want**.

#### Filter Episodes Like This

You usually know the episode you do not want before you know what it has in
common with the others. So let Cast work that out.

1. Go to an episode you would rather not see, such as a trailer.
2. Press Shift+F10 and choose **Filter Episodes Like This...**.
3. Episode Filters opens with a drafted rule already in it. The first line
   tells you why Cast chose it and how many recent episodes it catches.
4. Press **Preview** (Alt+V) and read **Preview results** (Alt+L) to see what
   would happen to each recent episode.
5. If it looks right, press **Save** (or Ctrl+S). The window stays open. If
   not, press **Edit Rule...** (Alt+E) and adjust it, or press Close to leave
   everything as it was.

Cast drafts the rule by looking, in this order, for:

- a publisher's mark: if the episode is marked as a trailer or a bonus, the
  rule catches every episode marked that way;
- a series name: if its title starts or ends like other recent titles, such
  as "Daily Briefing:", the rule catches titles that start or end the same
  way;
- length: if it is much shorter than this podcast usually is, the rule
  catches episodes about that short;
- and otherwise this exact title, which you can widen yourself.

Cast never offers a rule that would catch every recent episode. Nothing is
saved until you press Save.

#### Writing a filter rule yourself

Once you have tried Filter Episodes Like This, writing your own is not much
harder. Here is a rule that hides trailers, step by step.

1. Press Shift+F10 on the podcast and choose **Episode Filters...**.
2. Check **Filter new episodes of this podcast** (Alt+F).
3. In **When a rule matches** (Alt+W), leave "Keep everything except
   episodes a rule matches".
4. Press **Add Rule...** (Alt+A). The **Episode Filter Rule** window opens.
5. In **Name for this rule** (Alt+N), type something you will recognise,
   such as "Trailers".
6. In **Title match** (Alt+T), choose how to read the title:
   - **No title rule**, to match on length or other tests only;
   - **Wildcard**, where a star stands for any text and a question mark for
     any one character, so `Trailer*` catches every title starting with
     "Trailer";
   - **Regular expression**, if you know them.
7. Type the pattern in **Pattern** (Alt+P).
8. If you like, set **Minimum length in minutes** (Alt+M). An episode
   shorter than this matches. Leave it at 0 for any length.
9. Press **Try It on Recent Episodes** (Alt+Y) to see what the rule catches.
   "Trying a rule before you keep it", below, tells you more.
10. Press **Save** to keep the rule and go back to Episode Filters.

**Capital letters have to match too** (Alt+C) makes the words match only
with the same capitals. **This rule is switched on** (Alt+O) lets you keep a
rule but rest it for a while.

Back in Episode Filters, the **Rules** list (Alt+R) shows each rule in a
sentence, such as "Short things, enabled. Any one of: Episode type is a
trailer. Length in minutes is at most 5." Beside it, **Edit Rule...**
(Alt+E), **Switch Rule On or Off** (Alt+O) and **Delete Rule** (Alt+D) do
what they say. A podcast can have as many rules as you like, and an episode
needs to match only one of them.

##### Adding more tests to a rule

The title and the length are often enough. When they are not, a rule can
look at more. In the rule window, go to **More tests** (Alt+S) and press
**Add Test...** (Alt+A). A small window called **Episode Filter Test** asks
three things.

1. **Look at** (Alt+L): the title, the show notes, the people on the episode
   as the publisher lists them, the episode type (full, trailer or bonus), its
   length in minutes, its age in days, its season number or its episode
   number.
2. **Test** (Alt+T), which changes with what you chose to look at. For words
   it offers contains, does not contain, starts with, ends with, is, is not,
   fits the wildcard, and matches or does not match a regular expression. For
   a number it offers at least, at most, is and is not. For the type it
   offers the whole test in one choice, such as "is a trailer".
3. **Value** (Alt+V): the words, the pattern, or the number.

Press **Save**. **Edit Test...** (Alt+E) and **Remove Test** (Alt+R) change or
remove one later. If Cast cannot read what you typed, such as a number with
letters in it, it tells you why and puts you back in Value.

**Match when** (Alt+W) in the rule window decides how its tests combine:
**Every test has to match**, or **Any one test is enough**. With "any one",
"a trailer, or anything under five minutes" is one rule with one name.

A few things to know. Words are found anywhere in the text, and capitals do
not matter unless you check the capitals box. When a feed leaves out a fact,
such as an episode's length, number tests never match that episode. An
episode with no type counts as a full episode.

Some rules worth writing:

- Sponsor-only episodes: Show notes contains "sponsored by".
- Reruns: two tests, Title contains "rerun" and Title contains "best of",
  with Match when set to Any one test is enough.
- One strand of a busy feed, with "Keep only episodes a rule matches": Title
  starts with "Daily Briefing:".
- Old episodes the publisher re-uploaded: Age in days is at least 365.
- A guest you would rather skip: People on the episode is their name, as
  the publisher writes it.
- Everything from old seasons: Season number is at most 3.

#### Trying a rule before you keep it

You do not have to guess whether a rule is right.

In the rule window, **Try It on Recent Episodes** (Alt+Y) runs the rule as
it stands against the podcast's 50 newest episodes. Cast says how many it
catches and reads the first few titles: "Matches 7 of the 50 newest
episodes. Daily Briefing: Monday; Daily Briefing: Tuesday; and 2 more." The
same sentence stays in **Try it result** (Alt+U) so you can read it again.

In Episode Filters, **Preview** (Alt+V) does the same for all the rules
together and lists every recent episode in **Preview results** (Alt+L), with
what would happen to it first and then its title and length. Preview works
even while the filter is switched off, so you can check a "keep only" filter
before it takes effect.

Nothing changes while you try, so adjust and try again as often as you like.

#### Where a filter applies, and saving it

Under **Where this applies** (Alt+H), check what the filter does to matching
episodes. There are eight checkboxes. The first four are checked on a new
filter, and they keep an episode from reaching you:

- Keep them out of the Inbox.
- Never add them to the Play Queue automatically.
- Never download them automatically.
- Don't announce them as new episodes.

The other four hide an episode, and start unchecked:

- Hide them from this podcast's episode list.
- Hide them from New Episodes and Continue Listening.
- Keep them out of smart playlists.
- Leave them out of Search Everywhere, so Find skips them.

When you press **Save**, the filter works straight away, on episodes you
already have as well as new ones. The Play Queue is the one list you built by
hand, so Cast asks about it separately: **Save and clear them from the
queue**, or **Save, leave the queue alone**. The episode playing now keeps its
place either way. Unchecking a box later brings those episodes straight back.

If a "keep only" filter turns away every new episode of a check, Cast tells
you, and leaves a note in Episode Filters for when you next open it, in case
the publisher changed their titles.

> **QUILLBee's tip:** Give a new filter a week or two before you check any of
> the four boxes that hide episodes. While the episodes it catches are still
> in the podcast's own list, one it caught by mistake is a Shift+F10 away from
> Always Keep This Episode (Ignore the Filter). Once a rule has proved itself,
> hide away.

#### Getting a filtered episode back

Two ways, always:

- On one episode, press Shift+F10 and choose **Always Keep This Episode
  (Ignore the Filter)**. That episode is treated as if no filter existed, and
  editing the rules later does not change that. **Apply the Episode Filter to
  This Episode** undoes it.
- Uncheck a box under Where this applies, or switch a rule off, and its
  episodes come back at once.

If you checked **Hide them from this podcast's episode list**, a hidden
episode is not there to press Shift+F10 on. Uncheck that box for a moment,
keep the episode, and check it again.

### Saving an episode's audio

Sometimes you want the file itself: to send a clip to a friend, to keep an
interview, or to play it somewhere else. These are on an episode's menu, and
all of them are Quick Actions, so you can put one on Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9.

- **Save Episode Audio As...** saves a copy wherever you choose. Cast
  suggests a name made from the podcast and the episode. If the episode is
  not downloaded yet, Cast says "Preparing audio file for export", fetches
  it, and opens the Save window when it arrives.
- **Show in File Explorer** opens the folder with the file selected.
- **Copy File Path** puts the file's location on the clipboard, ready for an
  upload box or a message. Cast reads back the file name and folder.
- **Copy Podcast Link** copies the feed address, to give to another podcast
  app. **Copy Episode Link** copies the episode's own link.

Show in File Explorer and Copy File Path need a downloaded episode, and say
so if there is no file yet.

## Chapter 6: Making Cast yours

Cast plays podcasts well the day you install it, so nothing in this chapter is
required. After a week or two, though, you start to notice small things. One
host talks too fast. A list row says more than you want to hear. Enter plays
an episode when you would rather it downloaded one. There is a whole menu you
never open. This chapter is about those moments.

We will go through the places you can change Cast, from the broadest to the
most particular: Preferences for the whole app, Settings for This Podcast for
one podcast, what each list row says, what Enter does, which features you see
at all, and which keys do what. At the end are Quillins and AI help, for when
you want Cast to do a little more.

One tip before we start. Every window in this chapter answers **F1**. If a
setting is not described here, put focus on it and press F1 to hear what it
does. You never have to guess.

### Preferences: how Cast behaves everywhere

Preferences holds the settings for Cast as a whole. It also holds the shared
defaults, the values every podcast follows until you give that podcast its
own. Change the skip distance here and every podcast skips that far.

1. Press **Ctrl+,** (or choose Podcasts > Preferences). The **QUILL Cast
   Preferences** window opens.
2. Focus starts in the **Section** list. Arrow to the section you want.
3. Tab into that section's settings and change what you like.
4. Press **Save**, or Escape to leave without changing anything.

You can move between sections before you save, and Cast keeps what you
changed in each one. When you press Save, it tells you what changed. If you
get lost in a long section, Alt+S takes you back to the Section list.
Saving refreshes the current library place only when **Hide caught-up
podcasts** changes which rows are visible; other preference changes and
closing without changes do not trigger that reload.
Cast applies your choices before writing the podcast library, application
history, and any changed shared-family choices in the background. An
applied-changes message is not a saved-to-disk confirmation. If a write fails,
Cast tells you which save failed instead of reporting that everything saved.

Two sections have a shortcut by name. In the Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P),
**Fetching Preferences** opens Preferences on Fetching, and **Playing
Preferences** opens it on Playing.

Here is what each of the nine sections is for, so you know where to look:

- **When Cast opens.** Where Cast puts you when it starts, whether it picks up
  the last episode straight away, whether it checks your podcasts on opening,
  whether it starts when you sign in to Windows, and whether it looks for a
  new version of Cast. Where Cast's own sounds play, and which way the arrow
  keys move a slider (Slider Keys), are here too.
- **Playing.** Skip distances, skipping intros, what happens when an episode
  ends, the Winamp keys, and how show notes are copied.
- **Fetching.** How often Cast looks for new episodes, how many it downloads
  for you, and what happens if a download's connection drops.
- **The Inbox.** Which podcasts send new episodes to the Inbox, how many wait
  there, and how long episodes wait in the Play Queue.
- **The library.** How the Podcasts place is laid out, and the order of your
  podcasts, folders and episodes.
- **Chapters.** Whether Cast looks for chapters in episodes that do not come
  with any.
- **Telling you.** What Cast says out loud, which notifications you get, and
  how each list row is spoken.
- **The window.** What closing the window does, AI help, sharing your
  choices with other Quill apps, how the Play Queue is grouped, and how many
  episodes a podcast's list shows.
- **Data.** Where your podcasts and downloads are kept, when old downloads are
  tidied away, and how long your listening history is kept.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called

#### The settings worth a look first

You could spend an afternoon in Preferences. You do not need to. These are
the ones people change most, and the reasons they do.

- **Where to land on launch** (When Cast opens). To start with, Cast opens on
  what is new. If you always reach for the same place first, pick it here.
  Mine opens on the Play Queue.
- **Resume the last episode on launch** (When Cast opens). Turn this on and
  Cast starts playing where you stopped the moment it opens. Leave it off and

- **Start QUILL Cast with Windows** (When Cast opens). Start Cast when you

A few more you may come to like: **Desktop toasts** for new episodes and

### Settings for one podcast

Not every podcast wants the same treatment. A daily news show wants checking
every hour. A fast talker sounds better a little slower. An archive you love
should never have its downloads tidied away. Settings for This Podcast lets
one podcast be different from the rest.

1. Select the podcast in any list.
2. Press **Ctrl+Alt+,** (or press Shift+F10 and choose Settings for This
   Podcast).
3. Focus starts in the **Category** list. There are five: Arrival, Playback,
   Storage, Announcements and Curation. Arrow to one and Tab into it.
4. Change what you want and press **Save** (or Ctrl+S). The window stays
   open, so you can move on to another category. Press Escape when you are
   done.

keeps following Preferences, so if you change a shared default next month,
this podcast picks it up too.

When the podcast has its own value for something, a button sits beside it,
such as **Use shared default for Playback speed**. Press it and that one
setting goes back to following Preferences.

> first, and give a podcast its own value only where it really is different.
> Whatever a podcast leaves alone keeps following Preferences, so one change
> there next month still reaches every podcast that hasn't gone its own way.

What the five categories hold:

- **Arrival.** How often this podcast is checked, how much of its back

- **Playback.** Speed, sound, skip distances, intro and outro skips, and
  chapters.
- **Storage.** How many downloads to keep, how old is too old, and how much

- **Announcements.** How loudly new episodes are announced, how the name is

#### Podcast settings you will be glad you found

Most per-podcast settings are there for the day you need them. These few
solve problems people meet in their first month.

**Tidy episode titles** (Announcements). Plenty of podcasts start every title
with the same words: "Ep. 412 -", "[Bonus]", or the podcast's own name. You
hear that at the start of every row, and typing the first letter of an
episode stops working because they all start the same way. Add a pattern
here and Cast leaves those words out when it shows and speaks the title.
Press **Preview** to hear which titles would change before you keep it. The
podcast's real titles are untouched.

**Say this podcast's name as** (Announcements). For the podcast name your
speech engine always mangles. Type a spelling that sounds right, and Cast
uses it whenever the name is spoken. Written, the name stays as it is.

**What I care about in this podcast** (Curation). A sentence or two in your
own words, such as "the interviews, not the news round-up". Nothing uses it
yet. It is there so that AI help, when you turn it on, can suggest episodes
the way you would choose them. It stays on this computer.

**New episodes of this podcast are** (Announcements). Urgent tells you about
each new episode straight away, by name. Normal counts it in a summary.
Quiet says nothing, though the episodes still arrive, download and queue as
usual. Beside it, **May speak during quiet hours** lets one podcast through
when everything else is holding its tongue.

**Tell me if this podcast goes quiet** (Announcements). Podcasts end without
saying goodbye. Set a number of weeks, and if nothing new arrives in that
time, Cast tells you once. It never unfollows anything.

**Never delete this podcast's downloads** (Storage). Keeps one podcast's
downloads safe from every automatic cleanup, while the others carry on being
tidied as usual.

**Show at most this many episodes** (Curation). A podcast with thousands of
episodes makes for a long list. This shows only the newest ones you ask for.
Nothing is deleted, Find still finds everything, and raising the number
brings the rest back.

**Sort episodes by: Season and episode number** (Curation). For serial
fiction, audiobooks in a feed, and anything else meant to be heard in order.

**Labels** (Curation). Your own words for a podcast, as many as you like. A
podcast lives in one folder, but it can have any number of labels, and a
smart playlist can gather episodes by label.

**Also add new episodes to the playlist** (Curation). Pick one of your
playlists, and this podcast's new episodes join it as they arrive. They are
not queued or downloaded, and they still go to the Inbox as usual.

**Use this artwork instead** (Curation). A picture file of your own for the
podcast, shown in the tray and the window. It changes nothing your screen
reader says.

Most of the other rows in Settings for This Podcast have the same names as
the rows in Preferences, so the list in "Every row, section by section"
above is a good map of them too.

None of these deletes an episode or marks anything played.

Help > Tutorials has a lesson with the same name, **Podcast settings you will
be glad you found**, if you would like to try each one with a guide.

#### Changing a podcast setting in one keystroke

Three podcast settings get changed far more than the rest, so each has its
own row on a podcast's context menu (Shift+F10):

- **Episodes to Keep...** for how many downloaded episodes to hold on to.
- **Queue Expiry...** for how long this podcast's episodes wait in the Play
  Queue.
- **Playback Speed...** for this podcast's own speed.

Each opens a small window with the cursor already in its one control. Cast
tells you in words what you chose: "Keeping the 5 newest downloaded
episodes." They are Quick Actions too, which the section after next explains,
so if you change speed all the time you can put Playback Speed on Ctrl+1.

### Keeping your choices the same in every Quill app

If you use Quill Radio as well, you may want a choice you make here to hold
there too. In Preferences, in **The window** section, turn on **Share these
choices with my other Quill apps**.

Two choices are shared: **Announce dialog transitions**, which decides whether
a window's name is said as it opens and closes, and **When a one-key action
works**, which decides whether an action answers with a sound, words or both.
Every
Quill app with this turned on keeps them the same. When Cast picks up a choice
you made in another app, it tells you which one.

Your keys, where focus goes and how your screen reader works are never shared,
and an app that has not turned this on is left alone.

### Choosing what a row says

Your screen reader reads each list row in full, from start to finish. You
decide what goes into that sentence, and in what order. There are two places
to do it: Choose Columns for the order and contents of each list, and the
Telling you section of Preferences for how an episode row is spoken.

Choose Columns:

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+C** (Podcasts > Choose Columns, an Advanced row).
2. In **Columns for** (Alt+F), pick the list you want to change.
3. The **Shown** list holds the columns that are read, in order. Select one
   and use **Move Up** (Alt+U) or **Move Down** (Alt+D) to change where it
   comes. **Hide** (Alt+I) takes it out of the row entirely, and **Show**
   (Alt+W) puts a hidden one back.
4. **A row will read** (Alt+A) spells out what one row will sound like with
   your changes, so you can hear it before you press OK.

Two hidden columns are worth trying: **Time Left** on episodes you have
started, and **Podcast** in lists that mix podcasts. **Reset This List**
puts one list back the way it came.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Decide what a row says, and what Enter does**. It goes on to Quick Actions
and Customize Features.

#### How an episode row is spoken

Open Preferences (Ctrl+,), choose **Telling you**, and you will find a group
of settings that shape every episode row:

- **Read each row starting with** the episode title, the podcast's name, or
  the date. Whatever comes first is what you can jump to by typing its first
  letter, so choose the one you tend to look for.
- **Say the podcast's name**, in lists that mix podcasts.
- **Say when it was published**, as "Today", "Yesterday", "3 days ago", or
  the date.
- **Say how long it is**, either the whole length or how much is left. How
  much is left is handy when you are picking something to fit a car ride.
- **Say whether it is downloaded**, downloading, or streaming.
- **Say when it has chapters or a transcript**.
- **Say the season and episode number**, where the publisher gives one.
- **Read the episode's description**: off, a sentence of it, or all of it.
  Off is kinder to your ears in a long list.

One podcast can have its own choices, under Announcements in Settings for
This Podcast. That is handy for a podcast that already puts the date in every
title: turn the date off for that one alone.

> **QUILLBee's tip:** If a podcast starts every title with the same words,
> such as "Ep. 412 -", no choice here helps with that one. Tidy episode
> titles, under Announcements in its Settings for This Podcast, leaves those
> words out when the title is shown and spoken, so typing a first letter lands
> on the part that differs.

### Quick Actions: what Enter does

Quick Actions decides three things at once: what **Enter** does on a row,
what **Ctrl+1** to **Ctrl+9** do in an episode list, and the order of the
items on a context menu. To start with, Enter plays. If you always download
before you listen, you can make Enter download instead.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+Q** (Podcasts > Quick Actions, an Advanced row).
2. In **Actions for** (Alt+F), pick episodes, podcasts, or the Play Queue.
3. Tab to the **Order** list. The first action is what Enter does, and the
   first nine are Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9.
4. Select an action and use **Move Up** (Alt+U) or **Move Down** (Alt+D).
   **Make Default** (Alt+T) puts it first in one step.
5. Press OK.

Try it with the action you use most after Play. Put it second, and Ctrl+2
does it from now on. **Reset This List** (Alt+R) puts a list back the way it
came.

### Turning features off

Cast does a lot. If there are parts you will never use, you can switch them
off, and they stop showing up in the menus, the places and Go To. A smaller
Cast is easier to learn.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+C** (View > Customize Features).
2. Every feature is a checkbox, grouped under eight headings: Places,
   Playing, Library, Getting podcasts, Keeping things, Telling you, Listening
   data and Around the app. Everything starts on.
3. Uncheck what you do not want and press **Save**.

The menus and places change straight away. The status bar follows the next
time you open Cast.

Three profiles at the top set all the boxes for you, and you can still change
any box afterwards:

- **Everything** is how Cast comes, and your way back after experimenting.
- **Just Listen** keeps the places, Now Playing, the sleep timer, speed and
  skipping, show notes, Find, folders and Add Podcast. It turns off the
  extras: no automatic checks, no pop-ups, no statistics, no add-ons.
- **Earshot-like** matches the Earshot phone app: places, playback,
  downloads, the Inbox and filters, without the desktop extras.

If you forget where a feature went, the Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P) still
lists its commands and tells you they are switched off in Customize Features.

**Advanced Features** (View > Advanced Features, Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G) is a
different switch. It leaves every feature on and adds the menu rows you
need only now and then, such as Choose Columns, Quick Actions, backups and
the Quillins menu. Chapter 2, Finding your way around, has more on Simple
and Advanced.

#### Every switch in Customize Features

Here is every checkbox, under its heading, so you can see at a glance what
you could turn off. F1 on any box says what goes away with it.

- **Places**: Inbox, Play Queue, Downloads, Personal Audio, Playlists and
  Notifications.
- **Playing**: Now Playing window, QUILL Lineups, Transcripts and chapters,
  Show notes and your notes, Sleep timer, Speed controls, Sound Enhancements, Skipping,
  Winamp playback keys and Global hotkeys.
- **Library**: Folders and custom order, Find, Feed Check, Episode filters
  and smart playlists, Quick Actions and Choose Columns, Hide caught-up
  podcasts, and Watched folders.
- **Getting podcasts**: Directory search and Add by address, Follow ACB
  Media, Backups and OPML, Private feeds and directory credentials, and
  Spotify podcasts.
- **Keeping things**: Automatic downloads, Retention and housekeeping, Keep
  This Episode and Refresh Episode Audio, and Export my data and Delete all
  data.
- **Telling you**: Toasts, Sounds, New-episode digest, Feed failure and
  gone-quiet notices, Automatic feed check, and Dialog transition
  announcements.
- **Listening data**: Statistics, Recently played and Continue Listening, and
  Listening Places sync.
- **Around the app**: Status bar, Tray, Tutorials, Command Palette and Go To,
  Share and export audio, Quillins, and AI features.

### Changing keys

Every key in Cast can be changed. If a key is awkward for your hands, or
clashes with something your screen reader uses, move it.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+W** (Help > Keyboard Shortcuts).
2. Type part of a command's name to filter the list.
3. Select the command and give it a new key. Cast warns you if the key is
   already taken.

Keys are shared with QUILL and Quill Radio, so a change here changes them
there too. Most changes work at once. A few, such as Preferences on Ctrl+,,
wait until you next open Cast.

To see every key Cast answers to right now, including the ones you changed,
press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+K** (Help > Keyboard Shortcuts Sheet). Type
"download" in its filter and you hear only the keys about downloading.
**Copy All** puts the list on the clipboard.

#### Keys that work from any program

Global Hotkeys give a few Cast commands a key that works even when Cast is
not the window you are in. Pause a podcast from your word processor without
switching windows.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+H** (Help > Global Hotkeys).
2. Choose a command and give it a key. Cast offers Play/Pause, Stop,
   **Now Playing window**, which brings the player to the front from
   anywhere, **Time Remaining**, which says how much is left without leaving
   what you are doing, and Show or Hide QUILL Cast. Cast already shows and
   hides on Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F12, so you only need a key for that one if you
   would like a different one as well.

The first time, Cast warns you that a system-wide key can take over the same
key in other programs. If another program already owns a key, Cast leaves it
alone.

The Play/Pause hotkey does not bring Cast to the front. It also changes the
pending play choice while an episode is loading. Stop followed by Play can
load the episode again without restarting Cast.

The Keyboard reference at the end of this guide lists every key Cast comes
with.

The last steps of the **Keys** lesson, in Help > Tutorials, move a key and set
up a key that works from any program, if you would like company while you do
it.

### Quillins

Quillins are QUILL's small add-ons. Cast has its own **Quillins** menu, which
appears in Advanced mode. It comes with one sample Quillin, which signs in to
a private, members-only podcast. In this release only the Quillins that come
with Cast can run, and none run in Safe Mode.

### AI help

Some show notes are long. Cast can ask an AI about the show notes of the
episode you are on: sum them up, explain them, or answer a question such as
"when is the guest's book out?". It is the same AI help QUILL and QUILL Lite
have, on the same keys.

AI help stays off until you turn it on, and nothing is sent until you press a
key that asks. To turn it on:

1. Press **Ctrl+,** to open Preferences, choose **The window**, and check
   **AI help**. Press Save.
2. Open **Help > AI Features** and choose any row. The first time, the
   privacy agreement explains where the notes go. Nothing is sent until you
   accept it.

Now select an episode and press **Ctrl+Alt+Z** to ask about its notes, or
**Ctrl+Alt+G** for the Free AI Assistant, which sums up, explains or shortens
them. Show notes cannot be edited, so press **Copy** to take an answer to the
clipboard.

Everything on the AI Features menu:

| Row | Key | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ask | Ctrl+Shift+8 | A conversation about the episode you are on |
| Which AI Am I Using | Ctrl+F8 | Say which AI is in use; twice opens Choose Your AI |
| Choose Your AI | Ctrl+Shift+F8 | Choose where Cast's AI comes from |
| Free AI Assistant | Ctrl+Alt+G | Sum up, explain or shorten the show notes |
| Ask About These Show Notes | Ctrl+Alt+Z | Ask a question about the notes |
| Ask About an Image | Ctrl+Alt+F5 | Describe the podcast's artwork, a show-note picture or a file |
| AI Usage | Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F2 | How much each AI you set up has been used |
| Connect or Sign Out | Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F4 | Sign in to or out of the free service |
| Privacy Agreement | Alt+Shift+F2 | Read the agreement again, or withdraw it |
| Use My Own AI Key | Alt+F2 | Use your own OpenAI or Google Gemini key |
| Use My ChatGPT Subscription | Alt+F5 | Use the ChatGPT plan you already pay for |

The free service works for show notes. Ask About an Image needs an AI that can
see pictures: your ChatGPT plan, GitHub Copilot or your own Gemini key. With
the free AI in use its row says "(needs a connection that sees)".

#### Choose Your AI

Cast uses the same Choose Your AI window as QUILL and QUILL Lite, and keeps its
own choice, so choosing an AI here changes nothing in the other apps. Press
**Ctrl+Shift+F8**, or **Alt+F5** to land on the ChatGPT row ready to sign in.
The list has five rows: QUILL's free AI, My ChatGPT plan, My GitHub Copilot,
Codex, for tasks, and My own key. Each says its state, such as "in use" or "not
set up". Enter uses a row or signs in to it, and Delete signs a signed-in one
out. If you were already signed in to ChatGPT in Cast, nothing changes: it
stays in use.

**Which AI Am I Using** (**Ctrl+F8**) says which AI is in use, its model and
how much is left where the service says; press it twice quickly to open Choose
Your AI. The last cell of the status bar says the same thing in short, for
example "AI: ChatGPT plan", and Enter on it opens Choose Your AI.

Why not QUILL's Ctrl+4, Ctrl+6 and Ctrl+8? In Cast, Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 move
between your open windows, and in an episode list they run its Quick Actions,
so those keys were taken. Quill Radio uses the same keys as Cast.

#### Ask about the episode you are on

**Ask** (**Ctrl+Shift+8**) is a conversation about the episode you are on, on
whichever AI you chose. The show, the episode, where you are in it, the chapter
you are in, the chapter list and the show notes go with every message, read
again each time. The audio never does. The first line of the window says which
AI you are asking, such as "Asking My ChatGPT plan about this episode."

Type a question and press Enter; the reply is read aloud and added to the
conversation. The **Quick questions** list (Alt+Q) starts with **What did I
miss in this episode?** and **Is this episode worth my time?**, then **Explain
this simply**, **What can I do here?**, which Cast answers itself and sends
nothing for, and **Summarise what is in front of me**. Press Enter on one to put
it in the message box. Every conversation is saved on this computer, and
**Saved Conversations** opens Cast's own.

**What Did I Miss in This Episode?** is also its own row on the AI Features
menu (D). It sends the show notes, the chapters, where you are and, when Cast
has the transcript, the transcript up to that point, and asks only about what
came before. If you have not started the episode, it says so and sends nothing.

#### Ask about a picture

**Ask About an Image** (**Ctrl+Alt+F5**) offers the pictures Cast knows about
for the episode you are on: the podcast's artwork and any pictures in its show
notes, named by their own descriptions when the show wrote them, and last, a
picture file on your computer. Cast fetches the picture you choose then, once,
because you asked. It never downloads artwork in the background. The picture
is made smaller to suit the AI before it goes, and the answer starts by saying
what was looked at and which AI described it. Asking about the same picture
again answers straight away and sends nothing.

With an AI that cannot see in use, nothing is fetched or sent. Cast offers to
switch to one that can, and if the show notes described their pictures, it
reads those descriptions to you instead.

Each Quill app signs in to ChatGPT on its own, so signing in here does not
sign in QUILL or Quill Radio, and signing out of one leaves the others
signed in. If another Quill app is already signed in, your browser remembers
you and it takes one Allow. You stay signed in until you sign out or remove the app in ChatGPT's settings; a brief problem on OpenAI's side no longer signs you out.

The last step of the **Show notes, and what else the podcast sent** lesson, in
Help > Tutorials, turns AI help on with you.

#### Letting AI help with your podcasts

Below the rows above, the AI Features menu has a few more that are about your
podcasts rather than one episode's notes. Each one asks a question only when
you choose it, and each one that would change something shows you its
suggestions first. Nothing changes until you say so.

**Organise My Podcasts** (Help > AI Features, P). Following dozens of podcasts
and never got round to folders? Choose this, and AI help suggests folders for
them, using the folders you already have where they fit. A list opens with
one suggestion per row, such as "Move: Tech Talk -- from no folder to
Technology". Every row starts as Accept. Press **Space** on any row you do not
want and it changes to Skip. **Accept All** (Alt+A) and **Skip All** (Alt+K)
change every row. Press **Apply Selected** and Cast makes the folders and
moves the podcasts you accepted, and tells you how many. Cancel, or Escape,
changes nothing. If AI help names a podcast or folder you do not have, Cast
leaves it out and tells you how many it left out.

**What Is This Podcast About?** (W). On a podcast with a description that says
nothing useful, this gives you two or three plain sentences about what it is
and who it is for. Cast says the answer and shows it in a window you can read
at your own pace and copy from.

**Is This Episode for Me?** (F). First, tell Cast what you care about in a
podcast: in Settings for This Podcast (Ctrl+Alt+,), fill in **What I care about
in this podcast**, for example "the interviews, not the news round-up". Then,
on any episode of that podcast, choose this row. You get one sentence starting
with Yes, Probably, Probably not or No, and the reason. It never skips or
hides anything; it only tells you.

**Summarise This Episode** (S). A short summary of an episode, for deciding
whether to listen. If Cast has the episode's transcript, it summarises that,
and if not it summarises the show notes. The answer always starts by saying
which it used, because a summary of the show notes is not a summary of what
was said.

**Build Me a Listening Run** (L). "I have 40 minutes." Type how many minutes
you have, and AI help picks unheard episodes from your Inbox and New Episodes
that fit, in an order worth hearing. Cast measures the time itself, so the run
really fits. Review the list, then Apply Selected adds them to the end of your
Play Queue.

**Smart Playlist from a Sentence** (T). Describe the playlist you want, such as
"unheard news under 20 minutes from this week". Cast shows the rules it would
save, one per row, in plain words. Apply Selected saves the smart playlist;
you can change its rules later like any other smart playlist. A podcast or
folder you named that Cast cannot find is left out and said.

**Name These Chapters** (H). When Cast has worked out an episode's chapters
from its transcript (Analyse Chapters, in Chapter 3), their names can be
rough. This asks AI help for better names from what is said in each chapter,
and lets you accept or skip each name on its own, because one wrong name
should not cost you the other eleven.

#### Tidying the podcasts you follow

Over the years a library collects podcasts that stopped years ago, a podcast
followed twice under slightly different names, and a feed that no longer
works. **Help > AI Features > Tidy the Podcasts I Follow** (I) finds them for you.
It needs no AI and sends nothing anywhere, so it works even with AI help
switched off.

Each row says what it found and offers one action: **Pause updates** for a
podcast with nothing new in over a year or a feed that keeps failing, and
**Unfollow** for a second copy. Space switches a row between Accept and Skip,
as in the other lists. Apply Selected pauses the ones you accepted, and asks
before each unfollow, with No selected, so nothing goes by accident. A paused
podcast keeps every episode; resume it any time from its menu.

#### What AI help will never do

You can count on these, every time:

- It never asks anything unless you choose a row or press its key.
- It never runs in the background, during a check for new episodes, or while
  Cast tidies its downloads.
- It never deletes, unfollows or spends anything on its own. Anything it
  suggests waits for you in a list, and nothing changes until Apply.
- It is sent titles, descriptions, show notes and transcripts. It is never
  sent a feed address, a file on your computer, where you are in an episode or
  your support ID.
- It is off in Safe Mode.

## Chapter 7: Your podcasts, wherever you are

After a few months, your Cast is full of things you would hate to lose: the
podcasts you follow, the folders you sorted them into, your place in a dozen
half-finished episodes, notes, bookmarks, and a record of all the hours you
have spent listening. This chapter is about looking after all of that.

We will start with the fun part, your listening statistics and Year in
Review. Then we will make a backup, see how to put it back, move everything
to a new computer, and keep your place in step when you listen on more than
one computer or in both Quill Radio and Cast. By the end, a new laptop or a
bad day with a hard drive will cost you nothing but the time it takes to
copy a file.

Some of these commands are Advanced rows. If one is missing from the
Podcasts menu, turn on View > Advanced Features (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G**), or
find it by name in the Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P).

### Listening statistics

Ever wondered how many hours went into podcasts this year? Cast keeps count,
on your computer and nowhere else.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+S** (Episode > Listening Statistics).
2. In **Period** (Alt+P), choose this week, this month, this year, or all
   time.
3. Tab to the **Listening report** (Alt+R) and arrow through it line by line.

The report tells you how long you listened, how much time faster playback
gave back to you, how many episodes you finished, and your podcasts with the
most-listened at the top. Times are said as words, "3 hours, 47 minutes", so
your screen reader never reads them as a time of day.

The buttons under the report:

- **Copy** (Alt+C) puts the report on the clipboard, for bragging rights.
- **Year in Review...** (Alt+Y) tells the story of your year in a few
  friendly sentences: how long you listened, your top podcasts and what share
  of the year each took, your busiest month, and how many days you listened.
  Choose a different **Year** (Alt+Y) to look back further. **Copy** (Alt+C)
  puts the story on the clipboard, and **Save as Text...** (Alt+S) saves it
  as a file.
- **Export CSV...** (Alt+E) saves every listening session as a file you can
  open in a spreadsheet.
- **Clear Statistics...** (Alt+S) deletes the listening log and nothing
  else. Your podcasts, places and notes stay as they are.

Statistics and Year in Review are windows of their own. You can leave them
open, go back to your library, and come back from the Window menu. Press
Ctrl+Alt+Shift+S again and the same window comes to the front with the latest
numbers. Escape or Ctrl+W closes either one, and you are back where you
opened it.

If you enjoy a streak, open Preferences (Ctrl+,), choose **Data**, and check
**Show listening streaks in Statistics**. Your streak then shows in the report
and in Year in Review. A streak that ran through yesterday still counts, so
you have all of today to keep it going. The same section has **Keep my
listening history for**, from not at all to forever, if you would rather Cast
remembered less.

> **QUILLBert found something:** Year in Review doesn't wait for December.
> Press Alt+Y in Listening Statistics whenever you like, and its Year list
> switches between this year and last. He looked in March and found he'd
> already spent eleven hours on a podcast about bread. He isn't sorry.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**How much did I actually listen?**.

### Backing up your podcasts

A backup is one file holding your whole library: the podcasts you follow,
your folders and playlists, your Lineups, your place in each episode, your
notes, statistics, bookmarks and Go To order. Put it on a memory stick or in a cloud
folder, and you can always get back to where you are today.

Back Up My Podcasts and Restore from a Backup are Advanced rows, and their
keys work only while View > Advanced Features (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G) is on. In
Simple mode, type "back up" or "restore" in the Command Palette
(Ctrl+Shift+P) instead.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+B** (Podcasts > Back Up My Podcasts, an Advanced
   row).
2. Cast asks whether to include your downloaded episodes. The answer starts
   at No. Downloads can be huge, and Cast can fetch them again. Say Yes only
   if you want the audio files too.
3. Choose where to save the file and press Save.

Cast tells you when the backup is written. Do it now, while you think of it.
Then do it again whenever you have spent an evening sorting folders.

#### Restoring from a backup

When you need your library back, from a backup you made earlier:

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+F12** (Podcasts > Restore from a Backup, an Advanced
   row).
2. Pick the backup file.
3. Before it does anything, Cast tells you when the backup was made and how
   many podcasts it holds. If that is the one you meant, confirm.

The backup replaces the library on this computer. Anything playing stops
first, and your podcasts are back straight away, with no restart.

#### Exporting your data to read

Sometimes you want to look at your data, or use it in another program.
In Advanced mode, **Podcasts > Export My Data** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+E**)
writes everything Cast
knows about your listening to one readable file: the podcasts you follow,
folders, the Play Queue, playlists, notes, statistics and what you played
recently.

Cast cannot restore from that file. For a copy you can put back, use Back Up
My Podcasts. For a list of only the podcasts you follow, to take to another
podcast app, use Export OPML, which Chapter 4, Finding podcasts you will
love, covers.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Back it up, move it, and keep your place**. It makes a backup with you, then
covers the move to a new computer and keeping your place in step.

### Starting over

If you want a clean slate on this computer, turn on Advanced Features and
choose **Podcasts > Clear All Podcast Data from This Computer**
(**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+D**), an Advanced row. It unfollows every podcast and
clears your Play Queue, playlists, Inbox folders, statistics and history.
Cast asks twice, with No selected each time, then asks separately whether to
delete your downloaded files as well. Make a backup first if there is any
chance you will want it back.

It only ever clears this computer. Your phone, your other computers and the
shared folder you use to keep your place in step are left exactly as they
are, so tidying a laptop can never empty anything else.

### Moving your setup to a new computer

New computer? You can bring everything you have set up across in one file,
including the things a backup does not cover, such as your settings and the
keys you changed.

1. On the old computer, press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+X** (Help > Export My Setup)
   and save the file.
2. Copy it to the new computer, by memory stick, email or a cloud folder.
3. In Cast on the new computer, press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+N** (Help > Import My
   Setup) and pick the file.
4. Cast tells you what the file holds before anything changes. Confirm.
5. Close Cast and open it again, so it reads everything back in.

The file carries the podcasts you follow, folders, playlists, settings, your
Go To and Quick Action order, bookmarks, and any keys you changed. If you use
Quill Radio, your stations and saved places come too.

Passwords stay behind, for your safety. On the new computer you will enter
private feed sign-ins again, through **Feed Credentials** on the podcast's
context menu. Cast reminds you of this before it imports. Importing replaces
what is on the new computer, so do it before you start setting things up
there.

> **A note from QUILLBee:** Four commands in this chapter sound alike, and
> they do different jobs. Back Up My Podcasts makes a copy you can put back.
> Export My Setup is for a new computer, and carries your settings and keys as
> well. Export My Data is for reading, and can't be put back. Export OPML,
> from Chapter 4, is only the list of podcasts you follow, for another app.

### Keeping your place on more than one computer

If you listen on a laptop in the kitchen and a desktop in the study, you want
to pick up on one where you stopped on the other. Cast can keep your place in
each episode in step between them, through a folder you already sync with
Dropbox, OneDrive, Google Drive, iCloud Drive or a network share. There is no
account to make, and nothing goes anywhere except that folder.

The window has its own key, **Ctrl+Alt+F10**, two along from Restore from
a Backup's Ctrl+Alt+F12, because like a restore it is something you set up once
on each computer rather than every day. It works from anywhere in Cast's main
window. It opens the same window as the menu row, and it changes nothing until
you press OK there.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+F10**, or open the Podcasts menu (Alt+P) and choose
   **Carry My Place Between Machines...** (H).
2. Check **Carry my place between machines**.
3. In **Shared folder**, choose the synced folder. **Browse** helps you find
   it.
4. Choose how to share. **Encrypted, for my other QUILL machines** locks your
   places with a recovery phrase. **A plain file other apps can read** needs
   no phrase and works with other podcast apps that understand it. You can
   use either or both.
5. If you chose encrypted, press **Make a New Phrase** and write the phrase
   down. **Read My Phrase Aloud** says it to you. Your other computers
   need it to join.
6. Give this computer a name in **Name for this machine**, such as "Kitchen
   laptop".
7. Press **Sync Now**.

On your other computer, do the same, choosing the same folder and typing in
the same phrase.

Cast reads the folder when it opens and when you press Sync Now, so your
place never jumps while you are listening. Turn on **Sync when playback
stops, as well** to send your place every time you pause. When the two
computers disagree, the most recent place wins, so if you went back to hear
something again, that is where you will be.

With the plain file, **Include episode and file names** lets Cast name an
episode when it tells you about a disagreement. Turn it off if you would
rather the shared folder knew less about what you listen to.

**Also share the podcasts I follow, and their folders** goes one step
further. Follow a podcast on one device and it appears on the others,
in the same folder and at the same speed. Stop following it on one, and it goes
from the others too. Here is what to know before you turn it on:

- Anybody who can see the shared folder can read the list of podcasts you
  follow. That is why it is off until you choose it.
- Private podcasts, ones you sign in to or whose address carries a key, never
  go in the folder. Cast tells you how many it kept back.
- When a podcast leaves because you stopped following it elsewhere, only the
  follow goes. Your downloaded episodes stay on this computer, and so does
  your listening history. Follow it again and everything comes back.
- Every podcast added or removed this way is named when you sync, such as
  "Stopped following Blind Abilities, removed on Studio PC", so nothing
  changes without you hearing about it.
- If your phone puts a podcast in two folders, Cast files it in the first
  one and leaves the phone's second folder alone.

### Cast and the other Quill apps

If you use Quill Radio or QUILL as well, Cast shares with them, so you never
have to keep two of anything.

#### Your place between Quill Radio and QUILL Cast

If you also use Quill Radio, you never need to remember which app you were
in. Both apps share one place per episode on the same computer. Pause an
episode in Radio, open it in Cast, and it carries on, and Cast tells you so:
"Picking up where you left off in Quill Radio, at 1 hour 2 minutes 3
seconds."

An episode finished in either app is finished in both.

#### Lineups in both apps

Your QUILL Lineups live in one file that Cast and Quill Radio share. A Lineup
you make in one app is there in the other, both can be open and editing at
once, and Cast sends Quill Radio anything in a Lineup that only Radio can
play, such as a station. Each app keeps its own Start Here. Chapter 9 has the
whole story.

#### Sharing with QUILL

QUILL Cast, QUILL and Quill Radio keep your podcasts in one shared store on
your computer. Follow a podcast here and QUILL's Podcasts follows it too,
with the same Play Queue, places, notes and downloads. Your Personal Audio
files stay where they are on your disk.

Uninstalling QUILL Cast never deletes any of it. If you come back, your
podcasts are waiting.

### What you learned, and where to go next

Your listening is now safe and it can travel. You can see how much you have
listened, make a backup and put it back, move everything to a new computer,
and keep your place in step between computers and between Quill apps.

Help > Tutorials has **Back it up, move it, and keep your place** for the
backup and the move, and **How much did I actually listen?** for your
statistics and Year in Review.

If anything ever puzzles you or goes wrong, Chapter 8, When you need a hand,
is there for you.

## Chapter 8: When you need a hand

Sooner or later it happens to all of us. You wonder what a checkbox does.
You forget where a command lives. You unfollow the wrong podcast. An
announcement goes past while you were in another window. A feed will not
behave. This chapter is for those moments.

We will go through the help Cast has built in, roughly in the order you are
likely to need it: asking about the control you are on, finding a command
you have forgotten, taking back a mistake, hearing a message again, and
learning Cast with the tutorials. Then come updates, getting in touch with a
person, and answers to the questions people ask most. The good news first:
very little in Cast can go wrong for good.

Most of what follows lives on the Help menu, **Alt+H**.

### Press F1 anywhere

Every window in Cast answers **F1**. You hear what the window is for, then
what the control you are on does. On a checkbox and not sure what checking it
changes? Press F1 before you check it. Try it now on anything in the main
window.

### Finding a command you have forgotten

You know Cast can do something, and you cannot remember where it is. Three
things help.

The **Command Palette** lists every command in Cast, including the Advanced
ones and anything you have switched off.

1. Press **Ctrl+Shift+P** (Help > Command Palette).
2. Type a few letters of what you want, such as "sleep" or "backup".
3. Arrow to the command and press Enter.

If a command cannot be used right now, the palette tells you why.

**Go To** (**Ctrl+G**) is a short numbered list of places. Press a number
and you are there. Chapter 2, Finding your way around, shows how to choose
your own ten.

The **Keyboard Shortcuts Sheet** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+K**) lists every key Cast
answers to, including any you changed, with what each one does. Type in its
filter to narrow it.

When a menu item is dimmed, Cast tells you why, in the status bar and to
screen readers that read menu help: "Download All Episodes: nothing to
download, all 40 are already here." The same reason comes up if you press a
Quick Action key on a row where that action cannot run.

### Taking back the last thing you did

Unfollowed the wrong podcast? Press **Ctrl+Z** (Edit > Undo Last Action, and
the Edit menu is **Alt+I**). Cast brings back what you removed, files and
all, and says what it did: "Undid Unfollow. Brought back The Daily, with 412
episodes and 3 downloaded files."

Undo covers unfollowing, Remove All Episodes, Remove All Downloads, Mark All
as Played, deleting a recording, and the changes you make in the Lineups
window. Ctrl+Z takes back the most recent one.
Press it again and it takes back the one before.

Sometimes the mistake was a few steps ago, and you want to keep what you did
since. Choose **Edit > Undo History** (**Ctrl+Shift+Z**). It lists the last ten
things you can take back, newest first, each saying what undoing it would
bring back. Choose one and press **Undo This One**. Only that step comes
back; the others stay as they are. Quill Radio has the same list.

You never have to wonder whether something can be undone. When it can, Cast
ends its message with "Ctrl+Z undoes this." If part of it cannot come back,
such as a private feed's saved password, Cast says so in the same sentence,
and you can enter it again in Feed Credentials.

And remember that every question that would delete something starts on
**No**. Pressing Enter too soon is always safe.

### Hearing it again

Announcements go by fast, and you may have been reading something else.

- **Repeat Last Result** (**F9**) says the last thing Cast told you again,
  and what you can do about it.
- **Activity** (**Shift+F9**) lists everything Cast reported this session,
  good news and bad, newest first, one sentence a row. Press Enter on a row
  to take its first action. **Retry** tries it again, **Open Folder** shows
  the file in File Explorer, and **Copy Details** copies the row for a
  message to support.
- **Recent Problems** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+P**) keeps the failures from this
  session and earlier ones: feeds that would not load, downloads that failed,
  streams that dropped, each with its reason and time. **Retry** tries the
  selected one again. **Copy All** gives you the whole list as text for a
  message to support, without any passwords. **Clear List** empties it.
  Anything Cast tried to do on its own and could not, such as reading a
  podcast's chapters, processing a download's audio or looking at a watched
  folder, is written here too, even when it was not worth interrupting you
  for.

A few things take a while, and Cast keeps you posted without chattering. When
you press F5 to check every podcast, it says how far it has got every quarter
of the way, then one sentence at the end: "Checked 40 feeds: 6 new episodes
from 4 podcasts." Download All Episodes does the same for a batch. Those
closing sentences go into Activity, along with your exports and each podcast
you follow, so F9 can say the last one again and Open Folder can find a file
you just saved.

Cast never leaves you wondering whether a button worked. Every button and
every key ends in a sentence: what happened, or why nothing could. When
something is busy, Cast says so as it starts, such as "Searching..." or
"Fetching The Daily...", and says how it turned out when it finishes.

If Cast could not save your library when it last closed, perhaps because the
disk was full, it tells you once the next time it opens. Retry on that row in
Recent Problems tries the save again.

Nothing in Activity or Recent Problems leaves your computer. F9 and Shift+F9
work the same way in QUILL, QUILL Lite and Quill Radio.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**When something breaks**. It practises F9, Activity, Recent Problems, undo
and F1 with you, before you ever need them.

### Learning with the tutorials

Reading about Cast is one thing. The tutorials teach it by having you use it,
one step at a time, with Cast right there beside you.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+F1** (Help > Tutorials). The **QUILL Cast Tutorials**
   window opens.
2. The contents list the tutorials in five tracks: Your first hour, Keeping
   up, Listening well, One podcast at a time, and Making it yours. Each row
   says how long it takes and whether you have finished it.
3. Press Enter on one to start.
4. Read the step, go to the main window and do it, then come back. The
   Tutorials window stays open beside Cast the whole time.
5. Press **Next** (Alt+N) for the next step, or **Back** (Alt+B) to go back.

There are 24 tutorials, about two and a half hours in all. A few things make
them pleasant to use:

- **Try it** (Alt+T) does the step for you, such as opening a window, so the
  lesson can then talk you through what you are looking at.
- **Follow me** (Alt+M) watches what you do and reads you the next step when
  you get there, however you did it. Nothing is graded, and Next is always
  there if you want to move on.
- **Say it again** (Alt+G) repeats the step.
- Each step names the key you have for the command, even if you changed it.
- Close a lesson halfway and it opens at the same step next time.
- Type **here** in the contents filter to find the lessons about the window
  you came from.
- If you would rather read, **Read it all** shows a whole tutorial as one
  page.

**Forget my progress** clears what you have finished, if you want to start
the tutorials afresh.

### Keeping Cast up to date

Cast checks for a new version once a day when it opens, and says nothing
unless it finds one. To check now:

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+U** (Help > Check for Updates).
2. If there is a newer version, Cast downloads it and reads out the progress.
3. Choose **Install and restart now** to update straight away and have Cast
   open again afterwards, or **Install when I close** (Alt+C) to keep
   listening and update the next time you close Cast.

Help > About shows your version with a build number, such as 2.0.0 (build 6). The
build number tells you which build of a version you have: when a fix comes out
without a new version number, it is a newer build, and Check for Updates offers it.

If you are already up to date, Cast tells you so. To turn the daily check
off, open Preferences (Ctrl+,), choose **When Cast opens**, and clear
**Check for updates on launch**.

The user guide, the release notes and the version you have are on the Help
menu too: **User Guide** (Ctrl+Alt+D), **Release Notes** (Ctrl+Alt+R) and
**About QUILL Cast** (Ctrl+Alt+O).

**Help > What's New** (Ctrl+Shift+F1) opens QUILL Cast's changelog in your
browser: every change in every version, newest first, grouped by subject so
you can move through it by heading. The release notes welcome you to a
version; What's New is the full list of what changed.

### Release channels: Stable, Beta and Dev

Cast comes in three flavours, and you choose which one your copy follows.
They are called release channels.

- **Stable** is the version we recommend. It has been checked with JAWS and
  NVDA, and it is what most people use. Every copy starts here.
- **Beta** gets new features a few weeks early. It is mostly finished, but
  some things may not work right yet.
- **Dev** is the work in progress, sometimes several builds a week. Things
  will break. Only choose it if a developer asked you to, or you enjoy
  testing.

1. Open the **Help** menu, then choose **Release Channel...** (press **Alt+H**,
   then **L**). You can also open it from Preferences (Ctrl+,): under **When
   Cast opens**, choose **Change release channel**.
2. Arrow through Stable, Beta and Dev. The box below tells you what each one
   means and what choosing it would do. Nothing changes yet.
3. Press **Switch** to move, or **Close** (Escape) to leave things as they
   are.
4. For Beta or Dev, a short warning comes first: what could go wrong, how your
   podcasts are protected, and how to come back. Read it, check the **I
   understand** box, and choose **Move to Beta** (or **Move to Dev**). **Stay
   on Stable**, or Escape, changes nothing.

Beta and Dev versions aren't signed, so when you install one, Windows
SmartScreen may warn that it comes from an unknown publisher; that's expected,
and choosing **More info**, then **Run anyway**, installs it.

Before it moves, Cast saves a copy of your subscriptions, playlists, listening
places and settings. If it cannot, it stays where it is and tells you why.
Downloaded episodes are not copied, and updates do not change them. Then it
checks for updates on the new channel straight away and offers you the newest
version there, if there is one. Nothing is installed unless you say so.

**When Quill Radio or QUILL Lite is installed too.** These apps run on one shared engine on
your computer. When an app moves to Beta or Dev, it gets its own copy of that
engine, so the apps you leave on Stable are never touched. You can still move
several at once: check them under "Also move my other QuillVille apps on this
computer" and choose **Switch**.

**A note about disk space.** That second copy of the engine takes about 335 MB.
It goes away by itself when your last app comes back to Stable. A portable copy
carries its own engine, so it needs no extra space.

**Updates on Beta and Dev.** On Beta and Dev, Cast downloads a new version
quietly when its daily check finds one, so it is ready when you are. It never
installs anything without asking: when the download is done, it says so and
shows you what's new, and you choose when to install. It waits instead of
downloading on a metered connection, during Quiet Hours, and while Quill Radio is recording.
Update History tells you why it waited. On Stable nothing changes: Cast tells
you about a new version and downloads it only when you ask.

**If an update doesn't start.** After an update, Cast checks that the new
version really opens. If it doesn't open within two minutes, the update undoes
itself and puts back the version you had, and the next time Cast starts it
tells you so. If the new version is still running but stuck, it is closed
first. Nothing of yours is changed, and Update History has the details. If the
installer itself doesn't finish, nothing is changed at all: Cast opens again
straight away as the version you had and tells you the update could not be
installed. A portable copy is updated by putting the new version in the old
folder's place, with your `data` folder moved across untouched. The installer
updates quietly and doesn't ask for administrator permission, because Cast is
installed for you alone.
To make this possible, Cast keeps a copy of the installer for the version you
have: on Beta and Dev for as long as you have that version, and on Stable for a
week or three starts after each update. That copy takes about 200 MB of disk
space, which Stable gives back once the week or the three starts are up. After
an update, the previous installer is kept until the new version has started
three times, or for a week, and then it is deleted.

**Coming back to Stable.** Open Release Channel again and choose **Stable**.

- If the version you have is already a Stable one, you are back straight away
  and nothing is installed.
- If you have a newer Beta or Dev version, Cast checks whether Stable can
  read everything you have saved. If it can, choose **Go back to** (the Stable
  version) **now**. Cast downloads Stable and offers to install it, and your
  subscriptions and settings come with you.
- If Stable can't read something yet, Cast says so and gives you two safe
  choices. **Wait for Stable** keeps the version you have, stops test versions,
  and moves you to Stable by itself when Stable catches up. **Use the copy
  from** (the day you joined) puts back the copy of your subscriptions and settings saved
  when you joined, then installs Stable. Anything you added since then won't be
  in it, but a copy of how things are right now is saved first, so nothing is
  thrown away.
- If there is no saved copy, waiting is the only choice, and the window says
  so.

The **Update History** button in the same window shows what the updater has
done for Cast. Switching sends nothing about you: the only thing Cast ever
sends when it checks for updates is a request for the list of versions.

### When a media tool is missing

Cast uses a tool called FFmpeg to trim silence, even out volume, find
chapters in episodes that have none, and for Sound Enhancements and Smart
Speed. It comes with Cast, so you will probably never think about it. If it
ever goes missing, Cast tells you once when it opens.

**Help > Media Tools** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+M**), an Advanced row, checks for it
whenever you ask, and **Help > Get FFmpeg** (**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F**), another
Advanced row, puts it back. Turn on View > Advanced Features
(Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G) to see both and use their keys, or find either by name in
the Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P). Following, downloading and playing all
work without it.

### Getting help from support

Support is run by Community Access, and a person reads every message. If
something is wrong, or you have an idea, write to us.

1. Press **Ctrl+Alt+F2** (Help > Get Help from Support).
2. Choose what kind of message it is, add a subject, and say what happened.
   Add your email address if you would like a reply.
3. If you can, say what you expected and how to make it happen again. Those
   two help more than anything.
4. Press **Send**.

Your own mail program opens with the message ready to go, with Cast's version
and your version of Windows at the bottom. Nothing goes until you send it
from there, so you can read it over first. If you have no mail program, Cast
puts the message on the clipboard so you can paste it into webmail.

The address is **support@community-access.org**, and you can always write to
it directly. Say that you were using QUILL Cast and what happened. If you
have something from Recent Problems or Activity, paste it in.

### Troubleshooting

Here are the snags people run into most, each with what to do about it.

> **QUILLBee's tip:** Most of these start the same way. Press F9 to hear what
> Cast said, then Ctrl+Alt+Shift+P for Recent Problems, which keeps the reason
> and the time for anything that failed, earlier sessions included. Between
> the two, you often know what went wrong before you read any further.

#### A podcast will not add

Check that the address is the feed itself, and not the podcast's web page.
Typing its name in Add Podcast usually finds it.

#### Cast asks for a username and password

The feed is private. Enter the ones your podcast provider gave you. For
Patreon-style feeds they are next to the feed address on the provider's site.
If the question keeps coming back, copy both again.

#### A private feed says "feed sign-in failed"

The publisher may have changed your sign-in, or you are on a different
computer. Open the podcast's context menu, choose **Feed Credentials**, enter
the password again, and refresh with F5.

#### A download keeps failing

Press **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+V** (Downloads > Resume All Downloads). If it still
fails, check that **Reconnect and keep downloading automatically** is on in
Preferences, under Fetching. Recent Problems will have the reason.

#### Episodes vanished from the Play Queue

They waited longer than the podcast's queue limit. Go to Recently Expired
(**Ctrl+Shift+X**) and press Restore. To change the limit, use **Queue
Expiry...** on the podcast's context menu. Chapter 5, Keeping up without
keeping watch, explains queue expiry and Inbox limits.

#### The Inbox is missing episodes

A podcast's Inbox limits can move older episodes out of the Inbox. They are
still unheard in the podcast's own episode list, and nothing is deleted.

#### A download disappeared

A cleanup rule in Preferences, under Data, removed the file. The episode is
still there, ready to download again. Cleanup never touches an episode in your
Play Queue or one you have started.

#### Cast did not pick up my episode when it opened

Resume Last Episode on Launch (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L) only works when the episode
is still in your library. If you unfollowed the podcast or removed the
episode, press Enter on something new.

#### A letter key does something unexpected in a list

Letters in the main library and episode lists jump to matching rows. Winamp
playback letters are available only in Now Playing; if a main list does not
respond, check which control has focus and return to the list with Tab.

#### A command or a whole menu is missing

It may be an Advanced row, or a feature switched off in Customize Features
(Ctrl+Alt+C). The Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P) finds it either way and tells
you which.

#### Text looks blurry, or a window is the wrong size

QUILL Cast scales itself for each monitor. Text stays sharp at 125, 150 or 200
percent, and when you move a window to a monitor with different scaling;
windows and list columns keep their proportions. If a window looks wrong at
your scaling, you can turn this off to see whether it is the cause. Search
Windows for "Edit environment variables for your account", add a variable
named `QUILL_DPI_UNAWARE` with the value `1`, and start QUILL Cast again.
Windows then stretches it the old way, so text is blurrier. Delete the
variable to put scaling back, and tell support what you saw.

## Chapter 9: QUILL Lineups

A Lineup is a list of things to listen to, in an order you chose, that keeps
itself up to date. Most of mine are episodes, but one Lineup can hold any mix
of podcasts you follow and their episodes, radio stations and saved places
from Quill Radio, YouTube videos, playlists and channels, and Quill Radio's
Local Media files and recordings.

Things come into a Lineup two ways. You can add them yourself, from any
episode or podcast in Cast. And you can give a Lineup **rules**: "every
unplayed episode from my News folder published since yesterday", or "the
newest unplayed episode of every podcast I follow". Each rule also says **when
its items go**: once you have heard them, after some days, when they stop
matching, or never. And whatever the rules say, **your hands win**. What you
add stays, what you keep stays, and what you remove never comes back.

A weekday might look like this: open Lineups, arrow to Commute, press Enter on
the first episode, and Cast plays its short unheard episodes one after another
through the Play Queue, with yesterday's already gone because you heard them.

Your Lineups are shared with Quill Radio. Make one in Cast and it is there when
Radio opens, and you can edit it in either app, even with both open.

If you would rather be walked through it, Help > Tutorials has a lesson called
**Build a lineup that fills itself**, in the **Keeping up** track. It takes
about nine minutes.

### Open Lineups

Press **Ctrl+U** (**Episode > Lineups...**). The Lineups window opens. It is a
window of its own, like Now Playing, so it is on the Window menu and in the
Ctrl+Tab order, it has its own menu bar, and **Ctrl+1** takes you back to the
main window.

You can also reach it from:

- **Go To** (Ctrl+G). Lineups waits in the pool, so nobody's numbering moves:
  add it to your list with Settings inside Go To.
- The **Command Palette** (Ctrl+Shift+P): **Podcasts: Lineups...** opens the
  window, and **Podcasts: Add to Lineup...** adds the episode or podcast you
  are on to a Lineup.

If the window is already open, Ctrl+U brings it to the front rather than
opening a second one. Close it with **Escape** or **Ctrl+W** (**Lineup >
Close**). Whatever is playing carries on.

Lineups is one of the switches in Customize Features (Ctrl+Alt+C), called
**QUILL Lineups**, under Playing. Switch it off and the Episode menu row and
the Go To entry go away, and the Command Palette says the Lineups commands are
switched off. The Play Queue and your playlists keep doing everything they did,
and your Lineups are kept for when you switch it on again.

### Finding your way around the window

#### When you have no Lineups yet

The first time, the window says "You have no lineups yet", and your cursor is
on the **New Lineup...** button. Beside it is **Start from an Example...**,
which gives you a ready-made Lineup with rules.

#### The two lists

Once you have a Lineup, the window has two lists. Tab moves from the Lineups
list to the Show filter and then to the Items list.

- **Lineups** (Alt+L): your Lineups, in your own order. Each row says how many
  items it holds and how long they play, such as "Commute, 6 items, 44
  minutes". A Lineup whose rules brought in new things while you were looking
  at another one says how many, such as "2 new", until you choose it. The
  Lineup that opens when Cast starts says "start lineup".
- **Items** (Alt+T): what is in the Lineup you are on, in the order it plays.
  Moving through the Lineups list fills it straight away.

Each item reads as one sentence, always in the same order: the **name**, the
**kind** of thing, the **length**, where it **comes from**, and, only when
there is something to say, its **state**. For example, "Tuesday's Episode,
episode, 32 minutes, The Daily". The name comes first, so typing the first
letter of a name jumps to it. The states you may hear are:

| State | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| kept | You chose Keep This Item, so it stays whatever the rules say. |
| played | You have heard it to the end. |
| live | It never ends by itself, like a radio station. |
| missing | It no longer exists, for example an episode of a podcast you stopped following. |
| unavailable | Cast could not check it just now. It stays as it was, and is checked again later. |

Above the Items list, **Show** (Alt+O) narrows the list to one kind: All kinds,
Episodes and podcasts, Stations and places, YouTube, or Local media and
recordings. If you only ever add episodes, you will never need it.

The status bar sums up the Lineup you are on: how many items, how long they
play, and when the rules last ran. **Lineup Summary** (Ctrl+T, **Play > Lineup
Summary**) says the same thing and then reads you each rule.

#### The menus in the window

Every item on the menu bar shows its key:

- **Lineup** (Alt+U): New Lineup, New from an Example, Duplicate Lineup,
  Rename Lineup, Delete Lineup, Edit Rules, Start Here, Removed Items,
  Recently Left, Import Rules, Export Rules and Close.
- **Edit** (Alt+E): Undo, Cut, Paste Before, Paste After, Keep This Item,
  Remove from Lineup, Remove Missing Items, Select All, Why Is This Here and
  Refresh Now.
- **Arrange** (Alt+A): Move Up, Move Down, Move to Top, Move to Bottom and Move
  to Position.
- **Play** (Alt+P): Play, Pause or Resume, Next Item, Previous Item, Play This
  Lineup, Play Next, Add to Up Next, Stop After This Item, Skip Live Items and
  Lineup Summary.
- **Window** (Alt+W): your open Cast windows, as everywhere in Cast.

Press **Shift+F10**, or the Applications key, on an item or a Lineup, and
everything you can do with it is right there.

> **QUILLBee's tip:** The Lineups window has the same menus and keys in Quill
> Radio, so learning it once is enough. That also means its keys are its own:
> in the main window Ctrl+Right skips forward in an episode, and in Lineups it
> is Next Item. The full list is in Keys in the Lineups window, near the end of

### Make your first Lineup

#### Start from an example, step by step

The quickest way to see a Lineup working is to start from one of the examples.
Each one shows a different way of choosing how long things stay.

1. In the Lineups window, press **Ctrl+Alt+N** (**Lineup > New from an
   Example...**), or choose the **Start from an Example...** button.
2. Arrow through the examples. Each one says what it does.
3. Choose **New This Week** and press **Enter**.
4. You hear "Made New This Week. Its rules are running now." A moment later,
   its episodes arrive.

The examples are:

| Example | What it brings in, and how long it stays |
| --- | --- |
| Morning | The stations in your Quill Radio Favorites folder called Morning, always; then unplayed episodes from your News podcast folder published in the last day, newest first, each one gone once finished or after 2 days. |
| New This Week | Unplayed episodes from the last 7 days, newest first, at most 20, each gone once finished or after 7 days. |
| Latest From Each | The newest unplayed episode of each podcast you follow, gone as soon as it stops matching. |
| Commute | Unplayed episodes of 40 minutes or less, oldest first, up to 45 minutes in all, each gone once finished. |
| Saved YouTube | The YouTube videos you saved in Quill Radio, each kept for 14 days. |
| Live News | Stations with a news tag, and saved YouTube streams that are live, while they match. |
| Finish These | Episodes you started and have not finished, gone once finished. |

The four that are only episodes, New This Week, Latest From Each, Commute and
Finish These, work with nothing but Cast.

The example becomes yours, a copy you can change however you like. If an
example names a folder you do not have, such as News, its rule finds nothing
and its sentence says so: 'in folder "News", which does not exist'. Change the
folder in the rule to one of yours (see "Write a rule" below).

#### Make an empty Lineup, step by step

1. Press **Ctrl+N** (**Lineup > New Lineup...**), or choose the **New
   Lineup...** button. In the Lineups window Ctrl+N is New Lineup, not Add
   Podcast.
2. Type a name, such as Sunday, and press **Enter**.
3. You hear "Made Sunday. Add to Lineup in any list fills it; Edit Rules,
   Ctrl+Alt+R, gives it rules."

Two Lineups never share a name, whatever the capitals: a second Sunday is
called "Sunday (2)".

#### Rename, duplicate, delete and arrange Lineups

These work on the Lineup selected in the Lineups list:

- **Rename Lineup...** (F2).
- **Duplicate Lineup** (Ctrl+D) makes a copy with the same rules, settings and
  order.
- **Delete Lineup...** (Shift+Delete, or Delete in the Lineups list) asks first
  and names what goes: "Delete Sunday, its 2 rules and 14 items? The items
  themselves are not deleted." No episode, download or podcast is ever deleted
  by this. **Ctrl+Z** brings the Lineup back.
- **Alt+Shift+Up** and **Alt+Shift+Down**, in the Lineups list, move the
  Lineup you are on, and **Alt+Shift+Home** and **Alt+Shift+End** move it to
  the top or the bottom.

### Add episodes and podcasts by hand

Anything you add yourself stays in the Lineup until you remove it, whatever
the rules say.

#### Add to Lineup, step by step

1. Go to an episode or a podcast anywhere in Cast: the Podcasts place, the
   Inbox, New Episodes, the Play Queue or any other list of episodes.
2. Press **Shift+F10**, or the Applications key, and choose **Add to
   Lineup...**.
3. A short list of your Lineups opens, with **New Lineup...** at the end.
   Choose a Lineup and press **Enter**, or choose New Lineup... and type a
   name.
4. You hear "Added to Sunday. Ctrl+Z undoes this."

The Command Palette's **Podcasts: Add to Lineup...** does the same for the
episode you are on, or the podcast if you are on a podcast. Add to Lineup is
also one of the Quick Actions for episodes and for podcasts, so you can give it
a Ctrl+number in Quick Actions (Ctrl+Alt+Q); see "Quick Actions: what Enter
does" in Chapter 6.

If the Lineups window is open, the new item appears in it straight away.
Adding something the Lineup already holds makes it stay until you remove it,
and adding something you removed earlier takes it off Removed Items.

A **podcast** in a Lineup stands for its next episode: playing it plays the
podcast's next unheard episode, so a Lineup of five podcasts you follow is
always five things worth hearing.

### Arrange the items

Moving an item changes the order, never how long it stays. And the rules never
move anything: when they run, items they bring in arrive, items they let go
leave, and everything else stays exactly where you put it.

- **Alt+Shift+Up** and **Alt+Shift+Down** move the selected items one place.
  Cast says where they landed, such as "Moved to 3 of 14". At the edge you hear
  "Already at the top".
- **Alt+Shift+Home** and **Alt+Shift+End** move them to the top or the bottom.
- **Move to Position...** (Ctrl+J) asks for a number.
- For a long move, press **Ctrl+X** (**Edit > Cut**), go to where they belong,
  and press **Ctrl+V** (**Paste Before**) or **Ctrl+Alt+V** (**Paste After**).
- **Ctrl+A** selects every item, **Shift** with the arrows selects a run, and
  **Ctrl+Space** selects or deselects one item at a time, as in any Windows
  list.

### Write a rule

A rule says what to look for, where to look, which ones, in what order, how
many, and how long they stay. A Lineup can have as many rules as you like:
anything any rule finds is in the Lineup.

#### The Rules window, step by step

1. In the Lineups list, arrow to a Lineup and press **Alt+Enter**, or press
   **Ctrl+Alt+R** (**Lineup > Edit Rules...**) from anywhere in the window.
2. The Rules window opens on the list **Rules for** and the Lineup's name. Each
   rule is one sentence, including how long its items stay and how many it
   finds now, for example: 'Podcast episodes in folder "News", where Played is
   no and Age is at most 1 day, newest first. They go once finished, or after
   2 days. Finds 3 now.'
3. Use the buttons beside the list: **Add Rule...**, **Edit Rule...**,
   **Remove Rule**, **Move Up** and **Move Down**. Moving a rule changes where
   its new items go, not what it finds.
4. Below the list, in **This lineup**, are three settings for the whole
   Lineup:
   - **New items go**: with the other items from the same rule (the usual
     choice, so each rule's items stay together), at the top, or at the
     bottom.
   - **Play-through budget in minutes**: playing the Lineup through stops
     adding rule items once this many minutes are used. 0 means no budget.
   - **Skip live items when playing through**, the same as **Play > Skip Live
     Items** (Ctrl+Alt+K).
5. Press **Enter** for OK, or **Escape** to change nothing.

Nothing changes until you choose OK. Two questions may come first, so that a
rule change never takes something away silently:

- If the change would let items go, you hear how many: "This change lets 4
  items go. They will be in Recently Left. Make the change?"
- If you removed a rule that had brought items in, you are asked what to do
  with them: keep them as items you keep, or let them go to Recently Left.

#### Edit Rule, step by step

Edit Rule is laid out in the order the rule's sentence reads, in labelled
groups.

1. **What it looks for.** **Look for** (Alt+L) chooses one kind: Radio
   stations, Saved places, Podcasts I follow, Podcast episodes, YouTube
   videos, YouTube playlists, YouTube channels, Local media, or Recordings.
   The change takes effect when you leave the box. If the rule already has
   tests or places to look in, Cast asks first, because those belong to the
   old kind.
2. **Where to look.** **Narrow by** (Alt+N) chooses what to look in, such as
   Folder, Podcast or Label. It starts on "Choose what to narrow by", which
   means everywhere. Then **Values** (Alt+V) lists every one you have: press
   **Space** on each one to look in, and any selected one counts. **Filter
   values** (Alt+F) shortens a long list as you type.
3. **Tests.** The **Tests** list (Alt+E) holds what an item must be. **Add
   Test...** (Alt+A) asks for a field, a test and a value, such as "Length",
   "is at most", "40". **Match** (Alt+M) chooses whether an item must pass
   **All of the tests** or **Any of the tests**. With no tests, everything in
   the places you chose is found.
4. **Order and amount.** **Order** (Alt+O): as the source orders them, newest
   first, oldest first, shortest first, longest first, by name, or most
   listened first. Then three limits, each 0 for none: **Count limit**, **Per
   group limit** (at most this many from each podcast) and **Up to minutes in
   all**.
5. **When they go.** **Keep** (Alt+K), and the boxes it needs; see "Choose how
   long things stay" below. **Rule switched on** (Alt+S) turns the rule off
   without deleting it: off, it finds nothing new and lets nothing go.
6. **Try it.** Press **Try It** (Alt+T). Your cursor moves to the matches
   list, whose label carries the count, such as "Matches, 7 items". Nothing is
   saved by trying.
7. Press **Enter** for OK.

#### What a test can ask

A text test can be: contains, does not contain, starts with, ends with, is, is
not, fits the wildcard, matches the regular expression, or does not match the
regular expression. **Capitals must match** makes it exact. A number test can
be: is at least, is at most, is, or is not, and the box says the unit, such as
"In minutes." A yes-or-no test is yes or no.

**A number test never matches something that does not say.** A podcast that
publishes no length is never "at most 10 minutes", so a short-episodes rule
never fills up with episodes of unknown length. This is the same rule Episode
Filters and smart playlists follow.

What episodes and podcasts can be tested on:

| Kind | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| Podcast episodes | Title, Podcast, Folder, Label, Age (days), Length (minutes), Played, Started but not finished, Percent heard, Downloaded, Season, Episode number, Episode type, Days since last played |
| Podcasts I follow | Title, Folder, Label, Favorite, Paused, Language, Category, Unplayed episodes, Newest episode age (days) |

Rules over Quill Radio's kinds, such as stations and Local Media, have fields of
their own. Quill Radio's user guide lists them.

### Choose how long things stay

Tests decide what comes **in**. The rule's **Keep** choice decides when it
**goes**. The same episode can come in because it is new and leave because you
have heard it, and the rule's sentence always says both.

| Keep | An item goes when | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| While it matches | It stops passing the rule's tests. | Lists that mirror a fact exactly, such as the newest episode of each podcast. |
| Until finished | You have heard it to the end, or past the percentage you set (95 percent unless you change it). | Episodes you mean to hear. |
| For a number of days | It has been in the Lineup that many days, 1 to 365, whether it still matches or not. | Lists that should roll forward by themselves. |
| Until finished, or a number of days | Whichever comes first. | The usual podcast case: hear it this week or let it go. |
| Always | Never, unless you remove it. | The episodes you come back to. |

A few things worth knowing:

- **What counts as finished.** An episode you mark as played always counts.
  Otherwise it counts once you have heard past **Percent heard that counts as
  finished** (Alt+P).
- **A podcast is never finished**, and neither is a station or a channel.
  Rules over those offer only While it matches, For a number of days, and
  Always.
- **Max kept** (Alt+X) caps a rule: it keeps at most that many, and the oldest
  arrivals go first. A busy podcast cannot fill the Lineup.
- **Days are real days.** "For 3 days" means 72 hours from when the rule
  brought the item in, however often the rules run.
- **The usual choice follows the kind.** A new rule over episodes starts on
  Until finished, or 7 days. A rule over podcasts starts on While it matches.
- **Two rules can find the same item.** It appears once, and stays while any
  of its rules still wants it.

### Your hands outrank the rules

- **Keep This Item** (Ctrl+K, **Edit > Keep This Item**) holds an item
  whatever its rule says. You hear "Kept. It stays whatever the rules say."
  The row says "kept", and the menu item is checked. Press it again to give it
  back to its rule: "No longer kept: its rules decide again."
- **An item you added by hand** stays until you remove it.
- **Remove from Lineup** (Delete) takes an item out now, and **no rule ever
  brings it back**, however well it matches later. You hear "Removed. Removed
  Items can bring it back." With several selected, you hear how many. Nothing
  is deleted from your library: the episode is still in its podcast, and its
  download is still on disk.
- **Remove Missing Items** (Ctrl+Shift+Delete) removes every item that no
  longer exists.
- **Undo** (Ctrl+Z, **Edit > Undo**) takes back the last change: a move, a
  remove, Keep, a rename, a deleted Lineup, a rule change or an add. Press it
  again for the one before. It is the same Undo as the main window's, so
  **Undo History** (Ctrl+Shift+Z in the main window) lists these too.

> **QUILLBee's tip:** Delete never asks, because it never loses anything.
> Removed Items keeps the list, and Ctrl+Z puts the item back in the very same
> place.

### Why Is This Here

Press **Alt+Enter** on an item (**Edit > Why Is This Here...**). A short window
says why the item is in the Lineup and how long it will stay. Read it with the
arrow keys, and press **Escape** to close it. You might hear:

- 'In Commute because rule 1 found it: Podcast episodes, where Played is no and
  Length is at most 40 minutes, oldest first. They go once finished.' and, for
  an episode you have started, how much of it you have heard.
- "In Commute because you kept it. It stays whatever the rules say, until you
  remove it."
- "In Commute because you added it. It stays until you remove it."
- When two rules found it, it names both and says which one keeps it longest.

Alt+Enter means "tell me about this" everywhere in Windows, which is why it is
the reason on an item and the rules on a Lineup.

### Removed Items and Recently Left

Nothing leaves a Lineup without trace. Two lists, both on the Lineup menu,
remember what went:

- **Removed Items...** (Ctrl+Alt+Z): what you removed yourself.
- **Recently Left...** (Ctrl+Alt+L): what the rules let go, newest first, each
  with its reason and when, such as "finished", "2 days passed" or "it no
  longer matches rule 1". It remembers the last 100.

In either one, select one or more items and choose **Bring Back** (Alt+B).
They come back at the end of the Lineup as items you keep, so the rules do not
let them straight go again, and you hear how many came back. **Clear the
List** (Alt+L) empties the list after asking first. Clearing Removed Items
means a rule could bring those items in again.

### When the rules run

The rules run:

- when you open the Lineups window;
- when something they read changes, in Cast or in Quill Radio, such as a new
  episode arriving, an episode being played, or a favorite being added in
  Radio;
- every ten minutes while the window is open, so days pass on time;
- whenever you press **F5** (**Edit > Refresh Now**).

**The rules run while the Lineups window is open, and each time it opens.**
They do not run in the background with the window closed. Opening the window
always brings every Lineup up to date first.

What you hear:

- With the window in front, a change is said once, without moving your
  cursor: "2 added, 1 left." If the item you were on is the one that left, you
  hear that first, such as "Your item left: finished.", and your cursor moves
  to the item that took its place.
- When nothing changed, the rules say nothing, unless you pressed F5, which
  always answers: "No changes."
- Changes to Lineups you are not looking at show on their rows as "2 new".

Some promises the rules keep, whatever happens:

- **Nothing leaves because something could not be checked.** If your library
  cannot be read for a moment, the items it would have checked stay exactly as
  they were and say "unavailable".
- **Nothing leaves without trace.** Everything the rules let go is in Recently
  Left, with the reason.
- **Your order is never changed by the rules.**
- **A big loss is never silent.** If one refresh would take away more than half
  of a Lineup, and more than 10 items, for example after a long time away, it
  says so plainly and tells you Recently Left can bring them back.

### Play a Lineup in Cast

Cast plays podcasts, and it plays a Lineup through its own **Play Queue**, the
same queue Chapter 5 describes. That way a Lineup carries on by itself, even
with the Lineups window closed, and Next in Queue works as it always does.

#### Play from an episode, step by step

1. In the Items list, arrow to an episode and press **Enter**, or
   **Ctrl+Enter** (**Play > Play**).
2. Cast puts that episode, and the Lineup's episodes after it, at the front of
   the Play Queue, in the Lineup's order, and plays it. Episodes you have
   already heard, and ones no longer in your library, are left out. The rest
   of your queue waits behind them, in its own order.
3. When the episode ends, the queue carries on with the next one from the
   Lineup.

To play from the top, press **Enter** on the Lineup in the Lineups list, or
**Ctrl+Alt+P** (**Play > Play This Lineup**) from anywhere in the window.

While it plays:

- **Ctrl+Right** and **Ctrl+Left** (**Next Item**, **Previous Item**) move
  through the Play Queue, the same as Next in Queue and Previous in Queue in
  the main window.
- **Pause or Resume** (**Play > Pause or Resume**) holds and continues what is
  playing. Its key, **Ctrl+Space**, works everywhere in the window except the
  Items list, where Ctrl+Space selects or deselects the item under the cursor.
- **Play Next** (Ctrl+Shift+Enter) puts the selected episodes right after the
  one playing, and **Add to Up Next** (Ctrl+Alt+Enter) puts them at the end of
  the Play Queue. These two work on episodes.
- **Stop After This Item** (Ctrl+Alt+S) answers that in Cast the sleep timer
  does this: choose End of Episode in the Sleep Timer (Ctrl+Alt+T), or press
  **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+T** in the main window.
- A **podcast** in a Lineup plays its next unheard episode.

#### What Cast sends to Quill Radio

Stations, saved places, YouTube items, Local Media files and recordings are
Quill Radio's to play. When you press Enter on one in Cast, Cast sends it, and
the Lineup's items after it, to Quill Radio, opening Radio if it is not
running, and says "Sent to Quill Radio to play." Radio then plays them through.

When you play from an episode and the Lineup also holds things only Radio can
play, Cast plays the episodes through the Play Queue and tells you how many
other items there are: "The lineup's episodes are at the front of the queue.
Its other 3 items play in Quill Radio: Enter on one of them sends it there."

### Save Lineup and Apply Lineup on the Play Queue

The Play Queue place has had **Save Lineup...** and **Apply Lineup...** on its
Applications key for a long time. They now make and use QUILL Lineups, so
there is only one kind of lineup in Cast and Quill Radio.

#### Save the queue's order, step by step

1. Arrange the Play Queue the way you like it.
2. Press the Applications key on the Play Queue place and choose **Save
   Lineup...**.
3. Type a name, such as Tuesday, and press **Enter**.
4. You hear "Saved lineup Tuesday: 5 episodes, in this order."

The new Lineup holds the queue's episodes as items you added by hand, so they
stay until you remove them. Saving under a name you already used replaces that
Lineup's items, and you hear "Replaced lineup Tuesday" instead. If that Lineup
has rules, they are kept.

#### Apply a Lineup to the queue, step by step

1. Press the Applications key on the Play Queue place and choose **Apply
   Lineup...**.
2. Choose a Lineup. Only Lineups with episodes in them are offered.
3. The Lineup's episodes that are still unheard move to the front of the
   queue in its order, and the rest of your queue waits behind them. Nothing
   is thrown away.
4. Cast tells you how many it applied and how many it skipped because you had
   already heard them or they are no longer in your library.

Any Lineup with episodes can be applied, not only the ones you saved from the
queue, so a Lineup built by rules can fill the front of your queue too.

#### Lineups you saved before

Lineups saved by Save Lineup before QUILL Lineups were kept as playlists. The
first time this version of Cast starts, it copies each one into Lineups, once,
under the same name. The playlists stay in Playlists exactly as they were, so
you can delete them there when you no longer want them.

### Start Here: open Cast in a Lineup

You can choose one Lineup to open every time Cast starts.

1. In the Lineups list, arrow to the Lineup, such as Commute.
2. Press **Ctrl+Alt+H** (**Lineup > Start Here**).
3. You hear "Commute opens when Quill Cast starts." The menu item is checked,
   and the Lineup's row says "start lineup".

Next time Cast starts, the Lineups window opens in front of the main window,
on that Lineup, and its rules run as it opens. The main window is still there
behind it. Close the Lineups window, or press Ctrl+1, and you are back in it.

- To stop, choose **Start Here** again on the same Lineup. You hear "Quill
  Cast opens as usual." Choosing it on another Lineup moves it there, because
  Cast can start in only one place.
- The very first time Cast runs, its welcome comes first. Start Here applies
  from the next time.
- If the Lineup was deleted, Cast says once "Your start lineup no longer
  exists. Quill Cast opened as usual." and forgets the choice.

Quill Radio has its own Start Here, so each app can start in a different
Lineup, or neither.

### Sharing Lineups with Quill Radio

Cast and Quill Radio keep their Lineups in one file, `lineups.json` in the
shared data folder. The same Lineups, rules and order appear in both apps, and
the Lineups window has the same menus and keys in each, Ctrl+U included.

- **Both apps can be open at once.** An edit in one appears in the other within
  a couple of seconds, and neither app ever undoes the other's change. If you
  move an item the other app removed a moment ago, nothing is guessed: you are
  told so.
- **Each app plays what it can, and hands over the rest.** Cast plays episodes
  and podcasts and sends everything else to Radio. Radio plays every kind.
- **Backups.** A Cast backup (see "Backing up your podcasts" in Chapter 7)
  includes your Lineups.

#### Share a Lineup's rules

**Export Rules...** (Ctrl+Shift+X) saves a Lineup's rules and settings, not its
items, to a small file you can send to a friend or take to another computer.
**Import Rules...** (Ctrl+I) makes a new Lineup from such a file, whose rules
then find the same kinds of thing in your own podcasts.

### What Lineups cannot do yet

- **Rules never use the internet.** They read what is already on your
  computer, so they are instant and work offline. An episode rule sees the
  episodes Cast already knows about; it does not check feeds itself.
- **YouTube rules see what you saved and follow.** They see the videos and
  playlists you saved in Quill Radio and the channels you follow. The newest
  uploads in a channel are fetched from YouTube as you browse and are not kept
  on your computer, so a rule cannot see every new upload.
- **The rules run while the Lineups window is open**, and each time it opens,
  not in the background.
- **Cast does not play stations, YouTube or Local Media itself.** It sends them
  to Quill Radio, so those items need Quill Radio installed.

### What you learned, and where to go next

You can now make Lineups from examples or from nothing, fill them by hand from
any episode or podcast, and write rules that bring episodes in and let them go
when you choose. You know that your own hands outrank the rules, how to ask
why something is there, and how to bring back anything that left. You can play
a Lineup through the Play Queue, save the queue's order as a Lineup and apply
one later, open Cast in a Lineup, and share them all with Quill Radio.

The Keyboard reference that follows lists every key Cast comes with, for the
days you want them all in one place. Happy listening.
