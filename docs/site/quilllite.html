QUILL Lite: free AI help, in an editor that talks to you.

Select a paragraph. Press two keys. Hear it summarized, rewritten, proofread or explained - free, with no account, no password and no card. QUILL Lite is a small, free text editor for Windows, built from the ground up for people who use a screen reader, with AI help designed the same way: keyboard-first, spoken-first, and never leaving you wondering what just happened.

Windows 10 or later, 64-bit. Both downloads are digitally signed, so Windows can confirm they have not been altered. The installer puts QUILL Lite in its own folder with a Start Menu entry, and can optionally add it to the Open With list for .txt and .rtf files without ever making itself the default. The portable ZIP carries its own data folder, so it runs from a USB stick. Released September 25, 2026.

AI that is built for the way you work

  • Two keys to an answer. Ctrl+Alt+G opens the AI pad right where you are. Ctrl+Alt+Z opens it ready for a question about your document.
  • Five things it does. Summarize, Rewrite, Proofread, Explain, and answer a plain question about the document you have open.
  • You always know what is sent. Before anything leaves your computer, the pad shows you exactly what is about to go, in a box you can read line by line.
  • Nothing changes behind your back. The answer arrives in its own window, and goes into your document only when you press Replace or Insert. Ctrl+Z takes it back.
  • Your words are not kept. The service counts how many requests you make and how big they were. It never keeps what you wrote or what came back.
  • Free. No account, no password, no email address, no card, and no trial that runs out.

Your first answer, in about a minute

  1. Open the agreement. Press Ctrl+Alt+Shift+K, or choose Tools, AI, Privacy Agreement.
  2. Read it, and choose I Agree. That switches AI help on. There is no second switch to find.
  3. Connect this computer. The Connect window opens by itself with your eight-character code. Press Open the Connect Page, and your browser opens with the code already filled in: press Confirm.
  4. Try it. Put the cursor in any paragraph, press Ctrl+Alt+G, choose Summarize, and press Send.

How it works

How it knows what to send

If you have selected something, that is what is sent. If you have not, the paragraph your cursor is in is sent - you never have to select anything first. In a document with headings you can choose This section instead. A Send this much chooser switches between them, and a read-only box shows exactly the text that will go, updated the moment you change your choice. Nothing else from your document is sent.

Asking about a whole document

Rather than sending the whole file, QUILL Lite reads it on your own computer, finds the three passages most likely to answer your question - each about 180 words, each labelled with the heading it sits under - and sends only those, with your question. The pad shows you which passages it chose before you press Send, which is why a hundred-page document is a perfectly fair thing to ask about.

What happens to the answer

It arrives in its own window, in a box you can arrow through, with Replace My Selection, Insert Below, Copy and Try Again under it. Every edit goes through the ordinary undo. If you changed the text while the answer was on its way, Replace is not offered, so an answer is never written over words it was not about. The editor never freezes while you wait.

Free, and fair

There is a monthly allowance, with daily and hourly limits so a runaway loop cannot spend a month in an afternoon. A request only counts if an answer comes back. A new connection starts with a smaller allowance for its first 48 hours, then the full one applies by itself. Usage (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F9) and Help, About show exactly where you stand and when each count starts again. Need more for a course, a deadline or a book? Get Help from Support includes your support ID automatically, and support can raise your allowance.

Your privacy, in plain words

The passage you ask about is sent to QUILL's service and on to OpenAI, which writes the answer. The service keeps how many requests you made and how big they were - never what you wrote, never what came back, and never your name, because there is no name on the account at all. Nothing is sent as you type or in the background. ai.community-access.org explains the service and publishes what it costs to run.

And the editor underneath it

You already know how to use it: every key Notepad and WordPad have had for thirty years does exactly what it always has. What is different is that QUILL Lite answers.

  • F6 lands you in a status bar you can read, cell by cell: line, word count, encoding, line endings.
  • F1 anywhere tells you what window you are in and what the control you are on does.
  • Headings and lists announce themselves - "Heading 2, Installing", "Bulleted list, 5 items" - which no Windows edit control can tell your screen reader on its own.
  • Spell check spells the word out, letter by letter or in the phonetic alphabet, because "receive" and "recieve" sound identical.
  • Plain text, Markdown, HTML and rich text, each with its own formatting keys, and your file comes back byte for byte as it went in.
  • Nine spoken lessons (Ctrl+Alt+F1), each step saying what to press and what you should hear when it worked.
  • Every key is yours to change, with a Keyboard Manager that names the owner of a key before it takes it.
