Quill Converter: any sound or video, into the format you need.

Queue files or whole folders, choose what you want them to become, and press Convert. Quill Converter turns sound into sound, video into sound and video into video, fixes what is wrong with a recording on the way through, and lets you hear fifteen seconds of the result before you commit. Free, on your own computer, and built the way a screen reader user would build it.

Version 1.0.0, released 2026-09-28. Windows 10 or later, 64-bit. Two downloads, and everything is inside both: FFmpeg, the mpv playback engine, the web-link downloader and the cover-art library, so nothing downloads the first time you use a feature. The installer sets up the shared QuillVille Runtime if it is not already there and offers "Convert with Quill Converter" on the File Explorer right-click menu. The portable ZIP unpacks anywhere, a USB stick included, and keeps its settings beside QuillConverter.exe. Help > Check for Updates (Ctrl+Alt+U) offers whichever of the two you are running.

What it does

  • 34 formats out, more than 80 in. MP3, M4A, M4B audiobooks, Opus, FLAC, WAV, AC-3, WavPack, AMR phone audio and more, and nine video formats from MP4 to Ogg. Film and broadcast files, DVD VOB files, camcorder MTS, old RealMedia and tracker music all read. Only the formats the bundled FFmpeg can really write are offered.
  • Video, both ways. Take the sound out of a video, or make a new video: same quality, a smaller file for phones, or a new container with nothing re-encoded. Every audio track travels, so a described-audio track is kept, and MKV keeps the subtitles.
  • Effects named for the problem. Clean up speech, make a podcast, pass ACX for an audiobook, bring film dialogue forward, remove hum or background noise, cut long silences, or even out loud and quiet parts for night listening. Custom Effects (Ctrl+E) puts every switch on one page.
  • Hear it first. Preview (Ctrl+P) plays fifteen seconds exactly as the result will sound; Preview Original (Ctrl+Shift+P) plays the same fifteen seconds untouched.
  • Chapters that survive. Keep a file's own chapters, write your own in a text file beside it (lines like "0:00 Welcome"), find them at the pauses, or have one every few minutes, in any format. They land inside every format that can hold them, and in a .cue sheet beside the rest, so nothing is silently lost. The Chapter Workbench (Ctrl+H) lets you hear a book and edit its chapters at the playhead.
  • Join and split. Join into One File (Ctrl+J) makes a folder of chapters into one audiobook with a chapter per file; Split by Chapters (Ctrl+Shift+S) does the reverse, or cuts a long recording every 30 minutes or at its pauses.
  • Tags and cover art. Edit Tags (Ctrl+T) opens Audio Studio's Tag Editor inside Quill Converter: every tag of an MP3, M4A, M4B or MP4 and its cover art, with nothing else to install.
  • Told, not guessed. Progress counted inside each file, not just file by file: spoken every quarter, on a progress bar, and in the status bar and tray tooltip with about how long is left. Stop is immediate and removes the unfinished file. File Properties (Alt+Enter) describes any file, and a Conversion Report (Ctrl+R) says in plain words why anything failed.
  • Help from a person. Help > Get Help from Support (Ctrl+Alt+F2) writes an email to our help desk in your own mail program, with the details support asks for already filled in.
