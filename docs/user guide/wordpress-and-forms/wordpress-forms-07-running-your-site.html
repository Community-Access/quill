Chapter 7: Running your site

What this chapter covers

Beyond writing, someone has to look after a site: its health, its settings, its people, its plugins and its look. This chapter covers everything QUILL offers for that, in Tools > WordPress and its Manage, Design, Plugin Extras and Your Sites submenus. Most of it needs the Administrator role; the windows only show what your role can use. Everything that changes the site says what it will change, and asks first.

QUILL asks the site first

Sites differ. One runs the newest WordPress with a block theme, another an older version with a classic theme and a security plugin. QUILL reads what each site offers, once a session, before it offers anything. A command your site does not have, or your role cannot use, says why in one sentence, such as “Community Radio does not offer this. It may be turned off, or the site may need a newer WordPress.” Nothing is sent.

Site Check

Tools > WordPress > Site Check lists what the site says about its own health, critical things first: the WordPress version, HTTPS, the time zone, readable addresses, and every Site Health test the site lists, each read as one line.

Site Check also names the plugins QUILL recognises, such as The Events Calendar, WooCommerce, Yoast SEO, Rank Math and QUILL Accessible Forms.

If the authorization header test fails, Site Check explains that application passwords cannot work on that site until the host fixes it. That one line has saved many people an afternoon.

Site Settings

Tools > WordPress > Site Settings… shows the site’s title, tagline, language, time zone, date format, front page, posts page, site icon and more. Choose one and press Change. QUILL tells you the old value and the new one, and asks before it saves.

The site language matters more than it looks: it decides which voice a visitor’s screen reader uses for the whole site.

Users and passwords

These are in Tools > WordPress > Manage:

  • Users… lists everyone with an account and their role. Administrators can add a user, edit one, change a role and delete one. QUILL makes a strong password for a new user and never shows it; they choose their own with “Lost your password?” on the sign-in page. Deleting asks who gets the person’s posts first, so no writing is lost. My Profile changes your own name, biography and website.
  • Application Passwords… lists every app and computer signed in to the site with an application password, and marks this computer’s. Revoke shuts off an old laptop or an app you no longer use.
  • Sign Out This Site… revokes QUILL’s own password on the site, as Chapter 1 describes.

Plugins, themes and fonts

Also in Tools > WordPress > Manage:

  • Plugins… lists what is installed and whether each is active, and says what the well-known accessibility plugins do. You can activate, deactivate, install and delete plugins. A new plugin is installed by its WordPress.org name, and switched off at first, so it cannot surprise you.
  • Themes… lists the themes and what the active one supports. WordPress does not let apps switch themes, so Open in Dashboard takes you to the right page in your browser.
  • Fonts… describes each font in words, such as “serif, suits body text”, and installs more from the site’s font collections.

Patterns, widgets and the shape of the site

Also in Tools > WordPress > Manage:

  • Synced Patterns… lists the site’s reusable patterns and where each one is used. You can edit, rename, unsync (each use becomes ordinary blocks), delete an unused one, or make a new one from your selection. When your site sorts its patterns into categories, it asks which category to show first.
  • Widgets… lists every widget by the area the theme shows it in, such as the sidebar or the footer. Add Text…, Edit…, Move to Area…, or Remove…, which sends it to Inactive Widgets rather than deleting it.
  • Site Map… answers “where does this page live?”: every page under its parent, then every post under its categories, with the menus that link to each.
  • Template Outline lists a block theme’s templates and their blocks, starting with the one your open page uses. It explains the things on a page that are not in the page, such as a sign-up box the theme adds to every post.

Changing many pages at once

Also in Tools > WordPress > Manage:

  • Bulk Edit… lists your posts or pages. Select the ones you want, then change their status, author, comments setting or parent page, or add a category or tag. QUILL asks once, naming how many and which site, and Undo Bulk Edit puts them back.
  • Page Order… shows your pages as a tree. Moving one sets its parent and its place, which is how a site’s page structure is built.

The site’s look

These are in Tools > WordPress > Design, for sites with a block theme. Everything else about the look stays in WordPress’s Site Editor.

  • Style Variations… lists the ready-made looks your theme comes with. Each one is listed by name with how many of its colour pairings are too faint for body text, and Details reads out its whole Palette Report. Apply makes one the site’s look. QUILL keeps the look you had, on this computer, first, so Put Back the Previous Look brings it back.
  • Site Styles… lists your site’s colours and font sizes, by name. Each colour says how it reads on the page background, such as “Accent (dark green), #1a4d2e, 7.2 to 1 on the page background, passes”. Choose Change, type the new colour or size, and QUILL tells you the new contrast before anything is saved.

And in Tools > WordPress > Manage:

  • Previous Looks… lists your site’s earlier styles, dated, each with its colours. Go Back to This Look… restores one, and the look you had is kept, so you can change your mind.
  • Download a Copy of the Site’s Design… saves a zip of your block theme with your changes, a good thing to have before you try something new. On a classic theme it says only a block theme can.
  • Space Used says how much room your uploads, themes, plugins, WordPress itself and the database take, in plain words.

Plugins QUILL works with

QUILL needs no plugin for anything but forms. But where your site has one of these, QUILL makes use of it. The commands are in Tools > WordPress > Plugin Extras, and the fields are in Properties and File > WordPress > Plugin Fields….

  • The Events Calendar. Events Coming Up… lists every event from today, with when and where. New Event… makes a draft event and opens it. An event’s start, end and venue show in Properties and can be changed in Plugin Fields.
  • WooCommerce. A product’s price and stock show in Properties and can be changed in Plugin Fields; a price change asks first. Shop Orders… lists the newest orders, to read. QUILL never changes an order, and copies one only after asking, because it holds the customer’s name and address.
  • Yoast SEO and Rank Math. The SEO title and description show in Properties with their length, and in Plugin Fields a line under each keeps the count, telling you when the text goes over what search engines show.

AI Abilities and Ask the Site

WordPress 6.9 lets a site offer actions to AI, called abilities, such as “list upcoming events” or “create a draft”.

  • Tools > WordPress > Manage > AI Abilities… lists each ability your site offers, by name. Every ability is off until you turn it on, one by one, for that site.
  • Tools > WordPress > Manage > Ask the Site… sends your question to the AI you chose in Choose Your AI. If the AI wants to use an ability, QUILL asks you first, every time, and says exactly what it will be given. The answer ends by saying what ran on your site.

Across all your sites

These are in Tools > WordPress > Your Sites, and look at every site you have connected:

  • Coming Up… lists everything scheduled on all your sites, soonest first.
  • What Changed on My Sites… lists new comments, posts other people published, and health warnings since you last looked, across every site. It only appears when you ask; QUILL never interrupts you with it. Run it again and it says nothing is new.
