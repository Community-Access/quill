# WordPress and Forms in QUILL and QUILL Lite

This is the manual for two things QUILL and QUILL Lite share: looking after
WordPress websites, and writing accessible forms. Everything in it works the
same way in both editors. It is a book of its own, beside each editor's user
guide, because there is a lot to say and you may only need part of it.

## How to read this manual

- **New to it?** Read Chapters 1 and 2, in that order. They take you from
  nothing to a published post. Then dip into the rest as you need it.
- **Only want forms?** Start at Chapter 8. Chapter 9 covers putting a form on
  a site, which needs Chapter 1's connection first.
- **Looking something up?** Chapter 13 describes every window, field and
  button, and Appendix A lists every WordPress feature QUILL uses and what
  your site needs for each.
- **Something went wrong?** Chapter 11 has the sentences QUILL says when
  something fails, and what to do about each.

Every chapter starts with a short "what this chapter covers" and ends with
links to the chapters either side and back to these contents. Headings are
real headings, so your screen reader's heading keys move through each
chapter.

Throughout the manual, "Community Radio" stands for your own site's name,
which QUILL says in its place. The guided lessons in **Help > Tutorials**,
in both editors, walk through the commonest tasks on a test site, and tell
you what you should hear at each step.

## Keys at a glance

- **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Grave**, then a letter: the WordPress keys. Grave is the
  key below Escape. O opens a page or post, P publishes, M opens the Media
  Library, S switches site, W opens a page by its web address, D saves a
  draft to the site. Escape leaves without doing anything.
- **Ctrl+S** saves on your computer only. It never sends anything to a site.
- **Ctrl+Shift+P** opens the Command Palette, which finds every WordPress and
  Forms command by name.
- **Ctrl+Z** takes back any change a WordPress or Forms command made to your
  document.
- **F1** on any control says what it is for.

The Forms commands have no keys of their own: use the menus or the Command
Palette. [Chapter 12](wordpress-forms-12-keys.html) has every key.
