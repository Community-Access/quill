Chapter 6: Comments, categories, tags, people and menus

What this chapter covers

A site grows things around your writing: the comments visitors leave, the categories and tags that sort your posts, the people who write with you, and the menus that help visitors find your pages. You look after all of them in the WordPress Workbench.

In this chapter you will moderate and answer comments, tidy categories and tags, add and change the people on your site, and build a menu, adding items and putting them where they belong.

By the end your comments will be answered, your posts sorted, your team set up, and every new page reachable from your menus. Chapter 2 explains how the Workbench moves, if you have not read it yet.

Comments

Tools > WordPress > Comments… opens the Workbench at the comments waiting for you to approve. Each site’s Comments has a place for each of WordPress’s four lists: Waiting, Approved, Spam and Trash. Needs My Attention > Comments Waiting gathers the waiting ones from every site, and Held as Spam the ones the sites doubted.

Each row gives the comment’s author, the post it is on and when it came. Press Enter to read it in full in the details pane.

On a comment:

  • Item > Approve, or Not Spam (Ctrl+Shift+A) publishes a waiting comment, or releases one from Spam.
  • Item > Unapprove (Ctrl+Shift+U) takes an approved comment off the site, so it waits again.
  • Item > Spam (Ctrl+Shift+S) marks it as spam. The site’s spam filter learns from it.
  • Item > Reply… (Ctrl+Shift+M) answers the comment, published under your name.
  • Item > Not Spam, and Allow This Sender (Ctrl+Alt+W) approves it, and says whether that person’s next comments will go straight through.
  • Delete moves it to the Trash, and Item > Restore from Trash (Ctrl+Shift+R) brings it back.
  • Alt+Enter opens its Properties, where you can correct its text, such as a phone number somebody posted by mistake, or its date. The sheet also shows the IP address it came from.

Many at once. Select several comments and approve, spam or trash them in one go. You hear how each one went, so one refusal does not stop the rest, and Ctrl+Z puts back only the ones that changed.

For the whole site, the Site menu has:

  • Site > Comment Counts (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+C): how many wait, are approved, are spam and are in the Trash;
  • Site > Empty Spam… (Ctrl+Alt+E): delete every comment held as spam, for good, after you confirm;
  • Site > Empty Comment Trash… (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+E): the same for the Trash.

On a post in the Workbench, Item > Show Its Comments (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+M) lists just that post’s comments. In a comment list, the filter takes comment_type: as well as words, and View > Columns… can add the commenter’s website, IP address, the comment’s type and the comment it replies to.

The first time you use a site in a session, QUILL says how many comments are waiting, such as “Community Radio: 3 comments waiting.”

A walk-through: answering a question in the comments

  1. Choose Tools > WordPress > Comments…. The Workbench opens on the comments waiting.
  2. Tab to the list, arrow to the comment, and press Enter to read it.
  3. Press Ctrl+Shift+A to approve it, so it shows on the site.
  4. Press Ctrl+Shift+M, type your answer and press OK.
  5. In the tree, move to Spam and check nothing real has been caught there. If something has, press Ctrl+Shift+A on it.

The guided lesson on comments, in Help > Tutorials, does the same on a test site.

Categories and tags

Categories are the broad shelves of a blog, such as News and Events. Tags are the small labels, such as “volunteers” or “spring fair”. Only posts have them.

Tools > WordPress > Categories and Tags… opens the Workbench at the site’s Categories, shown as a tree, each under its parent. Tags is just below it. Each row gives the name, how many posts use it, and its slug. A plugin’s own ways of grouping, such as event categories or genres, are under More Taxonomies.

On a category or tag:

  • Enter, or Show Its Posts, lists the posts filed there.
  • Alt+Enter changes its name, slug, description and parent. A new name shows on every post that has it.
  • Item > Merge Into… (Ctrl+Shift+G) moves every post across to another and then deletes the empty one, in one step.
  • Item > Convert to Category or Tag… (Ctrl+Shift+K) turns a tag into a category, or the other way round, and keeps its posts.
  • File > New Here… (Ctrl+N) makes a new one.
  • Delete Permanently… removes it, after a question that says how many posts use it. The posts stay.
  • Copy to Site… copies it to another of your sites.

Choosing categories and tags for one post is in its Properties (Chapter 3). To rename, split or merge a term across all your posts and pages, see By Name in Chapter 9.

People

Tools > WordPress > Manage > Users… opens the Workbench at the site’s People > Users. Each row gives the person’s name, username, role and email. You need to be an Administrator to change anyone but yourself.

Adding a person. In Users, press Ctrl+N. Add a Person is one sheet: username, email, first and last name, website, language and role. QUILL makes a strong password and never shows it. WordPress sends no welcome email when a person is added this way, and the preview says so, so tell them yourself. They choose their own password with “Lost your password?” on the sign-in page.

On a person:

  • Show Their Posts lists what they wrote.
  • Change Role… gives them another role, after a preview.
  • Alt+Enter changes their name, email, website, biography and language, and shows how many posts they have written.
  • Their Application Passwords… lists the apps and computers signed in as them, with Revoke after you confirm.
  • Item > Send Password Reset (Ctrl+Shift+W) opens their wp-admin screen, where WordPress sends the reset email.
  • Set New Password… gives them a new password you type. QUILL never says it or keeps it.
  • Sign Out Everywhere… opens their wp-admin screen, where Log Out Everywhere is one button. WordPress keeps sign-ins out of its REST API.
  • Remove… takes them off the site. It asks who gets their posts first, and the preview counts them, so no writing is lost.
  • Who Has Never Signed In lists the people with no sign-in on record. WordPress itself does not keep sign-in times where QUILL can read them, so this works only when a plugin on the site records the last sign-in and shares it (as last_login). On any other site it says it cannot tell, rather than calling everyone new.

Select several people to change all their roles, or remove them, in one go.

QUILL will never take away your own Administrator role, or remove the last Administrator on a site, whatever you select.

Many people at once. Invite from a Spreadsheet…, on a person’s context menu, reads a CSV file with columns for email, role and name. The preview lists every row it will add and every row it leaves out, and why. Then it adds each person and says how each went.

Your own profile. Site > Your Profile… (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+P) changes your own name, email, website, biography and language on the site.

Application passwords. People > Application Passwords lists the passwords on your own account, with when each was made and last used. Ctrl+N makes a new one and shows it once, so copy it then. Delete revokes one. The one QUILL itself signs in with is marked, so you do not revoke it by accident.

Site > Roles and What They May Do (Ctrl+Alt+R) explains each role on the site in plain words.

WordPress cannot change what a role may do by itself; that takes a role plugin. When the site’s role plugin shares its roles with QUILL (QUILL looks for the Members plugin’s members/v1 routes), the same command lists the site’s own roles instead, each with how many capabilities it has:

  • Choose a role to change what it may do. Its capabilities are listed one a line; delete a line to take one away, or add a line to give one. The preview says what the role gains and loses, and Ctrl+Z puts it back.
  • Choose New Role… to make one. Give it a name, then its capabilities, one a line. Ctrl+Z takes the new role away again.

A change to a role reaches everyone who has it at once, so read the preview carefully. On a site without such a plugin, change roles in wp-admin, with Members or User Role Editor.

Menus

A menu is the list of links in your site’s header or footer that visitors use to find your pages. WordPress does not add a new page to a menu by itself, unless you ask it to, which is why QUILL asks “Add it to a menu?” the first time you publish a page.

Tools > WordPress > Menus… opens the Workbench at the site’s Menus and Navigation. Each row is a menu, with where the theme shows it and how many items it has.

Adding an item to a menu

Use this to add a page, a post, a category, a tag, an item of any other kind your site shows in menus, or a link to any address.

  1. Arrow to the menu in Menus and Navigation.
  2. Press Ctrl+N and choose A new item in the menu, which is first. Or press the Applications key and choose Add Menu Item….
  3. Under Kind of item, choose what the item links to, such as Pages, Posts, Categories, Tags, one of your site’s own kinds, or Custom Links.
  4. For anything but a custom link, type words from its name and press Enter, or leave the box empty to list them all, as View All does in wp-admin. Only published items are listed. For a kind with an archive page, such as Events, the archive is offered first.
  5. Choose the one you want. Its name becomes the item’s name.
  6. Under Under, choose where it goes: Top level of the menu, or the item it should sit under, as a dropdown.
  7. Under Position, choose First, or After one of the items already there. The end is chosen for you.
  8. Read the preview. It says what is added, where, as a place such as “3 of 7”, and how many items move down to make room. Press Add.

For a custom link, QUILL asks for the URL and then the Link text, the words the menu shows, and carries on from step 6.

Before it writes, QUILL reads the menu again. If somebody changed it in the meantime, nothing is sent, and you are told. Afterwards you hear what was added. Ctrl+Z takes the item out and puts the others back where they were.

Adding new pages automatically

WordPress can add each new top-level page to a menu by itself, the moment it is published.

  1. Arrow to the menu in Menus and Navigation.
  2. Press the Applications key and choose Add New Pages Automatically….
  3. The preview says whether it is on or off now, and what changes. Press Turn On, or Turn Off.

The same setting is a check box on the menu’s Properties (Alt+Enter), labelled “Automatically add new top-level pages to this menu”, beside the menu’s name and description. Check it or uncheck it and press OK.

Either way, QUILL checks the setting has not changed on the site since you looked, reads it back after saving, and Ctrl+Z turns it back. Pages already in the menu stay where they are when you turn it off.

More on a menu

  • File > New Here… (Ctrl+N) on a menu asks whether you want a new item in it or A new menu. With no menu selected, it makes a menu.
  • Alt+Enter changes the menu’s name and description, and shows where the theme displays it.
  • Earlier Versions… lists the states of the menu QUILL has seen. A classic menu keeps no history in WordPress, so QUILL keeps its own each time it reads one. Choose one to bring back the items removed since, after a preview. Ctrl+Z takes them out again.
  • Default Navigation Menu…, on a block theme, says which Navigation menu the theme shows where none is chosen. On a site with none, WordPress makes one, so QUILL asks first.
  • Copy to Site… copies the menu to another of your sites.
  • Delete Permanently… (Shift+Delete) deletes the menu. The pages it linked to are not touched.

Moving items around in a menu

The Menus window rearranges a menu’s items. To open it, select a menu in the Workbench’s Menus and Navigation, press the Applications key, and choose Arrange Items…. It also opens when you answer Yes, open Menus to the “Add it to a menu?” question, with the new page already added at the end of the menu you choose. Other Menu… in it opens another menu on the same site.

Each row says the item, what kind of thing it links to, its level and its place, such as “Sessions, page, level 1, 3 of 7”.

  • Move Up and Move Down change its place. Each press says the new one.
  • Move In puts it under the item above it, as a dropdown. Move Out takes it back up a level.
  • Rename… changes what the item says.
  • Remove… takes it out of the menu. The page itself is not touched.
  • Check Menu finds items with no name, and links to pages that are drafts or gone.
  • Locations… chooses where the theme shows the menu, such as the header or the footer.

Sites with a block theme keep their menus in Navigation blocks. Those open in the same window and work the same way. Tools > WordPress > Menu History… lists earlier versions of a menu, and can make a Navigation block’s menu exactly as it was.

A walk-through: putting a new page in the menu

  1. Publish the new page. QUILL asks “Add it to a menu?”.
  2. Choose Yes, open Menus, and choose the menu. The Menus window opens with the page added at the end.
  3. Press Move Up until it sits where you want it.
  4. If it belongs under another item, such as About, press Move In.
  5. Press Check Menu to make sure nothing points to a draft.
  6. Close the window. The change is already on the site.

If you would rather every new top-level page joined the menu by itself, turn on Add New Pages Automatically, as above.
