Chapter 10: Shop, events, SEO, memberships and redirects

What this chapter covers

Many sites do more than publish. They sell things, list events, chase search engines, run memberships, or send old addresses to new ones. Each of those needs a plugin, and QUILL works with the commonest: WooCommerce, The Events Calendar, Yoast SEO, Paid Memberships Pro and Redirection.

This chapter has a section for each. Skip the ones your site does not have. You will look after products, orders and customers, events and venues, each page’s search appearance, members and their levels, and redirects.

By the end you can run the parts of your site that plugins add, from the same Workbench as everything else. Forms plugins have chapters of their own, from Chapter 11.

How these work

Each plugin gets its own places in a site’s branch of the Workbench, but only when the site has that plugin. A site without it simply has no such place, or says so in one sentence.

Every change shows you a preview first. Before anything is sent, QUILL reads the thing again from the site, and if somebody changed it in the meantime, nothing is sent and you are told which part changed. Press F5 and try again. After the save, QUILL checks what the site kept, and tells you if it did not keep something.

Ctrl+Z undoes a change wherever putting the old values back really restores it. When something cannot be undone, such as a shipping zone deleted for good, the preview says so. A command your role cannot use is dimmed, and F1 on it says which role could.

In the editor, File > WordPress > Plugin Fields… changes the fields these plugins add to the page you are writing, such as an event’s date or a product’s price, and Properties shows them.

A shop: WooCommerce

Each site with WooCommerce has a Shop. Its list is your products, with price, stock and status. Under it are Draft Products, On Sale, Out of Stock, Shop Orders, Customers, Coupons, Shop Reports, Store Settings, Product Reviews, Shipping Zones, Tax Rates, Tax Classes, Attributes, Brands and Webhooks.

Products

  • File > New Here… (Ctrl+N) on Shop makes a new product, as a draft.
  • Edit Product… changes the regular and sale price, the stock, the status and the categories. Only what you changed is sent.
  • Show Its Variations lists a product’s variations, and Edit a Variation… changes one’s price, sale price, stock, or whether it is for sale.
  • Duplicate… makes a copy, and Move to Trash… sends one to WooCommerce’s Trash.

Orders

Shop Orders lists the newest orders. Read in Full reads one as a receipt, without the customer’s email, telephone or billing address.

  • Change Status… moves an order on, such as to Completed. The preview says which email WooCommerce will send the customer.
  • Notes… reads the order’s notes and adds one, kept private or emailed to the customer.
  • Refund… refunds one order. It checks the amount against what is left to refund, warns you whether the payment gateway really returns the money or only the record changes, and sends only when you type the amount exactly. A refund is never part of a batch, and cannot be undone.
  • Open in the Shop’s Dashboard opens the order in wp-admin.

These are shop orders. Membership orders, from Paid Memberships Pro, are a different thing, and each shop order says so.

Customers and coupons

Customers lists the shop’s customers, without their contact details. Customer Details reads one person’s email and addresses from WooCommerce at that moment, and Edit Customer… changes their name and email, their billing address or their shipping address. Both need a role that may see the site’s people.

Coupons: Ctrl+N makes one, Edit Coupon… changes it, and Move to Trash… removes it.

Reports, settings and reviews

  • Shop Reports says the sales, the top sellers and the main totals, each as a sentence with a small table.
  • Store Settings lists every group of WooCommerce’s settings, each in words. Change Setting… offers the choices WooCommerce gives, and previews the old and new value. Settings that are passwords are never read out, shown in a row, or changed here.
  • Product Reviews lists every review with its product, rating, reviewer and status. The reviewer’s email is never shown. Approve, Hold and Mark as Spam work on one review or several, with a report of how each went. Reply… puts your answer under the review, Edit Review… corrects its words or rating, and Delete moves it to WooCommerce’s Trash.

Shipping and tax

  • Shipping Zones lists each zone with the places it covers and its methods. Ctrl+N makes a zone. Locations… takes a list such as country US, state US:AZ, postcode 85001. Methods… lists the zone’s methods: choose one to turn it on or off, change its cost or remove it, or add flat rate, free shipping or local pickup. Rename… and Delete… are there too.
  • Tax Rates: Ctrl+N makes a rate, Edit Tax Rate… changes its name, percentage and where it applies, and Delete… removes one. A deleted rate comes back with Ctrl+Z.
  • Tax Classes: Ctrl+N makes one, and Delete… removes it. Deleting a class takes its rates with it, and the preview warns you.

Attributes, brands and webhooks

  • Attributes lists product attributes such as Size. Terms… adds, renames or deletes an attribute’s values.
  • Brands is there when the site’s WooCommerce has brands, from version 9.6.
  • Webhooks lists what each webhook sends, where to, and whether it is paused. Ctrl+N makes one: give it a name, choose what triggers it, and type the https address it goes to. WooCommerce makes the webhook’s secret itself, and QUILL never reads it or says it. Pause or Activate… stops or starts one, and Delete… removes it for good.

In the editor, Tools > WordPress > Plugin Extras > Shop Orders… lists the newest orders, to read.

Events: The Events Calendar

Each site with The Events Calendar has an Events Calendar, which lists the events coming up. Under it are Past Events, Venues, Organizers, Event Categories and Event Tags.

Each event says its time zone, and the time on your own computer too.

  • File > New Here… (Ctrl+N), or New Event…, makes an event as a draft, in the site’s time zone unless you type another.
  • Edit Event… changes its details, such as its title and times.
  • Events Between Dates… lists the events in a range you give.
  • Categories and Tags… lets you type the names the event should have, separated by commas.
  • Move to Trash… removes one.

Venues and Organizers each have New, Edit and Move to Trash. Event Categories and Event Tags list each one with how many events use it; Ctrl+N makes one, and Rename… and Delete… are on the context menu. Deleting one leaves the events alone.

An event that repeats, from Events Calendar Pro, says so. Its series is changed in wp-admin, because the plugin does not share the rule that makes it repeat.

In the editor, Tools > WordPress > Plugin Extras > Events Coming Up… lists every event from today, and New Event… makes a draft event and opens it.

Search engines: Yoast SEO

Each site with Yoast SEO has an SEO place. It lists the newest posts and pages, with what to look at on each.

  • Search Appearance reads how the item shows in a search result: its title, its address and its description, and where each one gets cut off.
  • Edit SEO… changes the SEO title, the meta description, the focus keyphrase, whether it shows in search results, and the social title, description and image. Anything the site does not share with QUILL, such as the social image on most sites, is named in the preview and left for the Yoast panel in wp-admin.
  • Bulk SEO Editor… opens a window that lists every post and page with how long its SEO title and description are. Press Enter on one to change both, right there in the list. Each is saved on its own, after a preview. It needs Yoast’s manage options right, which administrators and Yoast’s SEO Manager role have.

Site SEO, under SEO, gives the site’s SEO and readability scores in words. Open in Dashboard goes to Yoast’s own screens for everything else.

Rank Math’s title and description are in Properties and Plugin Fields in the editor, with a count that tells you when the text goes over what search engines show. The same goes for Yoast’s.

Memberships: Paid Memberships Pro

Each site with Paid Memberships Pro has Memberships, with Membership Levels, Orders, Subscriptions, Renewals Due and Discount Codes. Card and billing details are never shown. Needs My Attention > Membership Orders lists orders, on every site, that wait on a person: pending, in review, waiting on payment, or failed.

Discount codes. Look Up a Discount Code… reads one code, and New Discount Code… makes one after a preview. Where the site cannot list every code, the list says so and opens the wp-admin screen.

A member. On a person in People > Users:

  • Show Memberships… reads their current and past levels.
  • Check Membership Access… says whether they may see a post or page you choose, as Paid Memberships Pro decides it. Their Properties has an Access page with Check a Post or Page… for the same thing.
  • Change Membership Level… gives them another level.
  • Cancel Membership… cancels one of their levels.

Change and Cancel read the member and their subscriptions again each time. The preview names the subscription, the payment gateway, the amount and the next payment that stops, and says that nothing is refunded and that it cannot be undone. Paid Memberships Pro’s own checks still decide.

The full set needs WordPress 6.9 or later and Paid Memberships Pro 3.9 or later. On an older site, QUILL uses the plugin’s older way in, which does less, and says so where it matters.

Redirects: Redirection

A redirect sends visitors from an old address to a new one, so links in old emails and search results still arrive. Each site with the Redirection plugin has Redirects, listing where each one leads from and to, its code, how many times it was used, and whether it is on.

  • File > New Here… (Ctrl+N), or Add Redirect…, makes one after a preview.
  • Test an Address… follows the redirects from an address you give, and names any chains and loops. Nothing is sent to the site.
  • Change Redirect… changes where it leads, and Turn On or Off does what it says.
  • Delete Redirect… deletes it for good, because the plugin keeps no Trash.

404 Log, under Redirects, lists addresses visitors asked for that were not found. Redirect This Address… sends one somewhere, after a preview.

QUILL also offers a redirect at the moments you need one: when you change a page’s slug in Properties, and when Replace File gives a file a new address (Chapter 5). Say yes, and the old address keeps working.

A podcast

Podcast, in a site’s branch, lists the site’s podcast episodes: its podcast content type if it has one, or else the posts that carry an episode’s player. A site with neither says so. QUILL Cast and QUILL Studio can post episodes to your site (Chapter 5).
