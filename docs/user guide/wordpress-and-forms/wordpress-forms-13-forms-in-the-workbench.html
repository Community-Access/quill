Chapter 13: Forms in the Workbench, and Gravity Forms

What this chapter covers

Once a form is live, the work moves to the site: reading responses, keeping spam out, changing a form’s settings, and sometimes building forms in a plugin other than QUILL Accessible Forms. The Workbench does all of that, for QUILL Accessible Forms and for Gravity Forms.

In this chapter you will look after your forms and their responses in the Workbench, tune QUILL Accessible Forms’ spam checks, and build and change a Gravity Forms form in QUILL. Then you will work with Gravity Forms’ feeds, results, notes and test entries, and copy a form from one plugin to the other.

By the end you can run every form on your site without opening wp-admin.

Where forms live in the Workbench

Each site’s Forms lists its forms, from every form plugin QUILL reads. Under it:

  • Responses: what people sent;
  • Held as Spam: responses the site doubted;
  • Feeds and Results, on a site with Gravity Forms.

Needs My Attention > Form Responses gathers new responses from every site, and Needs My Attention > Held as Spam the doubtful ones, beside the doubtful comments.

On a form, Enter, or Show Its Responses, lists its responses.

QUILL Accessible Forms

Looking after a form

On a QUILL Accessible Forms form:

  • Change Form Status… makes it open and taking responses, closed (shown but not taking responses), or a draft that visitors do not see.
  • Form Settings… reads the form’s settings, such as its button text, checks, scheduling and entry limits, and changes one at a time, after a preview.
  • Saved Views lists the views saved for its responses.
  • Search Responses on the Site… finds responses by words, searched on the site.
  • Copy as a Gravity Form… makes the same form in Gravity Forms, if the site has it. The preview names anything that cannot carry over.
  • Copy to Site… copies it to another of your sites.
  • Delete Form… first says how many responses it has, then asks you to type the form’s name.

Responses

On a response:

  • Read in Full, or Enter, reads every answer in the details pane.
  • Item > Star or Unstar Entry (Ctrl+Alt+F) stars it to come back to.
  • Item > Mark Entry Read or Unread (Ctrl+Alt+U) marks it.
  • Spam (Ctrl+Shift+S) and Not Spam (Ctrl+Shift+A) move it out of or into the responses. They go through QUILL Accessible Forms’ own spam checks, so the site learns from them, and a held response’s emails are sent once you release it. A held response’s row gives the plugin’s reason for holding it.

Several at once works too, with how each one went.

Keeping spam out

QUILL Accessible Forms has spam checks of its own. You change them in the Workbench.

  1. Select the site, or anything on it.
  2. Choose Site > Site Properties… (Alt+Shift+Enter) and go to the Spam page.
  3. Change what you need:
    • each spam check, checked or unchecked;
    • the limits and thresholds, as whole numbers;
    • Use WordPress’s disallowed words and Use WordPress’s moderation words, which hold a form whose answers contain a word from those lists in Settings, Discussion;
    • Ask a question: when a visitor is asked a question before the form is sent, and which kinds of question;
    • the Send Anyway setting. Whoever takes Send Anyway is never turned away: their form is held for you to look at;
    • New OOPSpam key: type a new key to replace the saved one, or leave it empty to keep it. The box is hidden as you type. QUILL never shows, speaks or keeps the saved key, and only says whether one is saved. Remove the saved OOPSpam key takes it away when you save.
  4. Press OK. QUILL reads the settings again first, sends only what you changed, then reads them back and says what the site now holds.

The page also shows this week’s numbers. You need the site’s Manage Options capability, or the plugin’s own Manage Settings. A site still running the plugin under its old name, BITS Accessible Forms, shows its settings here to read, and they are changed in wp-admin until the plugin is updated.

Your own questions. Form Spam Questions…, on the site’s context menu, lists the questions of the site’s own that the plugin asks. Add one, or change or remove one, after a preview.

Week by week. Form Spam by Week, on the same menu, lists each week, newest first: the questions asked, answered, answered wrongly, and let through with Send Anyway. The Watch for form spam questions says each new week’s numbers without being asked (Chapter 8).

What your visitors read

Visitor Words for Forms…, on the site’s context menu, lists what the plugin says to the people filling in your forms: the thank-you after a form is sent, what it says when an answer needs another look or a question was left empty, the words around a text challenge (before it, after a wrong or empty answer, when it is replaced, and the Send Anyway offer), and the confirmation someone sees when their form is held for a person to read.

  1. If the site offers more than one language, choose the language first. Each one has its own words.
  2. Choose the words to change, and type new ones. Keep them plain: no markup, up to 300 characters. The help for the box has a suggestion. To go back to the plugin’s own words, leave the box empty.
  3. Check the preview, which shows what it was and what it becomes, and choose Save Words. QUILL reads the words again first, so a change made in wp-admin meanwhile is never overwritten, then reads them back.

You need the site’s Manage Options capability, or the plugin’s own Manage Settings. A copy of QUILL Accessible Forms that does not offer this yet says so, and its words stay as the plugin wrote them until it is updated.

Gravity Forms

Gravity Forms is a paid plugin many sites already use. On a site that has it, with its REST API turned on, the Workbench lists its forms and responses beside everything else, and you can build its forms in QUILL.

The Gravity Forms commands without a key of their own are also in Item > Gravity Forms.

Building a form

  1. In the Workbench, go to the site’s Forms.
  2. Press Ctrl+N, or choose New Gravity Form… on the context menu. To change a form that is there already, choose Edit Form… on it.
  3. The form window opens. Fill in the Title, Description, Button text and Label placement. Top aligned reads best on a phone and zoomed in.
  4. Confirmation message is what people read after sending the form. Notification to and Notification subject are for the email the site’s administrator gets. If the form sends people to a page instead, or routes its email by an answer, those boxes are dimmed, and Gravity Forms keeps them as they are.
  5. Tab to Fields, the questions in order. Insert, or Add Field…, adds one after the one you are on. Enter, or Edit Field…, changes one. Move Up and Move Down move it. Delete, or Remove Field, takes it out.
  6. Press Review and Save….

A field has a Type, such as text, paragraph, email, phone, number, drop-down, radio buttons, check boxes, date, time, website, name, address, consent, HTML, section or page break. Then its Label, Description, whether it is Required, a Placeholder, a Default value, an Admin label for the entries list, and a CSS class. For a question with choices, Choices takes one choice a line. To store a different value, write the text, a space, an equals sign, a space, then the value. Start a line with an asterisk to have that choice chosen at first.

Condition… sets when the field shows: show or hide it When an earlier Field is, or is not, a Value.

Checks before saving. Before anything is sent, QUILL checks the form, the same way it checks its own forms. It finds questions with no label, or only a placeholder, a required mark that is only an asterisk, empty choices, page breaks and sections that leave a page empty, two questions that sound alike, and conditions that name a missing or later field. Cancel takes you back to the form window, at the first one.

Saving. The preview lists every change. When you change a form, QUILL reads it again first, and if somebody changed it on the site meanwhile, nothing is sent. After saving it reads the form back and tells you of any difference. Ctrl+Z puts the previous form back. A new form goes to Gravity Forms’ Trash instead.

Responses are never read or changed by any of this, and every preview says so.

Fields QUILL does not edit, and conditions with more than one rule, are kept exactly as they were.

Changing one field’s type

Change a Field Type…, on a Gravity Forms form, turns one common field into another: text, paragraph, email, number, drop-down, radio buttons and check boxes. QUILL reads the whole form, previews the change, checks nobody changed the form meanwhile, sends it, and reads it back to make sure only that field changed. Address and list fields are never offered. It needs Full Workbench access, and Gravity Forms’ permission to make forms, which an administrator has.

Responses

On a Gravity Forms response:

  • Item > Entry Notes… (Ctrl+Alt+N) reads the entry’s notes, and adds one.
  • Item > Star or Unstar Entry (Ctrl+Alt+F) and Item > Mark Entry Read or Unread (Ctrl+Alt+U) do what they say, and Ctrl+Z undoes them.
  • Item > Resend Notifications… (Ctrl+Alt+I) works out who each of the entry’s emails goes to, lets you choose which to send, previews the recipients, then sends.
  • Spam and Not Spam work as for any response.

The response’s Properties has Answers and Notes pages to read, and saves Starred and Read.

Filtering responses. On Responses, the Filter Builder (Ctrl+Shift+F) offers the chosen form’s own fields, and the values each allows. The filter is sent to the site, so it searches every response, not only the ones loaded. With a second condition, it asks whether a response must match all of them, or any. The status bar says the site’s own count, such as “12 of the 40 responses that match are shown”.

Feeds and results

  • Feeds lists every form’s add-on feeds, such as Mailchimp, Stripe or Zapier, with the form, the add-on and whether each is on. Activate or Deactivate… and Rename… preview first. Delete Permanently… previews too, and Ctrl+Z makes the feed again. On a form, Show Its Feeds lists only that form’s.
  • Results shows quiz, poll and survey results: a row for each answer with its count and share, and each question as one sentence. On a form, Show Its Results opens it.

Trying a form

Test This Form…, on a form, lets you fill in test values. The site checks them first, and names any field it refuses. If the site is live, the preview says so, and that a practice site would be better. Ctrl+Z moves the test entry to the Trash.

Moving forms

  • Copy to Site… (Ctrl+Shift+Y) copies the whole form to another of your sites, as a new form, or replacing one with the same title. Responses are never read or sent.

  • Copy as a QUILL Accessible Form… makes the same form in QUILL Accessible Forms. Copy as a Gravity Form…, on a QUILL Accessible Forms form, does the reverse. Each preview names what cannot carry over, such as CSS classes, admin labels, conditions with several rules, and kinds of field the other plugin lacks.

  • Copy Old Entries into QAF…, on a Gravity Forms form, brings that form’s entries across too, once you have made its QUILL Accessible Forms copy. Choose the QUILL Accessible Forms form they go into, usually the one with the same title. The preview lists each entry with its date and how many answers it carries, and names what stays behind: uploaded files, payment details, and any field the new form has no question for. Only active entries are copied; spam and trashed ones stay in Gravity Forms, and the Gravity Forms entries themselves are never changed. Ctrl+Z moves the copies to QUILL Accessible Forms’ Trash.

    The plugin on your site has to be able to take entries for this to work. If yours cannot yet, QUILL tells you so in one sentence and nothing is made; update QUILL Accessible Forms and try again.
