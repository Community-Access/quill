Chapter 1: Getting started and adding your sites

What this chapter covers

This chapter gets you from nothing to a connected WordPress site. It explains what QUILL and QUILL Lite do with WordPress and what they leave alone, how to turn the feature on, where the commands live, and how to connect a site: with your browser, by pasting an application password, on WordPress.com, and when you look after more than one site. It ends with what your role on a site means, and how to sign out of a site for good.

What WordPress in QUILL is

If you look after a WordPress website, QUILL can write its pages and posts, open the ones already there, and publish your changes. You do it in the same editor you use for everything else, with Find, spelling, dictation, Read Aloud, bookmarks and all your usual keys. Everything in this manual works the same way in QUILL Lite.

Three promises run through the whole feature:

  • Nothing reaches your site until you send it. Saving with Ctrl+S only ever saves on your computer. Sending is always a separate command, and publishing always asks first.
  • Your site keeps its look. QUILL works on the words and pictures inside your pages. Your theme, its layout and its design stay in WordPress.
  • Nothing is deleted for good without a copy first. Pages go to WordPress’s own Trash. The one thing WordPress cannot put in a Trash, a file in the Media Library, is copied to your computer before it goes.

QUILL talks to your site through WordPress’s own REST API, the same doorway the block editor uses. You do not need to install a plugin for anything in this manual except forms, which need the QUILL Accessible Forms plugin on the site (Chapter 9 explains why).

Turning it on

WordPress and Forms are two separate features, and each can be switched on or off on its own.

In QUILL, both are on in every feature profile except Essential. If you do not see File > WordPress, choose another profile in Profiles and Features…, or switch on WordPress by itself there. Forms works the same way.

In QUILL Lite, WordPress and Forms are areas. Both are on in the Recommended, Everything and Developer profiles, and off in WordPad and Notepad, because neither of those programs ever published to a website or made a form. To turn one on without changing profile, check it in Tools > Customize Features.

Safe Mode turns WordPress off, along with the other features that use the network. If you started QUILL in Safe Mode, every WordPress command says “WordPress is off in Safe Mode. Start the app normally to use it.” Forms is partly different: writing, checking, trying out and saving a form to paper still work in Safe Mode, because they never leave your computer.

Where everything is

The commands sit where a Word user would look for them. Anything about a whole page or post, or about your sites, is in File > WordPress. What you do inside a page joins the ordinary menus. Looking after the site itself is in Tools > WordPress.

Menu What is there
File > WordPress New, Open, the three saves, Properties, versions, Trash, Send and Copy, your sites
Edit > WordPress Next Block, Previous Block, Where Am I, and two link tools
View WordPress Blocks, Preview Blocks, Block Notes, Play Media Here
Insert Blocks, pictures, links to your own pages, notes, icons, Make a Picture
Format > WordPress Block Settings, Image Size, Mark Language
Tools > WordPress Checking, the Media Library, comments, menus, settings, and five submenus

The five submenus of Tools > WordPress are More Media, Design, Plugin Extras, Manage and Your Sites.

Dimmed rows. The commands about the page in front of you are dimmed unless a WordPress page or post is open. If you reach one another way, such as from the Command Palette, QUILL tells you why it cannot run and how to open one, for example “Insert Block works in a WordPress page or post. Open one with File, WordPress.” The site-wide commands, such as Comments or the Media Library, work from anywhere, and ask which site when you have more than one.

The Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P) lists every WordPress command. Type “WordPress”, or part of a command’s name, and press Enter. It is in the Tools menu in both editors.

The WordPress keys. The everyday commands also have a key each: press Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Grave, let go, and press one letter. Chapter 12 has the whole table. You do not need them to get started.

What you need before you connect

You need three things:

  • Your site’s address, for example https://example.org. It must start with https. QUILL never sends a password to a site over plain http.
  • Your WordPress username, or the email address you sign in with.
  • An application password. This is a separate password WordPress makes for one app. It is not the password you sign in with. It is made of 24 letters and numbers, usually shown in groups of four, and you can take it away again at any time from your WordPress profile without changing your real password.

You do not need to make the application password yourself if your site is reasonably new. QUILL can ask your site for one through your browser, and the site hands it straight back.

Connecting a self-hosted site with your browser

This is the easy way, and the one to try first.

  1. Choose File > WordPress > Connect a Site…. The Connect a WordPress Site window opens on Kind of site.
  2. Leave Kind of site on My own WordPress site (self-hosted).
  3. Tab to Site address and type your site’s address, for example https://example.org.
  4. If you like, Tab to Name for this site and type a short name, such as “Community Radio”. QUILL uses it in every list and every confirmation. Leave it empty to use the site’s own title.
  5. Press Get Password with My Browser.

Your browser opens your site’s own Authorize Application page. Sign in there if it asks. The page names QUILL as the app asking for a password. Choose Approve.

What happens next depends on how new your site is:

  • On a newer site, the password comes straight back to QUILL, which checks it, saves it and says “Connected to Community Radio as Editor”, with your own site name and role. You can close the browser tab.
  • On an older site, WordPress shows you the new password on its own page, with a Copy button. Copy it and come back to QUILL, where a small window, Paste the Application Password, is waiting with one field. If the password is already on your clipboard, QUILL has put it in the field for you. Press Connect.

Either way, the password never passes through anything but your browser, your site and your own computer. It is kept in Windows’ own Credential Manager, never in a settings file.

If the site has application passwords turned off, usually by a security plugin, QUILL says so in one sentence: “Application passwords are turned off on Community Radio. Ask the site’s administrator to turn them on.”

Connecting by pasting an application password

Use this when the browser route does not suit you, or when your site’s administrator gave you a password.

  1. In your browser, sign in to your site and open your profile. In the WordPress dashboard that is Users > Profile.
  2. Find the Application Passwords section near the bottom. Type a name such as “QUILL on my laptop” and press Add New Application Password.
  3. WordPress shows the new password once. Copy it.
  4. In QUILL, choose File > WordPress > Connect a Site….
  5. Type the site’s address, then your Username, then paste the password into Application password.
  6. Press Connect.

QUILL checks the connection before it saves anything. It asks the site who you are and what the site is called, so a wrong password is caught here, in words, and not the first time you try to publish. When it works you hear “Connected to” the site’s name, “as” and your role.

If it does not work, the Connect window opens again with a Problem field at the top saying what went wrong, and everything you typed is still there. The commonest sentences are:

  • “Community Radio did not accept the application password. Check it belongs to this account, then reconnect.” The password was made under a different user, or a character was lost when you copied it.
  • “Community Radio does not know that username. Check it and reconnect.”
  • “QUILL only connects to WordPress sites over HTTPS, so your password is never sent in the clear. Check that the address starts with https://.”
  • “A security setting on Community Radio blocked QUILL. Ask the site’s administrator to allow the WordPress REST API and application passwords.”

Chapter 11 has the rest, with what to do about each one.

WordPress.com sites

The Connect window offers a second kind of site, A WordPress.com site. Choosing it greys out the username and password fields, because a WordPress.com site is meant to be connected by signing in to WordPress.com in your browser, on every plan, the free one included, with nothing to install.

That sign-in needs QUILL’s own WordPress.com app registration, and this build does not have it yet. If you choose A WordPress.com site and press Connect, QUILL says: “WordPress.com sign-in is not available in this build yet. If your WordPress.com plan allows plugins, connect with an application password at your site’s own address instead.”

So, for now:

  • On a WordPress.com plan that allows plugins, your site accepts an application password at its own address. Choose My own WordPress site, type your site’s address, and connect with the browser or by pasting, as above. Everything in this manual then works.
  • On a plan without plugins, wait for the build that brings WordPress.com sign-in. When it comes, you will choose A WordPress.com site, press Get Password with My Browser, approve QUILL on WordPress.com, and sign in once. QUILL will keep you signed in from then on.

Looking after more than one site

Connect each site the same way. There is no limit, and you can connect the same site twice with two different accounts, for example as yourself and as a test author.

File > WordPress > Sites… lists every site you have connected. Each row gives the site’s name, its address and your role there. From the list you can:

  • Rename a site, which changes only what QUILL calls it;
  • Verify it, which checks the password still works and says “Community Radio accepts QUILL. You are Editor there.”;
  • Make Default, so New and Open start there;
  • Remove it, which forgets the connection on this computer. Nothing on the site changes, and your local drafts for it stay.

The list also names the application password QUILL is using on each site, so you can match it to the one in your WordPress profile.

File > WordPress > Switch Site… is the quick way to change your default site. It opens a short list, and you hear “Default site:” and the name you chose.

A document always stays with the site it came from. Switching the default never moves an open page to another site. Every confirmation names the site as well as the page, for example “Publish ‘Spring Workshop’ to Community Radio?”, which matters most when two of your sites have pages with the same title. And if you publish a page to a site that is not your default, Check Before Publishing reminds you: “This goes to Community Radio, not your default site, Home Blog.”

What your role on a site means

WordPress gives every account a role: Administrator, Editor, Author, Contributor or Subscriber. QUILL asks the site what your account may do before it opens a window.

  • If your role cannot use a window at all, you hear one sentence, such as “Your account on Community Radio is an Author, which cannot manage users”, and nothing opens.
  • If it can use part of a window, you only get the buttons that will work.
  • If you are a Contributor, you cannot publish yourself. Publish or Update sends your post for review instead, and says so. An Editor or Administrator then publishes it.
  • If the site refuses something anyway, you hear why in words, such as “Your account on Community Radio can only change items you wrote yourself.”

Signing out of a site

Removing a site from Sites only forgets it on this computer. The application password still exists on the site.

Tools > WordPress > Manage > Sign Out This Site… goes further. It revokes the exact application password QUILL is using on that site, so it stops working everywhere, and forgets it on this computer. It asks whether to remove the site from QUILL as well, or keep it so you can connect again later. Your local drafts stay either way.

Use it when you give away a computer, or when you stop looking after a site.

If you used Publish in QUILL before

Older versions of QUILL had a Publish submenu in the File menu. It is gone, and File > WordPress does everything it did. Your saved connections are not lost: QUILL brings them in the first time WordPress loads, with the same saved passwords, and documents you opened from a site stay linked to it.

Learn it with a guided lesson

Help > Tutorials has a WordPress track in both editors, seven lessons that use a real test site. The first, Connect your WordPress site, walks through everything in this chapter step by step and tells you what you should hear at each step.
