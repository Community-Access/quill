Chapter 9: Forms on your site, on the web and on paper

What this chapter covers

A form you have written and checked can go four ways: onto your WordPress site through the QUILL Accessible Forms plugin, onto any website as a page of its own, onto paper in ordinary or large print, and into braille. This chapter covers each one, then reading the responses people send, bringing forms in from other form builders, and the optional AI help for forms. It ends with what is kept private and what Safe Mode turns off.

QUILL Accessible Forms

WordPress has no forms of its own. Something on the site has to show the form to visitors, check and store their answers, send the emails and keep spam out, and QUILL runs on your computer, so it cannot do that for your visitors. That job belongs to a plugin: QUILL Accessible Forms, made by the same people as QUILL, free and MIT-licensed. It used to be called BITS Accessible Forms.

The plugin reads the same form language QUILL writes, so a form you publish from QUILL is exactly the form you checked and filled in yourself. It also has its own accessible builder screens, if someone on your team prefers to work in the dashboard.

To use it, install QUILL Accessible Forms on your site in the usual way, or ask your site’s administrator to. Site Check (Chapter 7) names it when it is there. A site that still runs it under its old name, BITS Accessible Forms, works too.

Forms are published only to QUILL Accessible Forms. If your site does not have it, Save As Web Page, below, still puts your form on the web.

Publishing a form

  1. Open the form, and check it with Tools > Forms > Check This Form…. Publish will not send a form QUILL cannot read properly, such as one with a rule that makes no sense: it says, for example, “Publish Form stopped: this form has 2 problems to fix first.” Check This Form lists them.
  2. Choose Tools > Forms > Publish Form….
  3. If you have more than one site, choose the site that has QUILL Accessible Forms.
  4. The first time, QUILL asks Publish as: Live, so people can fill it in wherever it is placed, or a draft, which waits until you make it live, here or in the plugin.
  5. QUILL asks you to confirm, naming the form and the site.

You hear, for example, “Published Ask us anything on Community Radio, form 12, live. Insert Form Into Page puts it on a page.”

Publishing again updates the same form on the site; the question then says Update. If someone changed the form on the site since you last published, QUILL says so before it replaces it, and keeps the site’s copy on your computer first. It keeps the last ten copies of each form.

If the site does not have the plugin, you hear, for example, “Publish Form needs QUILL Accessible Forms on Community Radio, and the site does not offer it.”

Putting the form on a page

A published form shows wherever you place it.

  1. Open the page from File > WordPress > Open Page or Post….
  2. Put the cursor where the form goes.
  3. Choose Tools > Forms > Insert Form Into Page….
  4. QUILL lists the site’s forms, each with how many responses it has had. The one you published last from this computer is selected. Press Enter.

QUILL inserts the plugin’s form block in an HTML page, or its shortcode in a Markdown page. You hear “Inserted the Ask us anything form”, and that it shows on the page once the page is sent. Then Save Draft to Site or Publish the page as usual.

Test on Site

Tools > Forms > Test on Site opens the page that shows your form, in your web browser, so you can fill it in there with your screen reader, the way a visitor will. You hear “Opened Contact on Community Radio, where the form is.” If no page shows the form yet, QUILL opens the form in QUILL Accessible Forms on the site instead, and reminds you that Insert Form Into Page puts it on a page.

Reading the responses

Tools > Forms > Responses… lists what people sent, newest first, such as “Response 41, 3 November, 2:10 PM, Jane Smith, Event registration”. With a published form open, it goes straight to that form’s responses. Otherwise QUILL asks which site, then which form, each with how many responses it has had.

The buttons:

  • Read, or Enter: one response, a question and its answer on each line, in a window you can read and copy.
  • View…: the list; or grouped under a heading for each day; or grouped for each place the responses gave.
  • Find…: shows only responses containing some words. Leave it empty to show every response again.
  • Mark: marks or unmarks the response you are on, to delete several.
  • Export to CSV…: saves the responses shown as a CSV file, where you choose.
  • Open as Table…: does the same and opens the file afterwards, as a table you can walk cell by cell.
  • Export to Word…: saves the response you are on, or all of those shown, as a Word document.
  • Summarise…: asks your AI for a summary (see below).
  • Delete…: deletes the marked responses, or the one you are on, after telling you how many. They go to the Trash in QUILL Accessible Forms, not away for good.
  • Say New Count: turns on or off the spoken count of new responses.

The new response count. The first time you use a site in a session, QUILL says how many new responses each form has, for example “Community Radio: 4 new responses to Event registration.” The plugin has no “unread” mark, so “new” means new since you last opened Responses in QUILL.

Responses are people’s personal details. QUILL reads them only while the Responses window is open, keeps them in memory, never writes them to a log, and saves them only where you choose.

Save As Web Page

Tools > Forms > Save As Web Page… makes a web page of its own with the form in it, every question properly labelled, to upload anywhere. QUILL asks Send answers to: the https web address the form should send its answers to, such as a form service your organisation already uses. Leave it empty for a form to print or read, which sends nothing.

This is the way to put a form on a site without QUILL Accessible Forms. Such a form has no Responses window in QUILL; its answers go wherever you sent them.

Paper, large print and braille

Every form can be saved three more ways, without a site, even in Safe Mode:

  • Save As Word Document…: a printable form. Each question is a heading, with lines to write on and boxes to check.
  • Save As Large Print…: the same Word form at 18 point and up. You hear, for example, “Saved Ask us anything large print.docx, a large-print Word document, 18 point and up, with 5 questions, lines to write on and boxes to check.”
  • Save As Braille-Ready Text…: one question per block, choices lettered a, b, c, 40 cells to a line. QUILL asks Save as: text you can emboss or read on a display as it is, or, with the braille pack installed, a BRF file translated to UEB contracted braille by QUILL’s own braille translator.

All of these come from the same form, so the paper version always asks what the web version asks. Change the form, save again, and they all agree.

Bringing forms in from other builders

Forms made in other WordPress form plugins are brought in on the site, by QUILL Accessible Forms’ own Import and Export screen, which reads forms from Gravity Forms, WPForms, Contact Form 7, Ninja Forms and Google Forms, and lists anything it could not bring across in an import report rather than dropping it silently. The same screen can export a form back to those builders.

QUILL does not publish to those other plugins, only to QUILL Accessible Forms.

AI help with forms

If you have chosen an AI in Choose Your AI, four commands can help. Each one tells you what it sends before it sends it, and nothing changes until you have read the proposal and said yes. Undo takes it all out in one step.

  • Tools > Forms > Draft a Form…: describe the form in a sentence or two, such as who fills it in and what you need to know. Only that description is sent. The AI writes it as a form, which you read in full, and QUILL then asks whether to use it: in place of the form in front, or saved as a new form.
  • Tools > Forms > Plain Language…: sends your questions’ labels and help text, never anybody’s answers. You get simpler wording, each beside what is there now, to accept or decline as one step.
  • Tools > Forms > Suggest Autofill Hints…: for questions such as name, email, phone and address that have no autofill hint, and for questions with a format rule but no help. It sends their labels and kinds only.
  • Summarise…, a button in Responses: this sends people’s answers, so QUILL asks every time. It says how many responses would go, and that they may include names and email addresses. The summary opens in a window to read; check it against the responses before you rely on it.

Privacy and Safe Mode

  • Payment details and website passwords never pass through QUILL. Payment fields, CAPTCHA keys and the like stay on your website.
  • Nothing is sent without a deliberate Publish Form, Insert Form Into Page, Test on Site, Responses or AI command.
  • In Safe Mode, Publish Form, Insert Form Into Page, Test on Site, Responses and the four AI commands each say they are off: “Publish Form is off in Safe Mode, because it would reach your site or an AI.” Writing, checking, Fill It In Yourself and the four Save As commands still work.

A walk-through: from your computer to your site

  1. Check the form, then Tools > Forms > Publish Form…. Choose Live. Confirm. You hear the form’s number on the site.
  2. File > WordPress > Open Page or Post…, and open your Contact page.
  3. Put the cursor after the first paragraph, and Tools > Forms > Insert Form Into Page…. Choose the form.
  4. File > WordPress > Publish or Update….
  5. Open the form again, and Tools > Forms > Test on Site. Fill it in once in your browser.
  6. Tools > Forms > Responses…. Your test response is at the top. Read it, then Mark it and Delete it.

Payments: Stripe and PayPal

Payment collection requires a QAF WordPress plugin implementing payment contract version 1. An older plugin can still host ordinary forms; paid-form publication will ask you to update it. QUILL configures the form and reads entry status. The site handles checkout and keeps provider credentials.

  1. Open a form and choose Tools > Forms > Insert Payment Field…. Enter a positive amount, #id of a total or amount field, or donation. Donation minimum and maximum are optional. One payment field is allowed.
  2. Choose Form Payments Setup…. Select Stripe, PayPal, or both in your preferred order, currency, payment/donation mode and automatic/manual capture. Keep Test mode checked. Saving changes only the document, as one undo step.
  3. In your site’s WordPress administration, configure provider sandbox credentials and verified webhooks. QUILL never asks for these secrets.
  4. Choose Verify Payment Setup… and confirm the site. This reads the site’s readiness; it does not conduct a test charge. Then Publish Form. Both form and site must agree on test mode, and every selected provider and webhook must be ready. Publication status “Live” means the form is visible; it is separate from payment test/live mode.
  5. Place the form on a published page, then use Test on Site. QUILL checks the published configuration and refuses a live-payment checkout. With Stripe test keys, use 4242 4242 4242 4242, a future expiry and any three-digit CVC in Stripe’s hosted field. Use a sandbox buyer for PayPal. Never use real card details for testing. See Stripe’s testing guide and PayPal’s Orders integration.
  6. Open Responses. Payment status, amount, currency and provider are part of each response row. Read includes the transaction and receipt text. Refresh Payment Status confirms the site and reloads its recorded state. CSV and Word exports include the available payment metadata. An absent status is unknown, not proof of payment; an authorization is not a capture.

Only after successful sandbox and accessibility checks should the site administrator configure live credentials and you deliberately clear Test mode and republish. QUILL asks you to confirm real-money mode. Refunds and manual capture take place in the provider dashboard. Status depends on the site’s webhook processing. Fill It In Yourself and saved static web pages do not process payments. Safe Mode blocks payment network operations while allowing local authoring. The same commands and behavior are available in QUILL Lite.
