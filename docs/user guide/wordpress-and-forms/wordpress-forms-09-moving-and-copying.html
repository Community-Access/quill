Chapter 9: Moving and copying content

What this chapter covers

Sooner or later you need your content somewhere else: on a new host, on a second site, in a spreadsheet, or safe on your own computer before a big change. Or you need the same change on many pages, or on all your sites. This chapter is about all of that.

You will export a site as a WordPress export file and import one as drafts, copy posts and more to another of your sites, compare two sites, and take a snapshot and put it back. Then you will change a name everywhere it is used, make your sites agree with each other, and move lists in and out of spreadsheets.

By the end you can move content between sites with a preview at every step, and nothing published by surprise.

Everything here is in the WordPress Workbench. Chapter 2 explains how it moves.

Exporting a site as a WXR file

A WXR file is WordPress’s own export format, the file wp-admin makes under Tools > Export. Any WordPress site can import it. Use it when you move to a new host, or want a copy of your writing that is not tied to QUILL.

  1. In the Workbench, select the site you want, or anything on it.
  2. Choose Tools > WXR Files > Export Site as WXR…. The keys are Alt+T, then W, then E.
  3. Under Export, choose what goes in the file:
    • Everything: posts, pages, patterns, other types, files, menus, comments;
    • Posts only;
    • Pages only;
    • Posts and pages in a date range…, which asks for a From and a To day, written as year-month-day such as 2026-01-31. Leave From empty to start at the first post, or To empty to end today. Both days are included;
    • Posts in one category…, which lists the site’s categories, each with how many posts it has.
  4. Choose where to save the file. QUILL adds .xml if you leave it off.

QUILL reads the site and writes the file on this computer. A part of the site takes only the people, categories, tags and files its posts use, the same as WordPress’s own exporter. Comments and menus go only with Everything.

When it is done you hear how many of each kind went in, such as posts, pages, authors, terms and attachments, and whether the finished file passed its check. QUILL reads the file back to make sure it is a proper WXR file, such as “community-radio.xml is a well-formed WXR 1.2 file with 181 items.” If it does not pass, the file is kept so you can look at it.

A report then opens. It names the site and what you chose, what went in, the result of the check, and then what was left out and why. Some things only WordPress’s own exporter can carry, such as custom fields the site keeps private, comment, term and user details, post passwords, and plugin settings. Lists and groups in custom fields are written as text.

The file holds the address of each picture and file, not the files themselves, the same as a WXR from wp-admin. Keep the old site running until the new one has imported them.

Importing a WXR file as drafts

This brings content from any WordPress site into one of yours. Everything arrives as a draft, so nothing is published until you say so.

  1. In the Workbench, select the site you are importing into, or anything on it.
  2. Choose Tools > WXR Files > Import WXR as Drafts… (Alt+T, then W, then I).
  3. Choose the WXR file.

QUILL reads the file on this computer and matches it against your site:

  • People. Each author in the file is matched to a person on your site by their username, then by their email. If some have no match, QUILL asks Give unmatched authors’ items to: and lists your site’s people, with you chosen. The question names who did not match.
  • Categories, tags and other groupings are matched by their slug, and made where missing. Groupings your site does not have are left out, and named.
  • Posts, pages, synced patterns and your site’s own kinds come in, if your site has that kind. Menu items, and kinds your site lacks, are left out and counted.

The preview opens. It names the file and the site it came from, says that everything is made as a draft and keeps its original date, and lists every author match, every new category or tag, and every item, one line each. Then what is left out. Press Import as Drafts.

Pictures and files. If the export names files, QUILL asks about them next, under Files:

  • Download the files from the old site’s address and upload them to yours;
  • Skip the files; the text keeps pointing at the old site, which is chosen for you.

Downloading asks the named site for each file by the address in the export, over HTTPS, up to 64 MB each, sending no password. It names every host it will ask. Choose it only if you trust those addresses.

QUILL then makes each item as a draft. It points pictures and files at the uploaded copies, links between the imported items at their new drafts, and the old site’s address at yours. You hear, for example, “Imported 40 of 42 items from export.xml into Community Radio as drafts, with 18 files.” A report says what happened to each category, file and item.

Changed your mind? Ctrl+Z moves the drafts it made to the Trash. Uploaded files and new categories and tags stay, and the report says so.

Then open the drafts, read them over, and publish the ones you want.

Copying to another of your sites

Item > Copy to Site… (Ctrl+Shift+Y) copies what you have selected to another of your sites: posts, pages, media, menus, categories and tags, synced patterns and forms.

  1. Select what to copy in any list.
  2. Press Ctrl+Shift+Y and choose the site to copy to.
  3. Read the preview. It says what will be made and what replaced, each picture to upload, and anything left out.
  4. Press Copy.

QUILL matches authors, categories, tags and menus by name, uploads the pictures a post needs or reuses ones already there, and rewrites links that point at the first site. New items are drafts on the other site. If anything did not copy, a report says what and why.

In the editor, File > WordPress > Send and Copy > Publish to Another Site… does the same for the page you are writing (Chapter 3).

Comparing two sites

Site > Compare Sites… (Ctrl+Alt+K) asks for another of your sites, then reports what differs between the two: posts, pages, synced patterns, categories, tags and menus, each by its slug. It also says what it does not compare. Nothing is changed.

Snapshots

A snapshot is a copy of a site’s content on this computer. Take one before a big change, and you can set things back if it goes wrong.

Taking a snapshot.

  1. Select the site, or anything on it.
  2. Choose Site > Take a Snapshot… (Ctrl+Alt+S).
  3. Under Save, choose Everything but the media files, or Everything, with the media files too.

It saves every post, page, synced pattern, category, tag, menu, setting and media record. People, passwords, plugins, themes, revisions and comments are never saved. You hear what was saved, where, and what was left out.

Putting a site back.

  1. Choose Site > Put Back from a Snapshot… (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+S).
  2. Under Snapshot, choose one. The newest is first.
  3. The preview lists every change. Press Put Back.

Posts, pages, synced patterns, categories and tags changed since the snapshot are set back to it. WordPress keeps a revision of each post and page it changes, so even this can be undone from Previous Versions. Ones deleted since are made again as drafts, at new addresses. Menus, settings, people, plugins and media files are not put back.

If the snapshot came from the Practice Site and you are putting it on a real site, QUILL asks you by name first.

A snapshot is QUILL’s own format. For a copy any WordPress can read, export a WXR file instead.

Changing a name everywhere it is used: By Name

Each site’s By Name branch lists the values your content uses, a place for each kind:

  • Every Term: every category, tag and other grouping;
  • Field Values: the values in custom fields;
  • Authors;
  • Media Usage: every file, and where it is used;
  • Patterns Used;
  • Links: every address your pages link to;
  • Blocks and Shortcodes;
  • Forms Used;
  • Template Parts.

Enter on a row says Where It Is Used. The context menu also has:

  • Rename Everywhere…: a new name or value, wherever it is used;
  • Split…: move the items you choose to a new term, value or person, and leave the rest;
  • Merge Into…: move every item to another term or value, and delete the merged term;
  • Reassign…: move every item to another term or person, and keep this one;
  • Replace Everywhere…: another file, pattern, link, shortcode or form, in every page that uses this one;
  • Find Items Missing This Field, on a custom field: the posts and pages with no value.

Each is previewed item by item. Every item is read again before it is sent, and one that changed since the list was made is left alone. You hear how each item went, and each goes in Change History. Ctrl+Z undoes it where putting the old value back truly restores it. A merge deletes a term, so it says it is final.

A template part’s title can be renamed here, but not its slug, because templates find a part by its slug.

Across Sites

Go > Across Sites (Ctrl+0) shows things your sites share, side by side: Tags and Categories, Plugins, Themes, People, Settings, Menus, Synced Patterns and Application Passwords. Each row says the versions in use and which sites are missing it.

Site > Across Sites Scope… (Ctrl+Alt+Y) chooses every site, or one Site Set. Site > Site Sets… (Ctrl+Alt+T) names a set of your sites, such as “Branch sites”.

On a row:

  • Make Them the Same… asks which version wins, previews every site’s change, and says how each went. It covers categories and tags, plugins (on or off), settings, people’s roles, synced patterns, menus and themes. WordPress cannot switch a theme from outside, so for a theme it opens each site’s Themes screen in your browser. A copied menu is final.
  • Install on Sites Missing It… installs a plugin from WordPress.org on every site that lacks it, after a preview. For a theme, it opens each of those sites’ Add New screen.
  • Promote to Network… activates a plugin for every site of a multisite network. It is only offered where the site says the plugin can be, and WordPress refuses it unless you are a super admin.
  • Revoke Everywhere…, on an application password, revokes it on every site that has it, after a preview. It cannot be undone.
  • Compare Global Styles shows each site’s colours and font sizes, and which differ.

On Plugins, the rows also carry what WordPress.org says of each plugin, such as its rating and a warning for one that is closed or abandoned, once you have agreed to ask WordPress.org in that window.

Fleet, under Across Sites, has a row for each site, with columns for its health, backups, security and updates. Each one says known, unknown, unavailable or stale. If a site stops answering, its last answer stays, with when it came. The Fleet only reads.

Lists in and out

  • Edit > Export List… (Ctrl+Shift+X) saves the list you are in as an Excel file, a CSV file or a Markdown table.

  • Edit > Import Posts from a Spreadsheet… (Ctrl+Shift+I) makes posts or pages from an Excel or CSV file. It matches the columns to WordPress’s fields and shows you the match first. Rows marked published or scheduled come in as drafts, and you hear how each row went.

  • Edit > Import a Folder of Documents… (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+I) makes a draft from each Markdown, text or Word file in a folder, previewed file by file.

  • Tools > Plain-Language Batch… (Ctrl+Alt+B) lets you write a change in words, such as move all drafts by Sam to pending on Radio, add tag 2026 to posts in category Events on every site, or remove tag Old from all published posts by Ann. QUILL finds the exact items you meant. A name that matches nothing is said, never guessed. Then it gives you a checklist of the items. Uncheck anything that should not change, then send. It does not use AI.

    Any of these can say how old: older than a year, older than six months, newer than two weeks, before March 2024 or after 2023, all by the date of the post or page. To clear things out, say move every draft older than a year to Trash or trash all drafts before March 2024. You get the same checklist first, and Ctrl+Z puts everything back with the status it had. To ask instead of change, start with “how many”: how many drafts are older than six months? or how many pages are pending on every site? QUILL says the count for each site and changes nothing.

In the editor, File > WordPress > Send and Copy > Download My Writing… saves every page and post to a folder as Markdown or Word, and Send a Folder as Drafts… does the reverse (Chapter 3).

Reading a whole site as one document

Site > Read Site as Document (Ctrl+Alt+D) opens the site’s published pages in the editor as one document: the whole site, one menu or one category. It starts with the site’s address, what was read and when. It keeps headings, lists, links and alternative text, leaves out password-protected pages and counts them, and stops at 500 items, saying so. From there you can read it, search it, or save it in any format the editor writes.
