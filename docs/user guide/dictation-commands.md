# Dictation commands

Everything QUILL Lite and QUILL's Live Dictation understand, generated from the table the program itself reads -- so this list and the program cannot disagree. In either program, say **what can I say** while dictating to open the same list.

## How the commands work

- Punctuation and layout words can go anywhere in a phrase: "dear Sam comma new paragraph thank you for the letter".
- Commands only work as a whole phrase, said on their own after a pause. "Delete that line of text" in a sentence is just words.
- With "Just write what I say" switched on in Dictation Settings, commands are written as words too; punctuation, layout and the stop phrase still work.
- Say "literal" before any of these to write the word instead: "literal comma" writes comma.
- Moonshine, Whisper, every downloaded model, OpenAI and the speech providers add punctuation by themselves; saying it yourself always wins. With Windows speech recognition, say all of it.
- Some commands take words after them: select the cat, paste clip three, insert snippet sign off. They work only at the start of a phrase.
- The Markdown line marks -- "bullet", "numbered item", "block quote" and "heading one" to "heading six" -- count only at the start of a phrase, so "the heading two lines down" stays words.
- A snippet with blanks asks for each one by name: say the answer, or for a choice say one of the options or choose and its number. Say skip to leave one empty, scratch that to go back one, or cancel to type nothing. Then the whole snippet goes in as one phrase, and scratch that takes all of it back.

## The command word

- Say "QUILL" first to give a command instead of dictating: "QUILL scratch that". It counts only as the first word after a pause, so "I told QUILL about it" is written as words.
- Every command in this list works after the command word. If what follows it is not a command, nothing is typed: you hear "Not a command", and "QUILL type that" writes it after all.
- Say "literal QUILL" to write the word itself.
- With "Commands only after the command word" switched on in More Dictation Settings (it is on in QUILL Transcribe), a command said on its own is written as words, except the stop phrase.
- Speak a Command (F11 in QUILL and QUILL Lite, and in QUILL Transcribe the Command Key you choose) does the same without the word: the next thing you say is a command. Press it twice for command mode.

| Say, after the command word | What happens |
|---|---|
| "type that" or "write that" | Writes the words that were just refused as not a command, once. |
| "command mode" | Everything you say is a command, with no command word, until you say dictation mode or stop giving commands for a few seconds. You hear a falling tone when it ends. |
| "dictation mode" | Leaves command mode at once: what you say is written again. |

## Review before inserting

- Say "QUILL review that" to take the last phrase back out and hold it in Review Dictation (Shift+F4), where you can read it, edit it, ask the AI to change it and insert it once. Nothing is written until you insert it.
- While the review is open, what you say is written into it. Say the command word first to give an instruction instead, or turn on Talk About It in the window.
- Changing words with AI needs AI help switched on and your ChatGPT subscription or your own AI key. With AI off, the review still lets you edit and insert.
- "QUILL polish that" asks the AI to fix punctuation, capitals and small slips and keep your words. It works only after you turn on Allow polishing my dictation with AI in More Dictation Settings, and nothing is sent until you agree.

| Say, after the command word | What happens |
|---|---|
| "review that" or "review it" | Takes the last phrase back out, or the words held while paused, and opens it in Review Dictation to read, change and insert. With AI off, the words stay where they were written. |
| "make it shorter" or "more formal" or "fix the grammar only" | While reviewing: asks the AI to change the words. Anything else said after the command word while reviewing is an instruction too. You hear what changed. |
| "what changed" | While reviewing: says again what the last change did. |
| "read it back" or "read it" | While reviewing: reads the whole text. |
| "undo that change" or "undo the change" | While reviewing: takes back the last change. |
| "back to what i said" | While reviewing: puts back the words exactly as you said them. |
| "polish that" or "polish it" | Asks the AI to fix punctuation, capitals and small slips in the words, keeping your words; opens the last phrase in Review Dictation first. Only when Allow polishing my dictation with AI is on, and only after you agree to send. You hear what changed; nothing is inserted until you insert it. |
| "insert it" | While reviewing: writes the text where it was going, once. |
| "discard it" | While reviewing: throws the text away; nothing is written. |

## Profiles and formatting

- A profile is the kind of writing dictation is for: Standard, Email, Document, Markdown, Chat, Coding, Verbatim. Standard is your Dictation Settings as they are, and is where everybody starts.
- Choose one in More Dictation Settings in QUILL and QUILL Lite, or in QUILL Transcribe's Dictation menu (Dictation Profile, Ctrl+Shift+F) or tray menu. Or say its name after the command word: "QUILL email mode".
- A profile sets the pause, whether filler words are removed and whether punctuation is added, and how headings, bold and italic are written.
- Markdown and Coding write "heading two" as two number signs, and "bold that" as two asterisks either side. Document and Email use the editor's own Heading and Bold in a document that holds formatting; anywhere else they type nothing for them and say why. Chat and Verbatim write plain text.
- Lock Profile stops a spoken profile name switching profiles; you can still choose one by hand. Nothing ever switches profiles on its own.

## Correcting yourself as you talk

- With Fix it when I correct myself turned on in Dictation Settings, saying "no", "I mean", "no I mean", "sorry" or "scratch that word" between two days, months, numbers or names from My Words writes only the second: "Thursday, no, Friday" writes Friday. Anything else is written as said.
- When you correct the same word the same way twice, with "correct that" and "choose", or in QUILL's own documents with "correct" and the word, dictation offers to remember it: "Add a correction: Ann becomes Anne?" In QUILL and QUILL Lite a window asks. Where no window can be shown the question is spoken: say "QUILL yes" to add it or "QUILL no" within 30 seconds. Nothing is added without a yes.

## Commands: correcting

| Say, on its own | What happens |
|---|---|
| "scratch that" or "delete that" | Removes the phrase you dictated last. Say it again to remove the one before. A phrase you have typed into since is left alone. |
| "undo that" or "undo" or "undo last" | The same as pressing Ctrl+Z. |
| "select that" | Selects the phrase you dictated last, so you can change or format it with the keyboard. The next phrase you say replaces it. |
| "capitalize that" or "cap that" | Gives every word of the last phrase a capital: meeting notes becomes Meeting Notes. |
| "all caps that" or "uppercase that" | Puts the last phrase in capitals. |
| "no caps that" or "lowercase that" | Puts the last phrase in small letters. |
| "delete word" or "delete last word" | Deletes the word just before the cursor. |
| "delete sentence" or "delete last sentence" | Deletes from the start of the sentence the cursor is in up to the cursor. |
| "delete previous paragraph" or "delete last paragraph" | Deletes the paragraph just before the cursor, from its start up to the cursor. In another program, only words dictation typed there. |
| "read that" or "repeat that" | Reads aloud again everything the last phrase, snippet, clip or review put in. In another program, only what dictation itself typed can be read. |
| "read last sentence" or "read the last sentence" | Reads the sentence just before the cursor, with its punctuation said. |
| "read last paragraph" or "read the last paragraph" or "read this paragraph" | Reads the paragraph the cursor is in. |
| "read selection" or "read the selection" | Reads what is selected. |
| "correct that" | Reads the other things the speech engine thought you said, numbered. Windows speech recognition offers its own; with Moonshine, Whisper or a downloaded model, the other built-in engine listens to the last phrase again and its answer is choice 2. Then say choose and a number. |
| "choose one" or "choose 1" | After correct that, puts the first of the other guesses in place of the last phrase. |
| "choose two" or "choose 2" | The same, with the second guess. |
| "choose three" or "choose 3" | The same, with the third guess. |
| "spell that" | Selects the phrase you dictated last and listens for it spelled out. Say the letters and they replace it. The fix for a name the engine keeps getting wrong. |

## Spelling

- Say "start spelling", then letters. Everything you say is written as letters until you say "stop spelling".
- Say "capital" before a letter for a capital, and "space" for a space. Say "all caps" for capitals until "no caps" or the end of the phrase.
- Punctuation works while spelling, with no spaces, and each phrase joins the letters before it, so you can pause: "jay dot sierra mike india tango hotel", then "at sign echo x-ray dot charlie oscar" writes j.smith@ex.co. "Dot" is a full stop only here. A whole word that is not a letter is left out.
- Say "spell" and the letters to spell one word without starting spelling mode, and "spell that" to spell the last phrase over again.
- Letter names work ("bee", "see"), and the phonetic alphabet is clearer: alpha, bravo, charlie, delta, echo, foxtrot, golf, hotel, india, juliet, kilo, lima, mike, november, oscar, papa, quebec, romeo, sierra, tango, uniform, victor, whiskey, x-ray, yankee, zulu.
- Numbers are written as digits: zero, one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine.

## Limits

- How accurately and how quickly each engine hears you has not been measured yet. Those numbers wait on the dictation benchmarks; nothing in this guide is a claim about them.
- Commands that take words after them (select, go to, go after, correct, paste clip, insert snippet) work only at the start of a phrase.
- Words you ask for are looked for in the document's text. When they are not there, what you said is written as words and you hear "Not found, written as text." Scratch that takes it out.
- An asterisk and an underscore join the words either side of them (`two*three`, `snake_case`). For Markdown bold, use "bold that" with the Markdown profile.
- In another program (QUILL Transcribe, and Quill Inkwell's Dictate Anywhere) dictation can only type: dictating, punctuation, scratch that, the modes, spelling, paste clip, insert snippet and insert abbreviation work. Anything that has to read or select that program's text is refused with the same sentence: select, go to, go after, select again, select sentence, line or paragraph, copy all, copy that, show clips, show snippets, spell that, and read last sentence or read selection.
- In another program, a snippet or abbreviation that puts the cursor in the middle leaves it at the end instead, because moving it back would mean reading that program's text.

## Starting and stopping by voice

- With the wake phrase switched on in Dictation Settings, say "Quill dictate" to start dictation without touching the keyboard. Anything you say after it in the same breath is written.
- Say "stop dictation" on its own, after a pause, to stop dictation. "Stop dictation" always works too. With the wake phrase on, stopping goes back to waiting for the wake phrase.
- You choose both phrases in Dictation Settings; each needs at least two words.
