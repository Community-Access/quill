Chapter 10: AI, when you want it

This chapter is about the AI help built into QUILL, and the first thing to know is that you never have to use it. QUILL is a complete editor without it, and nothing you write is sent anywhere until you ask. If you would like a paragraph summarised, tidied or explained, QUILL’s own free AI is ready with no account and no key. If you already pay for ChatGPT or GitHub Copilot, or have your own key, you can use those instead: Choose Your AI (Ctrl+4) is the one place to pick, and Which AI Am I Using (Ctrl+8) tells you which one answers. Further on you will find document tasks, which hand a job with several steps to Copilot or Codex, and the library where you can save your own prompts, skills and agents. Every answer is text: no AI in QUILL draws charts, diagrams or pictures.

QUILL’s own free AI

You do not need an account, a key, or a decision about which company sees your writing. QUILL runs its own AI service, it is free, and it is the first thing in the AI menu (the &AI menu, so Alt then A opens it).

What it works on

The free AI works on the selection. With nothing selected it takes the paragraph you are in, or else the section. It never sends the whole file.

Nothing is applied without a keystroke from you. Every answer arrives in a window with Replace and Insert buttons, and both go through the ordinary undo list. If you typed over the text while the answer was on its way, Replace is not offered, and QUILL tells you so instead of writing the answer somewhere it does not belong.

Beside Replace is Review Changes…. It opens Review AI Changes, the same review window QUILL’s AI agents use: every change the answer would make to your selection, one per line, and for a small change the exact words that differ and the sentence they are in. Uncheck any change you want to keep as it was, then apply the rest. It goes in as one edit, so Ctrl+Z takes it all back.

Where the answer goes

The pad has a list called Put the answer in, with three choices:

  • The AI Result window (the first choice and the starting one). You read the answer first and then choose Replace, Review Changes, Insert Below, Copy, Try Again or Follow Up.
  • My document, at the cursor. The answer goes straight into your document where the cursor is, as one edit Ctrl+Z takes back, and QUILL reads it aloud.
  • The clipboard. The answer goes on the clipboard and QUILL reads it aloud. Your document does not change.

QUILL remembers your choice (the setting ai_answer_delivery) until you change it, and it applies to every answer: from the pad, the Chat Key and the Prompt Library.

How AI answers are spoken

AI models write their answers in Markdown, the plain-text formatting with # for headings, ** for bold and - for list items. On screen that is harmless. Read aloud, it is noise: your screen reader would say “star star” and “hash hash”, and read every link’s whole web address. So whenever QUILL speaks an AI answer, it speaks the words and leaves the symbols out.

This applies everywhere an answer is spoken:

  • an answer put at the cursor or on the clipboard (above), which QUILL reads aloud because focus does not move;
  • each reply in an AI Conversation, in this window or a saved one;
  • Ask Quill’s replies, whether announced, read aloud by QUILL’s own voice or by the AI voice (see Asking out loud and hearing the answer), and the progress Ask Quill announces while a long answer is still arriving.

What you hear, and what you get. Only the spoken copy changes. What goes into your document, onto the clipboard, into the AI Result window or into the conversation transcript is the answer exactly as it arrived, Markdown and all. If you asked for a Markdown table, you get a Markdown table.

An example. You set Put the answer in to My document, at the cursor and ask for a summary. The AI sends back:

## Summary

**Three** points:

- Costs went down.
- See [the report](https://example.com/report).

Your document gets those six lines exactly. You hear: “Answer inserted at the cursor. Press Control Z to undo. Summary Three points: Costs went down. See the report.”

What is left out, and what is kept:

  • Heading marks, bold and italic marks, list bullets and quote marks are dropped; the words they mark are kept.
  • A link is read by its words (“the report”), never by its address.
  • A picture is read as “image” and its description.
  • A block of code is read as “code block”. Code read aloud a symbol at a time is rarely what you want from an announcement; the code itself is in your document or the window, where you can read it character by character.

The answer in the AI Result window is not read out for you: focus lands on it and your screen reader reads it as text, symbols included, so you can check the formatting before you use it.

Ask (Ctrl+6)

Press Ctrl+6 (AI > Ask AI a Question…) to open the AI Conversation window about the document you are in. Its first line says which AI you are asking, such as “Asking My ChatGPT plan about this document.” Type your question and press Enter. With each message QUILL sends your selection, or else the paragraph or section the cursor is in, the document’s title and its headings, read fresh every time, so you can move around the document between questions. It never sends the whole file. Ask Quill (Alt+Q) is still there for the advanced companion; it has not changed.

Your prompts on the AI in use (Ctrl+7)

Ctrl+7 (AI > Prompt Library…) opens the Prompt Library, with the same prompts as the Prompts tab of AI > AI Library…: QUILL’s built-in prompts, the ones you wrote, and the ones your Quillins add. Choose a prompt and press Run with AI. The window closes, your selection is put where the prompt says {selection}, and the prompt goes to the AI in use as a general question; on the free AI that is one request. The answer arrives the way you chose. On the free AI, if the prompt with your text in it is bigger than the free limit allows, QUILL says so and sends nothing.

One list of your prompts, for the whole family. The prompts you save are kept in one file, prompts.json in QUILL’s data folder, and QUILL Lite’s Prompt Library reads the same file. The first time this version starts, QUILL brings in the prompts you had saved elsewhere: Prompt Studio’s, the image description styles you wrote, and QUILL Lite’s own list. A prompt that is the same in two places becomes one. Two different prompts with the same name are both kept, and the second is named after where it came from, for example “Tighten (from QUILL Lite)”. This happens once, quietly, in the background, and the old files are left exactly as they were for one release in case anything needs checking.

Searching the web (Ctrl+9)

QUILL’s free service can search the web for you, ten times a month. When the service has search switched on, the pad’s list ends with Search the web: type a question, press Send, and the answer comes back with the sites it used. Each search also uses one ordinary request.

Press Ctrl+9 (AI > Accounts and Usage > Web Searches Left) to hear how many of this month’s searches are left and when they start again. If the service is not offering web search at the moment, Ctrl+9 says that instead. The pad’s Search the web choice and its ten searches are part of the free service only. On your ChatGPT plan, the model can search for itself once you check Allow web search in Plan Options… (see Choose Your AI, below); with your own key there is no web search.

You never wait for it, either. Requests run in the background, so you can keep typing, save, switch documents or close the window while an answer is coming.

QUILLBee’s tip: Changed your mind about an AI edit? Press Ctrl+Z. It comes back out exactly like anything else you typed.

The Free AI Assistant pad (Ctrl+Alt+G)

The pad has a list called What do you want done? with seventeen choices. Press a letter in the list to jump.

  • The four basics: Summarize, Rewrite, Proofread and Explain.
  • Ten writing tools: Shorten, Simplify, Make more formal, Make friendlier, Turn into a list, Find action items, Suggest headings, Continue writing, Write an email reply and Translate. When you choose Translate, a Translate into list appears with twenty languages. If the answer comes back the same as your text, QUILL says so rather than showing it as a translation: the text may already be in that language, or the AI could not tell what language it was in.
  • Three that need you to type: Ask a question about the document, Ask a general question, and Have a conversation.

Every one of them is a single request of the same size, so the writing tools cost no more of your allowance than Summarize.

New tools from QUILL’s free service

QUILL’s free service can add a tool without a new version of QUILL. A tool it offers that your copy does not have built in appears at the end of the What do you want done? list, after Have a conversation. Press F1 on it to hear what it does. Send runs it like any other choice, and it uses one request.

The first time you open the pad, a new tool can take a moment to appear at the end of the list. These tools are offered only on QUILL’s free service: with your own API key or your ChatGPT plan, the list has the seventeen built-in choices.

Having a conversation

Choose Have a conversation in the pad, or press Follow Up in any answer window, and the AI Conversation window opens. Here you can talk back and forth, and each reply remembers what was said before.

  1. Focus is in Your message. Type, then press Enter.
  2. Each reply is read aloud as it arrives and added to the Conversation above. Press Shift+Tab to reach it.
  3. Beside Send are Copy Last Reply, Insert Last Reply Below, New Conversation, Messages… and Export Conversation… (both described below).

If you started with a question about the document, each follow-up keeps sending the same passages, so you can keep asking about them.

Every conversation is saved on this computer as it goes, in the same place Ask Quill keeps its sessions, so AI Session Branches shows them too. Press Saved Conversations… in the conversation window to see them, newest first. Press Enter on one to open it and carry on where it left off, or Delete to remove it (QUILL asks first). New Conversation starts a new saved conversation; the old one stays saved.

Going back over the conversation. In the conversation window:

  • Alt+1 reads the newest message aloud, Alt+2 the one before it, and so on up to Alt+0 for the tenth newest. Each is said with its place and its speaker, for example “Message 4 of 9, AI: …”, and the newest starts with “Newest”. Focus stays in Your message and your half-typed message stays where it is. With fewer messages than the key asks for, you hear how many there are, for example “There are only 2 messages so far. Alt+2 reads the oldest.”
  • Messages… lists every message, oldest first, one row each. Press Enter to open a message in a reading window where its headings and tables are real ones (in browse mode, H moves by heading and T by table), or Read as Text to see it exactly as it arrived in a plain text box. Close it with Escape and you are back on the same row; the list opens on that row again next time.
  • Export Conversation… saves the whole conversation as a Word document or a Text file you name. Every message is kept in order with who said it: in Word, each message is a level-2 heading such as “Message 3 of 8, AI” with the message beneath it and its tables as real Word tables; in a text file, each message is a paragraph starting “You:” or “AI:”. The Save As window asks before replacing a file, Cancel writes nothing, and your document is never changed. If the file cannot be written, you hear why and any file already there is left as it was.
  • A reply that arrives while you are reading the Conversation box does not move your place in it. If you are not in the box, it shows the end, as before.

Example: you ask “Plan a three-day trip to Lisbon”, then “Make day two cheaper”. Press Alt+3 to hear the first plan again, and Export Conversation… to keep both plans in a Word file, each under its own heading.

These work the same on QUILL’s free AI, your own key, your ChatGPT plan and GitHub Copilot, and every one saves to the same Saved Conversations.

On the free AI each message counts as one request, and conversations have a monthly share of 40 of your 100 requests. The conversation so far goes with each message only as far as the ordinary size limit allows, so no message costs more than any other, and a long conversation slowly forgets its beginning. The window tells you the first time that happens. With your own OpenAI or Gemini key there is no such limit: the whole conversation goes, billed to your account.

There is a monthly, a daily and an hourly allowance, and a smaller one for a computer’s first 48 hours. Free AI limits, later in this chapter, has every number and every rule.

Nothing is sent until you ask

The free AI makes no request when QUILL starts, while you type, when you save, when you are idle or when focus moves. If you leave it switched on and never use it, QUILL makes no network call for it at all.

The first time you reach for it, QUILL shows you exactly what is sent and what is kept, and nothing leaves your computer until you accept. You can come to that same agreement three ways: AI > Accounts and Usage > Privacy Agreement… (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+K), Preferences, and Customize Features. All three show the same answer, so they never disagree.

If you withdraw, QUILL signs this computer out of the free AI as well, so no sign-in is left behind for a service you have said no to. QUILL remembers which version of the agreement you accepted (the setting ai_privacy_accepted_version), so if what is sent ever changes in an important way, you are asked again.

Basic and Advanced menus

The AI menu starts out Basic. It shows the five free AI commands above, the Use Artificial Intelligence switch, and Show advanced AI features. Basic is for everybody who just wants a paragraph tidied up.

The full menu has much more: provider setup, Ask Quill, the agents, Proofread, Transform, Translate, Read Aloud, Transcribe, More, the AI Library and the AI Hub. It is worth having once you have chosen which company should see your writing and have a key to paste in.

Nothing is taken away in Basic. Every advanced command keeps its keyboard shortcut and stays in the Command Palette; Basic only changes what the menu offers. Check Show advanced AI features near the bottom of the AI menu and everything comes back at once. QUILL remembers your choice.

If you already had AI set up when you updated (you had run the wizard, or saved a provider key), QUILL leaves you in Advanced, so no menu you were using disappears.

Setting up AI with the wizard

The first item in the AI menu is Set Up AI…, labelled “start here” until you have used it. It is a short wizard that gets you to working AI in a minute, in plain words.

  1. Welcome. A short note on what QUILL’s AI does, and that it is optional, shown to you before anything changes, and private by default.
  2. How would you like AI to run? Pick one:
    • On your device with Ollama. Private and free. It runs on your computer with no account or key, and QUILL connects to Ollama installed there.
    • Use an AI account. The most capable models. Connect Claude, OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter or Ollama Cloud with a key you paste once, which QUILL stores safely on this computer.
    • Not right now. AI stays off, and you can set it up any time later.
  3. Connect. For an account, pick a provider, paste your key, and press Test connection before you go on. For on-device AI, QUILL points itself at Ollama.
  4. You’re all set. A short summary of what you can now do, and a Keep it simple checkbox that turns on Basic mode.

QUILL also offers the wizard when you reach for AI before it is set up, for example when you ask for minutes from a transcript, so you never hit a dead end. Run Set Up AI again whenever you want to change provider or mode.

Basic mode is the default on a new install, and it hides the whole advanced menu, not just the agents. The wizard’s Keep it simple checkbox turns it on, and Show advanced AI features turns it off.

Using AI for free

You never have to pay to use AI in QUILL. Besides QUILL’s own free AI, the Connect step of the wizard recommends two free paths and picks good settings for you.

  • Most private, free. Pick Ollama (on your device). A model runs on your own computer: no account, no key, it works offline, and nothing you write leaves your machine. The quality is more modest than the big cloud models. If Ollama is not installed yet, do not install it by hand. AI > Set Up Local AI (Ollama)… does the whole job: it reports your computer’s memory, graphics card and free disk space, downloads and starts the official installer, downloads a model that suits your computer, and connects QUILL to it. Each step tells you what to do next, and it never moves on without you.
  • Best quality, free. Pick OpenRouter. Choose Get API key to open OpenRouter’s key page in your browser, create a free key and paste it back. QUILL picks a strong free writing model for you (at the moment a free Llama 3.3 70B model). The model’s description says Free, so you always know a choice costs nothing.

Every provider that needs a key has a Get API key button beside the key box that opens the right sign-up page.

A few honest notes:

  • Free cloud models can be slower and are rate-limited. Because the key is your own, that limit is yours alone; nobody else shares it, and it costs nobody anything.
  • Free hosted models may keep or train on what you send, under their own terms. If your writing is confidential, use the private on-device option.
  • What works on free models: everyday help works well, such as Rewrite, Summarize, Expand, Continue, Fix Grammar, Table of Contents, translating and proofreading. Ask Quill works too; on a small model it keeps its steps simple so it answers instead of stalling. The longest multi-step agent tasks are where a stronger paid model still does better. QUILL tells you when a task would benefit from one, but never stops you trying on a free model.
  • QUILL tidies on-device answers for you. Small on-device models have a few habits. When you use Rewrite, Summarize, Expand, Continue or Shorten on the on-device model, QUILL gives the model worked examples first, then checks the answer for hedging (“it seems”, “arguably”), editorialising (“clearly”, “obviously”) and filler openings (“In today’s world”, “It is important to note”), and tries once more to remove them. Fix Grammar and Improve Reading Order are left alone, so your own wording is never changed. This happens only on the on-device model; cloud answers are left as they come.

You can change your provider or model any time in Use My Own AI Key (Alt+F2). The AI Hub no longer has a provider list of its own.

AI Assistant

Beyond the free AI, QUILL has a full AI assistant. You can run it on your own computer (llama.cpp with a local GGUF model) or connect a provider: Ollama (local), Ollama Cloud, OpenAI, Claude, OpenRouter, Google Gemini, or a custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Every provider is optional, and you choose it yourself. Keys are kept in the Windows Credential Manager, with a DPAPI-encrypted file as a fallback, and are never written to disk as plain text. Where your keys are kept, later in this chapter, has the details.

Setting up an AI provider

Open AI > Accounts and Usage > Use My Own AI Key… (Alt+F2) to choose a provider, save its key and pick a model. It used to be the AI Hub’s job; the Hub’s Provider page now just says which provider is in use and opens Use My Own AI Key.

  • OpenRouter. Paste your OpenRouter key. One OpenRouter key reaches many models, including Claude, GPT-4o and Gemini.
  • OpenAI. Paste your OpenAI API key.
  • Ollama on this computer. No key needed. Install Ollama (ollama serve) and choose it; QUILL looks for it at http://localhost:11434. If Ollama runs on a different port or computer, change the Server address.

You can also set a Default model for prompt runs (ai_prompt_default_model). Leave it blank and Ask AI and the Prompt Library use the same model, or name a more capable model here for prompt library work.

Ask Quill (Alt+Q) and Ask AI

AI > Ask Quill… (Alt+Q) opens the assistant as a conversation. You can ask questions, draft text, suggest edits, and run QUILL commands, and nothing changes until you approve it. The window shows the provider and model in use, and you can switch either one there.

Earlier versions also had a separate, plain Ask AI question window. It has been folded into AI > Ask Quill…, which is now the one chat window for questions with your own AI provider. For a quick conversation about the document on whichever AI is in use, use AI > Ask AI a Question… (Ctrl+6), described earlier in this chapter.

Check Grammar with AI

AI > Proofread > Check Grammar with AI… sends the selected text, or the whole document if nothing is selected, to your AI model and asks it to review the grammar. The answer lists each correction as the original phrase, the corrected phrase and the reason. Nothing is changed for you; you make the corrections yourself.

Out of the box, the model is told to list only the corrections needed and not to rewrite the passage. To give it different instructions, open the Prompt Library, find the built-in “Check Grammar” prompt and edit its text. The command uses your version from then on.

Free AI limits, and when they start again

QUILL’s free AI is free because everybody shares it, so it has limits. Here is every one of them in one place. AI Usage (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F2) shows where you stand.

Your allowance at a glance

These are the service’s current settings. QUILL reads them from the service each time, so they can change without an update, and Usage always shows the numbers that apply to you right now.

Limit How many Starts again
Requests a month 100 The 1st of the month
Requests a day 20 Every night
Requests an hour 8 At the top of each hour
Any one kind of request a month 60 The 1st of the month
A new connection’s first 48 hours 15 Rises to 100 on its own

“Any one kind” means Summarize, Rewrite, Proofread, Explain and Ask About This Document each have a monthly share of 60, so no single one can use up the whole month. The daily and hourly limits are there to stop something running away with a month’s worth in an afternoon. Ordinary work rarely comes near them.

When the counts start again

The service keeps time in UTC, not your own time zone.

  • The hour starts again on the hour.
  • The day starts again at midnight UTC. That is 8 PM Eastern or 5 PM Pacific in summer, an hour earlier in winter, so in North America the new day’s requests arrive in the evening.
  • The month starts again at midnight UTC on the 1st. Usage shows the exact date.

What counts as a request

One request is one press of Send that comes back with an answer. Try Again is another request. A request that brings no answer is not counted: if it was too long, if there was no connection, if the service had a problem, or if you had already reached a limit, nothing is used. Opening Usage or About never uses anything.

When the AI service is busy

Every AI service has busy moments: too many people at once, a server restarting, a request that took too long. QUILL now handles them the same way on the free AI, your own OpenAI or Google Gemini key, and a ChatGPT plan:

  • It tries the request up to three times, one second apart and then two, when the service answers that it is busy, overloaded, timed out or asked to slow down (the HTTP answers 408, 409, 429, 500, 502, 503 and 504).
  • When the service says how long to wait, QUILL waits that long instead, up to 20 seconds. If it asks for longer, QUILL tells you now rather than leave you listening to silence.
  • It never sends a request twice when the service says not to, because a second copy might be counted, or charged, twice. Your free AI allowance being used up, or your ChatGPT plan’s usage, is said at once too: trying again cannot change it.
  • You only hear the end of it. While QUILL is trying again you hear nothing new; if the last try fails, you hear that one failure, in the same words as before. If a try succeeds, you just get your answer.
  • A streamed answer that has already started to arrive is never started again, so you never hear the beginning twice.

For example, you press Summarize at a busy moment. The first try is answered “the service is busy”, QUILL waits a second and tries again, and the answer arrives. All you notice is that it took a second longer.

When you reach a limit

The AI window tells you which limit you reached and when it starts again, and focus moves to that message so you can read it again with the arrow keys. It ends with an error code, QUILL-AI-GATEWAY-QUOTA for a limit, which is worth including if you write to support. Nothing is used, nothing in your document changes, and the rest of QUILL carries on as normal. Wait until the time it gives you, or ask for more (see Asking for more, below).

If the message says QUILL’s free AI is paused, it is not about you. The service has one spending ceiling for everybody together, and when that is reached the free AI pauses for everyone until the 1st. This is rare, and QUILL tells you when it happens.

A new connection starts smaller

For its first 48 hours, a computer that has just connected gets 15 requests, and then the full allowance starts on its own. You do not need to do anything. It stops somebody connecting over and over for a fresh allowance each time, and someone trying the free AI out rarely uses 15 on the first day. While it applies, Usage and About say so and tell you exactly when it ends. Signing out and connecting again starts a new connection, so it never gets you more.

Computers that share an internet connection

A household, a classroom or a library usually reaches the internet through one address, and a few limits apply to that address as a whole: how many computers can be connected from it at once, how many new connections it can make in an hour, and a monthly total shared by every computer on it. They are generous for a home. The exact numbers are not published, so that nobody can use them to make connections by the hundred. Signing a computer out gives its place back.

If a class or a lab runs into these limits, write to support. Helping with exactly this is part of their job.

Asking for more

Choose Get Help from Support in the Help menu, and say what you need and why: a course, a deadline, a book. When this computer is connected, its support ID goes into the message by itself, and that is all support needs to find you; there is no account name or password to give. Support can raise your monthly allowance, and can end the new-connection allowance early. The change applies from your next request. You do not need to sign out, reconnect or update, and Usage shows the new number the next time you open it.

Your support ID is also in Help > About Quill, if you would rather write from somewhere else.

Using your own AI key

If you have an account with OpenAI, Google Gemini, Anthropic, OpenRouter, Ollama Cloud or another OpenAI-compatible service, or Ollama on your own computer, you can use your own key instead of QUILL’s free service, and every limit goes away: no monthly, daily or hourly allowance, no 2,250-word ceiling, and no smaller first 48 hours.

Setting up your key (Alt+F2)

AI > Accounts and Usage > Use My Own AI Key… (Alt+F2) opens one window. Its parts, in Tab order:

  • Provider. OpenAI, Google Gemini, Anthropic Claude, OpenRouter, Ollama Cloud, Custom OpenAI-compatible service, or Ollama on this computer. The last five used to live in the AI Hub’s Provider list; that list is gone and they are all here now. Everything else in the window follows this choice: where your text goes, which key, which models and whose prices. AI help uses the key saved for the provider you choose here, never another one. The choice is saved with your settings (ai_own_key_provider), and QUILL never guesses it from a key or a model name.
  • About this. A read-only box that says exactly where your text goes. Arrow through it once.
  • API key. Paste the key for the provider you chose. An OpenAI key comes from platform.openai.com, under API keys, and starts with sk-. A Gemini key comes from Google AI Studio at aistudio.google.com/apikey. The About box says where to get a key for each of the others.
  • Server address. Only for a custom service and for Ollama on this computer; it is off for the others, whose address never changes. Ollama usually listens at http://localhost:11434. An address you saved in the AI Hub before is filled in for you.
  • Model. Every model your key can use for text, filled in from your account once the key is checked. For OpenAI, Luna 6 first, then the other GPT-6 models; for Gemini, 2.5 Flash first, then 2.5 Pro; then the rest by name. Each row says roughly what it costs, for example “gpt-6-luna, about $0.57 per 100 requests (estimate)”, so arrowing down the list is enough to compare them. Your choice is saved as ai_own_key_model. Models that cannot answer in text (speech, transcription, images, embeddings) are left out.
  • Cost estimate. The chosen model’s estimated cost per request and per 100 requests, at that provider’s prices. These are estimates to help you compare, not the provider’s prices. A typical request is about a page in and a paragraph back. The real prices are at openai.com/api/pricing and ai.google.dev/pricing.
  • Status. Whether a key is saved for the chosen provider, what the last test said, and whether a key for the other provider is saved too.
  • Test the Key. Checks the key with your provider, fills the model list, then sends one tiny request to the chosen model and tells you whether it answered. That request costs a fraction of a cent.
  • Remove the Saved Key. Forgets the chosen provider’s key at once. If your own key was the AI in use, no AI is in use until you choose one; QUILL does not move you to the free AI by itself.

Press OK and the key is saved. QUILL says “AI help uses your own OpenAI key, with no limits”, or names your own Google Gemini key.

To change the model later, press Alt+F2. With a key saved, the list of models is there straight away. Pick another and press OK.

Saving a key is choosing it: press OK with a key saved and your own key is the AI in use, the same as Use This on its row in Choose Your AI. Remove the key while it is in use and no AI is in use until you pick one; the next AI command says “No AI is in use. Choose one.” Nothing goes to the free AI instead unless you choose it.

Ollama on this computer needs no key. Install Ollama from ollama.com, pull a model, and choose Ollama on this computer. The About box starts with “Runs on this computer. Nothing is sent.”, and that is the whole story: the model runs on your own computer, nothing is billed and nothing leaves it. The key box is off, the Model list is whatever Ollama says it has, and the test button reads Test the Connection, which checks Ollama is running. A model that Ollama says can read pictures answers Ask About an Image too.

One choice for all of QUILL’s AI. When you press OK, the provider you chose is also the one the advanced AI features use (Ask Quill, Run Agent, the AI Library, Proofread, Transform and the rest). You no longer set it up twice.

You can save both keys. The Provider list decides which one is used. With OpenAI chosen, a saved Gemini key just waits; the Status line tells you it is there, and choosing Google Gemini puts it to work. QUILL never decides from which key exists or from a model’s name, so a request never goes to a company you did not choose.

With a Gemini key, Ask About an Image works too. Gemini’s models can read pictures, so Ctrl+F5 answers on a Gemini key just as it does on a ChatGPT plan. Ollama on this computer can too, with a model Ollama says reads pictures. The free service, and an OpenAI, Anthropic, OpenRouter, Ollama Cloud or custom key here, cannot; with one of those in use, Ask About an Image says what it needs and offers to switch.

What changes with your own key

  • Your text goes straight from this computer to the provider you chose, on your account. Nothing passes through QUILL’s servers, so QUILL records nothing at all, not even the count it keeps for the free service. With Ollama on this computer, nothing leaves the computer at all.
  • That company bills you for each request, under its own terms and privacy policy. Ollama on this computer bills nothing.
  • While an answer is on its way, Send becomes Stop (Alt+S). Stopping closes a Gemini stream immediately; for another route it discards that request’s eventual answer. It never stops a request from another AI window.
  • AI Usage (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F2) has a row for your key: how many requests are left this minute, from the provider’s last answer, where it reports one. Manage Usage opens your provider’s usage page, such as your OpenAI or Google AI Studio account, where your requests and charges are. Help > About also shows the model and that page’s address instead of the free allowance.
  • You do not need to connect this computer, and you are not asked to accept the free service’s agreement, which covers QUILL’s servers. Your own key never touches them.
  • The pad, what it can do, and what comes back are the same, down to the instructions sent with your text.
  • There are no limits. Nothing is refused for being too long, the answer is not cut short, and Ask a question about the document sends the whole document instead of the three passages that match best, so nothing is missed.
  • You get warnings instead of limits. The About to send line tells you how many words are going and roughly what they cost with the model you chose (an estimate, not your provider’s bill), and that the answer costs extra. It also tells you when the text is more than the free service would accept, and, for something very large, that it may be more than the model can read at once. In that case the provider refuses it and charges nothing.

Where your own key is kept

In Windows’ own credential store, not a settings file. Once saved it is never shown again: the box stays empty and says a key is saved. A portable copy of QUILL keeps it in an encrypted file instead. QUILL and QUILL Lite share the key, so a key saved in either works in both, and removing it in either removes it from both.

If a request fails

You hear why, followed by the error code QUILL-AI-OWN-KEY-FAILED, and the message names the provider. The usual causes are a mistyped key, a model your account cannot use, an account with no credit, or the wrong provider chosen for the key you saved. Test the Key tells you which.

Which AI is in use

QUILL keeps one record of which AI answers you: QUILL’s free AI, your ChatGPT plan, your GitHub Copilot, Codex, or your own key. The choice is saved with your settings as ai_connection.

The first time you start this version, QUILL keeps what you already had. If you were signed in with ChatGPT, your plan stays in use. If not, and you had a key saved, your key stays in use. Otherwise it is the free AI. Nothing changes for you.

After that, signing in is choosing. Sign in with ChatGPT and your plan is in use. Sign in to GitHub Copilot and Copilot is in use. Save a key in Use My Own AI Key and your key is in use. Connect the free AI and the free AI is in use. Use This in Choose Your AI switches to a row you are already signed in to, without signing anything.

If you sign out of the one in use, no AI is in use until you pick one. QUILL does not quietly send your text to the free AI or to your key instead. The next AI command says “No AI is in use. Choose one.”

If OpenAI ends your ChatGPT sign-in itself, you hear “Your ChatGPT plan sign-in has ended. Nothing was sent. Press Ctrl+4 to sign in again or choose another.” GitHub ending a Copilot sign-in is said the same way. Nothing is retried, and nothing goes anywhere else. A limit works the same way, on any AI: you hear it once, for example “Your ChatGPT plan’s limit for QUILL is reached. Nothing was sent. Manage usage is in the Usage window.”, and QUILL neither tries again nor switches.

QUILL counts the requests it sends on your ChatGPT plan, today and this week, along with the token counts OpenAI reports back. That count stays on this computer and is always called a local count. OpenAI’s own usage page is the real answer about your plan.

Two more settings hold a model each: ai_copilot_model for GitHub Copilot and ai_codex_model for Codex. Empty means the service picks.

Answers are always plain text. QUILL asks every AI not to draw charts, graphs or diagrams, and if one sends something like that anyway, you get it as text, not a picture.

  • Model, the models that connection offers, from its own list. The first one is the service’s recommendation and says “(recommended)”. The free AI says “QUILL chooses the model”, and a row you have not signed in to says “Sign in to see the models”.

It only speaks when something happens that your screen reader cannot know: “Now using My ChatGPT plan.”, “Signed in to My ChatGPT plan. It is now in use.”, or “Signed out. No AI is in use until you choose one.” The row’s own words change at the same moment, so arrowing away and back tells you the truth.

My own key opens Use My Own AI Key, with its Provider list. QUILL’s free AI opens its Connect step, the same one as before, and only when you ask for it.

My GitHub Copilot uses the same small step, with one more field: a short code from GitHub. QUILL spaces the code out so your screen reader reads one character at a time, says it once, and puts it on the clipboard. It is now in use.” Cancel stops waiting straight away. Copilot Free works too; its model list has one entry, “Auto (Copilot Free chooses)”. QUILL signs in to GitHub on its own, separately from QUILL Lite and from the GitHub sign-in QUILL uses for your repositories.

Signing in to GitHub from inside QUILL needs QUILL’s own registration with GitHub. Until a build carries it, the row says “not available yet”, About this connection says “Not available yet: GitHub sign-in for QUILL has not been registered, so it comes in a later update.”, and Sign In… tells you the same thing instead of opening the step. Codex, for tasks runs on your ChatGPT plan, with the same sign-in. Once you are signed in to your plan, Use This on the Codex row is the only key to press. Codex is there for document tasks (below). While it is in use, single requests such as a rewrite, Ask or Ask About an Image go straight to your plan, the same as on the ChatGPT row, and the Codex program itself starts only when you begin a document task. Its usage is your plan’s, so AI Usage shows it on the ChatGPT row. If this copy of QUILL does not include the Codex engine, the row says “not available yet” and About says so.

While your ChatGPT plan’s row, or Codex’s, is selected and signed in, one more button appears beside the action button: Plan Options…. It opens two choices that used to live in the plan’s own window. Allow web search is a check box: when it is checked, the model may search the web through OpenAI to answer you, and it is saved as soon as you change it. Do a Document Task (Ctrl+3)

Some jobs take more than one step: “Fix the heading levels, then build a table of contents, then list broken links”, or “Turn these notes into actions grouped by owner.” AI > Do a Document Task… (Ctrl+3) hands a job like that to GitHub Copilot or Codex, whichever is in use, and it works through the steps with QUILL’s own editing tools.

Only those two can do tasks. On any other AI the menu says Do a Document Task (needs Copilot or Codex)…, and pressing it sends nothing and offers to switch, the same way as any other AI command.

The window has six things in it, in Tab order:

  • Conversation, a read-only record of the task: what you asked, each step, and the summary at the end.
  • Status, which says “Using GitHub Copilot” or “Using Codex on your ChatGPT plan”, and whether it is working.
  • Instruction, where focus starts. Type what you want done and press Enter. While a task is running, what you type here goes to it as a follow-up.
  • Send, which does the same as Enter.
  • Stop, which stops the task straight away. Changes already made stay, and each one undoes with Ctrl+Z.
  • Close, which also stops a task that is still running.

Nothing changes without you. Before a step that changes something, a small window, Allow This Step?, says in one sentence what will happen. Press Enter for Allow once, choose Allow for this task to stop being asked about that kind of step until this task ends, or press Escape for Don’t allow. Every edit to your document then opens Review AI Changes, the same review you already know, so you can accept or reject each change. Nothing carries over to the next task: it asks again.

QUILL says only what your screen reader cannot know. When the task ends, you hear its summary, for example “Done. 2 changes applied: Changed the document; Ran a command. Each can be undone with Control Z.” The summary is built from what actually happened, not from what the AI says it did, and it also tells you how many changes you declined. A task you stop starts “Stopped.”; one that could not finish starts “The task could not finish.” The AI’s own short summary follows in the conversation. The conversation and status fill in quietly; Shift+Tab takes you to them.

What a task can use. Only QUILL’s own document tools: reading the outline, a section, the selection or the whole document, checking accessibility, proposing an edit, and running a short list of QUILL commands that are safe to run. It cannot use the web, your files, a command window or anything outside the document, and the engine’s own tools for those are switched off. It is told to answer in plain text and never to draw charts or diagrams.

When there is no engine. If GitHub Copilot or Codex is in use but this copy of QUILL cannot start it, you hear “Document tasks need My GitHub Copilot or Codex. Press Ctrl+4 to choose one.” In Safe Mode, document tasks are off with the rest of AI.

What is kept. Nothing about a task outlives it: what the engine keeps while it works is deleted when the task ends, when you press Stop, and again before the next task starts, so even a crash leaves nothing behind. The changes you allowed stay in your document, where Ctrl+Z takes each one back.

Propose Alt Text

AI > Propose Alt Text… opens the same window as Do a Document Task with the request already typed in: give alt text to every picture in this document that has none. Press Enter to send it as it is, or change it first. It has no key of its own, because Ctrl+3 gets you to the same window.

The task lists the document’s pictures, Markdown and HTML alike, and proposes short alt text for each one that is missing it, one picture at a time. Each proposal opens Review AI Changes on just that change, so you hear exactly what will be written, and each one undoes with Ctrl+Z. Pictures that already have alt text are left alone.

If the AI in use can see pictures, the task may ask to look at one. Allow This Step? names the picture (“Send the picture beach.png from this document to your AI, to describe it”), and the first picture on each AI also asks whether pictures may go to that company at all. Only pictures saved on this computer are ever sent; a picture on a web address is never fetched, and the task writes its proposal from the words around it and tells you it could not see it. On an AI that cannot see pictures, every proposal comes from the words around the picture, and the summary says so.

A Word or EPUB file you opened through Import / Convert keeps its pictures inside the original file, so the task can propose alt text for them from the words only.

Project tasks: coding with an AI

A project task is Do a Document Task for a folder of code. You tell the AI what you want done in your project, and it plans, reads your files and proposes changes, but nothing is written until you accept it and no command runs until you allow it.

This section is for people who write code. Everything in it is part of the Developer feature profile, and nobody else meets any of it: not in the menus, not in the command palette and not in the tutorials.

Turning it on: the Developer profile

Choose the Developer profile in Profiles and Features…, or press Alt+Shift+P and pick it from the list. It used to be called Developer and Power Text, and it keeps the regular-expression, cleanup and analysis tools it always had. It is the only profile, apart from Full Quill, that turns on the coding features: project tasks, commands and tests, undo for every change an AI makes, coach mode, and the git extras.

The other developer tools in QUILL live behind the same profile now: the shell, Git and GitHub, Codespaces, IntelliSense, the Developer Console and the agent standards check. In every other profile they are off, not just quiet.

If you want these tools without the rest of the Developer profile, open **Help

Feature Profiles > Manage Individual Features** and check Developer Tools. That one feature carries all of them.

If you leave the Developer profile while a project task is running, the task finishes first and its window stays open until then. You hear “Developer tools are off now. The project task that was running will finish first; its window stays open until then.”

If you press a project command without the Developer profile, you hear “Project tasks are part of the Developer profile. Choose the Developer profile, or turn on Developer Tools in Customize Features.” In Safe Mode, project tasks are off with the rest of AI.

Which AI can do a project task

A project task needs an AI that can use tools. These can:

  • GitHub Copilot, on your own Copilot plan.
  • Codex, on your ChatGPT plan.
  • Claude, on My own key with the Anthropic Claude provider. QUILL drives it through the Claude Agent SDK, with your own Anthropic key, and only QUILL’s project tools are open to it.
  • Ollama on this computer, on My own key, as practice mode. QUILL labels it “Practice, on this computer. Nothing is sent. Slower and less capable.” It is a good way to learn how a task goes without an account, but it needs a model that can use tools, and it makes more mistakes.

QUILL’s free AI does not run project tasks. Choose the one you want in Choose Your AI (Ctrl+4).

Every engine gets the same tools, the same review and the same questions. What you hear does not change with the engine.

Opening a project

A project is a folder. Choose File > Open Project Folder…. The window lists your recent projects first; press Enter on one to open it, or choose Browse for a Folder… to pick any other folder. You hear “Project cli-tool is open, on branch main.” (or just “Project cli-tool is open.” when the folder is not a git repository).

The status bar gains a Project cell, the first time you open a project. It says “Project cli-tool, main”, and adds “, waiting for you” when the task has a question you have not seen. Enter on the cell opens the task.

File > Close Project closes it. If a task is still running, QUILL asks you to stop it or let it finish first.

The task window

With a project open, Ctrl+3 opens Do a Project Task instead of Do a Document Task. With no project open, Ctrl+3 is the document task, as before. You can also choose AI > Do a Project Task….

Focus starts in Instruction. Type what you want, for example “Add a –verbose flag to cli.py that prints each file as it is processed, and add a test”, and press Enter. While a task runs, what you type goes to it as a follow-up.

The window’s keys:

  • Alt+I Instruction, where you type.
  • Alt+S Send, the same as Enter.
  • Alt+C Conversation, the read-only record of the task.
  • Alt+P Plan puts the cursor on the latest plan in the conversation.
  • Alt+H Changes puts the cursor on the latest list of changes.
  • Alt+O Output opens the last command’s full output as a document.
  • Alt+R Transcript opens the whole task as a document.
  • Alt+U Undo Last Turn.
  • Alt+X Explain Last Turn.
  • Alt+A Coach mode, a check box.
  • Alt+W Allowed Commands.
  • Alt+L Engine Tools.
  • Alt+T Stop.

The status line says which AI is working, the project, and what is happening: working, waiting for you, coach mode, or “Commands run without asking” when you have turned that on.

QUILL speaks only what your screen reader cannot know: the plan is ready, the task needs you, a command’s result, a turn applied, undone, stopped or done. The conversation fills in quietly. No streaming text is read at you.

If you are working in another document when the task needs you, nothing pops up over your typing. You hear “The project task is waiting for you. Press Ctrl+3 to answer.” once, and Ctrl+3 brings the window back with the question open. If QUILL is not the window in front, it says nothing at all; the Project cell tells you when you come back.

The plan

Before it changes anything, the agent writes its plan as numbered steps in plain words, under a Plan heading in the conversation. You hear “The plan is ready. 4 steps.” Focus does not move. Press Alt+P to land on the plan and read it. If you want something different, type it as a follow-up, such as “don’t touch the README”, and press Enter.

The agent reads your files without asking, but only inside the project folder. Secret files, such as .env files, keys, certificates and credential files, are never listed, read or sent.

Review Changes

When a turn has changes, Review Changes opens by itself. Nothing reaches your disk until you accept it here.

The first list, Files (Alt+F), has one row per file, as a sentence: “cli.py, changed, 3 changes, taken”, or “tests/test_cli.py, new file, 24 lines, not taken”. The last word says what you have chosen: taken, not taken, or how many of the changes are taken. Space on a row takes or leaves the whole file. Enter on a row moves you to its changes.

Change details (Alt+D) shows one change at a time, with line numbers and where it is, and whether it is accepted, for example “Change 2 of 3, lines 40 to 44, in parse_args. Accepted.” The buttons:

  • Alt+A Accept takes the change shown.
  • Alt+R Reject leaves it out.
  • Alt+N Next Change, then the next file’s.
  • Alt+P Previous Change.
  • Alt+I Accept All in File.
  • Alt+L Reject All in File.
  • Alt+W Why This Change? reads you the agent’s reason for it.

Enter on the default button, Apply Accepted Changes, writes everything you accepted at once, after QUILL has kept a copy of the project for Undo Last Turn. Escape is Reject All, and nothing is written.

Files you have open

When a turn changes a file you have open in QUILL, the open copy is reloaded in place, with your cursor where it was, and you hear one line, such as “cli.py was updated by the task.” Focus stays where it was.

If the open copy has edits you have not saved, or is in rich text, QUILL leaves it alone and tells you, so your typing is never overwritten. Reload it yourself when you are ready to see the task’s change.

Run This Command?

When the agent wants to run a command, such as the tests, Run This Command? opens. One sentence says what will run and why, for example “Run pytest -q tests/test_cli.py in your project folder cli-tool, to check the new test.” The exact command is in a read-only Command field below it, so you can arrow through it and read it on a braille display character by character.

QUILL, not the AI, looks at the command and adds a Warning line when one of these applies:

  • “This may reach the internet.” for installers and network tools, such as pip install, npm install and curl.
  • “This deletes files.” for del, rm, Remove-Item and the like.
  • “This runs outside the project.” for a path that leaves the project folder.

The buttons are Allow once (Enter), Always allow this command here, and Don’t allow (Escape). Always allow is only offered when there is no warning.

Some things QUILL never runs for an agent, whatever you have allowed:

  • pushing to a remote, such as git push;
  • publishing a package, such as npm publish or twine upload;
  • forcing anything against a remote;
  • changing system settings.

The agent is told “The person keeps this step for themselves”, and tells you the exact command so you can run it yourself if you want to.

When the command finishes, you hear its result in one sentence, such as “Tests: 12 passed, 1 failed: test_verbose_prints_names.” The full output is a read-only document: press Alt+O in the task window, or choose View > Agent Command Output. A command that runs a long time asks “The command has run for 5 minutes. Keep waiting? Choose No to stop it.”

Commands run in your project folder, with your API keys, tokens and QUILL’s own settings taken out of their environment.

What QUILL cannot stop on Windows

Windows gives QUILL no simple way to fence a command in. Once you allow a command, it can reach the internet, read files outside the project, and do anything your own Windows account can do. QUILL cannot prevent that. Your approval is the safety line, which is why QUILL shows you the exact command and asks every time unless you choose otherwise.

Commands Allowed in This Project

Tools > Commands Allowed in This Project…, or Alt+W in the task window, lists the commands this project may run without asking. Always allow this command here adds to it, and you can add your own: a command, or the start of one, such as pytest, which then allows pytest -q tests as well. Only what you typed or chose counts, never a pattern the AI suggests, and never a command with a warning.

Run commands without asking is a check box in the same window. It is off by default. When you check it, QUILL asks you to confirm, and says plainly that the agent will then run any command it chooses without showing it to you, that pushing, publishing and system changes are still never run, and that Undo Last Turn cannot undo what a command did outside the project or on the internet. While it is on, the status line says “Commands run without asking”, and every command it runs is noted in the conversation.

The list and this setting are kept in QUILL, for this project only. A project folder cannot carry its own allow list, so a project you clone can never approve its own commands.

Undo Last Agent Turn

A turn can change many files, so one Ctrl+Z is not enough. Undo Last Turn (Alt+U in the task window) puts back every file the last turn changed, across the whole project, removes the files it made, and says what it did, starting “Undid turn 2.”

It works after you close the task window too: choose Edit > Agent Work > Undo Last Agent Turn, or press Ctrl+Shift+Grave, U (press the QUILL key, then U). QUILL keeps the last 10 turns of each project.

If you have edited one of those files since the turn, undo refuses rather than lose your work. It says which file, for example “cli.py has changed since the agent’s turn. Undo would lose your edits.”, and offers to open the file as it was before the agent’s turn, to compare. Yes opens that copy as a separate document. Your own file is not changed either way.

Undo puts files back. It cannot take back what a command did outside the project or on the internet.

Stop Agent

Stop (Alt+T) in the task window stops the task straight away, and the command it is running stops with it. From anywhere else in QUILL, choose **Edit

Agent Work > Stop Agent**, or press Ctrl+Shift+Grave, K (press the QUILL key, then K). It stops a document task too. If nothing is running, you hear “No agent task is running.”

Coach mode and Explain Last Turn

Coach mode (Alt+A) is a check box in the task window. When it is checked, the agent does not change files or run commands. It reads the project, explains, and points you to a file and line, such as “Look at cli.py line 40”, and tells you what you could type there and why. You make the change yourself in QUILL. It is meant for people learning to code.

Explain Last Turn (Alt+X in the window, or AI > Explain Last Turn) asks the agent to explain what it changed in the last turn and why, file by file, in plain words. Nothing is changed.

In Review Changes, Why This Change? (Alt+W) does the same for one change.

When a command fails, the agent is asked to say what the error means in one plain sentence before it tries a fix.

Agent notes in a project

Many projects carry notes for AI agents: AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, GEMINI.md, .github/copilot-instructions.md and the files in .github/instructions, Cursor’s rules in .cursor/rules, and the older .cursorrules, .windsurfrules and .clinerules. QUILL finds all of them, including an AGENTS.md in a subfolder.

The first time you start a task in a project that has them, Use This Project’s Agent Notes? asks once. You hear which files it found, for example “This project has notes for AI agents in AGENTS.md. Use them for tasks here?” Choose Use Them, Don’t Use Them, or Read Them First, which opens them read-only. QUILL remembers your answer for that project.

Text in those files is never an instruction QUILL obeys. Nothing in them can approve a command or a change; you still decide every one.

Engine tools, only if you choose

By default the AI uses only QUILL’s own project tools, which is what makes review and approval work the same on every engine. AI > Engine Tools for This Project…, or Alt+L in the task window, lists the engine’s own tools as check boxes: for GitHub Copilot, its file reader, search, file finder, editor, shell and web tools; for Codex, its command runner and patch tool; for Claude, its own reader, search, editor, shell and web fetch.

All of them are off until you check them, for that engine and that project only. Each use still asks you first. Know what you are turning on: an engine’s own edit tool writes straight to disk without Review Changes, and its shell can do anything your account can. Undo Last Turn still puts the project’s files back, because QUILL keeps its copy before the turn starts. A turn that has any of them on says so in the conversation and under the plan.

The transcript

The whole task is a document, with a heading for each turn and subheadings for Plan, Changes, Commands and Result, so your heading keys move through it. Press Alt+R in the task window, or choose View > Agent Task Transcript. It opens in QUILL like any other document, so you can find, bookmark, read aloud and braille it. It is Markdown already: Save As puts a copy wherever you want one.

Where QUILL keeps its records

Everything QUILL keeps about a project lives in QUILL’s own app data, in your user profile, never in the project folder: the recent projects, the transcripts, command output, the copies for Undo Last Turn, the allow list, your answer about agent notes and your engine tool choices. Nothing is added to your repository unless you save something there yourself.

The tutorial: Your first project task

It has you make a practice folder, open it as a project, ask for one change, read the plan, review the change, allow the test command and hear the result, undo the whole turn, and try coach mode. Each step tells you what you should hear. The track only shows in the Developer profile.

If the AI you chose is not available

QUILL never quietly answers with a different AI from the one you chose. If what you set up cannot be used right now, QUILL either tells you once, or asks you first; which one depends on where your text would go instead.

When your text would stay on this computer, you are told once. If the provider in your AI settings cannot be reached and Ask Quill falls back to the on-device model, you hear, as Ask Quill opens: “Anthropic was not available, so Quill is using the on-device model instead. Check your provider and key in the AI settings to restore it.” Nothing leaves the computer, so there is nothing to agree to.

When your text would go to a different company, you are asked first. A Use a Different AI Provider? or Send to a Different AI? box opens. The question is the whole message, so your screen reader reads it as the box opens, and No is the default button, so pressing Enter out of habit sends nothing.

  • Ask Quill and QUILL’s AI tools. The provider in your AI settings is unavailable, and Ask Quill’s own setup was once pointed at another cloud provider. Before this change, Ask Quill used that other provider without a word. Now it keeps the on-device model and asks, for example: “Anthropic is not available right now. Use openai instead? That sends your text to openai over the internet. Choose No and nothing is sent.” Yes uses it for the rest of this chat. No keeps the on-device model.
  • The pad, Ask, the Prompt Library, conversations, Ask About an Image and document tasks follow the one choice in Choose Your AI. If the AI you chose cannot take a request (its key has gone from Windows’ credential store, or OpenAI or GitHub ended its sign-in), nothing is sent and you hear why, for example “Your ChatGPT plan sign-in has ended. Nothing was sent. Press Ctrl+4 to sign in again or choose another.” If nothing is in use at all, you hear “No AI is in use. Choose one.” and Choose Your AI opens. QUILL never picks another AI for you; switching is always something you do.

Changing the AI yourself is never questioned: signing in or out, choosing Use This, or saving or removing a key all count as your choice.

Limits. A request that fails (no connection, a limit reached) is not a fallback: you hear why it failed, once, and Ask Quill may suggest switching to your on-device model, but it never switches for you.

Using your ChatGPT subscription

If you already pay for ChatGPT, QUILL can run its AI commands on your plan. There is no key to paste and no bill per request.

  1. Press Alt+F5 (AI > Accounts and Usage > Use My ChatGPT Subscription…). Choose Your AI opens on My ChatGPT plan, with its sign-in step ready. (Pressing Sign In… on that row in Ctrl+4 does the same.)
  2. Press Open the Page. Your browser opens on OpenAI’s own sign-in page. Allow “QUILL” to use your plan. If the browser does not open, the step shows the address to paste into one.
  3. Come back to QUILL. The step closes by itself and you hear “Signed in to My ChatGPT plan. It is now in use.”

You stay signed in until you sign out or remove the app in ChatGPT’s settings; a brief problem on OpenAI’s side no longer signs you out.

From then on the pad, its seventeen choices, Ask About This Document and conversations go straight from this computer to OpenAI on your plan, with no charge per request and nothing through QUILL’s servers.

Requests draw on your plan’s own usage limits, which are shared with ChatGPT itself and any other app you have connected. In ChatGPT, Settings > Usage shows how much each app used and lets you set a weekly limit for it. When the limit is reached, QUILL says so and does not switch to an API key; you can wait for it to reset, or choose another AI yourself.

Once you are signed in, everything about the plan is in Choose Your AI:

  • Model, on the plan’s row: your plan’s own models, read from your account, the first marked “(recommended)”. Whichever you choose is saved at once.
  • Plan Options…: Allow web search (off until you check it; when it is on, the model may search the web through OpenAI to answer a question) and Forget on This Computer.
  • Sign Out, the action button while the plan is in use. It asks OpenAI to cancel QUILL’s sign-in and forgets it here. Afterwards no AI is in use until you choose one.
  • AI Usage (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F2) has the plan’s row, and Manage Usage opens your plan’s own page, which shows its limits and what is left.

How the sign-in works

Each QuillVille app signs in on its own. QUILL’s sign-in is not QUILL Lite’s, QUILL Cast’s or Quill Radio’s; each appears under its own name in Apps in ChatGPT’s settings, and signing out of one leaves the others signed in. When another app is already signed in on this computer, the Status line says so, because your browser is then signed in already and Open the Page is a single Allow instead of a password.

Being signed in to your plan and having a saved key do not fight: whichever one you chose in Choose Your AI is the one in use, and the other waits. The plan needs neither the free service’s connection nor its agreement, which covers QUILL’s servers. QUILL keeps only a refresh token, in Windows’ credential store, under QUILL’s own name. About names the account and the model.

When something goes wrong, the message carries a code and tells you what to do next: QUILL-AI-CHATGPT-SIGNED-OUT (sign in again), -LIMIT (you have used the plan’s allowance for now), -UNAVAILABLE, -SIGNIN or -FAILED.

Ask About an Image (Ctrl+F5)

This is the one AI command that works on a file. Choose a JPEG, PNG, WebP or GIF picture, ask a question about it or none, and hear what the model sees: a description written for a blind reader, with any text in the picture copied out exactly. The answer opens in an Image Description window with Insert Below and Copy, and Ctrl+Z takes an insert back. It needs an AI that can see pictures: your ChatGPT plan or Codex, a GitHub Copilot model that sees, a Gemini key, or a picture-reading model in Ollama on this computer. On any other AI the menu says Ask About an Image (needs a connection that sees)…, and pressing it sends nothing and offers to switch to one that can. Only the picture and your question are sent.

Pictures and the editor: this, and Suggest alt text with AI in Insert > Image…, are the only AI features in QUILL that look at a picture, and both work only on pictures you choose. QUILL has no AI that looks at your screen or makes pictures or charts; every answer is text.

Tidy Dictated Text (Ctrl+F3)

This is the AI for dictation. The selection, or else the paragraph at the cursor, goes to the model with an instruction to correct what speech recognition got wrong (sound-alike words, names, words run together, punctuation, capitals, fillers and false starts) and to change nothing else. The Tidied Dictation window offers Replace My Selection, and Ctrl+Z takes it back in one step. It runs on any AI except the free one: your ChatGPT plan, GitHub Copilot, Codex or your own key, and never on the free allowance. On the free AI it says so and opens Choose Your AI on your plan’s row.

My Dictation Instructions

You can tell Tidy Dictated Text how you like your writing. Open Dictation Settings (Alt+Shift+F6), press More Dictation Settings… (Alt+A), then My Dictation Instructions… (Alt+I). A short file opens in QUILL. Write one instruction per line, in plain words, and save it:

  • Write numbers as digits.
  • Use British spelling.
  • QUILL is always written in capitals.
  • Never change legal terms.

From then on, Tidy Dictated Text follows them. They go with the passage you tidy, and only then: never with every phrase as you talk. The AI is told that your dictated text is something to correct, never a request to answer, so “ask Sam to send the report” comes back tidied, not answered.

Where your keys are kept

Portable mode and key storage

Normally QUILL keeps AI provider keys in the Windows Credential Manager, which ties them to your Windows user account. If you run QUILL from a self-contained folder, for example on a network share or a USB drive, portable mode keeps all your keys in that folder with the rest of your QUILL data.

Turning on portable mode

The portable bundle comes with an empty data\ folder next to quill.exe, and that folder is what tells QUILL to run portable. There is nothing to set: just run quill.exe from the bundle’s main folder. The Setup Wizard’s Data location page notices the portable install and offers the portable choice for you. To turn an installed copy into a portable one, copy the install folder to a USB drive and make an empty data\ folder at its top level; QUILL switches to portable mode the next time it starts from there.

If you used to set QUILL_PORTABLE=1, you no longer need to; QUILL ignores it now, and the data\ folder is what counts.

Nothing is written to the computer you plug into. Extract the zip, run quill.exe, and your settings, keymap and everything else go straight into the data\ folder beside it. No folder appears in %APPDATA% on that computer.

If you ran a portable QUILL before version 1.0 and your settings look as if they have reset, they have not. Older portable copies kept them in %APPDATA%\Quill on that computer. Copy that folder’s contents into the bundle’s data\ folder to bring them across.

You can still choose otherwise. Picking Windows profile in Preferences or the Setup Wizard is remembered and wins over the bundle, because a choice you make yourself always beats a default. The same goes for every QuillVille app with a portable build: Quill Radio, QUILL Cast, Quill Weather, Audio Studio, Converter, Inkwell, Beacon, Media Player and QUILL Lite.

Keys in portable mode

In portable mode, keys are kept in a file called keys.enc in the QUILL data folder. It is encrypted with Windows DPAPI, so it is protected by your Windows user account.

The encrypted file only opens for the Windows account that made it. Copy it to another computer or another Windows account and it cannot be read, so you will need to type your keys in again there. Portable mode gives you a self-contained folder on one computer; it does not carry your keys from one computer to another.

How QUILL protects your keys and sign-ins

Everything secret QUILL holds for you (AI provider keys, remote site passwords, and the sign-in tokens for any online service you connect) goes into one protected store. On Windows that is the Windows Credential Manager, or the DPAPI-encrypted keys.enc file in portable mode; on macOS it is the login Keychain. QUILL never writes a secret as plain text, and never puts one in a settings file, a log, a diagnostic bundle or its own program files.

When you sign out of a service, QUILL clears every secret that service stored, in one step, so nothing is left behind. No secret is ever part of QUILL itself, so there is nothing to dig out of the program: your keys and tokens live only in your operating system’s protected store, tied to your account. If QUILL ever adds a sign-in for another service, such as an online library, its tokens will be kept there too.

Keys from environment variables

For developers and test machines, keys can also come from environment variables, whichever storage mode is on. These always win and are never saved to disk.

The AI Library

AI > AI Library… is the one home for everything QUILL can do with AI on your writing. It has three tabs, all with the same buttons (Run, Edit, Enable/Disable, Import, Export):

  • Prompts. Single instructions you run on the selection or document, such as “Rewrite warmly” or “Summarize”. New, Edit, Delete, and Promote a prompt into a Skill.
  • Skills. Workflows of several steps, saved as .sqp packs you can share. Run, Import, Export and Remove them, and Promote a Skill into an Agent.
  • Agents. The catalogue of agents that can use tools (Writing Companion, Reviewer, Code Doctor and more), plus any you have saved yourself. Run them through the reviewed gateway, or Validate one against the agent standard.

The three grow into one another. Promote turns a Prompt into a Skill and a Skill into an Agent, so you can start simple and add power when you need it. Anything you build is yours to Export and share, and a friend or colleague can Import it.

Build an AI Action

On the Skills tab, Build Action… opens a simple form. It is the easiest way to teach QUILL something new, and there is no syntax to learn.

  1. Name your action, for example “My Monday standup notes”.
  2. Start from a built-in example (Meeting Minutes, Action Items and the rest) or a blank page. An example fills in the instructions for you to adjust.
  3. Describe what you want in plain language. That is all the “programming” there is: no Markdown, no metadata, no syntax.
  4. Attach a reference if you like: an agenda, your house style, or an earlier example you were happy with. QUILL matches its format and wording, as in “Make minutes that look like last month’s.”
  5. Save. Your action becomes a real Skill in the Library, ready to Run on any document, change later, Promote to an Agent, or share.

Prompt Library

The full prompt manager is the Prompts tab of AI > AI Library…, which holds Prompts, Skills and Agents in one window. (Type “Prompt Library” in the Command Palette, Ctrl+Shift+P, to open the older stand-alone prompt window this section describes.)

On the left is a list of every prompt; type in the search field to filter it by name or category. On the right is the selected prompt’s instructions, and an optional input field where you can type or paste text to use instead of the selection.

To run a prompt:

  1. Select a prompt in the list, or type in the search field to find it.
  2. If you like, paste text into the input field. Left blank, QUILL uses the selection in the editor, or the whole document.
  3. Press Run with AI. The result opens in the AI Response window.

Built-in prompts

QUILL comes with eighteen prompts you can use straight away or as starting points:

  • Editing and tone: Check Grammar, Fix Grammar, Improve Clarity, Make Concise, Active Voice, Formal Tone, Conversational Tone, Paraphrase.
  • Structure: Summarize, Convert to Bullet Points, Generate FAQs, Step-by-Step Instructions.
  • Writing: Continue from Here, Draft a Speech, Summary Email, Social Media Post (280 characters or fewer).
  • Research: Define This Term, Find Counterarguments.

The ones in bold are the newer one-shot tools.

Built-in prompts are always there. You can change their wording or disable them, but you cannot delete them.

Managing prompts

  • New Prompt. A small window: type a name, choose a category, and write the instructions. Put {selection} where you want QUILL to insert the text.
  • Edit. Change the name, category or instructions of a prompt you made. For a built-in prompt you can change its text; your version is saved and the original is kept.
  • Disable/Enable. Hides or shows a prompt in the list without deleting it. Handy for built-ins you never use.
  • Delete. Removes a prompt you made. Built-in prompts cannot be deleted.

Prompts from Quillins

Quillins can add prompts to the library with a prompts.json file. The bundled ai-writing-prompts Quillin adds 7 more: Expand This, Vary Sentence Rhythm, Make More Vivid, Write a Title, Generate Outline, Suggest Supporting Evidence and Plain Language. They appear in the list and run like any other prompt, but they are not saved into your own library file.

Sharing prompt packs

QUILL shares prompt collections as .pqp (Prompt Quill Pack) files.

  • Import .pqp. Choose a file, and every prompt in it is added. Prompts whose names are already in your library are skipped.
  • Export .pqp. Saves all the prompts you have made to a .pqp file you can share or keep as a backup.

A .pqp file is plain JSON that is easy to read:

{
  "schema": "quill.prompt-pack/1",
  "name": "My Writing Prompts",
  "prompts": [
    {
      "name": "Make Punchy",
      "text": "Rewrite this as a punchy one-liner: {selection}",
      "category": "Editing"
    }
  ]
}

Skill Library

A prompt is one instruction. A skill is a series of steps, and each step can use what the step before it produced.

Skills live on the Skills tab of AI > AI Library…. (The older stand-alone Skill Library window is still in the Command Palette, Ctrl+Shift+P, as “Skill Library”.)

A skill has a name, a description and a list of steps. If the skill asks for choices, such as tone, reading level or kind of output, QUILL asks you for them before it runs. Each step then sends its prompt to the AI in turn and hands the answer on to the next step.

Bundled skills

Four skills come with QUILL, in the ai-writing-skills Quillin:

  • Accessible Rewrite. Looks for plain-language problems in your text (long sentences, passive voice, jargon), then rewrites it for the reading level you choose: Grade 6, 8 or 10. The rewrite replaces the selection when you Accept.
  • Research and Draft. Finds the main topic of your selection, gathers five supporting facts, then drafts a paragraph in the tone and length you choose.
  • Meeting Notes to Action Items. Reads meeting notes, pulls out every action item with its owner and deadline, checks for any it missed, and writes a clean follow-up summary. The result goes to the clipboard.
  • Argument Strengthener. Works out how your argument fits together, finds its weak points and the counterarguments, then writes a stronger version for the audience you choose.

Running a skill

  1. Open AI > AI Library… and go to the Skills tab.
  2. Select a skill in the list.
  3. Press Run. If the skill needs choices, a small window appears; fill them in and press OK.
  4. QUILL says “Running step 1 of N…” as each step starts.
  5. When it finishes, the result window appears. Read it, then press Accept to put it in your document (for skills that do that), or Copy to copy it to the clipboard.

Each step waits for the whole answer before the next one starts, instead of showing it word by word. That keeps every step’s result complete and easy to read.

Writing and sharing skills

A skill is a .sqp (Skill Quill Pack) file: a plain Markdown document with YAML front matter at the top. Open any .sqp file in QUILL to read and edit it, and share a skill by sharing the file. The appendix on writing skills, at the end of this guide, walks you through it, and the full reference is section 20 of docs/quillins.md.

To check a skill file for mistakes before you share it, run:

python -m quill.tools.sqp_validator yourskill.sqp

The rest of the AI menu

Once Show advanced AI features is checked, the AI menu holds everything QUILL’s assistant can do. Here are the parts this chapter has not covered yet.

Quick writing actions

AI > Transform Selection has the small writing chores, so they never pull you out of the editor: Rewrite Selection, Summarize Selection, Expand Selection, Continue Writing, Fix Grammar and Generate Table of Contents.

Rewrite Selection, Summarize Selection, Continue Writing and Fix Grammar work with or without a selection:

  • Rewrite Selection and Fix Grammar act on your selection if you have one. Otherwise they use the paragraph at the cursor.
  • Summarize Selection acts on your selection if you have one. Otherwise it summarizes the whole document.
  • Continue Writing uses your selection as the lead-in if you have one. Otherwise it continues from the full document.

QUILL tells you the scope it chose before anything happens, for example “Rewrite paragraph (42 words)”, so you always know what the action will change. If there is nothing to act on, QUILL says so (“Nothing to rewrite”) and sends nothing. If AI is turned off, these actions say “AI is turned off. Enable ‘Use Artificial Intelligence’ in the AI menu.” and do nothing else.

On the on-device model, Summarize works in two passes. The first pulls the plain observations out of your text. The second writes a short summary from those observations, to a word budget, with the original out of view. This keeps a small local model much closer to what your text actually says, so it does not invent its own interpretation. Cloud providers do it in a single pass, because they do not need the help.

Rewrite & Improve Selection

AI > Rewrite & Improve Selection changes with the kind of document you are in, so it only offers what makes sense there. In ordinary writing it has Rewrite clearly, Shorten, Make warmer, Review and Check accessibility. In a code file it offers jobs for code instead. Select the text first and choose one. The rewritten text takes the place of your selection, and Ctrl+Z puts the original back if you prefer it.

Run Agent

An agent is a helper with one job and the skills for it. AI > Run Agent lists every agent QUILL has: Accessibility Editor, Citation & Link Fixer, Code Doctor, Data Cleaner, GitHub Maintainer, Markdown Publisher, Math Tutor, Meeting Notes to Action Items, Plain-Language Rewriter, PRD Architect, QUILL Concierge, Release Notes Builder, Researcher, Reviewer, Summarizer and Writing Companion. Choose one and it works on your selection or your document, whichever it was made for. One that changes your selection does it in place. One that changes the whole document shows you a preview first, and nothing changes until you accept it. One that only reports opens what it found in a new document.

Agents can only use a short, safe list of editing tools, and they still need AI turned on. In Safe Mode, or with AI off, the items stay on the menu but tell you why they cannot run. The AI Library, below, is where you look at an agent more closely, or make your own.

Your writing instructions

AI > More > Writing Instructions… opens a short document of rules the assistant always follows when it writes for you: who you are writing for, the tone you like, words you never want to see. The first time, QUILL starts it for you with those three headings to fill in. It is an ordinary document, so edit it however you like and save it. The assistant reads it again every time it writes, so a change counts straight away.

Writing Assistant, Prompt Studio and Agent Center

These three older windows are no longer on the menu, because the AI Library now does their jobs in one place. You can still reach them by name from the Command Palette (Ctrl+Shift+P):

  • Writing Assistant finds the QUILL commands that match what you type, and offers ready-made prompts for rewriting, summarizing, continuing and fixing grammar. It is also where Ask Quill Chat lives, described in the next topic.
  • Prompt Studio lets you build your own reusable prompts with template variables.
  • Agent Center writes a guided plan for a task. You review the plan before sending it to the Writing Assistant.

Run Python

Run Python…, a button in the Writing Assistant, runs a small piece of restricted Python against the current document text and selection, so you can transform text in ways no menu command offers. The restriction is a list of allowed imports and some limits on time and memory. It is not a security boundary. Only run Python code you trust or wrote yourself.

Connecting to an AI provider

The quickest way in is Use My Own AI Key (AI > Accounts and Usage > Use My Own AI Key…, Alt+F2). In short:

  1. Choose a provider: OpenAI, Google Gemini, Anthropic Claude, OpenRouter, Ollama Cloud, a custom OpenAI-compatible service, or Ollama on this computer.
  2. Paste the key, unless it is Ollama on this computer, which needs none.
  3. For a custom service or a local Ollama, check the Server address.
  4. Press Test the Key (for Ollama, Test the Connection). The Model list fills from your account or from Ollama.
  5. Pick a model and press OK. The basic AI commands and the advanced features both use it from then on.

Most cloud providers come with their host URL already filled in, so setup is mostly about the key. For an unusual OpenAI-compatible endpoint, choose Custom and set the host and model yourself. With Google Gemini, a model you pick from the list QUILL fetches works as it is. Gemini names its models models/gemini-..., and QUILL drops that prefix for you.

QUILL keeps any key you enter in Windows Credential Manager, with a DPAPI-encrypted file as a fallback, and speaks the result of every check in plain words.

Connection status messages

Each message tells you what to do next:

  • “Authentication failed. Check your API key.” The key was rejected (HTTP 401). Enter it again.
  • “Access denied. Your API key is valid but lacks permission for this model or region.” The key works but is not allowed for that model, region or billing tier (HTTP 403). Check the provider’s model access, billing or quota.
  • “The AI provider is warming up. Try again in a moment.” The model is still loading. QUILL retries briefly on its own before telling you this.
  • “The local AI server is not running. Start Ollama and try again.” QUILL could not reach your local endpoint.
  • “Rate limited by the AI provider. Wait a moment and try again.” You sent requests too quickly.
  • “Your saved API key could not be unlocked on this device. Open AI Connection and enter the key again.” This shows in the AI status line when a saved key cannot be decrypted. It can happen after you move a portable copy of QUILL to a different Windows account or computer. Open AI Connection and enter the key again.

Trust and privacy baseline

  • The first time you run QUILL, it shows a trust and privacy consent acknowledgement.
  • QUILL does not keep AI chat transcripts by default.
  • Nothing goes to a cloud service until you choose an AI action yourself.
  • API keys are stored in Windows Credential Manager when it is available, with DPAPI-encrypted storage as a fallback.

For the full policy, see the repository’s docs/legal/PRIVACY.md and docs/legal/RESPONSIBLE_AI_USE.md. The AI Privacy Reference at the end of this chapter lists exactly what each AI action sends, and where.

Ask Quill Chat setup (on-device AI)

Ask Quill Chat (AI > Ask Quill…, Alt+Q) is a message-style assistant. You type, it answers. It can also draft text, suggest edits and run QUILL commands for you, but nothing changes until you approve it.

It can run entirely on your own computer:

  • Windows and Linux: llama.cpp (llama-cpp-python, with a GGUF model).
  • macOS on Apple Silicon, macOS 26 or later: Apple Foundation Models.

To set up the on-device model:

  1. Install the dependencies: pip install -r requirements.txt
  2. Put a .gguf model in %APPDATA%\\Quill\\models\\ (Windows), or set the QUILL_LLAMA_MODEL environment variable to the model’s full path.
  3. Open AI > Ask Quill… and send a prompt. (The older Writing Assistant window is no longer on the menu; type “Writing Assistant” in the Command Palette, Ctrl+Shift+P, if you still want it.)

You can connect another provider instead of the on-device model: Ollama on your own computer or in the cloud, or a custom endpoint.

Getting around the chat

The whole conversation is an accessible web page. Each turn is a heading named for the speaker, so you can jump between turns with your screen reader’s heading keys. New replies are announced as they arrive, and the message box sits inside the page. Press Escape to close the chat. It has been checked with NVDA, JAWS and VoiceOver.

A bar at the top always shows the active provider and model, so you know what is answering. Select Change provider or model to open the picker right there. Choose a provider and model, enter a key if one is needed, and select Save. That choice also becomes the default for your next chat.

Asking out loud and hearing the answer

Press Ctrl+F9 in Ask Quill to ask a question by voice. Press it once to start recording and again to stop. QUILL turns your speech into text on your own machine and sends the text. Nothing you record leaves the computer.

AI > Voice Reply Settings… chooses how answers come back. The setting covers every reply, however you asked:

  • Announce a short summary: the default. Brief, spoken by your screen reader, offline and free.
  • Show as text only: nothing is spoken. Read the reply in the transcript.
  • Read aloud with QUILL’s own voice: the full reply, in whichever offline voice you use for Read Aloud (Kokoro, Piper, eSpeak, DECtalk, SAPI). Offline and free.
  • Read aloud with the AI voice: the full reply, in a voice from OpenAI or Google Gemini.

A few things to know about these choices:

  • When a reply is spoken, you hear all of it. The length limit is only for announcements, so a spoken reply is never cut off mid-sentence. Set the limit to 0 to have the whole reply announced too.
  • The length you set covers everything Ask Quill announces: answers, error messages, and the summary of an edit it suggests. Errors and edit suggestions are always announcements, whichever reply mode you pick, and follow the length you chose.
  • Choosing AI voice shows the provider, model and voice. The voice list always matches the provider you picked, so you cannot pair an OpenAI provider with a Gemini voice by mistake. Preview this voice reads a sample line so you can hear it first.
  • The AI voices cost money and are less private than the others. The reply text goes to OpenAI or Google to be spoken, and you are billed per character. The dialog tells you this and estimates the cost of a typical reply. QUILL never picks an AI voice for you.
  • If an AI voice cannot be used, for instance because there is no API key for that provider, QUILL reads the reply in an offline voice instead and tells you why. You never lose an answer because a voice was unavailable.

QUILLBee’s tip: Every turn in Ask Quill is a heading, so your screen reader’s heading key hops from answer to answer. When you are done, Escape closes the chat.

Putting chat content into your document

  • The Insert into document controls at the bottom put chat content straight into the editor. Choose the scope, Last response or Entire transcript, and the format, Plain text, Markdown or HTML, then select Insert. The whole transcript includes the speaker labels. The last response goes in as just its content.
  • Copy Last Response copies the most recent reply to the clipboard.

Reading back, listing and exporting the conversation

Three things help in a long chat, and they work the same way in the AI Conversation window (see Having a conversation, earlier in this chapter).

Hear a recent message without moving. Press Alt+1 to hear the newest message, Alt+2 for the one before it, and so on up to Alt+0 for the tenth newest. Each one is said with its place and its speaker, for example “Message 7 of 12, You: what about the budget?”. The newest starts with the word “Newest”. Nothing moves: focus stays in your message, and anything you had half typed is still there. These keys work from the message box and from anywhere else in the Ask Quill window.

  • With fewer messages than the key asks for, QUILL says how many there are, for example “There are only 3 messages so far. Alt+3 reads the oldest.”
  • The message is read as words, not Markdown: no hash marks, stars or link addresses. To read it exactly as it arrived, use the message list below.
  • Example: you ask for a summary, then ask two follow-up questions. The summary is now five messages back (your two questions and their two replies came after it), so Alt+5 reads it again while you keep typing your next question.

Message List… (a button at the bottom of the window; Tab reaches it) shows every message in one list, oldest first, each row like “Message 2 of 6, Quill: Here is the plan…”. The list starts on the newest message, or on the row you were on last time.

  • Enter (or Open) shows that message in a reading window where its headings and tables are real ones. In your screen reader’s browse mode, H moves by heading and T by table, and the table commands read a row or a column with its headers.
  • Read as Text shows the same message exactly as it arrived, Markdown and all, in a plain text box you can arrow through, select and copy.
  • Close either one with Escape and you are back on the same row of the list.

Export Conversation… saves the whole conversation to a file of its own. Choose Word document or Text file in the Save As window and name it.

  • Every message is kept, in order, with who said it. In a Word file each message is a level-2 heading, “Message 3 of 12, Quill”, with the message underneath. Headings inside a reply become level 3 and lower, and tables become real Word tables. In a text file each message is a paragraph that starts with its speaker, “You:” or “Quill:”.
  • If the file already exists, the Save As window asks before replacing it. Choosing Cancel writes nothing.
  • Your document is never touched. Exporting does not insert anything, change your document’s name or mark it saved. (To put chat text into your document, use Insert into document above.)
  • If the file cannot be written (a folder you cannot write to, or a drive that is full), QUILL says why, and any file already there is left as it was.

Headings in the chat. Each message is a level-2 heading naming who said it. A reply that has its own headings now has them at level 3 and below, so your screen reader’s heading list shows the conversation, and each reply’s sections sit inside their message.

If the chat window cannot show its web view (on a few computers the built-in browser component is missing), the conversation is a plain list instead, one row per message. Press Enter on a row to open that message with its headings and tables, and you come back to the same row. When a new reply arrives while you are reading the list, your place in it does not move.

What the assistant will and will not do

  • It answers in the chat by default. Greetings and questions never turn into document edits.
  • Anything that would change your document (insert, replace, run a command) waits for you to choose Approve or Discard.
  • If the model or runtime is unavailable, QUILL tells you plainly and changes nothing.
  • Train Writing Style (AI > More > Train Writing Style…) teaches the assistant your own style, from samples or from the current document.

Custom Instructions

Every AI task in QUILL comes with built-in instructions that tell the AI how to behave. For example, the spell check instructions tell it to leave technical terms alone and not to flag British spellings. Each set is plain text, sent ahead of every AI request for that task. The Instructions tab in the AI Hub lets you read them, add to them, replace them, or turn them off.

To change a task’s instructions:

  1. Open AI > AI Hub… and go to the Instructions tab.
  2. Select a task from the list on the left, for example “AI Spell Check”.
  3. The built-in default appears in the lower panel for reference.
  4. Type your own instructions in the editor above it. Leave the editor empty to use the built-in default. Tasks you have changed show a * in the list.
  5. Use Copy Default to Editor to start from the built-in text and change it, instead of writing from scratch.
  6. Use Reset to Default to throw away your changes and go back to the built-in text.
  7. Clear Enable custom instructions for this task to send no instructions at all for that task. The AI then gets only the base prompt.
  8. Select OK to save. Changes take effect at once.

What each task gets by default

Task Default instruction focus
Ask Quill / Chat Calm, direct expert partner; insert-only text returns without preamble
AI Spell Check Copy editor: genuine errors only, preserve technical terms and consistent spellings
Grammar and Style Professional editor: actionable issues, preserve author voice
Rewrite Improve clarity without erasing author voice; no added content
Summarize One-fifth length, same register, core argument only
Expand Develop into richer prose, match tone exactly
Table of Contents Explicit headings only, Markdown hierarchy, no invented structure
Translate Natural expression over literal fidelity, preserve formatting
AI Thesaurus Contextual synonyms ranked by interchangeability, register noted
Document Q&A Grounded answers only; “not in document” stated explicitly
Research Agent Key points, assumptions, gaps, and suggested next actions
Accessibility Tune-Up Plain language, short sentences, descriptive text suggestions

The Summarize default is what cloud providers get: one pass, aiming at about one-fifth of the original length. On the on-device model, Summarize runs its two passes instead, and the second writes to a fixed word budget of about 150 words, not a fraction of the original, which stops a small local model padding or inventing. Your own instructions still shape how that final summary reads.

Markdown documents keep their formatting

When the document you are working on is Markdown, QUILL adds one more sentence to the instructions for every task that hands your own text back: AI Spell Check, Grammar and Style, Rewrite, Summarize, Expand and Translate. (Check Grammar with AI is a Prompt Library prompt rather than one of these tasks; edit its prompt if you want it to say the same.) It tells the AI to keep the Markdown exactly as it is (headings, lists, bold and italic, links, pictures, tables and code), never to remove or convert it, and never to report it as a spelling, grammar or style mistake.

Before this, only Translate had that rule. A rewrite of a Markdown file could come back with its ## headings turned into plain sentences and its bullet list run together into a paragraph, and the grammar check could list **bold** as a typing mistake.

How QUILL knows it is Markdown. The same way the rest of QUILL does: the Document Language you pinned for this document, if you pinned one; otherwise the file’s name (.md, .markdown, .mdown or .mkd); and, for a document with no such name, what is in it (headings starting with #, list items, links or code fences near the top).

An example. In notes.md, select:

## Next steps

- email the team abuot the date
- book the room

and run AI > Proofread > Grammar and Style Check…. The check reports “abuot” and nothing else: not the ##, not the hyphens.

Limits. The sentence is added to your own instructions too, and to a task whose instructions you have turned off, because keeping your document’s structure is not a matter of style. Answers about a document (Document Q&A, the Research Agent, the AI Thesaurus) are new text rather than yours, so they are left alone. The Free AI Assistant pad and the AI Conversation window use QUILL’s free service’s own instructions, which are not covered by this setting.

Sharing instructions

Your custom instructions are saved in <AppData>/Quill/ai_custom_instructions.json. You can copy that file to another computer, or share single task prompts with other QUILL users.

How custom instructions reduce cost

QUILL sends your instructions as a separate system message, apart from the document text. That lets your AI provider cache the unchanging part of the request, so you are not billed in full for sending the same instructions every time:

  • Anthropic Claude: QUILL marks the system message with cache_control: ephemeral and sends the required beta header. Claude keeps it cached for five minutes, and each cache hit costs about 10% of the normal input price. If you run several AI tasks in one sitting, the instructions usually come from the cache on every call after the first.
  • OpenAI (GPT-4o and later): caching is automatic. OpenAI caches prompt beginnings longer than 1024 tokens and reuses them within a session at about 50% of the normal input price. Because the system message is always the same, it qualifies whenever it reaches that size.
  • Ollama and other local models: the model server handles caching itself. There is nothing to set up, and the system message is sent once per connection.
  • Gemini: instructions go in Gemini’s own systemInstruction field. Whether they are cached depends on Google’s current context-caching policy.

You do not have to set anything. As long as at least one custom instruction is on, or the built-in defaults are in use, QUILL uses caching with every provider that supports it.

AI engines

QUILL’s agent is the step-by-step help behind Ask Quill and the AI Library agents, where you review every change. It can run on more than one engine. The built-in Native engine always works, with whichever provider you connected above. Two other kinds are for people who already pay for something:

  • GitHub Copilot: if you already pay for GitHub Copilot, you can use that subscription directly. A short spoken device code signs you in, no API key needed, and it runs on your plan’s models.
  • Claude Agent and OpenAI Agents: these connect with the Anthropic or OpenAI API key you already have. They do not use a ChatGPT or Claude chat subscription, because those plans do not include API access.

To choose an engine, open AI Hub > Engines, the Engines tab of the AI Hub:

  1. Pick GitHub Copilot, OpenAI Agents or Claude Agent, or leave it on Native.
  2. Select Set Up / Install…. For Copilot, which now comes with QUILL, this is only the sign-in: the same step as Choose Your AI, with GitHub’s code spaced out and on the clipboard. For the other two, the first time, QUILL installs the small connector it needs. OpenAI Agents and Claude Agent use your own API key instead of a sign-in, so once the connector is installed, Set Up opens a small dialog where you paste, save or remove that key. It also tells you whether a key is already saved. The change applies at once, with no restart.
  3. Select Set as Active Engine. From then on, Ask Quill and the agents run on that engine, and a status line names it.

The AI Setup Wizard offers this too. Choose “Use an AI agent you already pay for” and it takes you straight to the Engines tab when it finishes.

Whichever engine you pick, your document is just as safe. QUILL makes every edit itself: the engine only suggests, and each change arrives as an accessible accept or reject preview that undoes in one step. The vendor agents run in a text-only mode with their own file-editing and shell tools switched off, so only QUILL can touch your document, and only with your approval. All of this is optional, off until you turn it on, and disabled in Safe Mode.

Signing in to GitHub for Copilot from inside QUILL needs QUILL’s own GitHub registration, which is built in. Until a build carries it, the Sign In button says sign-in is not available yet, and Copilot still works if you have signed in with the Copilot or GitHub command-line tool. Copilot keeps its own sign-in, separate from the one QUILL uses for your repositories, so signing out of one leaves the other alone.

AI Language Tools

These commands in the AI menu do language work on your document: put scrambled text back in order, check spelling and grammar, translate, and find the right word.

Improve Reading Order (AI)

Some documents arrive with their text in the wrong order: a two-column PDF that comes out as one jumbled stream, a page with sidebars or text boxes, or lines out of sequence. AI > More > Improve Reading Order… (also in the command palette) sends the current document’s text to your AI provider and asks it to put the text back in natural reading order. It merges columns into one flow, joins lines broken mid-sentence, and works out headings, lists and tables. It keeps your exact wording, and never summarizes, adds or removes anything.

  • It always asks first. Before anything is sent, QUILL names the provider, its host, and the document’s approximate size in pages. Choose No and nothing is sent.
  • Your document is never changed. The improved version opens as a new, unsaved document. Pressing Ctrl+S there offers Save As, so you decide whether and where to keep it. Your original stays exactly as it was.
  • Limits. It will not take a document longer than the page limit (Settings > reading_order_max_pages, default 40), so a huge, slow or costly request cannot happen by accident. It is unavailable in Safe Mode.
  • It uses your own AI provider. It is not tied to any one service. It uses whichever AI you set up in AI > Set Up AI: OpenAI, Claude, Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Ollama (local), Ollama Cloud, or any custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint. You bring the account and key. QUILL bundles none and adds nothing to your bill.
  • It can stay on your computer. If you have not set up a cloud provider, QUILL uses its bundled, on-device model: Apple’s on-device model on a supported Mac, otherwise a local model that runs on your processor. Then the whole repair happens on your computer with nothing uploaded. The confirmation always names the exact provider and host before anything is sent, and if AI is not set up at all, QUILL offers the setup wizard instead of failing quietly.

For a scanned PDF, which is only pictures of pages with no text, use File > Import > Import / Convert Document (OCR) instead. That reads the page images and can structure the result. Improve Reading Order is for a document that already has text, just in the wrong order.

AI Spell Check

QUILL can send your document to your AI provider for a spelling check. An AI connection must be set up and turned on, and no text is sent until you choose the command.

  • AI Spell Check (AI > Proofread > Spell Check…) sends your document and returns a list of corrections. A review dialog lets you accept, skip or override each one before anything changes.
  • AI Spell Check Interactive (AI > Proofread > Spell Check Interactive…) works paragraph by paragraph. While you review one paragraph, QUILL fetches the next one’s corrections in the background, so the dialog stays quick on long documents.

AI Grammar and Style Check

AI > Proofread > Grammar and Style Check… checks the document for grammar, punctuation, clarity, style and word choice. It has no key by default; you can give it one in the Keymap Editor. Issues appear in a list grouped by category, and you can:

  • Show just one category (Grammar, Punctuation, Clarity, Style, Word Choice).
  • Accept or skip single issues.
  • Accept or skip a whole category at once.
  • Accept all the issues that are left.
  • Press Apply and Close to apply the fixes you accepted as one undo step.

Translate Selection and Translate Document

AI > Translate > Translate Selection… and AI > Translate > Translate Document… translate your text into a language you choose in the dialog. Translate Selection has no key by default; you can give it one in the Keymap Editor.

Translate from is the language your text is in. It starts on Detect, which lets the translator work it out. Choose the language yourself when Detect gets it wrong: a short passage, or one that mixes two languages, is easy to misread.

You can choose between two providers:

  • AI Assistant (the default) uses your cloud or local AI. It works with any provider that can follow instructions: OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, Ollama and compatible endpoints.

  • LibreTranslate is a free, open-source translation engine that can run entirely on your own computer, with no internet connection once it is installed. This is the most private choice: your text never leaves your machine. To install it:

    pip install libretranslate
libretranslate

    Then enter http://localhost:5000 as the URL in the translation dialog.

When the translation is ready, you choose what to do with it:

  • Copy to Clipboard copies it without changing your document.
  • Replace Original puts it in place of the selection or the document.
  • Open as New Document opens it in a new, unsaved document tab.

When nothing was translated. Sometimes the text comes back exactly as you sent it. That usually means it was already in the language you picked: you asked for English and the passage was English all along, or the provider quietly turned Spanish into Spanish. QUILL does not dress that up as a translation. The result box says “The translation came back the same as the original. The source language may be wrong; choose it instead of Detect.” and takes focus, so your screen reader reads the sentence to you. Copy to Clipboard, Replace Original and Open as New Document stay unavailable, because there is nothing new to use. Spacing, line breaks and capital letters do not count as a difference, so a result that only rewrapped your lines gets the same message. Choose the language your text is in from Translate from, or pick another provider, and press Translate Now again.

AI Thesaurus

AI > More > AI Thesaurus… finds synonyms for a word using your AI provider. A printed thesaurus lists every sense of a word; this one reads the sentence the word is in and gives you synonyms that fit what you meant. It has no key by default; you can give it one in the Keymap Editor.

  1. Put the cursor on a word, or select it.
  2. Choose AI > More > AI Thesaurus….
  3. The dialog opens with the word filled in and synonyms loading.
  4. Arrow through the list. Each item gives the synonym and a short note on how it is used.
  5. Press Enter or select Replace Word to put it in your document.
  6. To look up another word without closing, type it in the search box.

Only the word (up to 80 characters) and the sentence around it (up to 400 characters) are sent. The full document is never sent for a thesaurus lookup.

Read Aloud with AI Voice

AI > Read Aloud > Read Selection Aloud and AI > Read Aloud > Read Document Aloud use a cloud speech service to read your text in a natural, expressive voice. They sit alongside the on-device Read Aloud, which uses local voices such as SAPI 5, Piper, Kokoro, eSpeak and DECtalk.

Choose the provider, model and voice under Settings > Read Aloud:

  • OpenAI: 11 voices (Alloy, Ash, Ballad, Coral, Echo, Fable, Nova, Onyx, Sage, Shimmer, Verse), with tts-1 (fast) or tts-1-hd (higher quality).
  • Google Gemini: 30 voices (Kore, Puck, Aoede, Fenrir, Zephyr and more), with Gemini 2.5 Flash (fast) or Pro (higher quality).
  • ElevenLabs (export only): premium, audiobook-grade narration, with Multilingual v2 (high quality) or Turbo v2.5 (fast). ElevenLabs is for audio export, not live reading. It needs the optional elevenlabs package (pip install quill[elevenlabs]) and an ElevenLabs API key credential, the same key the ElevenLabs transcription provider uses.

Add the API key for your chosen provider in the AI Hub. ElevenLabs uses your stored “ElevenLabs API key” credential.

  • AI > Read Aloud > Stop AI Reading stops the current reading.
  • AI > Read Aloud > Export Document as Audio… saves the whole document as an audio file you choose: MP3 for OpenAI and ElevenLabs, WAV for Gemini. The status bar shows an estimated cost before the export starts. If you pick ElevenLabs for live reading, QUILL reminds you it is for export only for now.

Long documents are split only between sentences, never in the middle of a word, so the audio never stops at an awkward spot. Gemini exports add a short pause at the end so the last sentence is not clipped.

Your text goes to the provider you chose (api.openai.com or generativelanguage.googleapis.com) in chunks split at sentences. QUILL stores no audio. The AI Privacy Reference below lists the ways to opt out.

Document Q&A

AI > More > Document Q&A… opens a window where you ask questions about your current document, or about a PDF or text file you choose. It stays open while you work, so you can switch back to your document or any other window and return.

  • Ask follow-up questions. The window keeps a history of your questions and the answers.
  • The AI answers only from the document’s text.
  • Each answer includes an excerpt showing where in the document it came from.
  • Copy or Insert puts the answer text at the cursor.
  • For a document longer than 80 000 characters, only the first 80 000 are used, and the window tells you exactly how much (“the answer used the first 80,000 of its 120,000 characters”), out loud as well as on screen. Status changes and errors in this window are spoken too.

To ask about a PDF, select Browse File and choose the file. QUILL pulls the text out on your computer; no internet connection is needed for that.

Reviewing what the AI suggests

Reviewing AI changes word by word

Whenever an AI feature wants to change your document, QUILL opens the Review AI Changes dialog and edits nothing directly. It is a checklist of changes you accept or reject one at a time, and whatever you accept is applied as a single undo step. Nothing touches the document until you say so.

Each change tells you exactly what it is, with the sentence around it, so you do not have to hear a whole line twice and listen for the difference. For example: “Changed ‘quick’ to ‘rapid’ at line 3.” The details pane adds Sentence before and Sentence after, so you can judge a one-word edit in context, and the full old and new lines are still there below for a complete review.

When a change really is a rewrite, with many scattered edits or whole passages replaced, QUILL shows it as old and new lines instead, because forty word pairs in a row are harder to follow than two whole lines. Changes to spacing alone are also shown as lines, never spoken as word edits.

Keeping some words of a change and not others

In prose, one change is often a whole paragraph, and the AI may have changed three words in it: one you like, one you do not, and one you are unsure about. You no longer have to take all three or none.

When the change you are on holds two or more word edits, a second list appears under Changes: Words in this change. Press Alt+W to go to it, or Tab from the Changes list. Each row is one word edit with the sentence it is in, so arrowing down the list reads you each edit in context. Every row starts checked. Press Space to drop an edit: the original words stay there, and the rest of the change still goes in. Alt+C takes you back to Changes, and Alt+D to Change details, which shows the sentence before and after for the word edit you are on.

For example, the AI rewrote “The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. It was very tired that day.” The Words list says:

  • Changed “quick” to “rapid”, in: The rapid brown fox leaps over the lazy dog.
  • Changed “jumps” to “leaps”, in: The rapid brown fox leaps over the lazy dog.
  • Changed “very” to “rather”, in: It was rather tired that day.

Uncheck the first and choose Apply Checked. Your document now says “The quick brown fox leaps over the lazy dog. It was rather tired that day.” You hear “Applied 1 change, 1 of them in part, as one undo step”, and Ctrl+Z takes the whole thing back.

A few things to know:

  • Drop every word edit in a change and the change itself is unchecked; you hear “No word edits left in this change, so it will not be applied”. Check one back and the change is checked again. Checking or unchecking a whole change in the Changes list checks or unchecks all its word edits.
  • Accept All still takes every change and every word edit, checked or not, and the result is exactly the AI’s version. Reject All still changes nothing.
  • A change with only one word edit has no Words list, because its own check box already says everything. A change shown as whole lines (a rewrite, or spacing only) has none either.
  • Changes to spacing between the words of a change go in with the change whenever any of its word edits does; nobody is asked about a space.

Suggest Document Metadata

Suggest Document Metadata… (in the command palette, and you can give it a key in the Keymap Editor) asks your AI to suggest front matter for the current document: a title, a one- or two-sentence summary, a few topic tags and a category. The suggestions open in a review dialog, one field at a time. You hear the field name, what it holds now, and the suggested value, then choose Accept, Accept and Next, Skip or Copy Value.

Two safeguards protect what you already have. If a field already holds something, QUILL asks before replacing it, and the default answer is No. And nothing touches your document while you review: only Apply Accepted writes the fields you approved into the document’s front matter, as one undo step. Close without applying and the document is exactly as it was.

Agentic Document Tasks

The AI menu has quick tasks that do one job on your document or selection and show you the result:

Command What it does
AI > Transform Selection > Rewrite Selection Rewrites the selected text for clarity.
AI > Transform Selection > Summarize Selection Produces a concise summary.
AI > Transform Selection > Expand Selection Develops a brief outline or passage into fuller prose.
AI > Transform Selection > Generate Table of Contents Reads the document and returns a table of contents as a Markdown list, with levels.

All four open an Agent Result dialog, where you can:

  • Read the result, with a step log if a second, refining pass was run.
  • Insert at Cursor: put the result in without replacing anything.
  • Replace Selection: put the result in place of the current selection.
  • Copy: copy the result to the clipboard.
  • Re-Run: run the same task again, if the first result was not right.

AI Privacy Reference

Every AI action in QUILL happens only when you ask for it. Nothing runs in the background. This table shows what each action sends, and where:

Action Data sent Service Opt-out
AI Spell Check Document text (chunked at about 60 000 chars) Your configured AI provider Do not invoke the action
AI Grammar Check Document text (chunked at about 40 000 chars) Your configured AI provider Do not invoke the action
Translate (AI provider) Selected text or full document Your configured AI provider Use LibreTranslate instead
Translate (LibreTranslate) Selected text or full document Your LibreTranslate server (default: localhost) Not needed
AI Thesaurus Word being looked up + one sentence of context (no full document) Your configured AI provider Do not invoke the action
Rewrite / Summarize / Expand / TOC Selected text or full document Your configured AI provider Do not invoke the action
Document Q&A Document text (up to 80 000 chars) + question Your configured AI provider Do not invoke the action
Read Aloud (AI Voice) Selected text or document (split on sentence boundaries) OpenAI (api.openai.com), Google Gemini (generativelanguage.googleapis.com), or ElevenLabs (api.elevenlabs.io, audio export only), per your Settings > Read Aloud choice Use a local voice (SAPI 5, Piper, Kokoro, eSpeak, DECtalk)
Transcribe Audio Audio file bytes (up to 25 MB) OpenAI Whisper (api.openai.com) Transcribe locally with a Quillin extension
Speaker Diarization Audio file bytes (up to 2 GB) Deepgram (api.deepgram.com) Disable diarization in the transcription dialog

What QUILL never does

  • QUILL never sends data in the background. Every AI call comes from something you chose to do.
  • QUILL does not log your document content, API keys or AI responses to any Anthropic or QUILL telemetry endpoint. QUILL has no telemetry.
  • API keys stay on your computer (Windows Credential Manager, with DPAPI as a fallback). They are never sent to a QUILL server.
  • The crash reporter (Help > Save Diagnostics) removes API keys, file paths and anything that looks like a secret before it writes the diagnostic bundle.

Keeping everything on your computer

If you would rather your text never left your machine:

  • Use Ollama (local) as your AI provider. Spell check, grammar and translation requests then go only to Ollama on your own computer.
  • Use LibreTranslate, installed locally, for translation.
  • Use Piper or Kokoro for Read Aloud. These are local voices.
  • Avoid the Transcribe and Diarize actions, which need cloud services.
