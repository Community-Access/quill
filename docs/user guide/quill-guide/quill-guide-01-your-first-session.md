# Chapter 1: Your first session

This chapter is your first sit-down with QUILL, and I will stay with you the
whole way. You will open QUILL, write a few lines, save them, and meet the
handful of keys that do most of the work. If you have never used QUILL before,
start here. If you have, skim the key list near the end and move on to Chapter
2.

## Getting started

If you only have five minutes, do this.

1. The first time QUILL starts, the **Personalise QUILL** wizard offers to set
   up your keyboard layout, your profile and any optional features you want. It
   takes about two minutes. Finish it, or close it and come back later from
   **Help > Personalise QUILL...** whenever you like.
2. Press `Ctrl+N` to make a new document, or `Ctrl+O` to open one you already
   have.
3. Type a few lines.
4. Press `Ctrl+Shift+P` to open the Command Palette. Type `guide`, `spell`,
   `compare` or `glow`, and listen to how quickly QUILL finds what you meant.
5. Press `F6` to move out of the editor and on towards the status bar along the
   bottom of the window. QUILL treats that strip as a place you can work, not
   just something to read. `Shift+F6` brings you back.
6. Try `F7` for spell check, `Ctrl+F` to search, or `Ctrl+K` to insert a link.
7. Press `F1` on any control to hear what it does and which keys work there.

If you ever feel lost, `F1` is the first thing to try. For ideas about the
document you have open, choose **Help > What Can I Say Here?**
(`Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F1`). Between them, they are like having a friend at your
elbow.

> **QUILLBee's tip:** Forgotten where something lives? Press `Ctrl+Shift+P` and
> type a word, like "sort" or "print". The palette says each command's key as
> you arrow past it, so you pick up the keys while you work.

## What QUILL is like to use

A few promises shape everything in QUILL.

- The keyboard always comes first.
- Screen readers are part of the plan from the start, never an add-on.
- Every major action can be reached from the menus, from the Command Palette and
  through the command system.
- Your documents open on your computer and stay there, unless you choose an
  action that uses the network.
- QUILL tells you what changed, where you are and what you can do next.

In day-to-day use, that means QUILL pays a lot of attention to where your focus
goes, gives you status updates that mean something, makes commands easy to
discover, and uses the standard Windows controls your screen reader already
knows. It also tries hard to take the worry out of things. Opening a recovered
draft, comparing two files or checking how well text came out of a scanned
document are all made to be safe and easy to undo.

## A first proper session

Here is what your first real visit to QUILL is likely to look like.

You start QUILL. There is no splash screen to wait through. The window appears
quickly, with a menu bar across the top, the editor in the middle and a status
bar along the bottom. If QUILL finds a screen reader running, it adjusts its
hints and the way it announces things to suit. If QUILL finds signs of an
earlier crash, or work it saved automatically, it offers to recover it for you
straight away.

From there, a good first session goes like this.

1. Open a file with `Ctrl+O`, or make a new one with `Ctrl+N`. If you would like
   a smaller file picker that is easier to use with a screen reader, open
   Preferences (`Ctrl+,`), go to **General** and turn on
   **Use simple file open dialog**.
2. Read or write in the editor.
3. Press `Ctrl+Shift+P` and explore commands, without memorising anything.
4. Open the **Navigate** menu to jump by line, heading, block, region or page.
5. Open the **Tools** menu for spelling, word count, checking extracted text,
   comparing files, macros and help with regular expressions.
6. Choose **Help > Open Keyboard Reference** to see every shortcut that works in
   your setup right now. QUILL builds this reference on the spot, so it always
   matches your current feature profile and any keys you have changed.

The habit this teaches is the most useful one in QUILL: you do not need to go
hunting. If QUILL can do something, you can reach it from wherever you are.

> **QUILLBert found something.** He pressed `Ctrl+Shift+Grave` twice in a row to
> see what would happen, and suddenly every letter he typed moved him around the
> document instead of writing. H took him to a heading, P to a paragraph. That
> is Quick Nav, and it stays on until you press `Esc`, which is how he got his
> typing back. Chapter 2 explains the QUILL key properly.

## The keys that do most of the work

If you want a small set of keys to learn first, start with these.

- `Ctrl+N` new document, `Ctrl+O` open, `Ctrl+S` save, `Ctrl+Shift+S` save as.
- `Ctrl+Shift+P` the Command Palette.
- `Ctrl+F` find, `F3` find next, `Shift+F3` find previous. `Alt+F3` turns Reveal
  Codes on and off, so you can hear the formatting that is normally hidden.
- `Ctrl+G` go to a line. `Ctrl+Shift+G` Document Statistics, the word count and
  more.
- `Ctrl+K` insert a link. `Ctrl+Enter` inserts a page break, as in Word; with
  the cursor on a link, it follows the link instead.
- `Ctrl+Shift+Grave, L` (press the QUILL key, then L) for the List Manager,
  `Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L` for the Structured List Studio, and `Ctrl+Shift+F2` for
  Insert Special Character.
- `Ctrl+F9` starts and stops Locked Dictation, and `Alt+F9` says what dictation
  is doing.
- `F9` says the last result again, if you missed it.
- `F7` spell check, `Ctrl+F7` next misspelling, `Ctrl+Shift+F7` previous
  misspelling, `Shift+F7` the thesaurus.
- `Ctrl+Tab` and `Ctrl+Shift+Tab` move between open documents.
- `F6` and `Shift+F6` move between the parts of the window.
- `Alt+Z` turns soft wrap on and off.

When those feel familiar, open **Help > Open Keyboard Reference** and let QUILL
show you the rest, taken from the keys you actually have.

## What you learned, and where to go next

You have opened QUILL, written something, found commands by name with the
Command Palette, and met the keys you will use every day. You also know the two
ways out of any muddle: `F1` on whatever you are on, and
**Help > What Can I Say Here?**.

If you would like to go over this with QUILL keeping you company, open
**Help > Tutorials...** (`Ctrl+Alt+F1`) and take the track called Your first
hour. Its lessons "Write and save your first document", "Do anything by name"
and "Getting unstuck" follow this chapter closely. On the website, [Your first
hour with QUILL](https://www.quillforall.org/tutorials/01-your-first-hour.html)
walks the same ground at a gentler pace.

When you are ready, Chapter 2, Finding your way around, takes you on a tour of
the window, the menus and the QUILL key.
