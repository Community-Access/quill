# Introduction

Welcome, and thank you for giving QUILL a try.

QUILL is a writing studio for Windows and macOS, made for people who use a
screen reader. I am blind, I use one every day, and I wanted an editor that
was built around the keyboard and the ear from the very first line. QUILL is
what came of that. You can write in it, of course. You can also open almost
any document, have it read to you, dictate into it, check its spelling,
compare two drafts, tidy up a scanned PDF, turn a manuscript into an
audiobook, and a good deal more, all without reaching for the mouse.

The name stands for **Quality, Usable, Inclusive, Lightweight, Literate**.
QUILL is free, and it is made by Community Access.

This guide is written for QUILL 1.0.0. QUILL 1.0 is not out yet, so now and
then the guide may describe something your copy does not have. QUILL is still
in beta, which means you will find rough edges and the odd surprise. If
something confuses you or does not work, please tell us. Chapter 15 shows you
how, and every report makes QUILL better for the next person.

## What you will be able to do

By the time you have read this guide you will be able to open, write and save
documents in all the common formats, and move around a long document by
heading, paragraph or bookmark. You will know how to search and replace,
check your spelling, format text, and have QUILL read your work back to you.
You will be able to dictate, transcribe a recording, and, if you want it, ask
the AI for help with a piece of writing. You will have QUILL set up to say as
much or as little as you like, with your own keys and your own choice of
features. And when something puzzles you, you will know where to find help.

None of this needs to happen on day one. Chapter 1 gets you writing in a few
minutes. Everything after that is there for when you are curious.

QUILL also has guided tutorials, short lessons you do with QUILL open in front
of you, on **Help > Tutorials...** (`Ctrl+Alt+F1`). Whenever a lesson covers
the same ground as part of this guide, the chapter gives you its name, so you
can choose between reading about something and being walked through it. If
you would rather learn by listening, [QUILL, Step by Step](../quill-step-by-step.md)
is a slower companion book written to be read aloud, one skill per chapter.

Now and then a short note from QUILLBee, QUILLVille's guide, is set apart as
a quote: a faster key, a word explained, or something worth knowing before it
catches you out. QUILLBert, the curious porcupine, wanders in once or twice
when he has found something. The guide reads perfectly well if you skip every
one of them, and you can meet the pair properly on the [Meet QUILLBert and
QUILLBee](https://www.quillforall.org/meet-quillbert-and-quillbee.html) page.

## Reading this guide with a screen reader

The guide is built from headings so you can move through it quickly. Each
chapter is a page of its own, and its title is the level 1 heading at the top.
The main topics in a chapter are level 2 headings. When a topic has parts of
its own, those parts sit one level deeper, at level 3, and sometimes at level
4. In JAWS or NVDA, press 2 to move between the main topics and 3 to step
through the parts of a topic. Shift with either number goes back. To see every
heading at once, press Insert+F6 in JAWS or Insert+F7 in NVDA and choose
headings.

The contents page, [QUILL User Guide](../userguide.html), lists every chapter with its main topics.
At the bottom of every chapter are links to the chapter before it, the one
after it, and back to the contents.

The notes from QUILLBee and QUILLBert are block quotes. In a web browser, Q
moves to the next one in JAWS and NVDA, and Shift+Q goes back, so they are as
easy to skip as they are to find.

Every chapter ends with a short section called What you learned, and where to
go next. If you are skimming, those are a quick way to hear what a chapter
covers before you read it.

Keys are written the way QUILL writes them in its menus. `Ctrl+Shift+P` means
hold down Ctrl and Shift together and press P. A menu path such as
**File > Save As...** means open the File menu and choose Save As. If you
have changed a key in QUILL, your key is the one that works; **Help > Open
Keyboard Reference** always lists the keys you have right now.

When a section walks you through something, the steps are numbered. Follow
them with QUILL open beside the guide and you will learn faster than by
reading alone. Alt+Tab moves you between the two.

You do not always need this guide to answer a question. Press `F1` on almost
anything in QUILL, a field, a button, a menu item or the editor itself, and
QUILL tells you what it does and which keys go with it.
