Introduction

Welcome, and thank you for giving QUILL a try.

QUILL is a writing studio for Windows and macOS, made for people who use a screen reader. I am blind, I use one every day, and I wanted an editor that was built around the keyboard and the ear from the very first line. QUILL is what came of that. You can write in it, of course. You can also open almost any document, have it read to you, dictate into it, check its spelling, compare two drafts, tidy up a scanned PDF, turn a manuscript into an audiobook, and a good deal more, all without reaching for the mouse.

The name stands for Quality, Usable, Inclusive, Lightweight, Literate. QUILL is free, and it is made by Community Access.

This guide is written for QUILL 1.0.0. QUILL 1.0 is not out yet, so now and then the guide may describe something your copy does not have. QUILL is still in beta, which means you will find rough edges and the odd surprise. If something confuses you or does not work, please tell us. Chapter 15 shows you how, and every report makes QUILL better for the next person.

What you will be able to do

By the time you have read this guide you will be able to open, write and save documents in all the common formats, and move around a long document by heading, paragraph or bookmark. You will know how to search and replace, check your spelling, format text, and have QUILL read your work back to you. You will be able to dictate, transcribe a recording, and, if you want it, ask the AI for help with a piece of writing. You will have QUILL set up to say as much or as little as you like, with your own keys and your own choice of features. And when something puzzles you, you will know where to find help.

None of this needs to happen on day one. Chapter 1 gets you writing in a few minutes. Everything after that is there for when you are curious.

How this guide is laid out

The chapters follow the order you are likely to want things. Read from the beginning if you are new to QUILL, or jump to the chapter for the job in front of you.

  1. Chapter 1: Your first session. A short, hand-held start: write something, save it, and learn the handful of keys that do most of the work.
  2. Chapter 2: Finding your way around. The window, the status bar, F1 help, the Command Palette, the QUILL key, and a tour of the menus.
  3. Chapter 3: Opening, saving and converting files. Every format QUILL reads and writes, converting between them, and files on other computers and on GitHub.
  4. Chapter 4: Writing and editing. Selecting, copying, the Copy Tray, inserting dates, symbols and emoji, abbreviations, and links.
  5. Chapter 5: Formatting, structure and markup. Headings, lists, tables, Markdown and HTML, maths and citations.
  6. Chapter 6: Moving around and finding things. Find and replace, jumping by heading, bookmarks and marks, Quick Nav mode, and Quill Eraser.
  7. Chapter 7: Spelling and words. Spell checking, the thesaurus, the dictionary and document statistics.
  8. Chapter 8: Reading, reviewing and listening. Read Aloud, Reveal Codes, comparing documents, OCR, audiobooks and keeping your place.
  9. Chapter 9: Dictation and transcription. Speaking into a document and turning recordings into text, on your own computer.
  10. Chapter 10: AI, when you want it. The free AI, your own keys, the assistant, and exactly what is sent where.
  11. Chapter 11: Accessibility, speech and braille. How much QUILL says, low-vision settings, sounds, braille files and GLOW.
  12. Chapter 12: Making QUILL yours. Feature profiles, keyboard packs, your own keys, Quillins and languages.
  13. Chapter 13: Keeping your work safe. Autosave, recovery, restore points, sessions and safe mode.
  14. Chapter 14: Projects, notes and the QuillVille family. Story Studio, the Vault, sticky notes, Git, Mastodon, and the apps that live alongside QUILL.
  15. Chapter 15: Help, updates and support. F1, tutorials, updates, release channels and getting help from a person.
  16. Chapter 16: WordPress. Writing, publishing and looking after a WordPress website.
  17. Chapter 17: Forms. Writing accessible forms, checking them, and filling them in yourself first.

After the chapters come the appendices: the Developer Console, writing your own AI skills, the full list of emoji QUILL can insert, notes from earlier versions, and the Developer Console’s design document. Last of all are the Glossary of QUILL Terms and Getting help.

QUILL also has guided tutorials, short lessons you do with QUILL open in front of you, on Help > Tutorials… (Ctrl+Alt+F1). Whenever a lesson covers the same ground as part of this guide, the chapter gives you its name, so you can choose between reading about something and being walked through it. If you would rather learn by listening, QUILL, Step by Step is a slower companion book written to be read aloud, one skill per chapter.

Now and then a short note from QUILLBee, QUILLVille’s guide, is set apart as a quote: a faster key, a word explained, or something worth knowing before it catches you out. QUILLBert, the curious porcupine, wanders in once or twice when he has found something. The guide reads perfectly well if you skip every one of them, and you can meet the pair properly on the Meet QUILLBert and QUILLBee page.

Reading this guide with a screen reader

The guide is built from headings so you can move through it quickly. Each chapter is a page of its own, and its title is the level 1 heading at the top. The main topics in a chapter are level 2 headings. When a topic has parts of its own, those parts sit one level deeper, at level 3, and sometimes at level 4. In JAWS or NVDA, press 2 to move between the main topics and 3 to step through the parts of a topic. Shift with either number goes back. To see every heading at once, press Insert+F6 in JAWS or Insert+F7 in NVDA and choose headings.

The contents page, QUILL User Guide, lists every chapter with its main topics. At the bottom of every chapter are links to the chapter before it, the one after it, and back to the contents.

The notes from QUILLBee and QUILLBert are block quotes. In a web browser, Q moves to the next one in JAWS and NVDA, and Shift+Q goes back, so they are as easy to skip as they are to find.

Every chapter ends with a short section called What you learned, and where to go next. If you are skimming, those are a quick way to hear what a chapter covers before you read it.

Keys are written the way QUILL writes them in its menus. Ctrl+Shift+P means hold down Ctrl and Shift together and press P. A menu path such as File > Save As… means open the File menu and choose Save As. If you have changed a key in QUILL, your key is the one that works; Help > Open Keyboard Reference always lists the keys you have right now.

When a section walks you through something, the steps are numbered. Follow them with QUILL open beside the guide and you will learn faster than by reading alone. Alt+Tab moves you between the two.

You do not always need this guide to answer a question. Press F1 on almost anything in QUILL, a field, a button, a menu item or the editor itself, and QUILL tells you what it does and which keys go with it.
