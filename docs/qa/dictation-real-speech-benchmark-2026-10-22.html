Dictation speech models tested on real speech, 2026-10-22

This is the record behind QUILL’s dictation model advice: which models QUILL, QUILL Lite and QUILL Transcribe recommend, which are being retired, and why. Every number on the help pages, in the release notes and in the apps’ own recommendation comes from this run. Until this run, the advice rested on ten recordings of a synthetic voice, which flattered every engine.

In one minute

  • English: NVIDIA Parakeet Unified 0.6B made the fewest mistakes of every model that keeps up on one processor thread: 3.2 percent of words wrong on audiobook recordings no model had ever been trained on, and 6.3 percent on Google’s FLEURS read speech. It is now the model QUILL recommends for English on a computer with 8 GB of memory and four cores.
  • Spanish: NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 got 4.4 percent of words wrong on FLEURS Spanish and 7.3 percent on Spanish audiobooks. The built-in Whisper tiny got 30.5 and 27.0, about one word in four. Parakeet is now the recommendation for Spanish on an 8 GB computer.
  • Modest computers: on 4 GB, or with little memory free, the built-in Moonshine (English) and Whisper (Spanish) are still recommended. They are the only models that fit beside a screen reader there.
  • Five English-only models are being retired: Distil-Whisper small.en, Whisper small.en, Distil-Whisper medium.en, Whisper medium.en and Distil-Whisper large-v3. Parakeet Unified beat each of them on accuracy and speed, in the same memory or less. They keep working until the next feature release, and nobody’s model is changed for them.
  • A bug found and fixed: Moonshine base wrote nothing at all for any phrase longer than about eight and a half seconds. It now hears long phrases in pieces (PR #1683), and scores 5.9 percent on the audiobooks.

How it was tested

The recordings. Real people, never a synthetic voice:

Set What it is Recordings Licence

LibriSpeech and FLEURS are public, so some models may have trained on them; those results are shown, but the decisions lean on the held-out audiobooks.

The harness. scripts/dictbench.py, run by scripts/bench_machine_run.py, feeds each recording to a model the way dictation does: one phrase at a time, on one processor thread (the speed budget is per thread, because the screen reader needs the rest), on the processor only. Each model runs in its own process, so its memory figure is its own. Word errors ignore capitals and punctuation; for Spanish they also ignore accents.

The measures.

  • Words wrong: the percentage of the reference’s words the model got wrong (substituted, left out or added). Lower is better.
  • Speed per thread: seconds of computing per second of speech on one thread. Dictation’s budget is 0.25: a quarter of a core, leaving the rest for the screen reader. The apps compare a model’s cost with the bundled Moonshine tiny’s, measured on your own computer by the two-second check, so the advice scales to your machine.
  • Peak memory: the most memory the model used on any set, because a model has to fit the worst minute, not the average one.

The computer. An Intel Core Ultra 7 265H laptop (16 cores, 64 GB), Windows 11, Python 3.14.8, sherpa-onnx 1.13.8, ONNX Runtime 1.31.0. It is much faster than the computers the advice is written for, which is why speed is judged as a multiple of Moonshine tiny’s rather than in raw seconds.

The limits. One computer, one day. Other work was running during some of the sets, so the absolute speeds vary from set to set; the speed factors in the catalogue come from the English FLEURS run, where eight models ran back to back under the same conditions. Screen-reader lag, battery draw and a real 4 GB computer with NVDA or JAWS running were not measured here; that check (B-8 in the dictation plan) still needs a person. Whisper medium and large-v3-turbo were too slow to re-run and keep their earlier figures.

What changed in the apps

QUILL, QUILL Lite and QUILL Transcribe share one dictation engine and one catalogue, so all three changed together.

  • The recommendation uses these numbers. English is ranked by the held-out audiobooks, Spanish by the mean of its two sets, and only models measured in Spanish are offered for Spanish. Speed factors and memory peaks are the real ones. The recommendation now says its figures come from real recordings.
    • 4 GB, two cores: Moonshine tiny for English, Whisper tiny for Spanish.
    • 8 GB, two cores: Parakeet TDT v3 for English and Spanish once the two-second check shows the computer is quick enough (about 0.07 seconds a second or less for Moonshine tiny); a slower or busier two-core computer keeps the built-in models.
    • 8 GB or more, four cores: Parakeet Unified for English, Parakeet TDT v3 for Spanish.
    • “The quickest response” still names Moonshine tiny.
  • An 8 GB baseline, and free memory counts. A computer with 8 GB may give dictation 1.6 GB beside the screen reader (it was 1 GB), and never more than is free when you ask, less half a gigabyte.
  • Tiers follow real speed. Parakeet TDT v3, Parakeet Unified and Whisper base.en are now Lightweight: they keep up on a modest computer. Nemotron is Balanced.
  • Five models are being retired. Each one’s line in the model list starts “Being retired: use NVIDIA Parakeet Unified 0.6B instead.” The first time dictation starts on one, QUILL says so once, in every QuillVille program together, with the measured reason, and says it keeps working until the next feature release. Nothing is switched and nothing is deleted. They are never recommended.
  • Moonshine base hears long phrases. Its decoder failed on anything over about 8.5 seconds and wrote nothing; long phrases are now heard in pieces of 8 seconds or less, cut at the quietest moment, and joined.

What this run could not settle

  • Does Whisper write the opening “¿” and “¡” itself? The scorer now counts them (PR #1684), but FLEURS and the audiobooks have almost no questions. Ten recordings from a native Spanish speaker, with questions and exclamations, would answer it.
  • A real modest computer with a screen reader running. Speed, memory, screen-reader lag and battery on a 4 GB Windows 10 computer with NVDA or JAWS (B-8) still need a person.
  • Whisper medium and large-v3-turbo were too slow to re-run here.
